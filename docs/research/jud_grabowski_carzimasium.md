## 1. The argument in brief

- **The text.** On his 949 embassy for Berengar II, Liudprand gave Constantine VII his own gifts, including "four *carzimasia*", the most precious of all. He glosses: "the Greeks call a child-eunuch, with testicles and penis cut off, a *carzimasium*", and "the merchants of Verdun" do this for "immense profit" and take them to Spain (§4, n. 8).
- **The manuscript.** Clm 6388 (Munich) was overseen by Liudprand himself (Chiesa 1994; contested by Hoffmann and Schreiner, n. 9). His Greek words there are written in Greek letters with a Latin gloss. *Carzimasium* is written only in Latin letters. Grabowski infers that Liudprand heard the word without knowing how to spell it, yet used it confidently as a technical name (§§6–7).
- **The old etymology: Khwarezm.** Georg Jacob (1891) derived the word from Khwarezm, a slave-exporting land. Browe (1936), Lévi-Provençal (1950: *khwarizmi*, "inhabitant of Khwarizm"), Tuchel (1998) and Kuefler (1996) followed (§§9–12). Verlinden reversed it: a slave *bound for* Khwarezm, cut at Verdun and carried east by Radhanite Jewish merchants through Constantinople (§13). Lewicki took it from the name of the *castrators* (§14). Grabowski notes the Khwarezm reading never explains why the word means only child-eunuchs (§9).
- **Grabowski's etymology: a Semitic "cut/short".** Sophocles' Byzantine lexicon (1900) saw a Semitic root meaning "cut". Jastrow gives Aramaic/Hebrew *q-ṣ-r*, "cut, reap, shorten". The *Lexikon zur byzantinischen Gräzität* (1994) proposes Arabic *qaraṣa*, "cut off" (§§22–24). Baldwin (1980) read the ending *-ion* as a diminutive, so: "little shortened one" (§25).
- **A better-attested Greek cognate.** The *Lexikon* also has χαρτζιμος, "eunuch". Ninth- and tenth-century scholia on Lucian's *De Dea Syria* say the Galli were then called by that word (§24, n. 40). Theophanes Continuatus III.43 has a related form (§8).
- **The conclusion.** Because castration was forbidden in Islam, the cutters "should be either Christians or Jews" (§27, citing Mez 1937). A technical slave term "rooted in Jewish language" would make Toch's denial of Jewish participation "improbable" (§27). The word is "strong evidence for the Jewish participation in the slave trade" (§29).

## 2. What *carzimasium* was

- A **child** castrated by **total** removal (testicles and penis). The word is a technical name for that one product, not for all eunuchs (§§5, 21). It was dear enough to be a gift to an emperor.
- Liudprand says it is a **Greek** word. Grabowski's best evidence (χαρτζιμος in Byzantine scholia, a related form in Theophanes Continuatus) actually supports Liudprand: the word was in Byzantine use by the ninth to tenth centuries (§28).
- Only Liudprand uses the Latin form (§8, citing Baldwin).

## 3. Where: Verdun, and which Verdun

- Most readers take it as **Verdun on the Meuse**, in Lotharingia. Grabowski calls its role in the slave trade "perfectly clear", citing Sabbe, Ashtor, Lombard, Verlinden and Trabelsi (§15, n. 24).
- **Alternatives** (n. 23): **Verdun-sur-le-Doubs** in Burgundy, preferred by Toch (2013, p. 183 n. 18) after Aronius (1902, no. 127), who took it from Hüllmann (1826). Hüllmann also allowed that Liudprand may have meant **Verona**.
- **Dissent on the castration centre.** Hirschmann (1996) shows that the whole Verdun reconstruction "is made on assumptions, not on sources". He doubts that Verdun was a castration centre, but still accepts that it lay on a slave route (§15). Constable (1996) notes that **no Arabic source** confirms Verdun's role (n. 24).
- **Independent corroboration of the route:** the *Life of John of Gorze* has John travelling to Córdoba with Verdun merchants (n. 24, via Stein 1922).
- **Arabic sources point to al-Andalus.** Pechina, and for some authors all castration, took place in Spain (§16, citing Roth 1994 and Rotman 2009). Al-Jahiz describes mostly adults (§16). Armenia is also named (n. 18).

## 4. Who did the cutting, per the sources, and how reliable each claim is

| Claim | Source | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| "Merchants of Verdun" do it for profit and take them to Spain | Liudprand, *Antapodosis* 6.6 (949 eyewitness of the gift; hearsay on the cutting) | The only primary witness. It names **no religion**. *Facere* ("do this") may mean "make", i.e. have them castrated, not cut with their own hands. Grabowski concedes Toch "rightfully notes that Liudprand does not mention Jews" (§17). |
| Cut at Verdun, carried east by Jewish Radhanites | Verlinden 1955, 1970, 1979 | A modern reconstruction built on the Khwarezm etymology Grabowski himself rejects (§26). Grabowski calls the route "improbable" (n. 20). |
| Castrated in Spain (Pechina), by Jews | Ibn Hawqal, al-Muqaddasi (via Ayalon, Roth) | Two tenth-century Arabic geographers, with no Latin confirmation. Grabowski does not discuss the "by Jews" part directly. |
| Christians did it | Al-Jahiz (via Ayalon) | Al-Jahiz blames Christians and, per Ayalon, not Jews. Grabowski does not cite this. |
| Muslims could not, so Christians or Jews did | Grabowski §27, after Mez 1937 | An inference, not a source. Ayalon (p. 64) records Muslim merchants who "learned that profession well". |
| The *word* is Jewish | Grabowski §§27–29 | **Weak**; see below. |

**Why Grabowski's conclusion does not hold:**
1. **His own etymon is Arabic.** The *Lexikon* proposes Arabic *qaraṣa*. Grabowski admits "there is no reason to see it as a word coming directly from the Jews" (§27). Two sentences later he concludes the terminology is "of Jewish origin". The Hebrew/Aramaic root he prefers (*q-ṣ-r*, via Jastrow) needs a metathesis he defends only by saying Greek avoided the cluster *sr* (n. 38). A Semitic root is not a Jewish root: Arabic was the language of the Andalusi buyers, and Aramaic was spoken by Christians across the Near East.
2. **A loanword is not a census.** Even a Hebrew etymon would show that a word passed through Jewish speech, not who held the knife. Grabowski's own n. 44 notes that Arabic eunuch terms (*khasi*, *khadim*, *majbub*) do not match the word.
3. **His evidence fits Liudprand's plain statement better.** χαρτζιμος for the Galli in ninth- and tenth-century Byzantine scholia points to an established Greek word (§24, n. 40), which is what Liudprand said.
4. **He concedes the field is against him.** Toch's view "is gaining higher ground" (n. 29, citing Holo 2009). Hirschmann and Irsigler doubt the Jewish element (§15). His criticism of Toch rests on the "Turks" of Ibrahim ibn Ya'qub, which he admits is unresolved (§18).
5. **Standing.** A short Varia article by an author then listed with a student address. It is a philological note, not a survey of the trade.

**Cross-reference to Phelan (2023).** Phelan shows how the "Jewish monopoly of the slave trade" passed from Milman (1829) and Jost through Graetz to Jacobs and the *Jewish Encyclopedia*, hardening into "established and uncontested fact" by repetition. He closes on the "tendency of historical interpretation to solidify into unarguable fact". Grabowski's article sits in the same pattern. Verlinden's Radhanite route, which Grabowski rejects, already did; and Grabowski's §29 turns a contested etymology into "strong evidence". The mod should not be another link in that chain.

## 6. Bearing on the Jewish slave-trade motif the mod must avoid

- **The rule stands and gains a reason.** The only primary witness for the *carzimasia* names no Jews. The modern case for Jewish involvement rests on reconstructions (Verlinden), a contested etymology (Grabowski) and two Arabic geographers. Phelan shows how such claims were built into "fact". None of it is strong enough to justify depicting Jewish castrators or traders, and the risk is high.
- **No Jewish character in the *carzimasia* or Verdun content.** No merchant named or coded as Jewish (Radhanite, "from the land of the Turks", Khazar). No events where Dorleyite *Rescue the Daughters* or *Ransom the Children of the Verdun Road* targets a Jewish character, court or *kahal*.
- **No etymology in loc.** If the word appears, gloss it as Liudprand did: "the Greeks' word for a boy cut wholly". Never "a Hebrew word" and never "from Khwarezm".
- **Khazaria again.** Grabowski's n. 12 notes the old confusion between Khwarezm and the Khazars. That is one more reason the Khwarezm reading should not leak into any Khazar or Kuzarism content (see the reading list's "do not seat it in Khazaria").
- **Ha-Nehpakhot must stay clear of all of this.** The Arles sect's whole gate is prayer and dress, with no knife (reading list finding 1). Its loc should never mention eunuch trading, castration for sale or Pechina, even to deny them. A denial would put the trope in the player's head.
- **Children.** As `byz_liudprand.md` already says: keep *carzimasia* content to rescue and adoption, and never depict the cutting.
