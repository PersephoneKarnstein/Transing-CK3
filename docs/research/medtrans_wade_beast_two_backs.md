# Wade, "The Beast with Two Backs" (2020): design reading

Source: Erik Wade, "The Beast with Two Backs: Bestiality, Sex Between Men, and Byzantine Theology in the *Paenitentiale Theodori*," *Journal of Medieval Worlds* 2, no. 1-2 (Spring-Summer 2020): 11-26. University of California Press. DOI: [10.1525/jmw.2020.2.1-2.11](https://doi.org/10.1525/jmw.2020.2.1-2.11).

Citation verified from the article's own first page and running heads. **Not** part of *Medieval Feminist Forum* 55.1: it cites that issue (Bychowski and Kim's introduction, MFF 55.1 (2019): 6-41) in n. 75-76. Read in full, notes included. Quotes are verbatim, with the journal's page numbers.

## 1. The argument in brief

- **Theodore's canon is the first English link between sex between men and bestiality.** The *Paenitentiale Theodori* (8th c.) collects the judgments of Theodore of Tarsus, the Greek-speaking Archbishop of Canterbury (arr. 669). Its canon gives ten years' penance to "he who often commits fornication with a man or with a beast" (p. 11).
- **The comparison is a Byzantine import, carried through a mistranslation.** The Greek canon 16 of the Council of Ancyra (314) speaks only of those who "have done the irrational" (p. 16). The Latin translators "hedged their bets" (p. 16, quoting Parvis) and rendered it as both beasts and men. Theodore's 15-year penance comes from St Basil, who wrote that those "who defile themselves with men and with beasts" deserve the same punishment (p. 17).
- **The direction of contamination is the reverse of what scholars assume.** The earlier Irish and British penitentials treated bestiality as minor, on a par with masturbation. After Theodore, "bestiality became a serious sin because of its association with sex between men" (p. 21). The men were not dehumanised by comparison to the beast; the beast was made grave by comparison to the men.
- **Why sex between men was grave: gender violation.** Theologians "saw it as disrupting gender boundaries" (p. 21).
- **The *bædling*.** The Old English translation (the *Canons of Theodore*) reads: "He who fornicates with a *bædling* or with another man or with cattle, [must] fast ten winters" (p. 22). The *bædling* is set apart from "another *wæpnedman*" ("weaponed-man"). Frantzen derives the word from *bæddel* ("hermaphrodite") or from *ge-bæd* ("oppressed one") (p. 22).
- **The glosses.** *Bæddel* survives twice, and only as a gloss (p. 22 n. 68). In one manuscript it glosses *Hermafroditus*. Beside it, the otherwise unattested *wæpenwifestre* ("weaponed-woman?") is an alternate gloss: the hermaphrodite "was seen as a woman with a 'weapon'" (p. 23). *Wæpned* is the phallus metaphor. Alfred's will divides his heirs into the "weaponed-half" or "spear-half" and the "spindle-half" (p. 23).
- **Range of readings, held open.** The *bædling* "might signify a gender-nonconforming person who was assigned male at birth and seen to be unmanly ... or who lived as a woman" (p. 23). It might also signify an AFAB person living as a man, or an intersex person. Clark finds the evidence too thin for a third gender. Wade: there is "evidence to suggest" a third gender "or some form of gender nonconformity" (p. 23).
- **The penance falls on the man.** The canon punishes the *wæpnedman* the same whether his partner is a *bædling* or another man: "there was no 'manly' way for a man to have sex with another man" (p. 24).
- **No uniform opinion.** Fulk and Clark caution there was "no uniformity of opinion on sex between men in early England" (p. 23).
- **Afterlife: a tool for policing minorities.** In 13th-century England the comparison became law, with an antisemitic clause. Bestiality charges "were wielded as a truncheon to police minorities" (p. 25), and British colonial Section 377 carried the same pairing to India.
- **Against the "self-made" north.** Wade cites Rambaran-Olm: Theodore's and Hadrian's foreign origins "are often downplayed or ignored" because they spoil the story of an English kingdom "self-made by white people" (p. 13).

### Primary-source cases flagged

- **Theodore of Tarsus** (d. 690) and **Hadrian** (African-born abbot): pre-867, so lore and loading tips only.
- **The Old English *Canons of Theodore*, canon on the *bædling***: the text for "The Glossary" event and for the "ten winters" hostile penance.
- **The glossary (Plantin-Moretus MS 32 / BL Add. 32,246)** with *bæddel* and *wæpenwifestre* beside *Hermafroditus* (p. 23 n. 74): an artifact for "The Glossary" event. It could also be a court artifact ("A Monk's Glossary") for a Bæddel ruler, giving +learning and unlocking the reclamation option.
- **Alfred's will**, spear-half and spindle-half: localisation only.
- **Not for use**: the 1222 Oxford deacon burned for marrying a Jewish woman, and the 9th-century execution burial. These are too grim for flavour and outside the faith's story.
