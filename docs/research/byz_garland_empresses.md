## 1. The argument and the evidence

### 1.1 The general case (Introduction)
- Power was formally the emperor's, but "a number of empresses played an important part in government and even took control of the empire in appropriate circumstances" (p. 1). Most of them were **regents**. Some, like Irene and Theodora, "chose not to" marry and ruled alone. Zoe and Theodora "ruled together as autokratores for seven weeks" (p. 1).
- **Titles.** "Augusta, basilis(sa) and despoina" were the usual ones. Empresses ruling alone "could adopt the masculine titles of basileus or autokrator: Irene used the title basileus in her Novels, while Zoe and Theodora during their joint rule were acclaimed autokratores" (p. 2).
- **The empress chose the emperor.** "On the death of an emperor, in default of a successor, the empress had the power to transfer the throne." Zoe "did so four times, to three husbands and an adopted son" (p. 3). The "principle of dynastic succession was not well established … in times of transition this gave empresses great influence" (p. 3).
- **Their one disadvantage.** Women who ruled alone "ruled in the same way as emperors", except that "they could not personally lead an army" (p. 4).
- **Eunuchs and the women's quarters.** The empress ran the *gynaikonitis* "where she had her own staff, primarily though not entirely composed of eunuchs." When "Irene, Theodora wife of Theophilos, and Zoe Karbounopsina came to power they often relied on this staff of eunuchs as their chief ministers and even their generals" (p. 5). The empress also presided over "the sekreton of the women", more than 1,000 court ladies headed by the *zoste patrikia* (p. 5).
- **The convent as a political prison.** Women were often "forced into nunneries to prevent their reappropriation of power by marriage" (p. 6).
- **Overall verdict.** Seven empresses ruled as regents between 527 and 1204. "In reality the validity of their rule was entirely recognised" (p. 7).

### 1.2 Irene (regent 780–90 and 792–97; sole ruler 797–802)
- As regent she put eunuchs in command:
  - Staurakios, logothete, "at the head of everything" (p. 76);
  - the sakellarios John over the Asiatic themes;
  - the patrician Theodore against Sicily (pp. 76–77).
- "Irene's use of eunuchs to lead important expeditions may well have caused hostility among the armed forces" (p. 77).
- In 790 the army acclaimed Constantine VI alone and would not accept Irene "as emperor". Constantine exiled "all the other eunuchs" (p. 82).
- In 797 she had her son blinded and became sole ruler (pp. 86–87).
- **Basileus.** "she struck gold coins with her portrait on both sides to emphasise that she was sole ruler and in at least one of her Novels used the title emperor ('Irene the pious emperor'), not empress, though she used the title basilissa or Augusta on her coins, as well as on her seals" (p. 87). Note 68 cites Zepos, *JGR* 1.45–50 and Dölger, *Regesten*. Note 43 to ch. 6 records "some doubt as to the attribution" of one Irene Novel; that is a different law.
- **The throne "vacant".** Pope Leo crowned Charlemagne in 800, "arguing that the imperial throne was technically vacant as it was occupied by a woman" (p. 87).
- **Her eunuchs' ambitions.** Under her sole rule the eunuchs Staurakios and Aetios competed to secure the throne for their relatives. "Although no eunuch had ever been emperor, he seems to have cherished imperial ambitions" (p. 88). Nikephoros's coup of 802 was set off by fears that Aetios would crown his brother (p. 89).
- She was "severely handicapped by being unable to lead an army", and "bypass[ed] experienced commanders … in favour of her own trusted eunuchs" (p. 91).

### 1.3 Theodora, wife of Theophilos (regent 842–56; alive in 867)
- Garland pairs her with Irene: "both empresses relied on eunuchs to help them rule" (p. 95). Theophilos left the eunuch logothete Theoktistos as her co-guardian (p. 100).
- She was removed by her brother Bardas and her son Michael III. She was alive and grieving over Michael's body in 867, and died after Basil I's accession, buried at the Gastria monastery (p. 107).
- Garland: "a woman of character … fully capable of governing the empire" (p. 108). **She is at court on the 867 start date.**

### 1.4 Zoe Karbounopsina (regent 914–19)
- Patriarch Nicholas exiled her and "had her tonsured as Sister Anna" (p. 119).
- She returned by coup in 914. "Her executive was formed from Constantine the parakoimomenos … the brothers Constantine and Anastasios Gongylios, who were also eunuchs, and a fourth eunuch Constantine Malelias." "Symeon of Bulgaria contemptuously considered her government to be dominated by eunuchs" (p. 119).
- Her coinage calls mother and son "Emperors of the Romaioi" (pp. 120–21).
- She fell after the Achelous disaster (917), "on the realities of the inadequate command of the armed forces" (p. 125).

### 1.5 Zoe and Theodora (1028–56)
- They were the "brotherless daughters of Constantine VIII … the legitimate successors and heirs of the dynasty" (p. 136). Basil II's "distrust of women in positions of influence" left the succession unplanned (p. 137).
- Zoe made emperors of Romanos III (1028), of Michael IV (1034, after Romanos drowned in his bath) and of her adopted son Michael V (1041).
- Michael IV confined her to the women's quarters, where "Her eunuchs and the most trustworthy of her maid-servants were dismissed" (p. 139). Between Michael IV and Michael V she decided "with the help of the eunuchs who had served her father" (p. 141).
- **The adopted son turns on her.** Michael V tonsured Zoe and exiled her to Prinkipo (April 1042). The city rose for "our ancestress and heir and mother Zoe … she who alone of all women is free … the rightful heir to the Empire" (pp. 142–43). Women and children joined in. About 3,000 people died.
- **The nun called out to rule.** "the populace, inspired by the palace eunuchs" dragged Theodora from the convent of Petrion, where Zoe had had her tonsured in 1030. She was proclaimed empress with Zoe and ordered Michael V blinded (pp. 143–44, 162).
- **Joint rule.** "They were acclaimed as autokratores" for seven weeks (21 April–12 June 1042). They issued coins inscribed "Mother of God, help the empresses Zoe and Theodora" (pp. 144, 163) and named the eunuch Nikolaos domestic of the Scholae of the East (p. 145). Psellos sneers that the court became "a gynaikonitis" (p. 145).
- **Zoe's last choice.** She married Constantine IX "from her own court circle, thus keeping power for herself" (p. 146). His mistress Maria Skleraina had earlier been granted "the charistikion of the monastery of St Mamas" in settlement of a debt (p. 147). This links back to the typika note's charistike.
- **Theodora's sole rule (1055–56).** At Constantine IX's death Theodora "was acclaimed emperor ('autokrator')." She refused accession donatives because she was "simply resuming the throne" (pp. 166–67). She "preferred to reign on her own … quite openly taking on the role of a man." Psellos: "She herself appointed her officials, dispensed justice from her throne … issued decrees" (p. 166).
- **Opposition.** "Rule by a woman was considered improper, and her appointment of clerics, deemed a masculine privilege … roused the enmity of the patriarch Michael Keroularios" (p. 166).
- **Her ministers.** "Initially she promoted eunuchs to the most important offices," then appointed Leo Paraspondylas, so that "except in the initial stages of her reign, she does not seem to have relied on her eunuchs." She was "the first woman since Irene" to rule as emperor (pp. 166–67).

### 1.6 Eudokia Makrembolitissa (regent 1067)
- Constantine X made her swear in writing not to remarry and not to bring her own relatives into government, on pain of anathema. She broke the oath to marry Romanos Diogenes, "the empire needed a competent military man" (pp. 169–70, 173).

### 1.7 The Komnenian women: adoption and regency
- **Maria of Alania** adopted the adult Alexios Komnenos as her son (1081). This made him adoptive brother to her son and gave him "the right of access to her with no possibility of slander." The "officials of the gynaikonitis" arranged it (p. 183). Garland quotes "if you want to get ahead get a mother: and Alexios had two" (p. 186). Maria later held an "alternative" court at Mangana (p. 185).
- **Anna Dalassene** was regent by chrysobull (August 1081). Her decisions were to stand "whether justified or unjustified." She had "control over the entire civil government" for perhaps fifteen to twenty years (pp. 190–92). Her seal reads "Anna Dalassene, nun, mother of the emperor." She retired to her own convent, the Pantepoptes. Anna Komnene's silence has raised suspicion that she was "involved in something questionable - maybe a heretical sect" (pp. 192–93). Garland calls this "only supposition."
- **Irene Doukaina.**
  - She was crowned only after Doukas pressure (1081). She bore nine children and was kept from decisions: "little more than a figurehead for her family network" (pp. 184, 194–97).
  - She went on campaign as nurse and as a guard against poison (pp. 194–95).
  - She founded Kecharitomene (p. 196).
  - In 1118 she tried to divert the succession from John II to Anna Komnene's husband Bryennios.
  - She refused the later plot to assassinate John: "in the absence of a successor it is necessary to seek an emperor, but a reigning monarch must not be removed" (p. 198).
  - She and Anna went into "semi-retirement at Irene's convent of the Kecharitomene, though not tonsured" (p. 198).
- **Anna Komnene** studied in secret "with the palace eunuchs" and later took part in "the revival of Aristotelian scholarship" (p. 196).
- **Garland's thesis.** "Women did not lose authority under the Komnenoi: they still retained it in periods of transition and crisis … At times of stability, when there was an adult legitimate male heir, they had always had little power" (p. 198). After 1261, foreign-born empresses without family networks "almost invariably" failed (p. 225).

**Contradictions or nuances to flag.**
- **Garland on Kecharitomene (p. 196).** She says the royal ladies may "receive male visitors." The typikon only lets male relatives reach the gate or the exonarthex, under supervision ([17], [80]), and says the house is "untrodden by men." Garland is summarising loosely; use the typikon wording (see `byz_thomas_hero_typika.md`).
- **The Introduction (p. 5) and ch. 9 (p. 166) differ.** One says Theodora was unusual in *not* having a eunuch chief minister; the other says she began with eunuchs and then switched. Use the ch. 9 version.
- **The Irene title claim is narrower than the popular version.** Basileus appears in her laws, not on her coins.

**Primary-source cases usable as events.**
- Zoe's 1042 restoration: the crowd chanting "our mother Zoe."
- Theodora dragged from Petrion to rule.
- Maria of Alania's adoption of Alexios.
- Anna Dalassene's chrysobull, valid "whether justified or unjustified."
- Eudokia's written oath.
- Irene Doukaina's maxim on not removing a reigning monarch.
- The 1042 coin legend.
