## 1. The argument in brief (only what bears on the design)

- **Jackson writes very little about eunuchs as such.** The index has no entry "eunuch". Only three office-holders are called eunuchs in the text:
  - **Rayḥān**, whom Jūzjānī calls "a baseborn Indian eunuch (*majbūb-u nāqiṣ-u az qabāʾil-i Hind*)" (p. 73, n. 71);
  - **Kāfūr**: "a eunuch (*majbūb*)" (p. 174, n. 31);
  - "indiscreet eunuchs", whom Baranī blames for ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn's last years (p. 175).
- **Rayḥān (1253–5)** (pp. 72–4):
  - He became *wakīl-i dar* when Ulugh Khān Balaban was ousted in 1253.
  - Jackson **rejects** the "anti-Turkish faction" reading as being "misled by Juzjani's polemic". Turks allied with Rayḥān, and Jūzjānī "specifically mentions Turks who were allied with Rayhan" (p. 73, nn. 70–71).
  - Jūzjānī's phrase shows Rayḥān was a slave, probably "the sultan's own slave".
  - He fell in 1254 because of "a compromise of which Rayhan was the immediate victim". Balaban had allied with the renegade prince Jalāl al-Dīn Masʿūd, who returned with a Mongol army.
  - He was sent to Badāʾūn, then to Bahraich, and was killed there in Rajab 653/August 1255 (p. 74, nn. 78, 82).
  - Afterwards Balaban seems to have "conceived an aversion" to promoting men of slave or low Indian birth (p. 79).
- **Malik Kāfūr "Hazārdīnārī"** (pp. 174–7; p. 157):
  - He was taken at Cambay in 698/1299 and named for the "thousand dinars" he cost. He was a eunuch: Jackson cites Amīr Khusraw's *Duwal Rānī* (p. 257) and Baranī's *Taʾrīkh-i Fīrūz Shāhī* (p. 368) in n. 31.
  - He was *bārbeg* by 1306–7, then commander against Deogīr and *nāʾib*.
  - He purged Alp Khān and the heir, and enthroned an infant prince when ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn died in 715/1316.
  - He "enjoyed power for a mere thirty-five days before being murdered by ʿAlaʾ al-Din's paiks" (p. 157).
  - His predecessor as *nāʾib*, Shāhīn, is called "the sultan's adopted son" by ʿIṣāmī (p. 175).
- **Khusraw Khān** (pp. 157–9, 177–8):
  - An Indian slave, Ḥasan, captured in Mālwa in 705/1305. He became *wazīr* and Qutb al-Dīn Mubārak Shāh's favourite.
  - He murdered his master in 720/1320 and reigned as Nāṣir al-Dīn Khusraw Shāh, "the only Delhi monarch … who was an Indian convert to Islam".
  - Jackson thinks his rule was "less repugnant than our sources would have us believe" (p. 159).
  - **Jackson never calls him a eunuch.**
- **Adoption is an ordinary channel of favour and inheritance:**
  - Tughluq's "two adopted sons", Tatar Malik and Bahrām Khān, held high posts (p. 182).
  - The fortress of **Zafarābād, "later renamed Jawnpur"**, was completed in 1321 and "conferred as iqṭāʿ on the sultan's adopted son Tatar Khan" (p. 200, nn. 46–7).
  - Fīrūz Shāh "did not disregard the hereditary principle" and passed Nāṣir al-Mulk's iqṭāʿs "to his adopted son Sulayman". Sulaymān's son Khiḍr Khān later held Multan and founded the Sayyid dynasty (p. 304, nn. 54–5).
- **Malik Sarwar, Khwāja Jahān** (pp. 307, 309–10, 318–19):
  - "Malik Sarwar Sultani, the shihna of Delhi", was one of Fīrūz Shāh's slaves. ʿAfīf says he had charge of the jewel-house (n. 77).
  - Prince Muḥammad made him *wazīr* "with the style of Khwaja Jahan", and he stayed loyal (p. 307).
  - In 794/1392 he had his rival Islām Khān executed on trumped-up charges (p. 307, n. 80).
  - In Rajab 796/May 1394 he was sent east "with an army, twenty elephants and the title of Sultan al-Sharq", entrusted with the lands "from Qinnawj to Bihar". He "set up his headquarters at Jawnpur and never returned to Delhi" (p. 310, nn. 89–90).
  - He is "the most obvious case of a loyalist who found autonomy thrust upon him". "At no time did Khwaja Jahan assume the style of sultan" (p. 318).
  - **"It was not until his death in 802/1399 … that his adopted son and successor at Jawnpur took the title of Sultan Mubarak Shah"** (p. 318, n. 157).
  - Jackson dismisses Saeed's claim that Sarwar styled himself *Atābeg al-Aʿẓam* and had the khuṭba read in his own name: "Bihamadkhani, the source cited, does not support him" (n. 157).
  - **Jackson does not say Sarwar was a eunuch.**
- **The Jawnpur Sultanate after 1399** (Epilogue, pp. 321–3):
  - It menaced Delhi in 1407, 1452, c. 1466 and 1479.
  - Bahlūl Lodī annexed it in 884/1479. "The last Sharqi sultan fled into Bihar."
- **Raḍiyya (r. 634–7/1236–40)** (pp. 46–7, 67):
  - She is "credited with all the attributes of a successful ruler except one, namely that she was not a man". She is Jūzjānī's only Shamsid "war leader".
  - "Tradition makes much of Radiyya's adoption of masculine garb and her public appearances riding on an elephant" (*Ṭabaqāt-i Nāṣirī*, Ibn Baṭṭūṭa, n. 17).
  - Her backers wanted a figurehead. "Her offence lay in her growing self-assertiveness" (p. 47).
  - Ibn Baṭṭūṭa found her tomb "an object of pilgrimage" (p. 46, n. 12).
  - Jackson suggests her Turkish slave backers were influenced by steppe precedent: two Qara-Khitan queens regnant (p. 67).

## 2. What matters for the design

1. **The Jawnpur adopted-heir succession is confirmed** (p. 318, n. 157). The founder governed Jawnpur from 1394 to 1399 as Delhi's viceroy, not as sultan. His adopted son proclaimed the sultanate.
   - The parallel to Take as Daughter is the **mechanism**: a childless officer's realm passes to an adopted child.
   - The Sultanate had a broad culture of adoption in office (pp. 175, 182, 200, 304). Even Jawnpur's first iqṭāʿ-holder (1321) was a sultan's adopted son.
2. **Sarwar's eunuch status is not in Jackson.**
   - The reading list and Sarwar and Pervaiz call him a eunuch. Sarwar and Pervaiz give him "Ethiopian origins" via Eaton (their n. 27).
   - Until a source that says so is read, the mod should describe him in game text only as a "Firuz Shahi slave" and "Khwaja Jahan". Calling him a eunuch is *unverified*.
3. **Resentment of eunuch and slave favourites was about birth as much as castration.**
   - Jūzjānī's insult for Rayḥān joins "castrated", "defective" and "of the tribes of Hind". Balaban's later aversion was to "the son of a Hindu slave" (p. 79).
   - So in Islamic Indian courts the slur is three-fold: slave, Indian, unmanned. Reaction text for Dorleyite eunuch officers in a Sultanate setting should carry all three, said by hostile nobles, never by the narrator.
4. **Eunuch regents in Delhi did not last.**
   - Kāfūr held power for 35 days.
   - Rayḥān's party held office for about a year before the compromise of 1254.
   - Khusraw Shāh reigned for a few months.
   - This confirms `tm_islamic_daughter_opinion`'s "valued as a servant and despised as a ruler" for the Indian case.
5. **Raḍiyya is a woman ruler, not trans-man data.**
   - Jackson's evidence is about a queen regnant's dress and self-assertion.
   - Under the hard rules (trans men out of scope; never call a woman's courage manly) she should be reclassified as **female-sovereignty reaction data**. She shows a Muslim court that once enthroned a woman with Turkish slave backing, and then killed her.

## 4. Design implications

- **Jaunpur event chain (c_jaunpur), "The Lord of the East".** An event setting, not a seat. Fires for a Dorleyite or Yutai visitor, or for any ruler holding c_jaunpur between 1394 and 1399.
  - 1. A loyal *wazīr* of slave origin governs the east for a puppet sultan and refuses the title (Jackson p. 318).
  - 2. On his death his **adopted** heir takes the crown.
  - Player choice for a Dorleyite courtier present: (a) endorse the adoption: +opinion with the new sultan, and a faith-voice line "a house may pass by adoption as surely as by blood"; (b) back the Delhi claim instead.
  - In-world faith voice only: Dorleyite clergy may cite Sarwar as a precedent ("the Lord of the East left his realm to a child he chose"). The narrator does not claim him for the faith, and does not call him a eunuch unless that is verified.
- **Sultanate reaction text** for daughters or Dorleyite eunuch officers at a Delhi-type court. Hostile nobles:
  - "Castrated, defective, and of the tribes of Hind." (after Jūzjānī on Rayḥān, p. 73)
  - "The sultan trusts lazy slaves and indiscreet eunuchs." (after Baranī, p. 175)
  - Keep the narrator neutral.
- **Kāfūr's thirty-five days** as a cautionary event for any eunuch or daughter regent at an Islamic court (`tm_islamic_daughter_opinion` already applies). A `tm_regent_of_thirty_five_days` decision or event: a regent who sets aside the heir's mother's kin faces a paik plot within months. Sources: p. 157 and pp. 175–6.
- **Adoption precedent text** for Take as Daughter in Islamic Indian courts. Tooltip line: "In Delhi, sultans gave fortresses to adopted sons; Zafarabad, which men now call Jawnpur, was one." (p. 200)
- **Raḍiyya** stays out of the transfeminine content. A possible female-rule omen for women rulers in the Sultanate region, if wanted: "Her tomb is visited as a saint's." (p. 46)
- **No change to `tm_eastern_daughter_opinion` from this source.** Delhi is Islamic and is covered by `islamic_syncretic_recipient`.
