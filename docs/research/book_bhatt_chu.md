### 2. Core beliefs / claims (Bhatt)

Each: one paragraph + exact quote + essay.

**2.1 Definition of transfeminism (materialist, radical-feminist descended).** Bhatt's transfeminism is explicitly a *materialist* radical feminism that takes the trans woman as its decisive case rather than its exception. It is not identity-politics "inclusion"; it is sex-class analysis extended to those "conscripted into the male sex" who are expelled from it. The book's own job description is to build vocabulary and frameworks for explaining transmisogyny as a structural force.
> "Within this tome, I present to you a year's worth of contentious, arduous, and sometimes backbreaking work. A project oriented around plugging linguistic gaps, formulating frameworks, and giving both transfeminists and those who seek a better understanding of trans issues the tools to properly elucidate how transmisogyny functions." — Preface, B:72
> "A clear, materialist analysis of how misogyny animates patriarchal society or a robust model of the social construction of sex is not bioessentialism. Rather, it is the best framework we have for understanding the basis of cissexism and transphobia." — Preface, B:66

**2.2 "Third wave" — she rejects the wave model; she positions herself as the "disowned daughter" of second-wave radical/lesbian feminism.** Bhatt does **not** call herself a "third wave transfeminist" anywhere in this book. The only use of "Third Wave" is dismissive: she objects to partitioning feminism into waves at all, insists feminism is "an arena rather than a solidified platform", and frames her project as radical feminism's unwanted heir cleaning up its mess. (This matters for the mod: in-game lore can present the faith as a *reformation* of an older "radical" mother-faith rather than a brand-new wave.)
> "Attempts to partition the history of feminism into easily-separable waves tend to be just as arbitrary and constructed as patriarchal gender. Audre Lorde and Leslie Feinberg are frequently claimed by 'Third Wave' feminism, a categorization that flies in the face of Lorde's two decades of friendship with Adrienne Rich…" — "Round Two", B:1374
> "Simply put: Radical feminism saw the most definitive real-world proof of its own theories in the transsexual, and sought to destroy her instead of embracing her." — "Round Two", B:1382
> "Every transsexual woman is the wretched, spurned daughter of the radical feminist thesis, the unwanted validation of its most fundamental tenets that it sought to terminate." — B:1384
> "After all, it always falls upon disowned daughters to clean up their foremothers' messes." — B:1396

**2.3 Transmisogyny as structural and material.** Transmisogyny is not an attitude but a mechanism of the heterosexual regime: the degendering of male-assigned deserters into a non-person who can be sexually exploited and brutalized with impunity, serving as a deterrent to all men. The trans woman is constructed *by* the attempt to destroy her.
> "What, then, is transmisogyny? It is the process by which those conscripted into the male sex under patriarchy are denaturalized and dehumanized, being demoted from potentially liberated agent to subjugated object. It is the intensification of misogyny in a manner that does not merely enforce sexual difference but explicitly penalizes the failure to uphold it… Transmisogyny is the reminder, the warning, the deterrent: 'Be the man you were meant to be, or else.'" — "Understanding Transmisogyny, Part Three", B:478
> "Speaking in plain terms, the tranny is constructed as the union of fag and whore." — B:476
> "There is a rich irony inherent to this destruction, however, which is that in attempting to destroy the transsexual, patriarchal society actually constructs her." — B:466

**2.4 Women as a sex class / sex-caste; "woman" defined by labor extraction.** Bhatt uses "sex-class" and "sex-caste" interchangeably and defines woman materially, as the subordinate class whose domestic, reproductive and sexual labor is extracted. Gender is a "labor relation". She explicitly uses caste/untouchable imagery for the trans woman's position.
> "Their definition of 'woman' is the patriarchal definition of woman: a member of the subordinate sex-class whose domestic, reproductive, and sexual labor is meant to be exploited by the hegemonic sex-class." — "Just Answer the Damned Question", B:1496
> "Gender is, ultimately, a labor relation, a set of rationalizations for a social paradigm of exploitation that leans on notions of biodestiny and specific embodiments being for specific purposes." — "Bury Me Deep", B:778
> "…requiring parents to resort to fairly explicit external gendered markers in order to distinguish which sex-caste their progeny has been sorted into." — B:432
> "Why would anyone attempt to inhabit the positionality of the degraded, occupy the position of the derided, willingly cast themselves down from the upper echelons of the castes to cavort about as one of the untouchables? It is profane, it is BLASPHEMY, it is PERVERSE and VULGAR and UNNATURAL!!!!" — B:412

**2.5 Misogyny = the reproduction of heterosexuality; heterosexuality is a regime to be abolished.** The central structural claim (via Wittig and Rich): heterosexuality is not an orientation but a political institution organizing property, marriage, and labor. The binary is one of human vs. non-human, master vs. property.
> "Succinctly, misogyny is the organizing principle by which heterosexuality is reproduced." — "Understanding Transmisogyny, Part One", B:270
> "Heterosexuality is a regime, and like every unjust regime, it too will be abolished." — "'Heterosexuality is a Regime'", B:348
> "The binary is the socially constructed division between who is considered human, and who is not." — "Understanding Transmisogyny, Part Two", B:404
> "The entrails of the last Priest must serve as a noose for the last King whose sword must be buried in the bosom of the last Patriarch. Until then, women will not be free. Manhood as an ideology must be abolished." — 'Antithesis', B:194

**2.6 Critique of "gender identity" liberalism.** Bhatt treats liberal "validity"/"choice" discourse as a hollow endorsement that leaves naturalized sex untouched and abandons trans women to the gender-conservatives. The "gender unicorn" model (gender social, sex immutable) is the real legacy of Raymond.
> "Liberalism is the erroneous belief that one can paper over systemic inequality with enough contract law, consent fetishism, and lip service to 'individual freedom'." — "Listen Up, Liberal", B:1334
> "It is, in a sentence, a Human Resources approach to managing rape culture." — B:1338
> "Crucially, when Gender-Conservatism asked on what basis we should consider trans women to be real, authentic women, liberal feminism simply shrugged and began babbling about category errors… They are women because they choose to be, and who are we to deny them that choice?" — B:1354
> "Macabre models such as the 'gender unicorn' and 'genderbread person' helpfully perpetuate a model of sex whereby its immutability is never challenged… 'no one is claiming that trans people actually change their sex'." — "The Transmisogyny Bible", B:934

**2.7 On transmedicalism: the word never appears; her position is "transsexuals change sex" + bodily autonomy, *against* gatekeeping.** Bhatt insists on the material reality of transition (hormones, surgery change sex) and uses "transsexual" deliberately, but she attacks medical gatekeeping and the forced performance of hyperfemininity for doctors. She is not a gatekeeper; she is anti-"subversivist" (against the idea that non-medical "transgenderism" is more radical than medical transition).
> "Whilst it would no doubt be an excellent party trick, I did not sprout tits through my sheer mastery of the social fabric. I had to take oestrogen for that." — "Radicalized Feminist", B:1444
> "The first time you witness a cis person call a trans woman 'TERF' for insisting that she changed her sex, or for describing herself as a 'transsexual', the spectacle elicits a hearty chuckle." — B:1450
> "In the West, trans women have long been held hostage by medical practitioners—largely men—who demand performances of hyperfemininity from us in exchange for desperately-needed healthcare." — "Bury Me Deep", B:786
> "In the final calculus, how 'subversive' bodily transition is should not matter to anyone more than the fact that transition care is an absolute necessity for many, many people". — "Subversivism and Transition", B:1234

**2.8 Lesbianism and trans-lesbian identity (Wittig: lesbians are not women; lesbians as fugitives).** Bhatt is a trans lesbian married to a lesbian; lesbian feminism is her primary tradition. She adopts Wittig's claim that lesbians are fugitives from the heterosexual contract, and documents the erasure of trans lesbians in India and the West.
> "I'm a lesbian happily married to a lesbian who helps me, heals me and completes me in a way no one ever has". — 'Antithesis', B:172
> "In The Straight Mind and Other Essays, visionary and prophet Monique Wittig declares that lesbians are not women." — "Abolition", B:626
> "Lesbians are runaways, those who have fled womanhood in order to seek an existence outside of its suffocating heterosexual trappings". — B:632
> "Womanhood is understood to be for men, transition understood as a social technology for people who wish to access particular relations to manhood… Lesbianism remains erased, buried, forbidden to cissexual and transsexual women alike". — "Bury Me Deep", B:792
> "Even now, when I call myself a lesbian and stress my lack of attraction to men, I am met with a truly bewildering amount of hostility… I have been accused of everything from biphobia to gatekeeping". — 'Antithesis', B:178

**2.9 Separatism: not advocated as a program, but "women's communes" are what the regime fears; the goal is abolition, not retreat.** Bhatt never proposes separatism. Her solidarity is universal among "dissidents from heterosexuality". Decter's horror of "women's communes and chaste nunneries" is cited as patriarchal anxiety; Bhatt's own program is abolitionist (Wittig's "mutual annihilation of 'man' and 'woman'").
> "Lesbians are pretending, play-acting, dabbling in juvenile fantasies of women's communes and chaste nunneries and masturbatory daydreams of liberation from intercourse, which lesbians of course cannot have." — summarizing Decter, "Monstress", B:506
> "Rather, the struggle for liberation is a struggle for abolition of this category, a mutual annihilation of 'man' and 'woman' such that social existence is no longer defined by a relation of extractive parasitism." — "Abolition", B:630
> "We are all dissidents from heterosexuality in the eyes of patriarchal society and are thus all subject to punishment for that desertion." — "Degendering and Regendering", B:684

**2.10 Sex-based vs gender-based oppression: sex is itself socially constructed and *policed*; the "femininity trap" is rejected.** Bhatt refuses the sex/gender split. Sex is socially assigned and essentialized; what is punished is deviation from reproductive utility, not "femininity". Butches are punished *for* masculinity; trans women are punished whether feminine or not.
> "When a gender-conservative uses the term sex, they mean naturalized, patriarchal, essentialist ideas of sex. When they say 'sex is real' or 'sex matters', what they actually mean is that sex is determined at birth and immutable." — "'Sex is Real'", B:890
> "For a Gender-Conservative, sex under patriarchy is policed." — B:1502
> "There exist certain feminist strains of thought that fall into what I would call the femininity trap, which is the idea that women are oppressed on the basis of being 'feminine'." — "Abolition", B:640
> "Just as transmisogyny punishes those coercively sexed as male for any perceived crossing of the gendered barrier, lesbophobia metes out this punishment in the opposite direction". — B:646

**2.11 Womanhood as achieved / relational / forged in refusal.** Bhatt's own womanhood is not innate identity but an achieved positionality: a refusal of manhood, burial of the male self, and the structural fact that patriarchy already treats her as a woman. She calls herself "a failed man… proudly so… man perfected".
> "Then, as the flames rose up to consume him, I placed my male self atop his corpse and left my old life behind in his ashes." — 'Antithesis', B:170
> "'I was always a woman' is something that's never resonated though… There had always been a thick barrier between myself and womanhood, and no matter how beaten-bloody my fists were against it, it remained unimpeachable." — B:164
> "For I have never seen myself as a 'man, made lesser'. A failed man? Yes, and proudly so, but never lesser. I am, if anything, man perfected in form and spirit, in a way only a being who fulfills her true potential can be." — "Just Answer the Damned Question", B:1524
> "Kill the man who wears your face, and tear his flesh apart to reveal your own." — "I Read It", B:740

**2.12 "TMA/TME" framing: the acronyms never appear; Bhatt's terms are "transfeminized" and "coercively sexed as male/female".** She consistently theorizes an asymmetry: the transfeminine are *degendered* and made exemplary, the transmasculine *regendered* and erased. (Count: "transfeminized" appears 12+ times; "TMA"/"TME" zero.)
> "The transfeminized serve as examples of what happens to gender traitors. The transmasculine, by contrast, are ignored or treated as little more than delusional, as people who reach above their station and are doomed to never succeed." — "Degendering and Regendering", B:668
> "Where transmisogynistic forces marginalize and ostracize the transfeminine from society, rendering us unworthy of any fate outside of being treated like sexual chattel, transemasculative forces deny the transmasculine any possibility of escaping reproductive exploitation and seek to re-gender the transmasculine… back into the confines of womanhood." — B:670

**2.13 Relation to Marxism: never named, but the method is materialist/class analysis.** The words "Marx"/"Marxism" do not occur. The analytical vocabulary is nonetheless Marxian: "labor relation", "extractive labor relation", "false consciousness", "ruling-class", "class-consciousness", "idpol", "wages of patriarchy", and the celebration of Feinberg's "socialism". She also warns that "anti-capitalist politics" are turning antifeminist.
> "Men are thus positioned as the beneficiaries of an extractive labor relation, one that they must enforce and uphold to continue reaping its rewards." — B:426
> "This internalization of misogynistic precepts also functions as a false consciousness, a form of identification with the oppressor's view of oneself that prevents the subjugated from self-actualizing and forming solidarity against their own oppression." — B:364
> "Feinberg's socialism streaks and highlights the pages with its brilliant red hues, reminding us all what it means to fight for rights and dignity: recognizing our common struggle." — "I Read It", B:698
> "Even anti-capitalist politics are taking on a chillingly antifeminist bent, with women's issues dismissed as 'idpol', a mere distraction from the primary contradiction of class." — B:1458

**2.14 Degendering, epistemicide, hermeneutical injustice.** Trans women are "third-sexed" into a non-category and denied epistemic authority; whole concepts (lesbian, transsexual lesbian) are absent from cultures, which is a deeper silencing than censorship.
> "No one believes trans women." — "You Left Me To Die", B:758
> "My friend would call this hermeneutical injustice—a silence and absence of ideas, concepts, and terminology deeper than ripping out tongues or censoring presses can achieve." — "Bury Me Deep", B:802

**2.15 Against third-sexing and against romanticized pre-colonial "venerated" genders.** A third category does nothing against male supremacy; it is a landfill for the sexually "defective" (hijra, barren women, widows, trans women). There is no utopian past.
> "The existence of a third sex does no more to challenge societal male-supremacy than does the existence of a fourth, fifth, or even second sex. Every sex that is not the First Amongst Sexes… simply becomes another sexual resource to be exploited." — "It's the Power Differential, Stupid", B:1186
> "Sex is not quite as binary as advertised, because the heterosexual regime has always regarded people as one of human, broodmare, or freak." — "Towards a Feminist Understanding of Third-Sexing", B:1216
> "There is no salvation awaiting us in a glorified past that does not exist… You do not want the 'veneration' that the holy men of my culture reserve for us." — "A Too-Short History of Transmisogyny", B:1170

**2.16 Rage as virtue; love of women entails hatred of what hurts them.**
> "Anger is the fire that keeps you warm in the bitter cold of meandering, hazy roads, the bright, burning beacon that lights your way no matter how murky." — 'Antithesis', B:138
> "To love women, to really love them and be with them, you can't not hate that which hurts them. A love that doesn't inspire ceaseless rage at injustice, inequity, harm and dismissal isn't much of a love at the end of the day." — B:184

**2.17 Program: "radical gendered autonomy" and chosen kin.**
> "And we can see just how far advocating for a radical gendered autonomy takes us." — B:1538
> "…even when her family bonds are forged in love and community ties rather than the flimsy shackles of blood." — "I Read It", B:696

### 2. Core beliefs / claims (Chu)

**2.1 Femaleness as a universal ontological condition of self-negation.** Chu's "female" is neither biological sex nor gender but an existential structure: the self hollowed out to host another's desire. Everyone is female; "woman" is just one way of coping with it.
> "The thesis of this little book is that femaleness is a universal sex defined by self-negation, against which all politics, even feminist politics, rebels. Put more simply: Everyone is female, and everyone hates it." — C:68
> "I'll define as female any psychic operation in which the self is sacrificed to make room for the desires of another… To be female is to let someone else do your desiring for you, at your own expense. This means that femaleness, while it hurts only sometimes, is always bad for you. Its ultimate toll, at least in every case heretofore recorded, is death." — C:70
> "Femaleness is not an anatomical or genetic characteristic of an organism, but rather a universal existential condition, the one and only structure of human consciousness. To be is to be female: the two are identical." — C:72
> "Men and women must therefore be understood not as irreconcilable opposites, or even as two poles of a spectrum, but more simply as the two most common phyla of the kingdom Females." — C:74

**2.2 Gender = internalized misogyny = someone else's desire; gender is given by others, not claimed.** Gender is "a social expression of someone else's sexuality"; transition proves that subjective identity alone is "basically worthless" and that recognition is "the structural generosity of strangers". This is Chu's critique of "valid"/affirmation politics.
> "Everyone is female, and everyone hates it. If this is true, then gender is very simply the form this self-loathing takes in any given case. All gender is internalized misogyny." — C:148
> "If sexual orientation is basically the social expression of one's own sexuality, then gender is basically a social expression of someone else's sexuality. In the former case, one takes an object; in the latter case, one is an object. From the perspective of gender, then, we are all dumb blondes." — C:150
> "On the contrary, if there is any lesson of gender transition—from the simplest request regarding pronouns to the most invasive surgeries—it's that gender is something other people have to give you. Gender exists, if it is to exist at all, only in the structural generosity of strangers. When people today say that a given gender identity is 'valid,' this is true, but only tautologically so… You do not get to consent to yourself, even if you might deserve the chance." — C:160
> "You do not get to consent to yourself—a definition of femaleness." — C:162

**2.3 Desire is nonconsensual; it descends from outside.** Against feminist models of desire suppressed by external power, Chu says desire *is* the external force. Her own wanting-to-be-a-woman arrived "like a tongue of fire, or an infection".
> "Too often, feminists have imagined powerlessness as the suppression of desire by some external force, and they've forgotten that more often than not, desire is this external force. Most desire is nonconsensual; most desires aren't desired. Wanting to be a woman was something that descended upon me, like a tongue of fire, or an infection". — C:304
> "I don't really want to tell anyone what to do; I want to be told. It's no accident that Valerie can sound like a dominatrix in SCUM." — C:102

**2.4 Sissy porn as the truth of all porn and all desire; autogynephilia universalized.** Sissy porn (Tumblr, c. 2013) makes explicit what all pornography does: it feminizes the viewer. Blanchard's "autogynephilia" is not a paraphilia but the structure of sexuality itself.
> "In other words, sissy porn takes the implicitly feminizing effect of all pornography (even the most vanilla) and promotes it to the level of explicit content—often with spectacular results." — C:296
> "Indeed, if everyone is female… then autogynephilia describes not an obscure paraphilic affliction but rather the basic structure of all human sexuality… To be female is, in every case, to become what someone else wants. At bottom, everyone is a sissy." — C:289
> "Getting fucked makes you female because fucked is what a female is." — C:298
> "Sissy porn did make me trans. At very least it served as a neat allegory for my desire to be female—and increasingly, I thought, for all desire as such." — C:304
> "Pornography is what it feels like when you think you have an object, but really the object has you. It is therefore a quintessential expression of femaleness." — C:257

**2.5 The political as anti-female; the sexual as the hidden truth of politics (manosphere, white supremacy, fascism as repressed femaleness).** Politics begins in the wish not to be female. The red-piller, the warlord fantasist, the negrophobe are all read as men desperately repressing a desire to be female; "force is invariably feminizing", so masculinizing institutions (fraternities, military) end in feminizing hazing.
> "The political is the sworn enemy of the female; politics begins, in every case, from the optimistic belief that another sex is possible… Politics is, in its essence, anti-female." — C:76
> "He radicalizes—shoots up a school, builds a wall—in order to avoid transitioning, the way some closeted trans women join the military in order to get the girl beaten out of them." — C:225
> "Men are not men. Men are never men." — C:247
> "After decades of tedious feminist debates over agency, one thing is clear: women may be capable of political action, but females never are. Ultimately, the phrase forced feminization is redundant: the female is always the product of force, and force is invariably feminizing." — C:331

**2.6 Critique of affirmation/feminism; feminism as expression of misogyny; Raymond "not entirely wrong".** Feminism wants women not to be female; it "opposes misogyny precisely inasmuch as it also expresses it." Chu refuses to call her book feminist, and partly concedes the TERF charge that trans womanhood is built from stereotypes — then universalizes it to all gender.
> "To be for women, imagined as full human beings, is always to be against females. In this sense, feminism opposes misogyny precisely inasmuch as it also expresses it." — C:78
> "For the record, I'm not sure if what you're reading is a feminist text, either. I'm not sure if I want it to be." — C:63
> "Raymond is obviously a bigot… But she's also not entirely wrong… Gigi is a TERF's worst nightmare: a shameless cosmetic miracle, assembled by a team of plastic surgeons, endocrinologists, agents, and marketers—a walking, talking advertisement. I love this about her." — C:138

> "Impotence is always grandiose, and vice versa". — C:100

**2.9 Hormones as pills/sacraments: the red pill and the blue pill.** Premarin (red, from pregnant mares' urine) and estradiol (blue) are read through *The Matrix*; the trans woman is Cypher choosing to re-enter the simulation.
> "At present, I take the blue pill twice a day, once upon waking and once before bed, sending myself back into the simulation. By this logic, the hidden trans woman of The Matrix is not the messianic Neo, but Cypher, the sleazy traitor". — C:227
> "Isn't that the whole point of gender—letting someone else do your living for you?" — C:231
