# Constantine VII, *The Book of Ceremonies* (trans. Moffatt & Tall, 2012): design reading

**Citation (checked against the PDF).** *Constantine Porphyrogennetos: The Book of Ceremonies*, translated by Ann Moffatt and Maxeme Tall, with the Greek text of the Bonn edition (Reiske, 1829), 2 vols. (Byzantina Australiensia 18), Australian Association for Byzantine Studies, 2012; paperback reprint Leiden–Boston: Brill, 2017. 913 pp. in the PDF. ISBN 978-1-876503-42-0 (set); e-book ISBN 978-90-04-34492-1. The translation's page numbers follow Reiske's, so "R257" below is both the Reiske page and the page of this translation. Vol. 2 includes Philotheos' *Kletorologion* (R702–R788; see the separate Bury note) and Haldon's three military treatises as the Appendix to Book I (R444–R508). **What was read:** not the whole book. By search and chapter list: the Introduction on the *Kletorologion* (p. xxxiii); I.9 (the augousta at Easter, R67); I.40–41 (coronation and nuptial crowning of an augousta, R202–R216, parts); I.50 (appointment of a *zoste patrikia*, R257–R261) and I.51 (appointment of a *praipositos*, R261–R263) in full; I.66 (crowning only before eunuchs, R298); II.12 (Blachernai, R552); II.15 (pearl torques, R584); II.21–25 (imperial birth, baptism, hair-cutting, appointment of a *koubikoularaia* and of a *koubikoularios*, R615–R627) in full; II.47–48 (spiritual kinship with foreign rulers, R680–R689, parts); the *Kletorologion* entry on the *zoste* (R711); Appendix A–C on imperial expeditions (R444–R446, R458, R482–R484); and every hit for Dorylaion, Phrygia, Anatolikoi, Opsikion and "eunuch". Translations are Moffatt & Tall's.

**Relevance:** high for court offices and the empress's household (including three real *women's* chamber titles); high for Dorylaion's place in imperial campaigns; low for adoption (no adoption ceremony exists in the book; see §1.5).

## 1. The argument in brief

### 1.1 The eunuch dignities and how they were conferred
- The editors' summary of Philotheos: 18 titles by insignia, "a further six such titles were reserved for eunuchs" (*praipositos, primikerios, ostiarios, spatharokoubikoularios, koubikoularios, nipsistiarios*). Eunuchs "could receive any of the titles conferred by nomination except for eparch, quaestor and domestikos", and ten offices were reserved to them, whose positions "notably involved the personal well-being of the emperor and the augousta" (p. xxxiii, nn. 3–4). This matches the Bury note.
- **Appointment of a *praipositos* (I.51, R261–R263).** Before the whole *kouboukleion* (chamber staff) standing "in the form of a Pi", the candidate kisses the rulers' feet and knees and receives "tablets without codicils". He then leads the court's groups in himself with two *ostiarioi*. "If the rulers also wish to make him a patrician, he receives other tablets with the codicils" (R263). Chapter undated.
- **Appointment of a *koubikoularios* (II.25, R624–R627).** The gold *paragaudion* is hung on the holy doors of the Chapel of St Theodore. The chief *praipositos* makes him promise "not to make himself subject to any non-eunuch other than the emperor, and not to be drunk and boastful", not to befriend "young men", "not to expose the privacy of the emperor", and to respect his superiors. Then: "See from where you assume your rank. It is quite clearly from the holy doors ... you receive this from the hand of the Lord" (R625–R626). He makes threefold obeisance and is robed at the altar screen.

### 1.2 Ceremonies that only eunuchs could see or do
- **Crowning.** "The emperor is never crowned in front of non-eunuchs, this tradition having been observed from the very beginning" (I.66, R298). The *praipositos* crowns him.
- **The Holy Casket at Blachernai.** "Non-eunuchs do not go in there but only the members of the *kouboukleion*" (II.12, R552).
- **Ornament.** "It is not normal form for a non-eunuch to put on such a torque with either pearls or even precious stones"; Constantine VII allowed it once, for display to Saracen envoys (II.15, R584). Pearls marked the eunuch.
- **At the throne.** Throughout Book I the eunuch *protospatharioi* stand directly behind the emperor, the "non-eunuch" ones further off (e.g. R67, R99, R127).
- **On campaign.** The bedchamber staff "proceed in the order of the patricians; a little behind proceed the *protovestiarios* and the steward of the table and behind them, at a stone's throw ... the eunuch patricians and eunuch *protospatharioi*" (App. C, R484).

### 1.3 The empress's household
- **A woman's chamber office, by the same rite as a eunuch's.** The *koubikoularaia* (II.24, R622–R624): the *praipositoi* bring her from "the bedchamber of the augousta" to the Chapel of St Theodore, where her gold tunic "in the form of a *paragaudion*", *propoloma*, white veil and white *charzanion* hang on the holy doors. The chief *praipositos* tells her the same words as the male *koubikoularios*: "See from where you assume your rank. It is quite clearly from the holy doors ... observing towards the emperor and the augousta true loyalty". She kisses the emperor's feet and is taken back to give thanks to the augousta (R623–R624). **The female and the eunuch chamber attendant were made by one rite.**
- **Women officers of the augousta's chamber.** The *zoste* pays fees to "the steward of the table of the augousta, with the *protovestiaria* and the *primikerissa* and the women of the bedchamber and *koubouklareai*" (*Kletorologion*, R711). So the augousta's chamber had its own female *protovestiaria* and *primikerissa*, alongside her eunuch table-steward and the *koubikoularaiai*.
- **The women's court.** At the augousta's coronation, after the men leave, "the court of the women goes in" in eleven groups, from "the girdled [patrician] women" down to wives of centurions (I.40, R203). At Easter the augousta receives the same ranks in the gallery, the *praipositos* leading them in on her sign (I.9, R67). After an imperial birth, the *praipositoi* are summoned "by the steward of the table of the augousta", and the women's ranks come to her bedchamber with gifts (II.21, R618).

### 1.4 The *zoste patrikia* (I.50, R257–R261; R711)
- The protocol is probably for Theoktiste, mother of Theophilos' empress Theodora, "in the 820s as she is the first attested with this title" (R257 n. 1, after Vogt).
- The rite: the *praipositos* brings her into the Church of the Pharos; she receives "the dalmatic and pectoral and white veil", then the *loros* and *propoloma*; she cannot fall prostrate in the *loros*, so she kisses the rulers' knees and receives "the tablets with the codicils" (R258–R259). The senate gives thanks for her. The factions acclaim her "as in the case of patricians"; the patriarch lays her tablets on a portable altar in Hagia Sophia and prays. Back at the Magnaura she stands "at the head, in the middle of the patrician women", and each makes obeisance and receives six *nomismata* from her (R260–R261).
- In the *Kletorologion* she is the fifteenth title by insignia, with "ivory tablets as for the patricians" (R711), and in largesse "it is necessary to add to the magistroi ... the girdled patrician woman" (R784). Her title ranks with the *magistroi*, above most patricians.

### 1.5 Adoption-like ceremonies (none of adoption proper)
- **No adoption rite appears in the book.** The nearest are:
- **Baptismal sponsorship** of an imperial son (II.22, R619–R620).
- **The hair-cutting of an imperial child** (II.23, R620–R622). The patriarch hands joined handkerchiefs to "those who will be recipients of the hair"; the first gold handkerchief goes to the *praipositos*. Under Basil I, for the future Leo VI, the recipients were "Leo the patrician and strategos of the Anatolikoi, the Krateros, and the strategos of the theme of Kappadokia", with all their *tourmarchai* and officers. The handkerchiefs stretched from the chapel to the Chrysotriklinos, "so that all the Anatolikoi and Cappadocian thematic archons were recipients of the hair" (R622). This is ritual sponsorship (spiritual kinship) used to bind a whole army corps to the heir.
- **Spiritual kinship in diplomacy.** The Bulgarian ruler is the emperor's "spiritual son" or "spiritual grandson"; the pope is "spiritual father"; Frankish kings "spiritual brother" (II.47–48, R681–R689).

### 1.6 Dorylaion, Phrygia and the Anatolikon on campaign
- **Dorylaion was the second *aplekton* (military base) on the road east.** "The military bases are: first military base at Malagina; second, Doryleion; third at Kaborkin; fourth at Kolonia; fifth at Kaisareia; sixth [in the theme of the] Armeniakoi at Dazimon" (App. A, R444). The strategos of the Anatolikoi met the emperor at Malagina or at Kaborkin (R444–R445). So the emperor marching east from Constantinople (crossing to Pylai, via Nikaia and Malagina) passed Dorylaion as the second muster point.
- **Phrygia bred the army's animals.** On mobilisation the emperor "gives orders to the logothete of the herds" for a distribution "to the yards of Asia and Phrygia" (App. C, R458). The logothete of the herds had a "protonotary of Phrygia" (R718).
- **How the emperor greeted a theme.** The strategos and officers dismount; the emperor asks "How are you, my children? How are your wives, my daughters-in-law, and the children?" (App. C, R483). Imperial kinship language again.
- Dorylaion also appears as a bishopric of Phrygia Salutaris under Synada in the Pseudo-Epiphanios list (Addendum, p. 823).

### 1.7 Supplement: Dumbarton Oaks seals, vol. 3 (skimmed)
**Citation.** John Nesbitt and Nicolas Oikonomides (eds.), *Catalogue of Byzantine Seals at Dumbarton Oaks and in the Fogg Museum of Art*, vol. 3: *West, Northwest, and Central Asia Minor and the Orient*, Washington, D.C.: Dumbarton Oaks Research Library and Collection, 1996. Skimmed by search only, for Dorylaion, Phrygia, Anatolikon, Opsikion and eunuch titles.

Findings, modest: **Dorylaion** was "a major city of Phrygia in the theme of Opsikion which also had a permanent camp for stopovers of the imperial army (*aplekton*), and was famous for the herds of imperial pack animals that were raised there" (no. 71.22, p. 124: Michael "the Nabataean", 11th-c. *episkeptites* of the Optimatoi and of Dorylaion, an imperial estate manager, possibly a Christian Arab). The editors link this estate to the 9th-c. **metaton of Phrygia**, the imperial stud for pack animals (nos. 95.1–2, p. 169: Gregory, *protonotarios* and *episkeptites*; Michael, imperial *strator*). The imperial estate of **Mesonakta** lay near Dorylaion, Kotyaion and Philomelion "on the military road to the East" (no. 58, p. 103). Phrygia Salutaris (Synada) "belongs to the Anatolikon theme" while Dorylaion was in Opsikion. **Eunuch title-holders** in the region are few: Leo(?) *primikerios* and chartulary of the Anatolikoi (X/XI c., no. 86.6, p. 146); John *spatharokoubikoularios* and *ek prosopou* (acting governor) of the Anatolikoi (XI c., no. 86.20, p. 151); Euthymios, a eunuch *protospatharios* of the vestiarion and judge of the Anatolikoi (XI c., no. 86.26, p. 153); and Constantine, patrician and *praipositos*, domestic of the Orient, probably Michael IV's eunuch brother (1037–41; no. 99.5, p. 174). So eunuchs did govern and judge in the Anatolikon in the 10th–11th c., but none is attested at Dorylaion itself.

## 3. In-world lines for loc

- "See from where you take your rank: from the holy doors. You receive it from the hand of the Lord." (appointment to a chamber office; after II.24–25)
- "No bearded man has ever seen the Emperor crowned." (court flavour; after I.66)
- "The Emperor already keeps women in his chamber: a protovestiaria, a primikerissa, the koubikoulariai. We only send him more." (Dorleyaian sermon or envoy)
- "She stood among the patrician women, and each bowed to her and took six gold pieces." (Zoste Patrikia appointment)
- "The Emperor halts at Dorylaion, and the whole army with him." (imperial campaign event)
- "How are you, my children? How are my daughters?" (Mother of Mothers greeting her Houses; adapted from the emperor's greeting to the themes)

## 4. Flags

- **Supports the Aristotelian main rite** more directly than any previous Byzantine note: one appointment rite for the *koubikoularaia* and the *koubikoularios* (II.24–25). Worth a line in beliefs.md G.
- **No adoption ceremony** in *De Cer.* Do not cite it for Take as Daughter; cite Macrides (adoption) and II.23 (hair-cutting as sponsorship).
- **Dorylaion is Opsikion**, consistent with faith_design.md; correct any loc that puts it in the Anatolikon.
- **Primary-source cases usable as events or characters:** Theoktiste, the first *zoste* (820s); Leo Krateros, strategos of the Anatolikoi and "recipient of the hair" of the infant Leo VI under Basil I (inside the 867 window); Michael the Nabataean of Dorylaion (11th c.); John, *spatharokoubikoularios* governing the Anatolikoi (11th c.).
