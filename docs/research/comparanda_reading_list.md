# Comparanda: reaction and expansion reading list

This list gathers medieval trans studies comparanda from five regions the mod does not yet cover. It serves two ends:
- **reaction**: how each region's churches, rabbis, jurists and courts would respond to the existing faiths;
- **expansion**: whether the region could host a fourth faith, with a frank fit assessment.

Each section keeps the format of `china_reading_list.md` (Use · Priority · Availability · Downloaded). Open-access copies sit in `docs/research/sources_pending/comparanda/<region>/` (gitignored), with source URLs in each `SOURCES.txt`. This file holds no extracted text beyond short quotes. Citations were checked on 2026-10-19 against Crossref, OpenAlex, Open Library, publisher or repository records, or the downloaded copy; anything unconfirmed is marked *unverified*.

Female-to-male cases appear only as reaction data: trans men are out of scope.

## Expansion at a glance

| Region | Verdict | Best candidate (gate · seat) | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latin Christendom | Poor | None; at most a declaration rite modelled on the canonists' Susa oath | The West's sanctioned crossings ran toward manhood; castration was punishment |
| Judaism | Poor as a full faith; fair as a small late current | *Ha-Nehpakhot*: a Purim "Turning" rite (Kalonymus, Dinah in Berakhot 60a) · Arles | Antisemitic tropes; never Khazaria; "six genders" only in a faith's own voice |
| Central and eastern Europe | Fair on the map, no gender evidence | Wendish faith: the sacred horse over the spears · Arkona (Rügen); or a Rhineland beguine-like house | Invention outruns sources; a beguine house would be a second Christian heresy |
| Northwest Africa | Weak to moderate | Possession sisterhood: "drum night", a spirit names the daughter · Fez | Best evidence (Leo Africanus) is c. 1500; Bori and Gnawa are later ethnography |
| South and Southeast Asia | Poor (South Asia); fair as a marked reconstruction (islands) | Visayan babaylan/asog faith · Cebu | Sources begin 1521; bissu invite third-gender romance; Oceania is off the map |

## Reaction at a glance

- **Catholic:** fix doubtful sex rather than persecute it; a man cut by force may stay a cleric, one who cut himself may not (Nicaea c. 1). The one clear male-to-female case in the Empire (Lübeck, 1380s) ended at the stake.
- **Jewish:** castration forbidden and the cutter lashed (Maimonides); a made eunuch stays a man barred from marrying a Jewish woman. Cross-dressing follows local custom and is permitted on Purim (Minz, Isserles).
- **Orthodox Rus':** crossing is festival play, punished by penance.
- **Maliki:** expulsion from the city (Ibn ʿAbdūn); intersex people are assigned a sex, never moved across.
- **Buddhist:** a monk whose sex changes joins the nuns with the same teacher and seniority (Vin III.35), though commentary blames bad karma.
- **Jain and Hindu:** no ordination for those born neither; Manu cuts them from inheritance but owes them food and clothing.

### 1. Findings that matter for the design

1. **Latin canon law tolerated bodies of doubtful sex but fixed them in place. A sex that "changes" was the problem.**
   - Rolker (item 1) finds "no evidence for hermaphrodites being persecuted in the Middle Ages" (p. 178).
   - The canonists sorted such people by the "prevailing sex" (Huguccio, pp. 192–193). A person in whom the female sex prevailed could not be ordained.
   - Hostiensis (d. 1271) recorded a case from his home town of Susa. The bishop of Turin had the person declare "which sex s/he chooses" and swear not to use the other (p. 199). Canonists accepted that the prevailing sex "could in fact change over time", and the oath was there to stop that from mattering (p. 199).
   - Peter the Chanter (d. 1197) held that someone who had used one organ must stay chaste rather than use the other, "to avoid even the appearance of the sodomitic vice" (pp. 195–197).
   - **Design use:** a Catholic court can *register* a daughter. It will not accept that she *changed*. A Catholic bishop's response should be "swear to one sex and keep it", not prosecution. Rolker dates the harsher court practice, which conflated anatomy, sex change and sexual deviance, to the sixteenth century (abstract, p. 178; pp. 209–219), which falls outside the game's dates.
2. **Self-castration made a man irregular for the clergy, while eunuchs cut by others were admitted.**
   - Nicaea canon 1 entered Gratian as D.55 c.7 (NPNF note; item 3). It removes from ministry anyone who "in sound health has castrated himself", but it admits men made eunuchs "by barbarians, or by their masters".
   - **Design use:** a Latin canonist would turn Adelphai Dorleyaiou's Houses upside down. Grandmother's House captives are blameless and could even be clergy. The women of Gift Alone, who asked for the knife, are the guilty ones.
   - This makes a good hostile Catholic event: "the knife you chose bars you from the altar; the knife you suffered does not."
3. **Aquinas forbids cross-dressing *because the pagans did it for their gods*.**
   - ST II-II q.169 a.2 ad 3 (item 2): wearing the other sex's clothes is "in itself sinful". Deuteronomy forbade it "because the Gentiles used to practice this change of attire for the purpose of idolatrous superstition". It is excused only by necessity, such as hiding from enemies or having no other clothes.
   - **Design use:** this is the Latin text behind the bishop's line "Cybele in Christian clothes". A Catholic theologian would read Adelphai Dorleyaiou as a revival of exactly the idolatry the precept was aimed at. The "necessity" exception offers a mild-path opinion that Catholic characters can hold.
4. **The church praised crossings toward manhood and had no category for crossings the other way.** (The first two examples are female-to-male cases, used here as reaction data only.)
   - Hotchkiss (item 9) counts dozens of saints assigned female at birth who lived as monks. Mills (item 8) shows Eugenia's trial at Vézelay (c. 1120–40) carved on a pilgrimage church.
   - Rykener, London 1394 (items 10–11), was recorded by the mayor's court as "John" and treated through the categories of prostitution and sodomy. Wingard (item 7) summarises the case for reading her as a persistent woman named Eleanor.
   - **Design use:** Catholic opinion of a woman who lives as a monk can be "holy". The same courts' opinion of a daughter is "a man in women's clothes", and the charge is sodomy or fraud, not heresy.
5. **Local church courts could be strikingly calm.** (Female-to-male or intersex case: reaction data only. The central-Europe search may also be covering it.)
   - In Rottweil in 1388, Katharina, who had become Hans, married a woman. The townsmen sent the couple to the church court at Constance to ask whether the marriage was valid. Hans was inspected and the couple were sent home together (Rolker, item 4, pp. 609–610).
   - Rolker reads the chronicler's tone as entirely unexcited. The entry sits among the onion and beer prices.
   - **Design use:** in the fourteenth century, before the Reformation-era panic, a low-hostility "the official sends them home" outcome is defensible for a Catholic diocese.

### 2. Expansion fit: Latin Christendom

**The fit is poor. I do not recommend a fourth faith seated in the Latin West.**

- **No gate.** The Latin West had no ritual institution that turned anyone toward womanhood. Its two crossings both ran toward manhood: the monk-saint and the "virago". Castration there was a punishment (Abelard; see Tracy, item 13) or a clerical disqualification, never a rite. Latin eunuchs held no court office, so nothing parallels the Byzantine posts.
- **Only a legal hook, not a gate.** The nearest thing to a gate is the canonists' oath (finding 1): before a bishop, a person of doubtful sex says which sex she chooses and swears to keep it.
  - A heresy could take that oath and open it to anyone ("the bishop of Turin let her choose"). That would make it a declaration rite rather than a body-changing one.
  - It is better used as **flavour for Declare Before Witnesses under Catholic lords**. Susa sits in the Piedmont, inside the diocese of Turin. Whether CK3 has Susa as its own county or only as a barony is *unverified in the game files*.
- **If a Latin faith is ever wanted**, the least bad frame is a Beguine-style lay women's network in the Low Countries or the Rhineland (Liège, Cologne), branded heretical "Free Spirit" in the fourteenth century.
  - It would work as Houses, not a gate, and would most likely need to borrow the Dorleyaian or Bædling rite.
  - **Risks:**
    - It would be an invention with no trans-feminine evidence behind it.
    - It would repeat the "secret matriarchal heresy" trope.
    - The Beguine sources are outside this search and *unverified* for this purpose.
- **Recommended:** keep the Latin West as a reaction zone, with three tiers of Catholic response:
  - the diocesan court, which registers her and makes her swear;
  - the theologian's idolatry charge (Aquinas);
  - the city court's sodomy or fraud charge (Rykener).

### Findings that matter for the design

1. **The knife is a hard halakhic line, and a cut man stays a man in Jewish law.**
   - Maimonides forbids destroying a male's reproductive organs "in every place", for humans and animals alike, "not with their own bodies, nor with the bodies of others". Whoever castrates is lashed by Torah law, even after someone else has cut first (*Mishneh Torah*, Issurei Bi'ah 16:10–11).
   - A potion that sterilises a man is also forbidden, but it carries no lashes. A woman may take one (16:12). This matters if any gate uses herbs instead of the knife.
   - Law sorts the eunuch by cause. A man maimed "by the hand of heaven" (born so) may marry a Jewish woman. A man cut by human hand may not (16:1, 16:9). This is the rabbinic pair *seris ḥammah* (the "eunuch of the sun", congenital) and *seris adam* (man-made), from bYevamot 80b (Strassfeld 2013, p. 136).
   - Strassfeld (2013, p. 176) concludes that in Tannaitic law the *seris* "is, for the most part, fully male".
   - **Reaction:** a Jewish court would read a Dorleyite daughter of the knife as a *seris adam*. That means a damaged man barred from marrying into Israel, made so by a forbidden act. A Jew who wielded or ordered the knife would be liable to lashes. The Aristotelian claim that she "rises a daughter" has no purchase in halakha. The Galenic "third thing" fares only a little better.
   - **Expansion:** a Jewish-adjacent faith cannot use the knife as its gate without ceasing to be Jewish-adjacent.
2. **The strongest form of "six genders in the Talmud" is one sentence, and it is about law and bodies, not identities.**
   - The core text is the *androginos* list in Tosefta Bikkurim 2 and Mishnah Bikkurim 4. The *androginos* is in some ways like men, in some like women, in some like both, and in some like neither. It closes: "the *androginos* is a created being of its own (*beriyah bifnei atzmah*), and the sages could not decide about him".
     - Manuscripts assign that sentence to R. Yose (Kulp's translation) or R. Meir (Sefaria's vocalized printed text).
     - Where the list belongs is itself disputed. Strassfeld (2013, p. 143) notes that it sits variously in the Tosefta and the Mishnah, beside the *koy*, a half-wild, half-domestic animal.
   - **The other categories are legal statuses defined by bodily signs:**
     - *tumtum*: genitals hidden; "sometimes a man, sometimes a woman", so a status of doubt;
     - *aylonit*: a woman who does not develop;
     - *seris ḥammah* and *seris adam*: the two eunuchs above.
     - Fonrobert, Strassfeld and Lev all read the system as a taxonomy that manages bodies against an assumed binary. It does not recognise self-declared genders.
   - **Where the popular claim comes from.** The "six/seven/eight genders" lists are activist appropriations, chiefly through Elliot Kukla's 2006 HUC thesis (titled with R. Yose's phrase "A Created Being of Its Own") and the Kukla–Zellman pieces on TransTorah.org (Strassfeld 2013, p. 97 n. 164; pp. 256–257).
     - Strassfeld is sympathetic. But he notes that the texts are "rhetorically freighted, outright violent, or subtly pejorative" (p. 257). He calls his own project "skeptical at the prospect of reclaiming 'authentic' intersex voices" (p. 248).
   - **Use:** only in a faith's or a character's own voice ("the sages themselves said she is a creature of her own"). The narrator should never say the Talmud recognised six genders.
   - None of these categories is a male-to-female crossing. The androginos and tumtum are intersex categories. Using them as trans-women precedent is precisely the appropriation scholars flag.
3. **The one medieval Jewish text that asks to be turned from male to female is Kalonymus ben Kalonymus's prayer in *Even Boḥan* (c. 1322). It grounds the wish in Talmudic precedent.**
   - The prayer lists God's transformations:
     - Abraham turned from the fire;
     - "You turned Dinah in her mother's womb" (והפכת דינה במעי אמה);
     - the staff to a serpent, the sea to dry land.
   - It then asks: מי יתן ותהפכני מזכר לנקבה, "would that You turn me from male to female" (Lemberg 1865 ed.: the lament runs pp. 15–19; the turnings and Dinah are on p. 17, the petition on p. 18; checked against the page images, `jud_kalonymus_even_bohan.md`).
   - It ends in acceptance. He calls the male body a permanent blemish (the phrase אי אפשי can mean either that it cannot be removed or that he does not wish to remove it; `jud_kalonymus_even_bohan.md`), and resolves to bless "who has not made me a woman" "in a low voice, in a weak tongue".
   - The Dinah precedent is bBerakhot 60a. Leah prayed, and "immediately [the fetus] was turned into a daughter" (מיד נהפכה לבת). The Gemara then limits it: one does not cite miracles, and the event is placed within forty days of conception.
   - **Reception history matters for the narrator rule:**
     - Chotzner (1900, p. 131) called it "one of the most humorous parts in the Eben Bochan".
     - Rosen (2000; 2003, ch. 8 "Circumcised Cinderella") reads it as Jewish "gender trouble", not plain jest.
     - Popular trans-history sites, and the Open Siddur's CC BY-SA "Prayer for Transformation", read it as dysphoria.
   - **Use:** a faith may claim "Kalonymus of Arles" in its own voice. The narrator presents the text as a satire whose sincerity is debated.
4. **Cross-dressing (*lo yilbash*, Deut. 22:5) was punishable, but its content was set by local custom, and Purim licensed it.**
   - Maimonides prescribes lashes for a man who "adorns himself as a woman" and for a woman who adorns herself as a man. What counts is "all according to the custom of the country" (*Mishneh Torah*, Avodah Zarah 12:10). A *tumtum* or *androginos* may not veil like a woman or shave like a man, but is not lashed.
   - Judah Minz of Padua (d. 1508; Responsa §17) permitted masks and men in women's dress on Purim "because of its innocent and joyful purpose". Isserles made this normative for Ashkenaz in his gloss on Shulḥan Arukh OH 696:8, allowing it only "for mere merriment" and as the town's worthies permit. The custom itself is older: Kalonymus describes young men in women's dress and necklaces on 14 Adar in Provence, c. 1322 (*Even Boḥan*, Lemberg ed., p. 29).
   - **Reaction:** Jewish courts would treat Bædling dress as a lashable offence in principle. In practice the Jewish minority had no power to enforce it on gentiles, so the response would be shunning.
   - **Expansion:** Purim, whose motto is *ve-nahafokh hu* ("it was turned around", Esther 9:1), shares the root *h-p-k* with Dinah's turning and with Kalonymus's ותהפכני. That gives a non-surgical gate built from the tradition's own grammar.
5. **The "Jewish eunuch-makers / slave-traders" material is contested and freighted. Keep it out of the mod.**
   - Ibn Ḥawqal and al-Muqaddasī put Jewish castrators at Pechina, already recorded in `hist_ayalon_eunuchs_caliphs.md` (L., pp. 349–352).
   - Liutprand's Verdun merchants making *carzimasia* for Spain are not named as Jews in his text. Grabowski 2015 nonetheless argues from the word's etymology against Toch's minimalism.
   - Toch 2013 argues that Jewish trade never formed a critical mass. Phelan 2023 traces the "Jewish monopoly of the slave trade" as a historiographic motif.
   - **Use:** no Jewish eunuch-trader characters, events or modifiers. Do not let the Dorleyite *Rescue the Daughters* war target Jewish characters as slavers.

### Expansion fit assessment

**Verdict: poor fit for a full fourth faith; reasonable fit for a small heterodox current inside Judaism, as events or a minor heresy, and good fit for reaction content.**

**Why a full faith is a poor fit:**
- **No community evidence.** Unlike Byzantium (court eunuchs), the Norse world (seiðr) or China (nüguan and temple societies), no medieval Jewish practice, office or group of male-to-female people has been found. The evidence is one literary prayer (Kalonymus), legal taxonomies about intersex bodies, a Purim licence, and late Kabbalistic speculation. A faith built on that would be invented almost whole, and it would be read as a claim about a real, living religion.
- **Halakha closes the obvious gates.** The knife is forbidden (finding 1). Women's dress is lashable except on Purim (finding 4). Any sect that keeps halakha has to work inside those limits. A sect that drops them stops being recognisably Jewish, which is the "Jewish-adjacent" problem.
- **CK3 geography.** In 1.20, Judaism's faiths are Rabbinic, Karaism, Haymanot, Malabarism, Samaritan and Kuzarism (`kabarism`, Khazar; holy sites include Itil and Panticapaeum).
  - The only Jewish realm is Khazaria at Itil (`c_itil`, barony `b_itil`).
  - A trans-feminine heresy seated at Itil has no evidence behind it. It would also step into the Khazar-origin myth, which antisemites use against Ashkenazi Jews. Stampfer 2013 doubts the conversion itself. **Do not seat it in Khazaria.**
- **Kabbalah runs the wrong way for this mod.**
  - Wolfson's reading (1995) is that the feminine is finally reabsorbed into the masculine. Green 2002 and Abrams 2004 dispute this, but neither makes Kabbalah a female-ward theology of the body.
  - Gender-crossing souls are mainly later. Scholem's EJ "Gilgul" says early Kabbalists tied transmigration to sexual and procreative sins. "A male soul enters a female body, resulting in sterility" belongs to later, Safed-era debate (16th c., outside the period).

**If a minor current is wanted anyway:**
- **Name.** *Ha-Nehpakhot*, "the women who were turned", from the shared root of Berakhot 60a (נהפכה לבת), Esther 9:1 and Kalonymus.
  - Avoid "Daughters of Dinah". Dinah's story continues into Genesis 34, which is freighted.
- **Gate: the Turning.** Prayer and a new name at Purim (14 Adar), "in a low voice" reversed into a loud one. A woman's garment is put on publicly on the one day the codes allow it and is never taken off.
  - No knife. No potion: 16:12 forbids it to men without lashes. That is a usable grey zone, but avoid it as a gate.
  - The faith's own claim is that she was turned "as Dinah was". The rabbinic counter-claim is "one does not cite miracles" and "it was within forty days".
- **Seat.** Arles: barony `b_arles` in `c_venaissin`. Kalonymus's birthplace (Chotzner p. 128: born at Arles "in the year 1287"; archive.org metadata gives 1286). Provence is also where early Kabbalah (the *Bahir*) circulated.
  - Alternatives: `b_narbonne` (in `c_carcassonne`) and Montpellier/Lunel (`c_montpellier`).
  - The seat should be a congregation (*kahal*), not a holding. Provençal Jews held property (notably at Narbonne) but not lordships, so the mechanic is a minority faith with no realm, much like the Way of the Jade Embryo before it gains a court.
- **Era.** Kalonymus fixes the earliest believable date at about 1322. A late-game spawn, 1300s, fits. Earlier is unsupported.
- **Community structure.** A confraternity (*ḥevrah*) inside the *kahal*. It is unsourced in this search, so it needs Goitein-type or Ashkenazic confraternity sources before design.
- **Culture.** Provençal Jewish (CK3 has no separate Jewish culture; use the local one with the faith).

**Risks:**
- Antisemitic tropes: castrators and eunuch-traders, Khazar origins, "Talmudic abstractions". `book_transgender_marxism.md` already notes Cohen 2019 on Nazi links between antisemitism and transmisogyny.
- Implying a real living community, such as modern trans Jews or named modern rabbis, as the faith's descendants.
- The narrator claiming Kalonymus, the androginos or the "six genders" as fact (findings 2 and 3).
- Treating intersex categories as trans precedent.
- Lurianic material (1570s) used as if medieval.

**Reaction content (recommended regardless):**
- Rabbinic and Karaite characters treat Dorleyite daughters as *seris adam*: "a man the law forbids to marry, made so by a sin". They may be heard, traded with and pitied, not married.
- Bædling *wæpnedmenn* in women's dress are judged under *lo yilbash*, with a Purim exception event.
- Karaites, who reject the Oral Torah, would not be bound by the androginos and eunuch distinctions, only by Lev. 22:24 and Deut. 22:5, 23:2. No Karaite source was found; treat this as inference.

### Findings that matter for the design

1. **The one clear male-to-female case in the German lands ended at the stake, in her own dress.**
   - Lübeck, 1382 or 1384. Hermann Korner's *Chronica novella* tells of a "hermafrodita" who served as a priest by day and lived as a woman with men by night.
   - Item 4, recension A §633, pp. 76–77. The same story is §964 in recensions D and B, p. 314, dated 1384.
   - She was burned "in habitu illo femineo, quo uti consueverat in noctibus" (in the woman's dress she used to wear at night) (p. 77).
   - Schwalm, the editor, thinks Korner was probably an eyewitness (p. III).
   - Rolker (item 1, p. 186) cites a close parallel from Lille in 1458, a Burgundian town on the Empire's edge. A man "dressed in women's clothes" who called himself both man and woman was burned for sodomy.
   - For a Bædling or Dorleyite daughter in an HRE court, the hostile rung is this: the dress itself becomes the charge and the means of execution.
2. **The courts were relaxed about crossing toward man and hostile to crossing toward woman.**
   - Rolker (item 1) finds no medieval prosecution of hermaphrodites as such (pp. 184–187).
   - In 1388 the church court at Konstanz inspected Katharina/Hans of Rottweil, who had married a woman, and "sent home again together" (p. 208). The chronicle text and translation are items 2 and 3.
   - Rolker says "tolerance" may have been limited to choices that fit the gender order (pp. 210–211). He also writes that "hermaphrodites living as men but … 'choosing' the female gender may have been in a much more uncomfortable position" (p. 210).
   - By the 16th century the practice had hardened. Worms granted Elisabeth/Johannes a change of name in 1527, and she was later burned (pp. 212–213).
   - Design consequence: a German Christian lord should treat a daughter's petition far worse than the reverse. The asymmetry is documented, not assumed.
   - Every 1388 and 1527 case is female-to-male, so it is *reaction* data only.
3. **The Orthodox reaction is canonical and festive, not forensic.**
   - Muscovy's Stoglav (1551) ch. 93 repeats the Quinisext (Trullo) canon 62: men and boys must not adorn themselves in women's dress, nor women in men's. It adds skomorokhi (minstrels) to the list (item 19).
   - Ch. 90 forbids clerics women's ornament.
   - In Rus' the offence is placed in festival mumming and "Hellenic" games, so a Rus' bishop would treat a crossing as pagan play to be suppressed with penance, not as a capital crime.
   - Rus' also accepted Byzantine eunuch prelates. The Primary Chronicle s.a. 1090 records Metropolitan John "the eunuch", whom onlookers called "a dead man come" (item 20).
4. **No source in the region describes a gender-crossing priesthood or rite.**
   - Thietmar, Helmold, Herbord and Saxo describe West Slavic priests who are powerful and male. Helmold says the Rani king is "of little esteem compared with the flamen" (II.12, p. 215).
   - The divination these sources describe is a sacred horse walking over spears (Thietmar VI.24, p. 148; Herbord in item 14, pp. 79–80).
   - Baltic priestly detail comes mostly from 16th-century writers (Grunau, Łasicki), whom specialists distrust (item 25).
   - Noaidi were male (see Tolley, already read).
   - Any fourth faith here is a design invention resting on cult structure, not on attested crossing. The README should say so.
5. **Poland's documented knife is a punishment.**
   - Thietmar VIII.2 (p. 240): under Bolesław the Brave, a man convicted of adultery or fornication was nailed by the scrotum to the market bridge, with a razor beside him. He had the "hard choice" of death or cutting himself free.
   - This matches the README's line that in the north castration "punished thieves and unmade enemies".
   - Use it as a hostile omen or event flavour, and never as a precedent for the Dorleyite knife.

### Expansion fit assessment

**Candidate A: a Wendish (Rani) pagan faith seated at Arkona. Best map fit; zero gender evidence.**
- *Seat:* `c_rugen`, barony `b_rugen` (Polabian, Polish and Pomeranian cultural name `cn_arkona`), under `d_nordmark` in `00_landed_titles.txt`.
  - In vanilla 1.20, `rugen` is already an *eminent* holy site of `slavic_pagan` (with `kiev`) and a holy site of `baltic_pagan`.
  - The faith would sit in `slavic_religion`.
- *Other holy sites:* `c_stettin` (Triglav's temples, item 14), `c_brandenburg`, and Rethra/Riedegost (no county; nearest is `c_mecklenburg` or the Stargard area, *map placement unverified*).
- *Gate idea:* the god's horse. The priest leads the sacred horse over laid spears (Thietmar VI.24; Herbord II.33; Saxo XIV). A clean crossing means the god has seen a daughter. This rhymes with the Bædling völva who "sees rather than makes", which is a risk: two faiths would share one gate logic.
- *Head of faith:* the flamen of Svantevit, who outranks the king (Helmold II.12). A powerful theocratic office, and there was a real temple treasury fed by tribute from every Slavic province (Helmold I.52, II.12).
- *Hooks:*
  - "variae deorum dearumque imagines" (images of gods and goddesses) on Rethra's walls (Thietmar VI.23, p. 147);
  - "Siwa dea Polaborum" (Helmold I.52, p. 107);
  - Otto of Bamberg condemning Pomeranian women for killing infant daughters (item 14, p. 52). The faith could take up the rescue of daughters, echoing *Rescue the Daughters*.
- *Arc:* a hard clock. Rethra falls in the 1060s–1120s, and Arkona falls to Valdemar I and Absalon in 1168 (Saxo XIV). The Wendish Crusade (1147) and the Danish conquest give the faith a heroic last stand.
- *Risks:*
  - Every source is a hostile Christian outsider (method: item 18).
  - No gender crossing is attested.
  - Romantic neo-pagan reconstruction (Rodnovery) is close by.

**Candidate B: an urban Rhineland house of sisters, beguine-like, labelled "Free Spirit". Best fit for the evidence; overlaps Adelphai Dorleyaiou.**
- *Seat:* Cologne (`c_cologne`), with Strasbourg (`c_strassburg`), Speyer (`c_speyer`), Worms (`c_worms`), Würzburg (`c_wurzburg`) and Lübeck (`c_lubeck`). The reaction cases (Lübeck, Konstanz/Rottweil, Speyer, Worms) cluster in these towns.
- *Gate idea:* a second naming, a rebaptism with a woman's name before the house. This inverts the attested 1527 Worms *Umtaufung* (rebaptism), where a person petitioned the episcopal court to be renamed and was granted it (Rolker, item 1, p. 212). The inversion is design; the procedure is real.
- *Structure:*
  - beguine houses as women's communities without perpetual vows, self-supporting and under suspicion (items 9, 11);
  - a ready-made persecution ladder: the Council of Vienne decrees *Cum de quibusdam mulieribus* and *Ad nostrum* (issued 1311, promulgated 1317; Deane, item 9), and Strasbourg after 1317;
  - Carolina art. 116 (1532, after the game window) shows where the law was heading.
- *Risks:*
  - It is a second Christian heresy beside Adelphai Dorleyaiou.
  - The "Free Spirit" may be largely an inquisitors' construct (Lerner, item 10), so the faith should own the label as slander, the way the Bædlings own *ragr*.
  - No beguine source attests male-to-female crossing.

**Candidate C, Baltic (Romuva/Romowe, the Criwe, Vilnius fire-keepers): poor fit.**
- The fire-keeping *vaidilutės* (virgin priestesses) and Grunau's hierarchy are 16th-century or later.
- Dusburg's "Criwe" (1326) is a male quasi-pope.
- There is no crossing evidence, and the material is heavily romanticised.
- If wanted, the seat would be `c_konigsberg` (Sambia; Prussian culture, `baltic_pagan` in 867) or `c_kernave`, with `pokaini` and `torun` already vanilla eminent sites.

**Candidate D, Rus' mummers (Rusalii, skomorokhi): poor as a faith, good as events.**
- Festival cross-dressing is seasonal inversion, condemned as "Hellenic" play (item 19).
- "Double belief" (dvoeverie) framing is itself contested (item 22).
- Use it for Bædling events in Kyiv rather than a faith.

**Candidate E, Sámi noaidi: do not use.**
- The office is male, and Vivallen is one grave.
- Tolley warns against reading saga "Sámi magic" as evidence.
- This is a "third gender" trap.

**Recommendation.**
- If the fourth faith must be pagan and fill the map gap, take A. Be frank in the README that the gate is invented on a real oracle.
- If it may be Christian, B carries the strongest regional evidence. Its reaction ladder writes itself, but it needs a clear doctrinal line against Adelphai Dorleyaiou: no knife, the name rather than the body.
- The region's best immediate value is as *reaction* content for the existing three faiths:
  - an HRE rung "burned in her dress";
  - a Rus' rung "penance for mumming";
  - Polish punitive castration.

### Findings that matter for the design

1. **Maliki al-Andalus was hostile, and grew more hostile under the Almoravids. Córdoba's Zahiri dissent was much milder.**
   - Ibn ʿAbdūn's Seville *ḥisba* manual (c. 1100) orders that the *hāwī* be expelled from the city and punished wherever found, and kept away from Muslims and festivals (García Gómez and Lévi-Provençal 1948, pp. 157–158, quoted by Bollo-Panadero 2023, item 1, pp. 111–112).
   - Bollo-Panadero follows the translator's note (p. 57): *hāwī* can mean either "homosexual" or "male prostitute". It is not the word *mukhannath*.
   - Citing Tahiri, she contrasts this order with the more tolerant caliphal and taifa periods.
   - On liwāṭ, Ibn Ḥazm reports that Mālik held both partners are stoned whether or not they are muḥṣan, that is, married (Adang 2003, item 2, p. 17). Ibn Ḥazm himself, a Zahiri, caps the discretionary punishment far lower, at ten lashes for indecent kissing (p. 12 n.).
   - For the game, a Maliki ruler should treat the faith with hostility and expulsion, not tolerance. A Zahiri-leaning scholar is the one Andalusi voice of restraint.
2. **The law makes room for intersex people but none for a man becoming a woman.**
   - Gesink 2018 and Alipour and Gesink 2026 (items 3–4) show that Sunni jurists, Malikis among them, had detailed rules for the *khunthā*. Some later Maliki jurists treated the *khunthā mushkil* (one whose sex cannot be determined) as a third category (Alipour, AAR 2025 abstract, item 5).
   - That is accommodation of an ambiguous body, assigned by signs. It offers no route for a man to become a woman.
   - The faith's tenet should therefore be framed against the *khunthā* rules: "a daughter is not an undetermined body."
   - Do not import the "third category" result as tolerance. Tolino (item 7) argues that even Fatimid eunuchs were not a "third gender" but were gendered by context, which fits Bhatt's critique.
3. **The best medieval-adjacent evidence for organised male-to-female life in the region comes from Fez, c. 1500, and it is hostile. LATER.**
   - Leo Africanus (al-Ḥasan al-Wazzān), writing in 1526, describes the innkeepers of Fez (Pory's translation, 1600, Hakluyt ed. 1896, vol. 2, p. 429). They:
     - "goe apparelled like women, and shaue their beards";
     - imitate women's speech and sit down to spin;
     - are all "of one familie called Elcheua";
     - have a consul over them and pay tribute to the city governor;
     - must victual the army when the king campaigns.
   - On pp. 457–459 he describes a women's possession society. Its members are possessed by red, white and black spirits, which speak through them in changed voices. They tell a sick woman she cannot be cured "vnlesse she be admitted into their societie", and her husband then hosts a feast with dancing to drums. Leo calls these women *sahacat* (fricatrices).
   - This is the only attestation I found in the region of:
     - a corporate, taxed, recognised body of people living in women's dress (the innkeepers);
     - an initiation that admits a person by declaration (the possession society).
   - Both come through a hostile convert's polemic. The term "Elcheua" is unexplained in the edition.
     - My hypothesis, *unverified*: it may be the same root as Ibn ʿAbdūn's *hāwī*. This should be checked against Arabic-literate scholarship such as Davis 2006 (item 15).
4. **Sahelian courts had powerful eunuch offices but no attested male-to-female tradition before 1453. Bori's gender-crossing 'yan daudu are documented only in colonial and later ethnography.**
   - The Kano Chronicle (Palmer 1908, item 12) says:
     - Queen Amina of Zaria "first had eunuchs and kolas in Hausaland" (p. 75);
     - Muḥammad Rumfa (1463–1499) "began the custom of giving to eunuchs the offices of state" (p. 78).
   - The chronicle was compiled in the 19th century and is LATER as a text. Amina's date is disputed.
   - Its pre-Islamic cult of the sacred tree Tchunburburai (pp. 62–63) is a priest-and-drummer sacrificial rite. It shows no gender crossing.
   - The Galadima Daudu of Abdullahi Burja's reign (1438–1452, pp. 75–76) is a war leader. A modern claim online links him to the Bori spirit behind the name 'yan daudu. That claim is *unverified*; do not use it.
   - Okagbue 2008 and Gaudio 2009 (items 13–14) are 20th–21st-c. ethnography. Gaudio explicitly avoids equating 'yan daudu with trans women.
5. **Ethiopia and Coptic Egypt react to male-to-female crossing with refusal. Their only praise for crossing is FtM.**
   - Al-ʿUmarī (item 16) reports that the ruler of Amḥara (the Solomonic emperor) banned castration. Slave boys were castrated at Washlū outside his realm and brought to Hadiya to recover, where "more of them died than survived".
   - The Coptic legend of Hilaria, the emperor's daughter who lived as a monk, sits in a genre the church praised: holy women disguised as men (Davis 2002, item 17). **FtM.** The mirror case of a man living as a woman has no such genre.
   - Christian Iberia also borrowed eunuchs (*ciclaues*, from *ṣaqāliba*) in the 10th–12th centuries (Gallon 2024, item 8). This is a small precedent for northern Christian courts using Andalusi categories.

### Expansion fit assessment

**Overall: a weak-to-moderate fit, and the region is better used for reaction than for a fourth faith.** If a southern faith is wanted, the least-bad candidate is a **Maghribi possession sisterhood seated at Fez**. Every other sub-region is a poor fit.

**Candidate faith (working name only): "the Sisters of the Drum", in Fez and the Rif.**
- **Evidence base:**
  - The Fez women's possession society that admits members by declaration (finding 3).
  - The Fez innkeepers in women's dress, organised under a consul (finding 3).
  - The Ghumara heterodoxy of Ḥā-Mīm (925 CE). His Berber Qur'an had the faithful say "Je crois à Tanguît": his aunt Tanqīt was a diviner and magician, and his sister Daddjū a diviner consulted in war (al-Bakrī, item 10, pp. 197–198).
  - The Barghawāṭa's own Berber scripture (al-Bakrī pp. 259 ff.).
- **What this gives:** a precedent for Amazigh prophetic movements that wrote women's sacred authority into a creed.
- **Gate:** the drum night. The candidate is "sick" and can be cured only by admission. Possessed sisters dance her to the drums, the spirit names her a daughter, and the household holds the feast.
  - This follows Leo's structure (diagnosis, admission, feast with drums).
  - It also fits the mod's Declare Before Witnesses: naming before the rite.
  - It differs from the other three gates (the knife, the high seat, the embryo), because the change comes by a spirit's word.
- **Seat:** Fez (county and duchy of Fes in CK3). An alternative is the Ghumara coast near Tetouan, Ḥā-Mīm's mountain; check the CK3 county name. Fez fits the "Houses" mechanic: Leo's innkeepers already form a city corporation that pays tribute and victuals armies.
- **Culture:** CK3's Berber cultures (Masmudi, Zenati, Sanhaji in vanilla; check the names in 1.20), with Andalusi refugees after 1250.
- **Era arc:**
  - 925: Ḥā-Mīm.
  - c. 1100: the Almoravid crackdown (Ibn ʿAbdūn).
  - 13th c.: Marinid Fez.
  - c. 1500: Leo's Fez.
  - The arc's best evidence sits at its end, just after 1453.

**Risks:**
1. **The sources are all hostile.**
   - Leo was writing for Italian patrons after his conversion, and calls the women "abominable witches".
   - al-Bakrī calls Ḥā-Mīm "le faussaire" (the forger).
   - Ibn ʿAbdūn wants expulsion.
   - The faith would be read against the grain. That matches the mod's method, but it should be said plainly in the README.
2. **Romanticisation.**
   - Leo's *sahacat* are women, and the possession cult is a women's institution. It is not evidence for trans women.
   - The innkeepers are not attested as women; they are men who dress as women and keep concubines.
   - Do not call either a "third gender" (Bhatt; Tolino).
3. **Projecting back from Gnawa, Bori or zār.** These are 19th–20th-c. ethnography (LATER).
4. **Kahina.** She is a 9th-c.-onward legend that has been reworked by Arab, French, Berber-nationalist and feminist writers (Hannoum 2001, item 11). Do not make her a founder.
5. **No Ibadi evidence was found.** The Mzab and Jabal Nafusa Ibadis are a gap.

**Sub-regions judged a poor fit:**
- **Sahel and Hausaland:** no pre-1453 gender-crossing evidence. Bori and 'yan daudu are LATER.
- **Ethiopia and Nubia:** the emperor banned castration, and no male-to-female tradition was found.
- **Fatimid and Mamluk Egypt:** a strong eunuch elite, but Adelphai Dorleyaiou already covers the eunuch-court niche, and Tolino rejects a third-gender reading. Better used as reaction: Fatimid courts would respect a daughter's service, as eunuchs were respected, and Mamluk moralists would mock her.

**Reaction summary by faith.** Each line gives a suggested opinion and modifier direction.
- **Sunni Maliki (al-Andalus, Maghrib, Sahel):** hostile. Expulsion of *hāwī*, and stoning for liwāṭ in Mālik's view. The *khunthā* is accommodated but only by assignment. Items 1–5.
- **Zahiri (Ibn Ḥazm):** hostile in doctrine, mild in penalty. Item 2.
- **Fatimid Ismaili:** eunuchs as trusted ministers (Jawdhar, Barjawān), gendered by context. Positive toward a daughter as servant; uncertain toward her as ruler. Items 6–7.
- **Coptic and Ethiopian Orthodox:** they praise women who lived as men (FtM) and refuse castration. Hostile. Items 16–18.
- **West African traditional religions:** no evidence either way before 1453. Keep neutral and avoid inventing.
- **Iberian Catholic (León, Navarre, Aragon):** used eunuchs sparingly, borrowing from al-Andalus. Neutral-to-hostile. Item 8.

### 1. Findings that matter for the design

1. **The Pāli Vinaya treats a monk who changes sex as an administrative transfer, not a scandal. The commentaries then make the male-to-female direction a karmic fall.** This is the strongest reaction datum in the region.
   - At Vin III.35, when "the sexual features of a woman appeared on a certain monk", the Buddha allows "the same teacher, the same ordination, the same rainy seasons" among the nuns. The same rule runs for a nun who becomes a man.
   - Anderson's summary: "Nothing changes for the monk (now a nun) … except their biological sex, their gender, and the fact that they now have to belong to the Order of nuns" (Anderson 2017, item 2, p. 1, section "Sex Change in the Pāli Canon").
   - Buddhaghosa's *Samantapāsādikā* (5th c.) adds the cause and the asymmetry: "The male sexual features disappear due to powerful bad actions" (item 2, p. 2). It also stages the change at night, while the monk is asleep.
   - For the game:
     - A Theravāda court should accept a daughter's *standing*: her seniority and ordination carry over, and she moves to the women's side.
     - It should read her *change* as demerit.
     - It never calls her a third thing.
   - One caution from the bhikkhunī lineage. The Sri Lankan nuns' order is usually said to have lapsed after the Chola sack of Anurādhapura (1017). On that view, from c. 1050 a transferred daughter would have no Theravāda order to enter. This is not verified from scholarship here (see the Need list).
2. **Monastic law needs a "third" category to exclude, and both Buddhist and Jain law bar the congenital third from ordination.**
   - Both Jain sects forbid ordination to the congenital *napuṃsaka*. They hold that "a hermaphrodite may not receive ordination" (Jaini 1991, item 5, p. 11).
   - The Digambara argument against women's liberation is built on the napuṃsaka as its agreed example (p. 112).
   - But the Śvetāmbaras will ordain a castrated man (*kṛtrimaklība*) as "essentially a male" (p. 190 n. 35).
   - So a Jain court would count a daughter of the knife as a man, and a congenitally Marked woman as barred.
   - Gyatso (item 1) and Cabezón (item 3) are the standard accounts of the Buddhist paṇḍaka exclusion. Both are P1 and not open access.
3. **The Brahmanical law books cast the *klība* as defective, not sacred.**
   - Manu 9.201 bars "the impotent [*klība*] and the outcast" from inheritance.
   - 9.202 asks heirs to give them food and clothing.
   - 9.203 lets the children of a *klība* who marries inherit. (Checked against the Sanskrit and an English rendering.)
   - The Kāmasūtra's *tṛtīyā prakṛti* (2.9) is a passage about sexual service, not a priesthood (Doniger, item 8).
   - This is exactly Bhatt's "dumping-ground" point. A Hindu faith in the game should *react* through law: the daughter is maintained but disinherited. It is not a model for a trans-women's faith.
   - Hinchy (item 10) shows that hijra history is overwhelmingly 19th-century. Claims of hijra antiquity are a historiographical problem, not a datum.
4. **The Delhi Sultanate gives the closest analogue to the Dorleyite court and to the faith's line.**
   - Eunuchs held the highest offices:
     - Malik Kafur was army commander and *nāʾib*.
     - Malik Sarwar became *wazīr* with the title Khwāja-i Jahān in 1389, governed the east, and founded the Jaunpur sultanate in 1394. Correction (`sea_jackson_delhi_sultanate.md`): Jackson does not call Sarwar a eunuch, and Sarwar never took the title of sultan; his adopted son did after 1399. Treat "eunuch" as *unverified*.
   - Native nobles resented them. Turkish and Tajik maliks forced out ʿImād al-Dīn Rayḥān, an Indian-born eunuch, in 1253–54 (Sarwar and Pervaiz 2026, item 12, pp. 38–39, after Jackson 1999).
   - Malik Sarwar's succession by his adopted son is a match for "Take as Daughter". Confirmed in Jackson 1999.
   - Sultan Raḍiyya (r. 1236–40) appeared in men's dress (Jackson, item 13). This is *female-to-male reaction data only*: a court that tolerated a woman in male dress as ruler.
5. **Almost nothing in Southeast Asia is dated before 1453. The one pre-1453 witness is hostile. Oceania is entirely post-contact.**
   - The single pre-1453 witness is Zhou Daguan at Angkor (1296–97). He records that "the country has many *erxing ren* [persons of two forms]", who gather daily in groups of ten or more in the market and court Chinese visitors. He calls this "shameful and detestable" (《真臘風土記》, section 人物; Harris trans., item 15).
   - Jackson (item 14) finds Thai kathoey culture to be a modern formation. Its earliest European witness is late 19th-century.
   - The bissu and baylan witnesses start in 1521 (Pigafetta) and 1545 (Paiva).
   - In Polynesia, "the few direct testimonies" from first contact "all refer to Tahiti" (Besnier and Alexeyeff, item 17, p. 37). These date from the 1760s–80s.

### 2. Expansion fit assessment

**South Asia (Hindu, Jain, Buddhist, Sultanate): poor fit for a faith; strong fit for reaction.**
- No pre-1453 institution here treats a man-become-woman as a *woman* with a rite of passage.
- The categories (paṇḍaka, napuṃsaka, klība, tṛtīyā prakṛti) are defined by deficiency. Inventing a "hijra faith" in 867–1453 would project 19th-century ethnography backward, which Hinchy, Reddy and Bhatt all warn against.
- Best use: tune vanilla Hindu, Jain and Theravāda reactions to the daughters (findings 1–3), and give Sultanate courts the Dorleyite eunuch offices with native-noble resentment (finding 4).
- If a South Asian *location* is wanted, Jaunpur (c_jaunpur) is a strong event setting for an adopted-heir dynasty, not a seat (its founder's eunuch status is unverified).

**Theravāda splinter ("the Order that keeps the changed"): possible but thin.**
- It would be a Buddhist heresy that takes Vin III.35 at face value and refuses the commentary's karmic reading.
  - Gate: *waking changed*, a dream rite in the rains retreat.
  - Seat: Anurādhapura (b_anuradhapura) before 1017, or Pagan (c_pagan).
- This is the best-*sourced* option in the region, but it has no historical community behind it. Like Adelphai Dorleyaiou, it would be an invented heresy.
- Risk: Buddhist law presumes sex change happens *to* you. A rite of asking cuts against the text. That tension could be the faith's doctrine.

**Island Southeast Asia: the only real candidate.**
- Candidate A, Visayan, after Brewer's "femininity as the vehicle" reading:
  - Gate: the call by the spirits, then the asog taking women's dress and the babaylan's office.
  - Seat: Cebu (c_PHI_bool_and_cebu) or Panay (c_PHI_panay).
  - Vanilla already names its neuter priest "babaylan".
- Candidate B, Bugis:
  - Gate: *irebba*, the shrouded ritual death and return.
  - Seat: Luwu (c_SuMa_luwu), the I La Galigo kingdom.
- Risks:
  - Every primary witness is from 1521 or later; projecting back to 867 means three to six centuries without evidence.
  - The bissu are not trans women. Andaya places them *outside* both genders, includes female bissu, and some bissu lived as men outside the rite. Making them the model risks exactly the third-gender framing the brief forbids.
  - Brewer's asog fits the mod's doctrine better.
- No castration appears in either tradition, so this would be a gate without the knife, like Bædling.
- Verdict: a fair fit as a fourth faith *only* if it is marked as reconstruction in the faith's own voice, with Candidate A preferred.

**Mainland Southeast Asia (Khmer, Thai, Mon, Burmese): poor fit.**
- Zhou Daguan is the only medieval datum, and he is hostile.
- Nat kadaw and kathoey material is 19th–21st century (items 14 and 16).

**Majapahit and Java: poor fit on current evidence.**
- The Old Javanese *Wirāṭaparwa* (dated 996 CE) keeps Arjuna's year disguised as the dance-master Wṛhannalā (Zoetmulder, item 18). That is a literary motif, not an institution.
- No Majapahit-period gender-crossing office was found. The Old Javanese terms *wandu* and *kĕdi* are a lead to check in Zoetmulder's dictionary (*unverified*).

**Oceania: out.** It is off the map, and the evidence is post-contact.
