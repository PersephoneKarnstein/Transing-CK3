**What was read.** The whole chapter (16 pp.): the introduction (pp. 205–207); Liu Yiming's *Xiuzhen biannan* 修真辯難 (1798), questions 91–95, Chinese text with Valussi's Italian translation (pp. 207–211); the verse *Nüdan fa* 女丹法, from *Huixin waiji* 會心外集 (1801), Chinese and Italian (pp. 211–217); bibliography. The text is in Italian; all English below is our translation of her Italian, checked against the Chinese she prints.

**Date warning.** Everything here is Qing (1798 and 1801). Valussi says nüdan texts "of a century earlier" still lacked "a precise procedure and a coherent language" (p. 212). None of it can appear in narrator text for 867–1453.

## 1. The argument and the texts

- **What the chapter is.** A short memorial piece: Valussi's student translations of the two Liu Yiming passages most often reprinted in women's-alchemy anthologies. Her prose is introduction and footnotes. The value lies in the Chinese text and her notes.
- **Women's practice starts at the breasts, not the belly.** In nüdan, blood goes from the heart to the point between the breasts (*ruxi* 乳溪), to the breasts, where it leaves as milk, and to the uterus (*xuehai* 血海), where it leaves as menses (*jing* 經, *chilong* 赤龍, *chimai* 赤脈). The woman's practice "begins exactly at the point between the breasts" (p. 207). The two paths have names: "Supreme Yang refines Breath" for men, "Supreme Yin refines Form" (*taiyin lianxing* 太陰煉形) for women (p. 207).
- **Q91: what the red dragon is for (pp. 208–209).** The man "subdues the White Tiger" (*baihu jiang* 白虎降) and "becomes a child's body" (變為童體). The printed Chinese for the woman says that when the red channel is cut she "becomes a male body" (赤脈斬，則變為男體). **Valussi's Italian, though, translates the woman's outcome as "she will again have an adolescent body"** ("tornerà ad avere un corpo adolescente", p. 209). Her translation follows the "child's body" reading, not the 男體 her own Chinese prints. The woman "can go out of death and enter life" (可以出死，可以入生), which Valussi notes inverts *Daodejing* 51 (p. 209 n. 11). Xin & Xu (2026, p. 14) quote the same sentence from Yan Zehuan's c. 1900 reworking with 童體 ("child's body") for the woman too. **The end state is a live textual variant: "male body" in one witness, "child's body" in another.**
- **Q92: women are faster (pp. 209–210).** A man needs three years to "hide the breath", a woman one; a woman may finish in "three to five years". The condition is backhanded praise: she must be a *nüzhong zhangfu* 女中丈夫, "a great man among women", with "a hundred times" a man's strength (p. 210). Valussi traces the phrase to Chan Buddhist praise of heroic nuns (n. 13).
- **Q93: "The Way is the same, the methods differ"** (其道則同，其用則異, p. 210).
- **Q94: how the dragon is cut (pp. 210–211).** Transform the breath first, then "the blood turns its direction and rises, enters the breasts, and the red is transmuted into white" (以赤變白). When the fire of desire is out, "the True Fire emerges".
- **Q95: the key passage on "woman becomes man" (p. 211).** The disciple asks whether taking the elixir makes "women turn into men and the old into children" (女轉成男，老變為童). Liu answers: "This speaks of the principle, not of the form" (此言其理，非言其形). A woman who has stripped away all yin has "a body of pure Yang, equal to men who complete the path; therefore it is said: women turn into men." Likewise "the old turn into children". "We are not speaking of a change of the illusory image" (非言其變幻像也). Valussi glosses the illusory image as outward bodily marks, "the distinctions of gender (man/woman) or of age" (n. 15).
- **The *Nüdan fa* verse (pp. 211–217).** Its points:
  - The Great Dao "does not distinguish men and women" (大道不分男與女); the oral instructions separate them "as far as two poles" (p. 212).
  - The first step is cutting the red dragon, after which "no calamities" follow (p. 213).
  - The physical change: "the turbid blood transforms … the breasts shrink against the chest, the trigram turns over" (兩乳縮胸卦倒顛, p. 214). Valussi explains that Kan ☵ swaps its middle line and becomes Qian ☰ (n. 23).
  - The next line: "Although [now] the image of the woman's body resembles a man's, her foundations are solid and do not change" (雖然女像男子體，基址堅固沒變遷, p. 214).
  - The embryo is "warmed and nourished carefully for ten months" (謹慎溫養十個月), and then "a sudden thunderclap reveals the heaven beyond heaven" (霹靂一聲天外天, p. 216).
  - The end is the Queen Mother's Jasper Pool (王母瑤池駕彩鸞, p. 216).
  - Liu wrote the poem so that "the heroines of the women's chambers" (閨閣英雄) could study it alone (p. 217). Valussi notes that women could not go out to find masters (n. 37).
  - The poem mocks Buddhist incense, vegetarianism and sutra recitation as women's empty piety (pp. 217–218).
- **The body as a trap.** On 太陰煉形女蹄筌, Valussi cites Zhuangzi's fish trap: "once the fish is caught one forgets the trap", so the female body is "a net or trap" that dissolves to reveal the essence (p. 212 n. 17).

**Small corrections to the reading list.**
- Valussi translates the last half-line 駕彩鸞 as the name of a deity, "He Cailuan" (p. 216 n. 36). The characters more naturally read "riding the coloured *luan*-phoenix". The female immortal usually named in this context is **Wu Cailuan** 吳彩鸞, a Tang figure. Check before using either name.
- `china_reading_list.md` item 5 says Liu's insistence that the adept does not become a man is on pp. 207–210. It is on **p. 211 (Q95) and p. 214 (the verse)**. Q91 on p. 208 says the opposite (變為男體).

## 3. In-world lines (faith's voice; no attribution to Liu Yiming)

- "The Way is one; only the methods differ." (after Q93)
- "They say she turns into a man, and mean a principle. We say she is a woman, and mean her."
- "Warm it ten months. Then thunder, and heaven beyond heaven."
- "The red turns to white, the breath rises, and the fire of wanting goes out." (narrator-safe as a generic neidan image)
- Hostile (an alchemist): "The woman's body is the trap; when the fish is caught you forget the trap." Faith's answer: "Our body is not the trap. It is the fish."
