# Geng, "The Body, Gender, and Religious Practices" (2025): nüdan and Buddhist female-to-male change, design reading

**Citation (verified from the PDF).** Geng Qiongke 耿瓊珂 (Shandong University), "The Body, Gender, and Religious Practices: A Comparative Study of Daoist Inner Alchemy for Women and Buddhist Thoughts on the Female-to-Male Transformation," *Religions* 16, no. 10 (2025): 1222, 18 pp. DOI 10.3390/rel16101222. Received 20 July 2025, accepted 19 September 2025, published 24 September 2025. CC BY 4.0. The citation in `china_reading_list.md` item 8 is correct, with one page fix (below).

**What was read.** The whole article: §1 introduction; §2 nüdan's "women change their bodies to become men" (pp. 2–5); §3 Buddhist "transform a woman into a man" (pp. 6–10); §4 comparison (pp. 10–15); §5 conclusions; notes 1–5. The long excursus on the *Mānava-Dharmaśāstra* (pp. 8–9) was skimmed.

## 1. The argument

- **The question.** Nüdan's *nühuan nanti* 女換男體 ("women change their bodies to become men") and Buddhism's *nüzhuan nanshen* 女轉男身 ("transform a woman into a man") both seem to say that "women who practice religious meditation must be transgender" (abstract, p. 1). Geng raises this reading in order to reject it.
- **What *nühuan nanti* means in the texts: smaller breasts.** The *Xiwangmu nüxiu zhengtu shize* 西王母女修正途十則 (revealed 1795) defines it so. "If her cultivation makes her breasts become as tight and small as a virgin's or a child's, then this is women's changing their bodies to become men" (煉得乳房如處女小兒形，便是女換男體, p. 2). The *Dacheng jieyao* and Hu Fuchen's 1995 *Dictionary of Daoism* define it the same way (pp. 2–3). **The textbook definition of "a man's body" is a girl's breasts.**
- **"Male body" = yang body.** After the red dragon is cut, "the term 'male body' solely denotes yang characteristics and carries no other connotations" (p. 3). It "is interchangeable with … 'yang body (yangti 陽體)'" (p. 11). Liu Yiming states that the term "does not mean that a woman is physically transformed into a man" (p. 4).
- **Return to before puberty.** Women's primordial qi peaks at fourteen and is spent through menses, birth and nursing until about forty-nine (n. 3). Cutting the red dragon "restores the female body to its pre-pubescent condition" (p. 3). The *Qiaoyang jing* method:
  - cross the hands over the breasts and rub twenty times;
  - take thirty-six breaths;
  - until "the menstrual cycle has stopped, and breasts are the size of a man's" (p. 4).
- **Nüdan also restores menses** to women past menopause before cutting them (n. 2, p. 16). Cutting is done on a healthy cycle, not on an absent one.
- **Buddhist material (§3), all canonical and so available in 867–1453.**
  - *Shisong lü* 十誦律 (Vinaya of Ten Recitations): a bhikkhunī who "lost her female organs and acquired male organs" was admitted to the monks "according to the number of years since her initial ordination" (p. 6). An institution re-registers a person by changed sex and keeps their seniority.
  - *Lotus Sūtra* ch. 12: the eight-year-old dragon girl "transformed her female form into a male appearance, complete with the thirty-two major marks" (p. 6). This confirms the reading-list finding against Cave 331.
  - The Medicine Buddha's eighth vow: women "utterly weary" of womanhood who hear his name "all attain the transformation from woman to man" (轉女成男, p. 7).
  - *Foshuo zhuannü shen jing* 佛說轉女身經: a prayer to "abandon this impure female body and attain a new and better male body" (p. 13). The same sutra also says "At the most fundamental level of truth, there is no distinction or image of male and female" (於第一義中，無有男女相, p. 15).
- **Geng's thesis.** Both formulas are "symbolic metaphors"; they "do not advocate the actual transformation of women into men" (p. 15). Both traditions "embody profound principles of gender equality" (p. 13). This is an apologetic reading: the Buddhist material on loathing the female body is explained away as a "solution to the problem of the female body, which is a symbol of desire" (p. 10).
- **Self-mastery.** Nüdan lets women "take ownership of their bodies", glossed with Ge Hong's "My destiny is mine, not heaven's" (我命在我不在天; Geng quotes the variant 我命由我不由天, p. 13). Ge Hong (283–343) is early and safe to use in period. (Geng's note 5 misprints his dates as "283–243".)

**Citation and accuracy flags.**
- Geng quotes 女子赤脈斬，則變為男體 as from the anthology text *Nü jindan* 女金丹 (Dong and Sheng 2012, p. 6). Those are the exact words of Liu Yiming's *Xiuzhen biannan* Q91 (Valussi 2022, p. 208). The anthology is reprinting Liu, so this is not independent evidence.
- Geng's Liu Yiming gloss (p. 4) is attached to *nühuan nanti*, but Liu's Q95 uses 女轉成男. The substance is the same.
- `china_reading_list.md` finding 2 cites "Geng pp. 3–4" for *nühuan nanti*. The definition is on **p. 2**, the dictionary entry on pp. 2–3, and Liu's gloss on p. 4.
- Geng does not mention the reverse vinaya case, a monk whose body becomes female and who is sent to the nuns. **To verify outside this batch:** the Pāli Vinaya, Pārājika 1 (Vin III 35), is reported to rule both directions. If confirmed, it is a canonical, period-available precedent for an institution re-registering a male-to-female change.
