# Dorleyism: Byzantium, Pessinus and Islam reading list

This list deepens the README bibliography "Byzantium, Islam and the eunuch traditions" for the Byzantine faith, *Adelphai Dorleyaiou*. The section has nine works already read: Ayalon 1999, Marmon 1995, Neil & Garland 2013, Neville 2019, Rapp 2016, Rowson 1991, Sanders 1991, Tougher 2008 and Tougher 2013. They are not repeated here. Five works were cited but not read: Ayalon 1979, Kuefler, Messis, Mullett and Ringrose. Roller 1999 was cited in the notes. These six are listed below with full details so they can be read.

**How to read an entry.**
- **Use** names the design hook:
  - *gate*: the Rite of the Knife and the three Houses;
  - *Aristotelian/Galenic*: the main rite against the planned splinter;
  - *matriline and typika*: adopted daughters and convent foundation rules;
  - *Pessinus nostalgia*: the gallae of the Mother;

  - *Sister-Making*: modelled on adelphopoiesis;
  - *Islamic neighbours*;
  - *Phrygian heresy model*.
- **Priority:** P1 = must-read, P2 = useful, P3 = background.
- **Availability:** **OA** = free full text at the URL; **Preview** = partial or borrowable; **Library** = paywalled or print, with ISBN or DOI.
- **Downloaded** = a copy sits in `docs/research/sources_pending/byzantium/`, which is gitignored. Source URLs are in `SOURCES.txt` there.

This file holds no extracted text. Every citation was checked on 2026-10-18 against Crossref, publisher, library or repository records, or against the downloaded copy. Anything not confirmed is marked *unverified*.

**Four findings that affect the design now**
1. **The Kecharitomene typikon is the matriline model, and it was staffed by eunuchs.** Empress Irene Doukaina's rule (1110–16) passes the *ephoreia* (protectorship) to her direct female descendants, then to female in-laws, then to other suitable female relatives, "in perpetuity". The same convent required its spiritual father, steward and physician to be eunuchs. Item 30, no. 27. So a real Byzantine institution already combined inheritance through women with eunuchs placed on women's side of the wall. The Dumbarton Oaks introduction adds that some foundations barred eunuchs, including some nunneries. At Attaleiates' foundation, though, the monks had to be eunuchs. Both facts can serve as event text.
2. **In hagiography, "eunuch" is the cover a woman uses to live among monks.** Matrona of Perge cuts her hair, dresses as a eunuch and lives as "Babylas" in a men's monastery. She leaves saying she will "no longer be thought a eunuch … but am soon once again to be a woman". Mary/Marinos is likewise taken for a eunuch (item 13). This is the strongest native material for the Aristotelian rite's claim that a person read as a eunuch can be a woman underneath. Read it with Bychowski (item 12) and Szabo (item 11).
3. **Liudprand supplies both halves of "calls its eunuchs angels and buys them as boys".** In *Antapodosis* 6.6 the Verdun merchants make *carzimasia* to sell into Spain. In *Antapodosis* 4.10 Tedbald castrates captured Greeks and sends word that "your holy emperor attaches a special value to eunuchs." That second passage is also a precedent for the Dorley prisoner path (item 47). For the angel half, see Sidéris (items 6 and 7).
4. **Dorylaion was an imperial estate and army staging post.** It lay in the theme of Opsikion and had an *aplekton* (an army camp) and herds of imperial pack animals. An 11th-century seal names Michael the Nabataean as *episkeptites* (estate manager) "of the Optimatoi and of Dorylaion". The nearby imperial domain of Mesonakta is also attested (item 64, nos. 71.22 and 58, citing TIB 7: 238–242). The Mother of Mothers therefore sits on crown land. That is useful for court-office events and for the post-1071 loss of the plateau.

## 1. Byzantine eunuchs: court, theory and the angel ideology

1. **Ringrose, Kathryn M.** 2003. *The Perfect Servant: Eunuchs and the Social Construction of Gender in Byzantium*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.
   - Use: *court and era decay*, *Aristotelian/Galenic*. The main book-length argument that Byzantium treated eunuchs as a distinct gender. Read it critically: it is the scholarly source of the "third kind" view that the Galenic splinter adopts and that the main rite rejects (after Bhatt).
   - P1 · Library, ISBN 978-0-226-72015-9, DOI 10.7208/chicago/9780226720166.001.0001 · Not downloaded.
2. **Ringrose, Kathryn M.** 1994. "Living in the Shadows: Eunuchs and Gender in Byzantium." In Gilbert Herdt (ed.), *Third Sex, Third Gender: Beyond Sexual Dimorphism in Culture and History*, 85–110. New York: Zone Books.
   - Use: *Aristotelian/Galenic*. The short version of item 1, in the volume that made "third gender" a category. Useful precisely as the framing the design pushes against.
   - P2 · Library, DOI 10.2307/j.ctv16t6n2p.5 · Not downloaded.

## 5. Law, medicine and the body (Aristotelian against Galenic)

    - Use: *Aristotelian/Galenic*. The famous one-sex thesis. Cite it only alongside item 41.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 0-674-54349-1 · Not downloaded. (The Park & Nye critique, *The New Republic*, Feb. 1991, is *unverified*.)
43. **Paul of Aegina.** *The Seven Books of Paulus Ægineta*. Trans. Francis Adams. Vol. 2. London: Sydenham Society, 1846.
    - Use: *gate*. Book VI §68, "On Castration": the Byzantine surgical procedure (by compression and by excision). Adams adds a commentary. This is the technical basis of the Rite of the Knife.
    - P1 · OA (public domain) at https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/70532 · **Downloaded.**
44. **Noailles, P., and A. Dain (eds. and trans.).** 1944. *Les Novelles de Léon VI le Sage*. Paris: Les Belles Lettres.
    - Use: *gate*, *court and era decay*. Leo VI's Novels on castration and on whether eunuchs may marry or adopt. The legal frame the rite works inside, at the game start. Greek and French. Check the novel numbers before citing them; *unverified*.
    - P2 · Library · Not downloaded.
45. **Humphreys, Mike (trans.).** 2017. *The Laws of the Isaurian Era: The Ecloga and Its Appendices*. Translated Texts for Byzantinists. Liverpool: Liverpool University Press.

## 7. Brother-making and same-sex ritual

52. **Boswell, John.** 1994. *Same-Sex Unions in Premodern Europe*. New York: Villard.
    - Use: *Sister-Making*. The thesis that adelphopoiesis was a same-sex marriage rite. It prints and translates the euchologion texts. Rapp (already read) rejects the thesis. Use Boswell for the liturgy, and Rapp for what the rite meant.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-0-679-75164-9 (Vintage pbk) · Not downloaded.
53. **Krueger, Derek.** 2016. Review of Rapp, *Brother-Making*. *The Medieval Review* 16.12.09.
    - Use: *Sister-Making*. It notes that canonists dealt with men and women "joined in a making of siblings", which is a precedent for Sister-Making across lines of sex.
    - P2 · OA at https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/23164 · Not downloaded (a web page).
54. **Radle, Gabriel.** 2017. Review of Rapp, *Brother-Making*. *Byzantine and Modern Greek Studies* 41: 310–313.
    - Use: *Sister-Making*. A liturgist's check on Rapp's reading of the euchologia.
    - P3 · Library, DOI 10.1017/byz.2017.7 · Not downloaded.

## 9. Phrygia, Dorylaion and Anatolian heresies

    - Use: *Phrygian heresy model*, *court*. The gazetteer. The Dorylaion entry is at 238–242 and Mesonakta at 338–339. This is the base for every county and holding name.

    - Use: *court*, *Phrygian heresy model*. Actual officials of Dorylaion, Amorion and the Opsikion (finding 4). Gives real title and name forms for characters.

66. **Trevett, Christine.** 1996. *Montanism: Gender, Authority and the New Prophecy*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
    - Use: *Phrygian heresy model*. A Phrygian heresy led by women prophets (Priscilla, Maximilla), with a holy centre at Pepouza. It is the closest real template for how Constantinople would see a Phrygian sisterhood.

68. **Hamilton, Janet, and Bernard Hamilton (trans.).** 1998. *Christian Dualist Heresies in the Byzantine World, c. 650–c. 1450*. Manchester: Manchester University Press.
    - Use: *Phrygian heresy model*. The primary texts on the Paulicians, including Peter of Sicily. Shows how 9th-century Byzantium wrote about, and made war on, an Anatolian heresy.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-0-7190-4765-7 · Not downloaded.
69. **Lemerle, Paul.** 1973. "L'histoire des Pauliciens d'Asie Mineure d'après les sources grecques." *Travaux et mémoires* 5: 1–144.
    - Use: *Phrygian heresy model*. The critical history behind item 68. In French.
    - P2 · Library · Not downloaded.
70. **Starr, Joshua.** 1936. "An Eastern Christian Sect: The Athinganoi." *Harvard Theological Review* 29.2: 93–106.
    - Use: *Phrygian heresy model*. A Phrygian sect, associated with Amorion and the Amorian emperor Michael II, persecuted under Michael I. It is a local precedent for a tolerated-then-persecuted Phrygian sect in the 9th century.
    - P2 · Library, DOI 10.1017/S0017816000033241 · Not downloaded.
