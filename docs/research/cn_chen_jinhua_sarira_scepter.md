**Chapters, one line each.**
- Intro (33–37): Wu's relic cult is understudied; physical relics vs dharma-relics (sutra texts as relics).
- I (37–48): Famensi finger-bone brought to the palace 660–662 for Gaozong's health; Wu gives her bed-curtains and nine nested reliquaries; she is de facto ruler by end of 660.
- II (48–61): 677 "discovery" of 10,000+ relics in Chang'an's Guangzhai quarter, 49 grains to every prefecture in 678, on the Buddha's birthday, into the Sui founder's old pagodas.
- III (61–80): the 690 *Great Cloud* commentary recasts the 678 distribution as fulfilled prophecy; Dayun monasteries in every prefecture; relic cults at Yishi (692) and Jingzhou (694); the Axis of Heaven.
- IV (80–103): Songshan pagoda (c. 700), Di Renjie's remonstrance, the Qibaotai (c. 700–703), the Famensi relic brought to Luoyang in 705, the coup of 20 Feb 705, Fazang's betrayal.
- V (103–117): dhāraṇī texts as relics (*Uṣṇīṣavijaya*, *Wugou jingguang*); Wutaishan and Mañjuśrī as Wu-family propaganda; the 706 printed dhāraṇī in Korea.
- VI (117–128): Wu modelled her Buddhist kingship on her kinsman Sui Wendi; the *Baoyu jing* forgery modelled on Wendi's Candraprabha interpolation.
- Conclusion (128–133).

## 1. The argument in brief

- **Thesis.** Wu's relic cult "started and ended with the Famensi relic" and was "modeled at least as much on Sui Wendi as it was on ideas taken from Buddhist canonical literature" (pp. 128, 130). Relics were legitimation, healing, and at the end repentance.
- **Why she needed it: a female usurper.** Under the Heavenly Mandate a relative of the reigning house could not be its successor; "she was not only a usurper, but also a female usurper — in imperial China, political ethics forbade a woman from assuming supreme power" (pp. 122–123). Both she and Wendi "turned to Buddhism for legitimating their usurpation" (p. 123).
- **The *Great Cloud Sutra* route: rule *in* a female body, no sex change.** On 16 August 690 ten bhadanta monks led by Xue Huaiyi presented the commentary on the *Dayun jing* (S 6502, a Dunhuang manuscript; Forte 1976) (p. 61). The sutra's prophecy concerns the goddess Jingguang 淨光 (Vimalaprabhā), "that she was to appear in the world as a female Cakravartin" (p. 77 n. 102). "Empress Wu similarly presented herself as an incarnation of the Devi Jingguang" (p. 124).
- **The *Baoyu jing* forgery (693).** Wu's ideologues "altered the original of the Ratnamegha sutra, to which they added some passages aimed at glorifying Empress Wu's image as a female cakravartin sanctioned by the Buddha" (p. 124 n. 233). The added prophecy: a devaputra, Candraprabha, in Mahācīna in the last age, "manifesting himself in a female body, will assume the position of" sovereign, named Yuejingguang 月淨光 (pp. 127–128). Chen's new point: the forgery was modelled on Wendi's own interpolation into the *Dehu zhangzhe jing* (p. 128).
- **Answer to the sex-change question.** Across 100 pages on Wu's Buddhist legitimation, Chen never cites the Lotus dragon girl, the *Sūtra on Transforming the Female Form*, or any female-to-male doctrine. The prophecies used were explicitly *female-bodied*: the bodhisattva takes a woman's body in order to rule. This is the opposite of the sex-change sutras. (Burton-Rose's claim in `east_transgender_china.md` that Wu "drew upon the dragon princess's refutation" is not supported here.)
- **Titles.** "Two Sages" from 664; Heavenly Empress (*Tianhou* 天后) 674, when "the empress then hung a curtain [and listened] from behind it" (p. 60, *Zizhi tongjian*); "Sacred Mother and Divine Emperor" (*Shengmu shenhuang* 聖母神皇) 688 (p. 119); Golden-Wheel cakravartin titles 693–695, one adding "the Maitreya"; all renounced 27 May 700 (p. 83 n. 118).
- **Institutions.** Edict of 5 Dec 690: a Dayun monastery 大雲寺 in both capitals and every prefecture to keep the sutra and commentary (p. 63). The Yishi Dayunsi stele of 692 is a "Multi-story Maitreya Pavilion", "on behalf of the Sacred and Divine Imperial Majesty" (pp. 63–69). The Jingzhou inscription of 694: "her brilliance matching that of the 'Great Clouds'" (p. 77).
- **The vow.** The 690 commentary says Wu in a former life "made the grand vow that she would build eight million and forty thousand treasure-pagodas" (p. 62): an Aśoka figure a hundredfold.
- **The Sui precedent and a nun.** Wendi was said to have been raised to age 13 in a nunnery by a "Divine Nun" who prophesied he would restore Buddhism; he put her picture in every relic pagoda (p. 41 n. 16). The Sui stele calls her "Divine Mother" (*shenmu* 神母), which Chen ties to Wu's title "Sacred Mother" (p. 119).
- **Hostile scholar-official voice.** In 700 Di Renjie knelt before her horses: "The Buddha was the deity of the barbarians, while Your Majesty is the lord of people under the heavens. Your Majesty needs to hide yourself behind the layered curtains" (p. 82). She turned back. Women's seclusion and anti-Buddhism are one argument.
- **Palace instruments.** Facheng (Wang Shoushen), an Investigating Censor in Wu's secret-police years, became a monk to escape the judicial work and was probably the "mastermind behind the Guangzhai relic campaign" (pp. 50, 53, 130); he built a release-life pond in Chang'an with Princess Taiping (p. 52).
- **The fall.** Wu brought the Famensi relic to Luoyang in 705 for her health; a week later the coup came. Fazang, who directed the ceremony, helped the plotters: "a politically opportunistic and shrewd monk" (p. 102). Buddhism survived her fall by distancing itself from the Zhou (p. 102).
- **Daoist trace.** On 29 May 700 Wu had a gold slip inscribed begging Daoist deities to erase her name "from the records of the sinners" (p. 131 n. 248). Songshan was her mountain; kinsmen retired there as recluses (pp. 91–92, 131–132).
- **In-window relevance.** Famensi relic processions to the palace: 660, 705, 756, 790, 819 (Han Yu's protest), **873** (Yizong) (p. 47). Wu's ideology long outlived her as an image of a female Buddhist sovereign (pp. 131–132).
- **Dunhuang.** Only as the archive (S 6502, the commentary; S 523, a colophon naming the Qibaotai, p. 95). **Nothing on Mogao Cave 331 or Cave 96.**
- **Not in the article:** eunuchs, Daoist nüguan, the Lotus dragon girl, sex change.
