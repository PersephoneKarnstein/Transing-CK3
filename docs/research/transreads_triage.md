# Transreads batch: triage verdicts

Triage of four books from the user's `transreads` folder (the 2026-10-21 batch), plus a record of two items in the same folder that were already read. Each verdict rests on the table of contents and the introduction or opening, read from text extractions kept outside the repo. Full notes for the other books in this batch: `book_hornsby_stone_bible_trouble.md`, `book_macwilliam_prophetic_marriage.md`, `book_ford_dress_codes.md`.

## Siobhan B. Somerville (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to Queer Studies* (2020)

**Citation.** Siobhan B. Somerville (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to Queer Studies*. Cambridge Companions to Literature. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2020. DOI 10.1017/9781108699396.

**Read:** the chronology's preface; Somerville's Introduction (pp. 1–16), on the two senses of "queer" (an umbrella for non-normative identities, and a critique of normativity) and on the essays; the openings of all fifteen chapters.

**Verdict.** A survey of queer *literary and cultural* studies since about 1990, mostly about the United States and modern texts, film, performance and digital culture. It has no medieval or premodern chapter and nothing period-true for an 867 start. Its value to the mod is as theory background for the narrator's stance, and that ground is already covered more directly by the TSQ notes (`tsq_keywords_2014.md` and the others) and the Stryker and *Transgender Studies Reader* notes. Low priority.

**Worth a later full read:**
- Ch. 4, Cáel M. Keegan, "Transgender Studies, or How to Do Things with Trans*". A short map of the field since *TSQ* 1.1 (2014); useful only as a check on how `tsq_keywords_2014.md` frames the field.
- Ch. 14, Richard T. Rodríguez, "Diaspora, Displacement, and Belonging: The Politics of the Family and the Future of Queer Kinship". On chosen kin and "houses". Possibly useful as theory for the faiths' Houses and Badenya's "mother's children", but it concerns modern communities and must not be mapped onto a faith.

Not worth reading for the mod: chs. 1–3 (genealogies of queer theory, African queer writing, queer of colour critique), 5 (Indigenous film), 6 (disability), 7 (ecology), 8–13 (poetics, narrative, performance, popular culture, digital culture, diaspora), 15 (regionalism).

## Jasbir K. Puar, *Terrorist Assemblages: Homonationalism in Queer Times*, 10th anniversary ed. (2017)

**Citation.** Jasbir K. Puar, *Terrorist Assemblages: Homonationalism in Queer Times*. Tenth anniversary expanded edition, with a foreword by Tavia Nyong'o. Durham, NC: Duke University Press, 2017 (first edition 2007).

**Read:** contents; Nyong'o's foreword (opening); the Introduction, "Homonationalism and Biopolitics" (opening pages).

**Verdict.** A study of how the post-2001 United States folded some gay and lesbian subjects into the nation ("homonationalism") while marking Muslim, Arab, Sikh and South Asian bodies as perversely sexual "terrorists". Chapters on the sexuality of terrorism, Abu Ghraib, *Lawrence v. Texas*, turbans and profiling, and a postscript on the Trump years. Nothing period-true. Its one design use is a caution the mod already follows: a faith's acceptance by a ruler should never be shown as proof that one civilisation is more advanced than another, and no faith is defined against a demonised neighbour (the standing rule: no antagonist nation). No chapter needs a full read for the mod.

## Jin Haritaworn, Adi Kuntsman and Silvia Posocco (eds.), *Queer Necropolitics* (2014)

**Citation.** Jin Haritaworn, Adi Kuntsman and Silvia Posocco (eds.), *Queer Necropolitics*. Social Justice. Abingdon: Routledge, 2014. Prologue by Sunera Thobani.

**Read:** contents; acknowledgements; the editors' Introduction (opening).

**Verdict.** Essays on which queer and trans lives states and movements let die and which they protect, in modern settings: AIDS activism and Black radicalism, the military, transnational adoption, Iran, Palestine, prisons, policing in Washington DC, anti-Blackness. Nothing period-true, and much of it describes violence against living people. Not usable as in-game material.

**Worth a later full read:**
- Ch. 6, Aren Z. Aizura, "Trans feminine value, racialized others and the limits of necropolitics" (pp. 129–147). On how trans feminine lives are valued for their labour and beauty and how that value is racialised. Relevant as theory to §16 rule 7 (no daughter is accepted for a sacred role or for being useful) and to the Marxist strand (`book_transgender_marxism.md`). Theory only.

Ch. 8, Elijah Adiv Edelman, "'Walking while transgender'" (pp. 172–190), is about the policing of living trans women of colour; do not mine it for game content.

## Mohamad Abdouni (ed.), *Treat Me Like Your Mother: Trans\* Histories from Beirut's Forgotten Past* (2022)

**Citation.** Mohamad Abdouni (ed. in chief), *Treat Me Like Your Mother: Trans\* Histories from Beirut's Forgotten Past*. Texts and interviews by Joy Stacey; Arabic and translation by Rayyan Abdelkhalek. *Cold Cuts* 4. Beirut: Cold Cuts, digital pressing, March 2022. Bilingual English and Arabic.

**Read:** front matter and the opening of the editors' foreword; searched the English text for dates and places.

**Verdict.** Oral histories and personal photographs of trans women who lived in Beirut from about the 1960s, through the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), to the present, with archive photographs (Arab Image Foundation). The editors note that it is a subjective account of individual lives and memories. The interviews include family rejection, rape and wartime injury. **These are living people. Note only: nothing from this book may be used as in-game material, quoted in loc, or modelled as a faith or a precedent.** Its value is to remind the designers that the mod's daughters stand beside real women whose own words exist; it is not a source for the mod.
