## 2. What the episodes say

### CI-B: Nanda, the third sex, and Serano in 2026

- **The third sex is the regime's failure bin.**
  - Bhatt: the third category is "considered a fail state, basically" (0:32:20). It is "an endemic feature of patriarchies" (0:32:38), not a way to shatter them.
  - What shatters patriarchy is demanding that the third be treated like the first: "The existence of a third sex doesn't fix patriarchy any more than the existence of the second sex did" (0:33:05).
- **Everyday third-sexing in the West** (Bhatt and Serano, 0:28–0:31).
  - People call a trans woman "she" until they learn she is trans, then switch to "they" or "it". Serano: "my culture lumps me into a third category all the time" (0:30:05).
  - Third-gendering is how people sort those they cannot place, not a gift.
- **Serano now stands with Bhatt on Nanda.**
  - In 2015 and 2018 Serano softened her *Whipping Girl* critique of Nanda and recommended Nanda's books (`serano_blog_essays.md`, "Where they part" (1)). In this episode she attacks Nanda's *Gender Diversity* (2000).
    - Its transsexualism/transgenderism chapters cast transsexuals as upholding the gender system and praise transgender people as progressive. They do not mention that Bornstein and Feinberg, the best-known transgender writers of the day, had themselves transitioned (0:46–0:47).
    - Nanda cites Anne Bolin's *In Search of Eve* yet ignores Bolin's evidence that the trans women she interviewed differed widely and wanted careers (0:48–0:50).
  - Serano: Nanda and Raymond were "just picking and choosing what they wanted to hear" from the people they interviewed (0:49:58).
- **Serano does not reject every third category.**
  - Serano values the 1990s transgender umbrella as a coalition of everyone the binary marginalises: transsexual, non-binary, intersex. In her words it was "essentially a third sex category" chosen from below, a group that bands together to fight the marginalisation it shares (0:45:20–0:46:40).
  - Bhatt: the third sex is a dumping ground of people who have "more to gain from working together" than from splitting hairs (0:59:58).
  - **The distinction is a third category chosen from below versus one imposed from above.** That is the mod's existing line between the cross-faith alliance (rule 6) and the Galenic third thing.
- **Testimony recorded, then overridden.**
  - Bhatt retells a hijra story Nanda prints in full. A hijra tells a magistrate "I am a lady"; he asks whether she can give birth, and rules she is no woman. Nanda glosses it as showing that hijras know they are not women (0:52:20–0:53:20).
  - The same happens in Raymond's *Transsexual Empire*, where a clinic patient tells Raymond that they must perform femininity for the doctors, and Raymond writes it down and goes on as before (0:55:50–0:56:10).
  - Serano: cis interpretation "has more power and more weight than anything we say about ourselves" (0:57:40); trans people are a Rorschach test onto which any theory of cause is projected (0:14:55, 0:57:20).
- **Kept from the bride as if barren.** Nanda reports that at a wedding the bride is kept away from the hijra troupe, lest their "impotence" spread. Bhatt rereads this: the belief in India is that infertility spreads from a barren woman to a fertile one, so "she had just reported that the hijra were being treated like infertile women" (1:04:02). (Already in `book_bhatt_third_sex.md`; here it is tied to what follows.)
- **Stigma as contamination** (Serano, from *Sexed Up*, 1:12–1:14).
  - Stigma is imagined as a contagion: one touch contaminates for good.
  - Women are coded as sex, and sex as dirty, so anything feminine contaminates a man. Whoever falls out of manhood joins the stigmatised class.
  - This gives the mechanism behind the bride taboo. It is also why rule 10 keeps "contagion" a hostile word only.
- **Manhood is easy to fail.**
  - Bhatt (prob.): "Emasculation, expulsion from the category of man, shockingly, breathtakingly easy" (1:10:58). The fallen man is "misogynized", so transmisogyny "is fundamentally a type of misogyny" (1:11:30).
  - Zakharuk compares this to whiteness as she has seen it applied to Slavs. The ruling category is defined by extracting labour, so it must be easy to fail and must keep producing its underclass (1:13–1:16).
- **The festival costume** (Zakharuk, 0:16:00–0:16:50). In her home region, men put on women's costumes at the end of the pagan year, as parody. People are taught from the cradle that a man as a woman is "for the holidays to dress up and laugh at, but this is not to live as" (0:16:20). Seeing someone live it sincerely is what provokes.
- **Respectability in both directions** (Serano, 0:38–0:41).
  - "True transsexuals" have disowned transgender people as perverts and fakes. Genderqueer people have called transsexuals conservative. Serano's names for the second are the "radical conservative gender binary" (0:07:26) and "compulsory gender queerness" (0:05:49): "we just keep creating these gender binaries" (0:40:30).
  - Bhatt (0:34:40–0:36:40): Western academics insist hijras are a third gender, "not trans", while upper-caste trans women in India call themselves respectable transsexuals, "nothing like" the hijra. Both make the same false split. Meanwhile hijras transition, and their writing seeks "solidarity with trans women" (0:34:50).
  - "the hijra are not venerated in India. They're quite oppressed" (0:36:55).
- **Serano's earlier evidence.** Two trans authors, Roughgarden and Califia, report people from cultures with celebrated third genders who transitioned once they could. That "makes the whole house of cards fall down" (0:22:00–0:23:30).

### CI-A: Chu's *Females*

- **The thesis is a tautology** (Bhatt, 0:18:20). Chu's "everyone is female" means the universal wish to be desired. Bhatt calls it "almost a tautology" with no explanatory power. Zakharuk's version: to be a woman is to be constructed as object and sacrifice, which is also against human nature, so "it's a nice thought … but it's not true" (0:16:50).
- **Bhatt's feminist rewrite.** "We all live in fear of being [ef]feminized" (0:20:20): everyone fears sliding down the gender hierarchy. The question Chu does not ask is who is empowered not to be female (0:21:00).
- **Against shame about desire** (0:30–0:41). Bhatt rejects both lesbian-feminist self-policing of desire and the view that desire is beyond judgement. Chu's worry, in *On Liking Women*, that wanting to be a woman makes her less feminist than the TERFs, gets "girl. No, they don't" (0:36:40).
- **"ultimately, our gender is selfish. And there's no reason for it not to be"** (prob. Zakharuk, 0:39:20). Nobody transitions to do feminism or to destroy it. Any feminist or anti-feminist story about one's gender justifies "what you already wanted to do anyway" (0:40:10).
- **No inherited ethnos** (Zakharuk, 0:27:06). Queer forms such as butch/femme are "not a people, it's not an ethnos … you cannot inherit it". Each holder's meaning differs, and the difference can feel like an attack.
- **Mothers and the daughters they did not want** (Bhatt, 0:46:20–0:47:10). Bhatt's way of romanticising the second wave is mothers and the disappointed daughters they "never wanted to have", who clean up their messes anyway. Many of these feminists "would hate the fact that I'm their strongest soldier today" (0:47:00).
- Closing wish (Zakharuk, prob.): that women, lesbians especially, be "less ashamed of the things that they want" (0:47:42).

## 4. Guardrails

- **Transcripts stay out of the repo.** Short quotes with timestamps only.
- **Speakers unlabelled.** Do not put a quote marked "prob." into in-game text attributed to a named host. In-game text never quotes these hosts in any case. The faiths are fiction, and the podcast grounds the design, not the loc.
- **No private people.** The podcast's patrons and a friend mentioned by first name are not named here or anywhere in the mod.
- **Living communities.** Bhatt speaks about hijras as an Indian trans woman. Her reading grounds the mod's refusal to third-sex its daughters. It does not license presenting hijras, or any living community, as a faith (README).
- **Tone.** The episodes are comic and profane. Loc borrows arguments, never the hosts' voice or jokes.
