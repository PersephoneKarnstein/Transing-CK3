# Raha & van der Drift, *Trans Femme Futures* (2024), design reading

Source: Nat Raha and Mijke van der Drift, *Trans Femme Futures: Abolitionist Ethics for Transfeminist Worlds*
(Pluto Press, 2024; JSTOR PDF). Read in full: Introduction ("Trans liberation is between us"), ch. 1 ("'They would
plant the rose garden themselves': femme, complicity, and the rewiring of the sensuous"), ch. 2 ("Transfeminist ethics
and practices of care"), ch. 3 ("'It takes a nation of managers to hold us back': trans liberalism and liberation amid
currents of empire"), ch. 4 ("Medical institutions, collective care"), ch. 5 ("Abolitionist transfeminist futures:
solidarity, generosity and love"). Endnotes skimmed for sex work, kinship and family abolition. Quotes are verbatim and
cited by chapter, because the PDF's page-number glyphs are corrupted. The extracted text stays in the session
scratchpad, not in the repo. Raha's chapter in *Transgender Marxism* (see `book_transgender_marxism.md`, ch. 5, STAR
House) is the earlier version of the ch. 2 argument about social reproduction.

**What the book does not supply.** It has nothing on premodern, religious or non-Western historical gender traditions.
It is set in the UK and the Netherlands in the 2010s and 2020s: the Gender Identity Clinic, the Hostile Environment,
Brexit, Gaza and COVID mutual aid. Its one remark on the past is a warning against nostalgia (ch. 5). Its use for this
mod is ethical and structural: how a house of sisters should run, how it should relate to lords, and what it should
refuse. It gives no historical veneer. The femme it describes is "open to sociality, regardless of gender" and
includes trans men (the Frente Trans Masculin, ch. 4). The mod's scope stays as it is.

## 1. The argument in brief

- **Ethics, not rights.** "We choose the term ethics, in contrast to the spaces of politics, rights, and norms which endeavour to counter, break or assimilate trans life." They "describe collective making of forms of life as ethics", and "Ethics entails finding a way to live already in a post-capitalist future." (Intro; ch. 2)
- **Abolition is the opposite of enclosure.** "Violence – locking people out, enclosing people in – is the normative method of wrestling control. Abolition is the opposite." "The ethos of prison abolition proposes that no one is disposable." (Intro; ch. 2)
- **Inclusion is conditional.** "Institutions suggest that the pressures upon trans lives may be solved by inclusion. Abolitionist approaches, by contrast, help us understand why inclusion is conditional." Inclusion "for some will always be only conditional ... what is given at one time, can easily be taken away at another time." And: "Rights might protect one from management, but they will not liberate one from it." (Intro; ch. 3)
- **Office inside the institution is not liberation.** "it is not particularly liberating to be arrested by a trans cop" (after Che Gossett), and "we have seen Robin Hood join the cops too." Individual power backed by an institution makes one "a little ruler in a little realm"; Lugones calls it the domain of "managers, foremen, lesser officials, and upholders of the institutional apparatus." (ch. 3)
- **Complicity, not innocence.** Claims to innocence renew "the bond with institutionalised force": "Innocence introduces force, and force demands allegiance with power." The authors embrace complicity: "The refusal of perfection leads to a refusal of innocence, and an embrace of sociality." Victims "do not get to occupy the space of innocence versus the perpetrator who needs to be cast out." (chs. 1–3)
- **Collectives, not community.** "Collectives, on the contrary, exist through direct relations between people." They are horizontal, they run on unseen "femme labour", and they may end: "Sometimes it is better if a group disbands itself and ceases to be." Breakups spread what a group knew: "Fallouts make possible the transfer of knowledge and methods to new groups." (chs. 1, 3)
- **Care is mutual or it is servitude.** "we don't want to give up care, because care is a mutual relation – what we give up is servitude." "care is not the problem, but the lack of mutuality is." "A lot of people want to give, but don't want to receive," which reproduces philanthropy and its hierarchy. Benevolent caregiving "has a history rooted in white supremacy through the gendered labour of bourgeois women (and white supremacist practices of Christianity)." (chs. 1–2)
- **Beyond the family, warily.** The household is "the primary domain of privatised care." Chosen family pools "skills, knowledge, and resources" and includes "intergenerational parenting (that may not be defined by age difference)". Even so, "Chosen families may focus on the domestic space, and in that sense may reproduce gendered norms." STAR House, "where homeless sex worker trans and gay youth, predominantly people of colour, made a home together", is the model, and the authors add that "the social conditions that give rise to their necessity are also what we are trying to transform". (chs. 2, 5)
- **No police, so other skills.** "Given that we have no overarching structures of our own (such as the police) to call to protect our spaces, we find other ways of dealing with difficult situations." Accountability "cannot be forced upon people, but is something that comes out of a desire or will to change" (after Kaba). Hate-crime law is "the promise of violence that is offered as a solution to social anxieties" (after Lamble). (chs. 2–3, 5)
- **Gatekeeping is ownership.** Of the Gender Clinic: "'who guards something, owns it'", and trans people end up "running off with the fun part of it anyway." "Trans healthcare does not require institutionalised control." "self-medding saves lives." Forced sterilisations "were carried out under the banner of" the four pillars of medical ethics. (ch. 4)
- **Liberatory Harm Reduction over individual autonomy.** Its core is "we do not force others to change"; "what makes it radical, transformative, and loving is that it emphasises collectivity without coercion." Help from institutions "usually comes with harm and control" (Daoud). Care should be "a body-loving practice", the opposite of the clinic's "body-hating" regime. (ch. 4)
- **Against the third legal sex.** The X marker "proposes to add a third legal gender to the binary categories," and the cis/trans divide is "working to separate trans and non-binary people into a 'third gender' (of sorts)." (chs. 3, 5)
- **Against purity and separatist sameness.** Breakaway groups are "telling of pressures faced. However, homogeneity does not provide safety." "Separability is at its most reductive when it starts to be posited as a basis for arranging social dynamics." "transmisogyny is misogyny." (ch. 5)
- **Sex work is work.** Sex work sits in the list of social reproductive labour beside "cooking and feeding, birthing and rearing children, housework and laundry". Feminism "needs to overcome its harassment of sex workers". From the notes: "All power to the trans sex workers making a living through this work." The book argues for decriminalisation, not for honouring sex work. (chs. 1–2, 5; notes)
- **Survival economies.** "squatting, unionising workplaces, being jobless, being on welfare ... being homeless, having nothing to fall back on except community resources". The squats and social centres that were lost had served as "a low-key ethical maintenance" of movement foundations. (chs. 2, 5)
- **What "futures" means: prefiguration, with no victory.** "a futurity that starts today, not in a promise of a better tomorrow." "Justice is reached by the means of coming together, but not by the defeat of our 'enemies'." "We build together and fight outside." Quoting Quinn: "Any country that ever had a victory, an all-out overnight victory, it doesn't last long." (chs. 1, 5)
- **Invitation, not conversion.** "we cannot change anybody else's form for them. But what we can do is extend an invitation to join us." Many futures "may start with eating together (we hope you'll come eat with us)." (ch. 5)
- **On memory.** "There's no need to wax lyrical with nostalgia over what has been lost". "part of the work of movements is to re-learn over and over the same lessons in different generations". Read backwards, identities "store insights, knowledge, and practices". (ch. 5)
- **Transition as sensory remaking.** "Transness is not rebellion against what is pre-ordained by god or nature ... Transness is a different hunger ... a hunger for a life not lived yet, that we are rehearsing in the underground." They quote Rimbaud's "long, immense and logical derangement of all the senses", and Hayward on hormones: the "proprioceptive sense is as radically changed as external presentation". (ch. 1)

### Holding the tension: abolitionist ethics in a game about feudal rulers

The book cannot be satisfied in CK3. The player character is by default a landholder who taxes peasants, raises levies,
imprisons and executes, and passes land through a family. Lugones's "little ruler in a little realm" describes a CK3
count exactly. Pretending the faith escapes this would be the innocence the book condemns. The mod can hold the
tension in four ways.

1. **A non-ruler playstyle.** Let the player be an **Elder Sister of a Mother House**: a courtier, or a landless
   adventurer if 1.20's Roads to Power content allows it (verify). Her play is the House's strain, arrivals, care
   events, splits and Spring the Cell. Bæddel's outlaw war-band is the martial version. This is where the book's
   ethics can actually be played.
2. **Complicity for ruler play.** A ruler of the rite is not innocent and the game should say so. Recurring events put
   the sisters' needs against the realm's: levies taken from a House county, a tax season, a prisoner in the dungeon who
   is someone's sister. The best options cost the ruler something (control, gold, opinion of other vassals) and are
   never free virtue. Harney and Moten's "theft" from the institution has a mechanical form: an *Open the Granary*
   decision moves realm gold into House treasuries.
3. **No victory screen.** The faith never wins. Era decay withdraws inclusion and the game cannot remove the hostile
   world. Legacy is counted in Houses and lessons kept, which fits "a futurity that starts today".
4. **Honesty about the engine.** CK3's dynasty and house *are* the family form. Take as Daughter repurposes them; it
   does not abolish them. The design doc should say this in one line, the way §0 says there is no antagonist.
