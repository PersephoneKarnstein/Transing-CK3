# S.527: bylaws of a Dunhuang women's association, 959 (transcription and design reading)

**Source:** British Library Or.8210/S.527, 女人社再立條件 ("the women's association renews its bylaws"). The date is 顯德六年己未歲正月三日, the 3rd day of the 1st month of Xiande 6 (jiwei), early February 959.
- A single sheet, 30.2 × 62.2 cm (Galambos 2020, fig. 60). The recto has 13 lines of bylaws, a member list of 15 names with marks, and a 4-line closing oath.
- The verso has only later annotations: 顯德六年書 and 此書名目不清大約是立社規條 ("this document's title is unclear; probably a she founding bylaw"). These are probably cataloguers' notes, likely from Stein's Chinese secretary Jiang Xiaowan, though that is not confirmed. Also present: "A.D. 959", "7582" (the Giles catalogue number, inside Giles's "Club rules and circulars" run 7572–7624, per Galambos p. 196), and a red library number.

**Method:** read from the IDP images in the scratchpad (stitched recto and verso plus three recto details), with 2× crops. Cross-checked against Galambos 2020, pp. 203–206, which transcribes and translates the preamble only and describes the member list.
- Hao 2021 names S.527 and says it follows the model bylaw S.6537v (p. 63) but gives no text.
- The standard edition, Ning & Hao 1997 (《敦煌社邑文書輯校》), was **not** available.
- Teiser 2020, "Terms of Friendship: Bylaws for Associations of Buddhist Laywomen in Medieval China" (Brill, pp. 154–172), is the English study to get next.

**Conventions:** `（ ）` gives the standard character for a loan or variant. `[?]` marks an uncertain reading and `□` an illegible character. Line breaks follow the manuscript. A blank space before 君王 is a reverence space (*jingkong*; Galambos p. 200 explains the device for P.3544).

**Checked against Galambos, pp. 203–206.**
- His preamble text matches lines 1–5 as read here, including 滋（茲）, 城（誠）, 儀（義）, 值（志）, 世語（與）, 中（終） and 大者若姊小者若妹.
- He reads 讓語（與）先登 and 立條件與後; the manuscript has 与.
- His description of the list agrees: 15 women; the Manager (*sheguan*) is a nun; the other officers and members are lay; the Elder is "simply Nüzi 女子"; Wu Fuzi, Fulian and Yiding; cross-like marks; the Secretary signs with a cross.

## 2. What it says (translation; the preamble after Galambos, the rest my own)

**Preamble.** On New Year's day 959 the women's association, "in an expression of each member's good will, hereby renew the bylaws" (Galambos p. 204). An association needs written rules. Parents give the body, but friends raise the will (父母生其身，朋友長其志). Friends help each other in danger and rescue each other in hardship. One keeps faith with friends (a loose quote of *Analects* 1.7, Galambos p. 205). **大者若姊，小者若妹: "the older ones as elder sisters, the younger as younger sisters"**, each yielding place to the other. They swear by the mountains and rivers never to break it.

**Article 1: deaths.**
- "Within the society, for funerals and auspicious events, and in the name of mourning a kinswoman's (or kinsman's) grief" (親痛之名), each member gives **one *he* of oil, one *jin* of white flour and one *dou* of millet**, and must help prepare the food and wine.
- If a member herself dies (若本身死亡者), the whole society provides **the white pall and carries her** (蓋白躭拽; my reading of 躭拽 as 擔拽, "shoulder and draw", is uncertain). It sends the gifts at the same scale as above.
- The bereaved host's hospitality is not to be judged generous or mean, and she is not fined for it (其主人看待不揀厚薄輕重，亦無罰責).

**Article 2: New Year merit-making and discipline.**
- On the one day of New Year merit-making (正月建福一日), each member pays **one *dou* of millet and one cup of lamp oil**. They mould clay stupas and print [Buddhas] on sand (脫塔印砂), first to repay the ruler's grace (報君王恩泰) and second to make merit for their parents. Printing Buddhas on sand is one of Hao's attested she liturgies (Hao 2021, p. 72).
- **Brawling.** A member who ignores precedence between elder and younger, breaks the rules, **brawls at the feast** (席上喧拳) and will not heed her seniors' words (不聽上人言教) is visited at her own door by the whole society. She is fined a feast of sweet wine and rich food, which the society eats (眾社破用).
- **Leaving.** Whoever wants **to leave** the society gets **three strokes of the stick**, then a fine of a feast. "None is exempt" (的無免者).

**Closing oath.** We have noted each article above. We will live "like water and fish" (如水如魚) and not gossip about right and wrong. "The mountains and rivers are our oath, the sun and moon our witness" (山河為誓，日月證知). Because people may break faith, we inscribe these rules for later reference.

### What S.527 does *not* say (corrections to `china_reading_list.md` finding 4)
- It sets **no common store and no loans**. The only collective goods are dues collected per death and per New Year and fines eaten as feasts. The common store and loan ledgers come from other she (Hao 2021, pp. 71–72).
- It says nothing on property, inheritance or adoption.
- It does not say the nun holds the purse. Her office, *sheguan* (Manager), was the day-to-day administrator in Dunhuang she generally (Galambos pp. 199, 213), and she heads the list.
- "Called its members sisters" is right as a simile, 若姊/若妹 ("*like* elder and younger sisters"). Members are titled *sheren* 社人 in the list, and none is addressed as 姊 by name.
