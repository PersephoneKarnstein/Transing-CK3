# The Way of the Jade Embryo: China reading list

This list deepens the bibliography for the Chinese faith, the Way of the Jade Embryo (*Yutai*). At present the README section "China, the steppe and Southeast Asia" mixes it with the bissu, the baylan and the Mongols. Treated on its own, China has six works already read:
- Chiang (ed.), *Transgender China* (2012), with chapters by Chiang, Burton-Rose, Wong and Zhou;
- Dale 2018;
- Höckelmann 2020;
- Sommer 2024;
- Tsai 1991 and 1996.

They are not repeated here. Two works were cited in the notes but not read: Furth, *A Flourishing Yin* (item 31) and Jay 1993 (item 26). They are listed below with full details so they can be read.

**How to read an entry.**
- **Use** names the design hook:
  - *gate*: the jade embryo, the ten-month gestation and the birth from the crown;
  - *rectified names*: the zhengming tenet ("the palace calls us eunuchs so it need not call us women");
  - *temple societies*: goods held in common, adopted heirs, crown confiscation;
  - *nüguan clergy*;
  - *palace politics*: the stage-2 faction against the scholar-officials;
  - *holy sites*;
  - *Dunhuang seat*;
  - *era arc*: 867–1453;
  - *hostile reaction*: omens, renyao, the censor's register.
- **Priority:** P1 = must-read, P2 = useful, P3 = background.
- **Availability:**
  - **OA** = free full text at the URL;
  - **Preview** = partial, or borrowable on archive.org;
  - **Library** = paywalled or print, with ISBN or DOI.
- **Downloaded** = a copy sits in `docs/research/sources_pending/china/`, which is gitignored. Source URLs are in `SOURCES.txt` there.

**Four findings that affect the design now**
1. **Cave 331 holds the dragon girl, but nothing found ties it to Empress Wu. In the sutra she becomes a man.**
   - Zhang Yuanlin (item 19, pp. 136–137, fig. 14) places the Devadatta-chapter scene on the east wall of the early Tang Cave 331: the dragon girl rises from the dragon palace. The Dunhuang Academy names no patron.
   - The caves linked to Wu are others. Cave 96, the giant Maitreya, is tied to her order for Great Cloud monasteries (Zhang p. 126). Cave 321 was once read as a *Baoyu jing* tableau (a sutra reworked to justify a female ruler) and has since been re-identified (Wang Huimin 2010).
   - In Lotus Sutra ch. 12 the nāga king's daughter "instantly transform[s] into a man" before attaining enlightenment (item 76, pp. 184–185), after Śāriputra's objection that the female body "has five obstructions".
   - Recommendation:
     - Drop "funded by Empress Wu" from Cave 331. If Wu is wanted, use Cave 96.
     - Use the dragon girl only in the faith's own voice, as proof that a body can change in an instant and that her offering was accepted *before* the change.
     - The narrator should never call it a precedent for becoming a woman.
2. **The ten months and the birth from the crown belong to the period. Women's alchemy (nüdan) does not, and its rhetoric runs the other way.**
   - Zhang Boduan's *Wuzhen pian* (1075) gives the ten months (Wang Mu, item 2, p. 100).
   - Pregadio (item 1, pp. 17, 65–66) shows the embryo leaving through the crown, after 100 days, ten months and nine years.
   - Nüdan texts are 17th-century and later. The *Xiwangmu nüxiu zhengtu shize* was revealed in 1795. Sun Bu'er (1119–1182) is Quanzhen, but the poems attributed to her are late.
   - Nüdan's own term for the result is *nühuan nanti* 女換男體, "the woman exchanges her body for a man's" (Geng, item 8, pp. 3–4). Yutai can name this and reverse it: she is born as herself, not as a man.
3. **The omen tradition reads "a man becomes a woman" as a sign that castration is being overused. That is the confusion Rectified Names refuses. The Song court once answered a body out of category with ordination.**
   - *Han shu* 27C1 (Wuxing zhi, the Five Phases treatise) quotes Jing Fang: 男化為女，宮刑濫也, "a man changing into a woman means castration is being used to excess".
   - *Song shi* 62 records the case of 1124. A tavern-worker's wife of about forty suddenly grew a beard, "just like a man", and "by special edict was ordained a female Daoist" (特詔度爲女道士).
   - In 978, *Taiping guangji* 367 gathers sixteen entries under the heading *renyao* 人妖. All its gender-crossing entries are women who lived as men. The comment on Lou Cheng reads 此人妖也。陰為陽，事不可 ("this is a renyao: yin acting as yang cannot be allowed").
   - Hostile text in the game should use this omen sense, not the Qing "predator" sense. Sources: item 78 and the downloaded texts.
4. **A Dunhuang women's she (lay association) called its members sisters, had a nun as its officer, and kept a common store.**
   - The bylaws of S.527 (959 CE) say 大者若姊，小者若妹 ("the elder like elder sisters, the younger like younger sisters"). The officer (社官) is the nun 功德進.
   - Each of the 15 members owes oil, flour and millet when a death strikes a member's family, and the she carries the coffin. Societies also lent from a common store.
   - Hao (item 51, pp. 70–72) calls funeral mutual aid their most important activity.
   - Hao (item 52, pp. 84–90) shows Dunhuang monks and nuns living at home and holding property. The monk Chong'en's will provides for his *adopted daughter*. The new nun Guangxian built her own kitchen and would not share it.
   - So Temple Societies, the word "sisters" and nüguan who adopt daughters are all attested at the faith's seat. The S.527 transcription is on Galambos's EFEO page (item 56).

## 1. Daoist inner alchemy (neidan) and women's alchemy (nüdan)

1. **Pregadio, Fabrizio.** 2019. *The Way of the Golden Elixir: A Historical Overview of Taoist Alchemy*. 3rd ed. (1st ed. 2012). Mountain View: Golden Elixir Press.
   - Use: *gate*. The embryo, the 100 days, the ten months, the nine years, and the exit through the crown (fig. 7, p. 17; pp. 65–66). The shortest reliable map of the whole process.
   - P1 · OA (publisher's free PDF) at https://www.goldenelixir.com/files/The_Way_of_the_Golden_Elixir.pdf · **Downloaded.**
2. **Wang Mu.** 2011. *Foundations of Internal Alchemy: The Taoist Practice of Neidan*. Ed. and trans. Fabrizio Pregadio. Mountain View: Golden Elixir Press.
   - Use: *gate*. A stage-by-stage manual that the month-3, month-6 and birth trials can follow. "The Barrier of the Ten Months" and "Embryo of Sainthood" are on pp. 99–100, which quote the *Wuzhen pian*. Wu Shouyang's "this does not mean there is truly an infant" (p. 100) is the line Yutai reverses.
   - P1 · Preview: the 68-page publisher sample covers pp. 99–100. ISBN 978-0-9843082-5-5 · **Sample downloaded.**
