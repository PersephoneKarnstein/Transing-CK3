# Pfeil, "For trans emancipation: transsexuality and anarchism" (2020): design reading

**Citation.** Cello Latini Pfeil, "For trans emancipation: transsexuality and anarchism," The Anarchist Library (theanarchistlibrary.org), English translation dated 07.10.2020, anti-copyright. The translation note gives the original as an article in the Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro journal at revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/estudoslibertarios/article/view/36238 (*Revista Estudos Libertários*). The text gives no volume, issue, pages or DOI for the original, so none is given here.

**What was read.** The whole essay and its bibliography (`scratchpad/books/tr/08.txt`). Page numbers are the Anarchist Library PDF's (1–18).

**Quotes.** Verbatim, each 25 words or fewer, checked against the text.

**Author and scope.** Pfeil writes from a Brazilian trans masculine position, and much of the essay is about trans men. That part is out of scope and not used. The witness statements quoted from a Brazilian psychology council report are by private people and are not used.

## 1. Argument in brief

- **Three pillars of cisnormativity.** After Viviane Vergueiro: "pre-discursivity, binarity and permanence" (p. 7). Sex is read off the body before the child can speak; only two options exist; and the assignment is meant to last. Intersex infants are operated on without hesitation, while trans adults face endless hesitation (p. 7).
- **The "true transsexual".** Clinics demand proof, so patients rehearse the expected story. Quoting Berenice Bento: "what actually happens is self-diagnosis" (p. 8). Patients must hate their bodies and want to look cis; "our bodies are not our own" (p. 8). Pfeil names "the necessity of proving that we are who we are to cisgender people" (p. 4).
- **Tutelage.** The "regret" argument keeps trans people under supervision "as a way of protecting them from their own desires" (Bento and Pelúcio, p. 11). "Instead of a policy of care, we perceive a policy of tutelage." (p. 11).
- **Passing.** Seeking to pass is survival, not betrayal; conforming and transgressing are both punished (pp. 6–7, 12–13). Freedom means "to know that if one were to express oneself in any other way, there would be no danger" (p. 12).
- **Mutual aid.** Elders advise the young on hormones and doctors; shelters and informal hormone networks are run on cooperation, not profit (pp. 13–14). This is anarchist federalism in practice.
- **Emancipation.** "The emancipation of one must mean the emancipation of all" (p. 16).

## 2. Relation to other notes

- **Bhatt.** Agrees on gatekeeping: patients passing the clinicians' criteria hand to hand is Bhatt ch. 8, l.905 (citing Stone), and both treat the "true transsexual" test as a power relation. Bhatt's call for power "outside of the family, outside of the state" (ch. 7, l.813) is close to Pfeil's mutual aid. They part on frame: Pfeil's enemy is authority as such; Bhatt's is male supremacy, which she will not dissolve into a general anti-authority politics (Concl., l.996). Pfeil leans on Vergueiro's decolonial framing, which Bhatt's ch. 5 would question.
- **Serano.** The rehearsed narrative and regret talk match Serano's notes on gatekeeping and detransition (`serano_blog_essays.md` #52–#56).
- **Heaney volume.** Same ground as Bey on confession and Kristjansson and Heaney on shared hormones (`book_heaney_feminism_against_cisness.md` §2.3, §2.7).
- **betts et al.** Same anarchist tradition; see `tsq_betts_black_anarchism.md` for the anti-state caution.

## 4. Cautions

- **No suicide content.** The essay discusses the "suicidal gaze" argument (pp. 4, 11). Use the regret theme only as in row 3, never with self-harm.
- **Violence.** The 1987 São Paulo police operation against travestis and the "deception" murders (p. 15) are modern violence against a living community. Do not use them, even as pattern. Rule 3's "deceiver" shape already covers the trope.
- **Travestis.** A living Latin American identity (see `tsq_silva_ornat_travestis.md`). Never a faith or role.
- **Trans men out of scope.** Pp. 6–9 and 12–13 are largely about trans masculine experience. Not used.
