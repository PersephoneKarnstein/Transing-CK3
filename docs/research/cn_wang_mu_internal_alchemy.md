Mountain View, CA: Golden Elixir Press. Paperback 2011, ISBN 978-0984308255; Kindle ed. 2012; **2nd revised Kindle ed. 2014 (the copy read)**. The e-book has no page numbers, so citations give the section: §1 = "The Basis: Essence and Spirit"; §2.0 = "The Four Stages"; §2.1–2.4 = the four stages; §3 = "The Arts of the Way"; "n." = Pregadio's endnote to that section. **Caveats.**
- Wang Mu writes inside the Longmen (Quanzhen) tradition and "accepts virtually all traditional details concerning ... doctrine, lineage, and textual authorship or date" (foreword). He reads the four-stage scheme back into Zhang Boduan.
- Pregadio warns that 1980s PRC conditions pushed Wang Mu to present neidan as Qigong and "scientific" healing; words like "materialism" are "the price to pay" (foreword).
- Most of Wang Mu's quotations on the embryo are **late Ming and Qing** (Wu Shouyang 1574–1644, Liu Yiming 1734–1821, the *Xingming guizhi*). Inside the game window are only: the *Wuzhen pian* (c. 1075) and Zhang Boduan's other works (two disputed), the *Ruyao jing* (Cui Xifan, 10th c.), the Zhong-Lü texts, Chen Tuan's chart, and Bai Yuchan and Li Daochun (13th c.).

**Chapters, one line each:**
- Foreword (Pregadio): publication history; Wang Mu's Longmen ordination; PRC context.
- §1 The Basis: the *Wuzhen pian* and three other works of Zhang Boduan; Xing and Ming; "doing" before "non-doing"; Weng Baoguang's code-name table (the "Lovely Maiden" list); secrecy of transmission.
- §2.0 The Four Stages: the Zhong-Lü four-stage scheme in brief.
- §2.1 Laying the Foundations (the longest chapter): Three Treasures; superior and inferior virtue; the three Cinnabar Fields and the Mysterious-Female; the Eight Vessels; River Chariot; tripod and stove; harmonisation; eyes, teeth and voice. **Gendered language.**
- §2.2 Refining Essence into Breath (Barrier of the Hundred Days): External, Internal and Great Medicine; Fire Times; history of the stage scheme from Shi Jianwu to Wu Shouyang. **Dating.**
- §2.3 Refining Breath into Spirit (Barrier of the Ten Months): Great Medicine, seven days' enclosure, Embryo of Sainthood, bathing, Greater Celestial Circuit, "true Zi hour". **The gestation.**
- §2.4 Refining Spirit to Return to Emptiness (Barrier of the Nine Years): moving and delivering the embryo (Zhong-Lü only); Zhang Boduan and Chan; body and spirit. **The birth.**
- §3 The Arts of the Way: Zhang Boduan as practical healer.
- Tables; Glossary of Chinese characters; Notes.

## 1. The argument in brief

### 1.1 The period texts and the stage scheme
- **The *Wuzhen pian* (悟真篇) is c. 1075 and deliberately unsystematic.** It "does not provide a systematic arrangement of the stages", and "its poems are obscure and do not follow a precise sequence" (§1; print p. 3).
- **The Zhong-Lü texts give three stages and no "ten months".** The *Zhong-Lü chuandao ji* 鐘呂傳道集 and *Xishan qunxian huizhen ji* are both by Shi Jianwu. Wang Mu treats them as Tang, though he admits there were two men of that name, one of them Song. They name the stages:
  - "refining the form to transmute it into Breath" (*lianxing huaqi* 煉形化炁);
  - "refining Breath to transmute it into Spirit" (*lianqi huashen* 煉炁化神);
  - "refining Spirit to join with the Dao" (*lianshen hedao* 煉神合道).

  The *Xishan* adds a fourth, "refining the Dao to achieve sainthood" (*liandao chengsheng*) (§2.2, conclusion). The *Cantong qi* and *Ruyao jing* do "not yet clearly set forth" the stages.
- **Chen Tuan (c. 920–89)** carved the *Wuji tu* on Mount Hua. From bottom to top it shows the Gate of the Mysterious-Female, refining Essence into Breath, the five agents, "taking from Kan to fill Li", and "returning to the Ultimateless" (§2.2). This is a Five Dynasties image that could go on a guan wall.
- **Zhang Boduan himself** says only that Essence becomes Breath, Breath becomes Spirit and Spirit becomes Emptiness: "the three flowers gather at the sinciput" (*sanhua juding* 三花聚頂) (§2.2, from the *Jindan sibai zi* preface). The labels "three barriers" (*sanguan* 三關) and "the three flowers gather in the tripod" are Li Daochun's, c. 1290 (§2.2). The full four-stage system was "already accomplished" by Wu Shouyang's time, the late Ming.
- **The durations are conventional.**
  - The Hundred Days "does not mean that this stage necessarily requires one hundred days" (§2.2; print p. 66).
  - The nine years allude to Bodhidharma and "do not refer to the time required" (§2.4; print p. 109).
  - Only the ten months has a period anchor in a poem (below).

Notes on the table:
- **The key in-period poem** is *Wuzhen pian* "Lüshi" 14 (§2.3, n. 1): 三家相見結嬰兒，嬰兒是一含真氣，十月胎圓入聖基.
  > When the three families see one another, / the Infant coalesces. / The Infant is the One / holding True Breath; / in ten months the embryo is complete — / this is the foundation for entering sainthood.
- **The *Ruyao jing* line is the best 10th-century source for the birth vocabulary**: "At first coalesce the Embryo, watch your fundamental destiny. As you finally deliver the embryo, watch the four cardinal points" (§2.3, n. 13). *Tuotai* 脫胎 ("slip out of the womb") is the in-period word for the birth. *Chutai* is Wu Shouyang's.
- **Wang Mu names no month-by-month events.** Nothing in the book marks month 3 or month 6. The only internal dangers he names are the mind wandering while "bathing" ("as soon as thoughts run unrestrained, dangers occur", §2.3) and too much heat ("if you again increase the fire, you will be in danger"). **The month-3 and month-6 trials are the mod's invention.** Their external framing (after Serano) fits a source that names no fixed-month ordeal.
- **The "red dragon" (month 6) is not in this book.** Wang Mu never mentions women's practice, menstruation or *zhan chilong* 斬赤龍. That term belongs to Qing nüdan (see the Valussi and Huang/Geng notes), well outside the window. Flagged for `tm_embryo.0011`.
- **There is no nursing stage.** Later neidan's "three years of nursing" (*rubu*) is absent. After the delivery come the Nine Years (Zhong-Lü) or, for Zhang Boduan, emptiness straight away.

### 1.3 Where the embryo grows and where it leaves
- **Lower field** (behind or below the navel): the Medicine is stored and the Great Medicine formed (§2.1).
- **Middle field**, "above the navel": "Here the Embryo coalesces in the second stage" (§2.1).
  - Zhang calls it "formed by the joining of Qian and Kun".
  - Wang Mu identifies it with the Yellow Court and with the **Mysterious-Female** (*xuanpin* 玄牝).
- **Zhang Boduan's preface**: 身中一竅，名曰玄牝 … 結胎在此矣，脫胎亦在此矣. "The One Opening at the center of the person is the Mysterious-Female … the coalescing of the Embryo, and the delivery of the Embryo are all found there" (§2.1, n. 19). **So for Zhang Boduan both conception and delivery happen in the Mysterious-Female, not at the crown.**
- **Upper field**, the Palace of the Muddy Pellet (*niwan gong* 泥丸宮), also called the Palace of Qian: "the point where the Yang Spirit rises" (§2.1). Cui Xifan: 產在坤，種在乾, "It is born in Kun and is planted in Qian" (§2.1).
- **Gate of Heaven open, Door of Earth closed.** Zhang's *Book of the Eight Vessels*: if you know the Yin Heel vessel, "the Gate of Heaven (*tianmen*) will be constantly open and the Door of Earth (*dihu*) will be forever closed" (天門常開，地戶永閉, §2.1). The same vessel's names include "Door of Death" (*sihu* 死戶) and "Barrier of the Return to Life". The *tianmen*/*dihu* pair is a ready-made in-period image for a crown birth set against bodily birth.

### 1.4 What the embryo is
- ***Wuzhen pian*:** "the Infant is the One holding True Breath" (above).
- **Liu Yiming (Qing):** "the Infant, the One Breath prior to Heaven, the Embryo of Sainthood, the Golden Elixir" (§2.3).
- **Wu Shouyang (late Ming), the denial.** Two passages (§2.3, from *Xian Fo hezong yulu*):
  - "The Embryo is nothing but Spirit and Breath. It does not mean that there is truly an infant" (print p. 101).
  - "one generates an embryo of Spirit in the Heart. The worldly people hear the word 'embryo' and say that within the womb there is truly an embryo, which then leaves and becomes 'a body outside the body' … This is truly risible."
- **Wang Mu:** the masters speak "with some exaggeration" of "pregnancy in the middle Cinnabar Field" (*zhongtian huaitai* 中田懷胎), but "the texts, however, never say that this should be understood in a literal way" (§2.3).
- **Self and other.** An alchemical text quoted in §2.2: the precelestial True Yang "pertains to the 'other' (*bi*)" and is the External Medicine. Once it returns, "this pertains to the 'self' (*wo*)", and it is the Internal Medicine. The embryo is made from something received from outside and made one's own.
- **In Daoism the body matters, unlike in Buddhism (§2.4).**
  - In Buddhism "the corporeal frame is seen as a temporary lodge". In Daoism "form and spirit should be in agreement", and the Yang Spirit "can re-enter the corporeal frame".
  - Zhang Boduan: even someone awakened to the true Nature "has not yet escaped reincarnation" (未免拋身卻入身). Hence "refining the form" (*lianxing* 煉形).
  - This is the line the Way can use against its Buddhist neighbours: **the body is not a lodging to leave; it is what is refined.**

### 1.5 Gendered language
- **There are no women practitioners anywhere in the book**: no female adept, no nüdan, no Sun Bu'er, no menstruation.
- **The practice body is male by default.**
  - Zhang Boduan's *Book of the Eight Vessels* puts the Yin Heel vessel "before the Caudal Funnel (i.e., the coccyx) and under the scrotum" (陰蹺脈在尾閭前陰囊下) (§2.1, n. 34).
  - Bai Yuchan sets the alchemical essence against "the essence of the intercourse" (*jiaogan jing*, "i.e., semen") (§2.1).
- **The aim is Yang over Yin.**
  - The Eight Vessels "pertain to the Yin Spirit" and are closed. Immortals open them "by infusing them with Yang Breath" (§2.1).
  - "the Yang will grow and the Yin will be extinguished" (陽長陰消, §2.1).
  - *Wuzhen pian* "Lüshi" 13: 群陰消盡丹成熟, "When all of Yin is entirely dispelled, the Elixir ripens" (§2.4).
  - The product is the Yang Spirit (*yangshen* 陽神).
- **The feminine names are emblems inside that male body.**
  - Spirit's code names include "Lovely Maiden" 姹女, "Lovely Maiden of the Two Eights" 二八姹女, "Female Mother" 雌母, "Woman in Green Attire" 青衣女子 and "Green Beauty" 青娥 (§1).
  - The go-between Intention is the "Yellow Dame" (*huangpo* 黃婆), a matchmaker (§2.1).
  - The embryo grows in the Mysterious-Female, and the base product is the "mother of the Elixir".
  - The alchemists "hide the mother and talk of the son" (§1).
- **The womb is borrowed, and then Yin is extinguished.** The mainstream keeps the womb vocabulary and throws away the woman. This is the strongest single support for the Way's "radical reading" frame (see the Pregadio note, row 4).

### 1.6 Initiation and secrecy
- "the selection of disciples is severe, and the methods are transmitted under oath. The most important points … are transmitted by word of mouth" (§1; print p. 8).
- Masters judge disciples by "their eyes, their teeth, and their voice" (§2.2; print p. 65). Eyes show Spirit, the voice shows Breath, the teeth show Essence (§2.1).
- "Doing" comes first and "non-doing" after: 始於有作人難見 … 豈知有作是根基 (§1).
- "Your destiny does not depend on Heaven" (我命不由天, "Jueju" 54; §1, §2.4).
