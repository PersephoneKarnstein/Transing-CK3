# Karras & Pierpont, *Sexuality in Medieval Europe: Doing Unto Others* (4th ed., Routledge 2023) — research notes for Transbian Maximalism (CK3 mod)

Source file: `scratchpad/books/karras_sexuality_medieval_europe.txt`. All quotes verbatim from the text; chapter given for each. Page numbers in brackets are the printed page headers where visible. "Ch." = chapter. Entries are numbered E1, E2 ... and cited by number in Sections 3 and 5.

> Working caution from the book itself (Ch. 1): "Gender non-conforming people were considered deviant in the Middle Ages." The book supplies *precedents*, not endorsements; the mod's "veneer of non-anachronism" rests on reframing genuinely attested practices (eunuch-priests, transvestite saints, chastity as gender-escape, adoptive/spiritual kinship, courtly-love idiom) rather than on any medieval society having actually held the modern beliefs.

## 1. Book map

| Ch. | Title | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sex and the Middle Ages (pp. 1-40) | Sets out the thesis: medieval people understood sex as a transitive act one person *does to* another (penetration), so the active/passive distinction mapped onto masculine/feminine "regardless of the gender of one's partner." Argues sexual identity existed but was organised around chaste vs. sexually active, not object choice. Surveys sources (literary, legal, religious, medical, visual), the one-sex and "third gender" debates, queer/trans/intersex studies as lenses, and the claim that medieval gender was binary and non-conformity was deviance, even where hyper-masculine or hyper-feminine. |
| 2 | The sexuality of chastity (pp. 41-88) | Treats chastity as a medieval sexual identity/orientation (compared to modern asexuality, with caveats). Traces Matt. 19:12 eunuch-saying, Origen's self-castration and Nicaea's ban, Jerome/Ambrose/Augustine on virginity as release from gender roles ("honorary men"), Perpetua's sex-change vision, monks as a possible "third gender," viragos, east/west divergence on eunuch clergy, the Gregorian reform and clerical celibacy (Lateran I/II), heresy accusations against lay chastity, tertiaries, chaste marriage, two-seed medicine, and "erotic chastity" (Christ as bridegroom/lover, Christ's side-wound as vulva/womb). |
| 3 | Sex and marriage (pp. 89-131) | Marriage as the majority's expectation but not "heterosexuality." Covers early-medieval polygyny, concubinage and *Friedelehe*; the church's capture of marriage (seven degrees of consanguinity, Lateran IV's reduction to four, indissolubility, Innocent III vs. Philip Augustus); consent vs. consummation (Lombard vs. Gratian), clandestine marriage; Jewish and Muslim polygyny and divorce (Gershom's ban, ribats for divorcées); wifely subordination and inheritance; Cathar anti-marriage doctrine and the orthodox revaluation of marriage; impotence trials and impotence magic; contraception; purity rules (forbidden days, churching, mikveh); the marriage debt, onah, women's pleasure; marital positions; Margery Kempe's negotiated chastity; the *droit du seigneur* myth. |
| 4 | Women outside of marriage (pp. 132-188) | Organised by category of female "deviance": adultery (double standard, legitimacy anxieties, courtly love and the Occitan "courts of love," sympathetic literary adulteresses); unmarried women's fornication (leyrwite, *sub pena nubendi*, servants, slavery); concubinage and quasi-marriage (Islamic umm walad, Katharine Swynford's legitimation, *barragania*, priests' concubines, Béatrice de Planissoles, Heloise's preference for concubinage); sex work (meretrix definitions, municipal brothels, sex workers as community-markers and expert witnesses); women's same-sex activity (thin evidence, penitentials, Etienne de Fougères, Hetzeldorfer as possible trans man, cross-dressing motives, Yde and Olive, ghulamiyat, midwives' manual relief); violence and coercion (raptus/abduction, Islamic schools, incest including godparent-affinity, slavery, Melkorka); women's sexuality as threat (erotic magic, Malleus Maleficarum's stolen penises). |
| 5 | Men outside of marriage (pp. 189-236) | Masculine privilege: fornication and brothel-going as "proof of social and physiological normality"; obscene bynames; rape as class privilege (pastourelles, Logres custom, Venice/Bologna statistics); bastards inheriting under Norman custom *more danico*; Henry I's twenty bastards. Then the long treatment of sodomy: active/passive over object-choice (Halperin's four categories; al-Jahiz on eunuchs; mukhannathun), Norse *argr* insult law, sodomy as marker of the Other (French, heretics, Muslims, "florentzen"), sin against nature, Peter Damian, Lollard critique, Byzantine *adelphopoiia*, Peter of Abano's "natural" passives, Dante, Lateran III and death-penalty statutes, Edward II, Arnold of Verniolles, Florence's Office of the Night age-structure, Rolandina Ronchaia and Eleanor Rykener read as trans women, love between men (Aelred, Richard and Philip, impaled-arms tomb 1391, David and Jonathan, Amis and Amiloun, Ganymede, Roman d'Eneas), and the evidentiary dilemma of desire. |
| Afterword | Medieval and modern sexuality (pp. 237-247) | Asks how central sex was to medieval life and how central the Middle Ages are to the history of sexuality. Concedes (via Bynum) that food/fasting and death/resurrection mattered more than eros, but argues sexual norms, not alimentary ones, are what the Middle Ages bequeathed; sex was witnessed more and depicted less; childhood/nubility line set at 12/14; celibacy was a visible social identity; erotic language expressed the divine in all three religions. Then: medieval sexuality is "antecedent" to modern, not Other; canon and secular law "placing the government in the bedroom" is medieval; legal marriage was defined as the union whose children can inherit; monogamy/indissolubility elaborated then; Trent (1563) first required a priest; medieval/modern difference lies in sex as transitive act and in femininity = passivity. Closes against both a Golden Age and a "one big earth festival" nostalgia. |

### Where vanilla CK3 menus already map onto the history

- **Homosexuality (accepted / shunned / crime):** *shunned* is the early-medieval and northern norm (penitential one year, E13; one defamation case in 5,000, E159); *crime* fits the Latin West after Lateran III (1179) and especially 13th-c. Castile, Portugal, Italian towns and French counties (E171); *accepted* has a soft precedent only for women (E9, E52, E123) and in Arabic literary culture (E122, E163). Set Transbian = accepted; Catholic/Latin rivals = crime after c. 1200; Norse 867 = shunned-with-insult-law (E160).
- **Clerical gender:** vanilla *female only* is the slot for a women-centred priesthood; the Byzantine eunuch clergy (E33) is the attested non-male-bodied clergy, so the mod should let remade (castrated) characters qualify as "female" for this doctrine (#2).
- **Clerical marriage:** *disallowed* is the post-Lateran II (1139) Latin position (E36); *allowed* is the Byzantine position (E40, E56). A Transbian priesthood that forbids marriage but permits sworn sisterhood (#7) mirrors the Latin line while re-gendering it.
- **Consanguinity:** vanilla tiers map to the 9th-c. seven degrees vs Lateran IV's four (E60); the mod must add that *spiritual/adoptive* kinship creates incest bars (E41, E135), which vanilla does not model.
- **Bastardry:** *none* / legitimation-by-acknowledgement matches Islamic law and Norse/Norman custom (E106, E151, E138); *legitimization* by letters patent matches England (E108); strict exclusion matches the post-12th-c. Latin West (E59).
- **Divorce:** *allowed* for women matches Icelandic law (E75) and the Jewish *moredet*/onah leverage (E68, E84); *disallowed* matches Innocent III's prerogative (E61).
- **Concubines / polygamy:** vanilla concubinage = Merovingian/Norse/Iberian practice (E57, E111, E151); vanilla polygamy = male-only in every attested system (E64) — the mod's female-centred version has no precedent and should be built as concubinage-among-women or sworn sisterhood instead.
- **Adoption tenet/tradition:** the historical content is de facto adoption by wives (E107, E153), Augustine's spiritual motherhood (E25), and godparent/confessor kinship (E41, E135); use it as the engine of #4-#5.
- **Castration parameter:** vanilla treats castration as a *punishment*; the mod must add a *voluntary rite* path (#1) and the *re-gendering* consequence (#2). Abelard (E10) shows an adult cleric surviving castration and continuing a career; Nicaea (E19) shows why Catholic rivals forbid clerics who did it.
- **Female-only / equal inheritance traditions:** vanilla female-preference succession is the slot for #5; Occitania is the only region the book names where "inheritance law allowed women to hold land" at scale (E67, E98), so an Occitan-adjacent cultural homeland is the most defensible placement.

### Three design cautions carried from the book

1. **Register:** write doctrine in the medieval idiom of status, receptivity and renunciation (E1, E199, E207), never in the modern idiom of inner identity; the veneer fails the moment a tenet says "identifies as."
2. **No utopia:** the authors reject both the Golden Age and the "one big earth festival" (E208); give the Transbian polity costs — rival-faith crime doctrines, *argr* insults, castration panic, the eunuch/mukhannath fault line (E156) for internal friction (k).
3. **No child rites, no priest-required weddings, no droit du seigneur:** E198/E33, E62/E204, E89 respectively.
