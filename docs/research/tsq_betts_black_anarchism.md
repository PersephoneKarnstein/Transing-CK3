# betts, Suékama and g, "Black Transfeminist Anarchism" (TSQ 2024): design reading

**Citation.** edxi betts, NZ Suékama, and g, moderated by Ren-yo Hwang, "Black Transfeminist Anarchism," roundtable, *TSQ: Transgender Studies Quarterly* 11, no. 1 (February 2024): 34–65. DOI 10.1215/23289252-11131676. The moderator calls it part of "this issue on anti-imperialism, anarchy, and abolition" (p. 34).

**What was read.** The whole roundtable, notes, bios and references included. Page numbers are the journal's.

**Speakers.** Three Black trans femme organisers and independent scholars, named here as the journal's published authors: edxi betts (she/her), NZ Suékama (they/she; full name in the references, Nsambu Za Suekama), and g (she/they). The roundtable keeps their conversational style on purpose (p. 34). It mentions social media and an online publication; this note quotes none of that.

**Quotes.** Verbatim, each under 25 words.

**Hard rule.** The roundtable names living communities and modern roles (two-spirit people, muxe, hijra, 'yan daudu, māhū, ball-culture femme queens). None becomes a faith, a rite or a group.

## 1. Relation to existing notes

This is a mixed source. It agrees with Bhatt on the two things the mod cares about most, and it disagrees with her method.

**Where it agrees with Bhatt.**
- **"Veneration" is a trap.** NZ describes being told that in Indigenous societies trans femmes "were spiritual leaders", and answers: "I can't help but to anticipate some type of exploitation and some type of coercion being forced onto me" (p. 49). Trans femmes "find ourselves only ever validated or invalidated within these discourses of spirit or of labor" (p. 49). That is Bhatt's "veneration is marginalisation" (`book_bhatt_third_sex.md` §1), from a different politics.
- **The "colonial import" charge.** g and NZ were both told their gender was "'white' or 'a colonial import'" (p. 38; also pp. 36, 53–54), and say this charge is used to take their labour while excluding them (pp. 38–39). Bhatt says the same of regimes that call queer rights imperialism (`book_bhatt_brown_trans_les.md` §2.5).
- **Nationalism needs a strict sexual dualism.** NZ left a Black nationalist group because "nation-building" philosophy required it (p. 37). Compare Bhatt ch. 4 (natalism, nativism, nationalism) and ch. 6 (honour culture: "Black unity at all costs", p. 37).
- **Records come from the state.** g: Black trans women "are written about, but the academy definitely doesn't want us being there and writing about ourselves" (p. 54). The historical record of them comes mostly from arrests and courts; her example is Mary Jones in New York in the 1830s (p. 54). This is Bhatt's epistemic vandalism, and it supports the counter-record event (Bhatt §5 row 6).

**Where it disagrees with Bhatt.**
- **Bhatt's ch. 5 targets are its sources.** g draws on Oyěwùmí's "world senses" against Western feminism's universal patriarchy (p. 40). NZ cites Oyěwùmí's "body-reasoning" (p. 49) and Lugones's "coloniality of gender" (p. 48) with approval. Bhatt's ch. 5 is a close attack on exactly these two.
- **Against "sex class".** g calls radical and Marxist feminism's shared view of patriarchy "the ouroboros of Western feminism" (p. 40). NZ mocks opponents who say "womanhood is a political class" (p. 62). Bhatt reconciles intersectionality with MacKinnon's dominance feminism, which treats sex as a class hierarchy (`book_bhatt_brown_trans_les.md` §2.2). Direct clash.
- **A precolonial absence.** NZ says Tanzanian Ujamaa imposed the nuclear family and a binary household where none had been entrenched (p. 61). Bhatt rejects claims that the binary arrived with colonisers.
- **"Two-spirit" and Somé.** NZ cites Malidoma Somé's "Gays: Guardians of the Gates" (p. 39) for a Dagara view that does not single out same-sex feeling. This is the "spiritual guardians" story the same speaker warns against on p. 49. The source is a 1993 men's-magazine interview reposted on a blog (references). Do not use.

**Where it cuts both ways.** NZ also says anti-Blackness predates capitalism: the "Hamitic hypothesis" in the Abrahamic faiths, used by the early medieval period to justify enslavement (pp. 47–48). This attacks the class-reductionist left, not Bhatt; Bhatt also says patriarchy and hierarchy predate modern empire. It matters for Badenya's hostile voices (§5).

## 2. Argument in brief

- **Personal as political (pp. 35–39).** Each speaker found trans-misogynoir in every movement she joined: electoral, Marxist-Leninist, Black nationalist, Indigenous, white anarchist. Comrades took her labour and refused her gender.
- **Materialism (pp. 40–45).** Real materialism studies social relations, not "two sexes are biology" (g, p. 46). NZ rejects the "evolutionary adaptation" defence of queer and trans people, because the same logic turns on them: "Suddenly we become maladaptive" (p. 42). NZ's "nexus" idea: social forms that "nex", that is constrain, embodiment (n. 1, p. 64).
- **Afro-pessimism (pp. 46–50).** Useful on anti-Blackness, weak as "ontological reduction"; "Inversion is not subversion" (NZ, p. 47).
- **Black anarchism (pp. 51–56).** Oral tradition over written doctrine (edxi, pp. 50–51); informal hierarchies inside anarchist groups are "statecraft" too, as when one Black trans femme carries most of a group's labour (NZ, p. 55).
- **Co-optation (pp. 56–63).** States and parties take over mutual aid and movements; "trans rights" pursued through the state will not protect Black trans women equally (g, p. 60).

## 3. What is usable

1. **Not only holy.** If a faith values its daughters chiefly as priestesses, seeresses or temple women, it repeats the trap on p. 49. Each faith should give daughters worldly standing too, and its text should never say they are accepted *because* they serve the sacred. Bhatt's "veneration" critique says the same.
2. **Who carries the work.** "Statecraft" inside a sisterhood (p. 55) is the shadow of the Houses: one daughter carrying the labour while others take the standing. It joins Bhatt §5 row 17 (seniority as a ruse), and can apply to every faith's House, not only Badenya.
3. **No usefulness theology.** NZ's point about adaptation (p. 42): any defence of daughters by their use to the realm (raising others' children, filling a social gap) can be turned into an attack when the use is no longer wanted. The faiths should accept daughters as women, not as a useful function.
4. **The archive of arrests.** g's point (p. 54) supports Bhatt §5 row 6. No change.

## 5. Cautions

- Do not use Somé (p. 39), the Igbo figures NZ names as gender-expansive ancestors (p. 52), or any of the roles listed on p. 49 as precedent. Most are living communities; the rest are unchecked claims in conversation.
- The roundtable is modern US movement politics. It says nothing about the medieval world, and its claims about precolonial Africa (pp. 52, 61) are asserted, not argued. Do not feed them into Badenya.
- Its anti-state politics do not map onto a game about crowns. Use its points about labour and veneration, not its programme.
- Its account of a 2010s coalition split and a pepper-spray attack (pp. 57–58) involves named groups and unnamed people. Leave it out.
