## 1. What was surveyed, and how it is cited

**Sources.**
- **Blog**, juliaserano.blogspot.com. The full Atom feed (`/feeds/posts/default`, paged by `start-index`) gives **221 posts** dated 2011-08-24 to 2026-09-17. The 43 posts dated August 2011 are reposts of her LiveJournal posts from 2007 to 2011, and each says "Originally posted on LJ …". Where it matters the original year is given first (e.g. 2008/2011). After about 2017 the blog is mostly announcements that link to Medium or Substack.
- **Medium**, juliaserano.medium.com. The profile page sits behind Cloudflare, so the list was built from the RSS feed (last 10 posts), her writings page (juliaserano.com/writings.html, which dates every Medium essay and gives no-paywall friend links), and the Wayback Machine's URL index. That gives **93 URLs: about 90 posts plus 3 short reply stubs**, from August 2015 to September 2026. Texts were read from Wayback snapshots, since Medium itself was not reachable. Member-only snapshots are truncated, so archived friend links were used for those.
- **Substack**, juliaserano.substack.com. It has 23 posts since October 2024, and every one is cross-posted to Medium. Its public API gave the full text of the 2024–2026 essays.
- **Counts.** About 311 distinct items in all. **About 85 were read in full and about 15 in large part** (listed in §2). The rest were triaged by title or a skim (§6). Every index row gives a reachable URL. No page dumps are kept in the repo.

**Citation.** Each post is cited as *title, date, URL*. In the running text a short title and year are given in brackets, and the full URL is in the §2 index. Quotes are verbatim and each is 25 words or fewer. Commenters, the people Serano quotes from social media, and the senders of hate mail she reproduces are left out. The public writers and figures she argues with are named only where her argument depends on them.

**Relation to existing notes.**
- `book_serano_whipping_girl.md` has the book's arguments and 21 design rows, and many of them were applied in §12, "Revisions after Serano…": gates recognise rather than test, Declare Before Witnesses, arrivals name themselves, and Houses are not separatist. This note does not repeat them. What the blogs add is Serano's **later** vocabulary: unmarked/marked, stigma-contamination, reverse discourse and the "doom loop", holistic gender, the two filing cabinets, and trans-antagonistic, trans-suspicious and trans-unaware. They also add her responses to her critics, including a partial retraction about Indigenous "third genders".
- `book_bhatt_third_sex.md` and `book_bhatt_chu.md`:
  - **"Third-sexed".** Bhatt's "Degendering" chapter uses Serano's term, as `book_bhatt_chu.md` row 9 notes. The blog source is the 2008 *Whipping Girl* FAQ: transsexual and intersex people are "non-consensually “third-sexed”" by a transgender politics fixed on shattering the binary. Serano and Bhatt agree that **imposed** third-sexing is a weapon.
  - **Where they part.** (1) On non-Western third genders Serano softens, where Bhatt hardens. In 2018 she uses "third gender" neutrally for categories "fairly common in non-Western cultures" and recommends Nanda and Herdt. In 2015 she regrets her *Whipping Girl* critique of Nanda and Roscoe as a white Western writer's contribution to Indigenous erasure. Bhatt attacks Nanda, the umbrella, and decolonial romanticism. (2) On womanhood as a hierarchy, Serano's 2025 essay rejects "a political condition defined by its place on a hierarchy of violence and domination" as gender artifactualism. That is close to Bhatt's "the binary is a hierarchy", though Bhatt is no artifactualist about bodies. (3) On sex and gender, Serano calls the claim that "women are oppressed because of their sex, not their gender" ludicrous: both are at work [Transgender People and "Biological Sex" Myths, 2017]. Bhatt places oppression in reproductive sex-class. Both insist trans women's bodies change, and both reject socialisation and "male privilege" arguments.
  - **The third-sex list.** Bhatt's dumping-ground includes barren women. Serano's gamete essay makes the same point from biology: people who make no gametes, through infertility or removal, leave her asking "Are such people “sexless”" on that definition [Gametes, 2024].

## 3. Core arguments relevant to the mod

**3.1 Transmisogyny is about the direction of the crossing.** Trans feminine people draw most of the hostility because they cross toward the feminine. Femininity in an assigned boy is what gets punished, and only trans women's motives are sexualised; trans men are presumed to want male privilege. "the marginalization of trans female/feminine spectrum people is not merely a result of transphobia, but is better described as trans-misogyny" [Trans-misogyny primer, 2012]. In 2021 she adds two anecdotes: a man on a doorstep calls a trans woman's clothes "shit", and a feminist conference laughs at a trans client's outfit. Femininity is judged more than masculinity, and "artificial" is built into it. TMA/TME are "potentially useful", but some people meet misogyny and transphobia in other ways, and for them "we need additional language" [What Is Transmisogyny?, 2021].

**3.2 Two sexisms, and the fault lies with the perceiver.** Oppositional sexism punishes anyone who strays from the sex others perceive. Traditional sexism ranks the feminine below the masculine in her "Table of Opposites" (strong/weak, natural/artificial, sincere/manipulative). The remedy is not a better way of *doing* gender. Move the conversation "away from how people “do” their genders, and toward how we (all of us) see and interpret other people’s genders" [Masculinity, Femininity, and Meanings, 2023]. Femme: "What makes our femininity “femme” is the fact that we do it for ourselves." It is powerful, but not more subversive than straight women's femininity [What makes femininity "femme"?, 2014].

**3.3 Subversivism and the "reinforcing" trope.** Ranking genders as transgressive or conservative builds a new hierarchy, and the target moves with the movement: radical feminists said lesbians reinforced the sex-class system, and lesbian feminists then called straight women "male-identified". "Drag is not inherently conservative, or subversive, or assimilationist, or liberating. It is simply an expression of gender." [drag, trans women, and subversivism, 2014]. No one reinforces or subverts the binary, which is a habit of perception: it is "driven by non-consensually projecting gendered expectations, assumptions, and meanings onto other people" [Nobody reinforces…, 2015]. Feminism should aim at the end of sexism, since no one can say what the "end of gender" would look like or who would decide [Bringing an end to the "end of gender", 2014].

**3.4 Holistic gender against artifactualism.** Gender is shaped by society but is not "just a construct": shared biology, biological variation, shared culture and individual experience combine. Artifactualism is no more liberating than determinism: Freud, Money and Zucker were artifactualists. It also licenses policing, since if gender is pure culture everyone can and should change on demand. "gender artifactualism can be used to promote sexist beliefs just as readily as gender determinism can" [What is gender artifactualism?, 2013]. In 2025 she answers the claim that womanhood is a "political condition" on a hierarchy of violence. She sorts artifactualist accounts of trans people into four: trans men as victims and trans women as male oppressors; the reverse; all trans people corrupted by maleness; or "some people happen to be trans". Only the fourth sits with trans feminism. "Nobody believes in “nature versus nurture” anymore—that debate is long over. The answer is it’s obviously both." [Is Gender Only a Construct…, 2025].

**3.5 Unmarked and marked.** The marked are seen as having something extra, and it is "sticky": questions, motives, causes and stereotypes attach to it, while the unmarked pass as unquestionable. Marked people are treated as public spectacles who give off "phantom invitations", so any backlash is read as invited. "people who are marked are generally viewed as “having something” that unmarked people do not have" [How Double Standards Work, 2015]. Some binds have no right answer: pass/reveal, accommodating/angry, dupes/fakes, harmless/dangerous. "the double bind is impervious to our responses, as it exists first and foremost in the minds of those who mark us" [Prejudice and the Unmarked/Marked Mindset, 2026]. Communities that crown one side of a bind as the righteous response exclude those who chose the other. Not every marked person is stigmatised: celebrities and the powerful are marked and glorified.

**3.6 Stigma is what spreads.** "Social contagion" and "grooming" are one charge: an outgroup seen as unclean will contaminate the pure ingroup, and above all its children. "this is merely the failed theory of “degeneration” all over again" [Grooming and Social Contagion…, 2022]. Apparent outbreaks are better explained by homophily (like finds like) and by a reduction of restraints. "there wasn’t really a rise in left-handedness so much as there was a rise in left-handed acceptance"; the real pressure was "EVERYBODY PRESSURE" toward being cis [Transgender Agendas…, 2017]. The closet was a "self-imposed quarantine", and "born this way" won acceptance partly by promising non-contagion [Superstition…, 2018]. "the contagion in “transgender social contagion” is actually anti-trans/LGBTQ+ stigma" [Stigma-Contamination Mindset, 2026]. She mocks the reach for the Dancing Plague of 1518 [Mass Psychogenic Illness, 2018]. "Groom" carries two senses, preparing someone for a role and abusing them, so any supporter of a trans child can be called a groomer [Origins of the "Groomer" Slur, 2026].

**3.7 Moral panic as conspiracy and coalition.** The Catholic Church coined "gender ideology" in the 1990s. In anti-trans use it works as a conspiracy theory: an all-powerful cabal, "Yet we are also (paradoxically) depicted as incredibly weak", with a smoking gun always about to surface [Gender Ideology…, 2024]. Raymond's tactic was to paint the enemy "at the same time too strong and too weak" [GC/TERF Primer, 2024]. Negativity bias makes people hunt causes only for outcomes they think bad: smothering mothers, privilege-seeking, pollution, pornography, demonic possession [Grooming…, 2022]. Opponents will use "any argument or ideology (whether it be religious, feminist, and/or pseudoscientific in origin)" [Putting the… Debate to Rest, 2018]. Backlash is framed as a natural reaction to "activists gone too far": "the marked group will likely be viewed as “inviting,” “provoking,” or “asking for” the backlash" [Prejudice…, 2026]. "TERFs don't want trans people to exist, in any capacity." [TERF talking points on transition, 2018]. Opponents also come in kinds: trans-antagonistic (an ideology, unlikely to move), trans-suspicious (tolerates trans people but doubts most of them) and trans-unaware (often won over by contact) [TERFs and TUMFs, 2018].

**3.8 Gatekeeping, "real transsexuals" and reparative therapy.** Claims about who is a "real transsexual" (finished electrolysis, surgery, an HBS diagnosis) repeat the gatekeepers' sorting. When challenged over surgery she said nothing, because "it is nobody’s fucking business what I do with my body" [TS vs TG Intervention, 2011; Am I the only person offended…, 2009/2011]. Zucker's clinic belongs to the history of reparative therapy, which targeted expression and aimed at conformity [Zucker's clinic, 2016]. "For trans female/feminine people, “male socialization” was our conversion therapy." Socialisation is like waves in the sea: people swim against them, and the waves do not stop after transition [On "Male Socialization"…, 2023]. "Male privilege" used against trans women is misgendering, and it denies them the closet: a childhood "forced against my will into boyhood" [AMAB closet, 2022].

**3.9 Detransition.** People transition by trial ("You won’t know for sure unless or until you try"), and trajectories shift. People detransition for many reasons: a partner's sake, work, harassment, lack of access, or a change of identity. Some stay trans-identified and some later retransition [Guide, 2016]. "the vast overwhelming majority of social pressure happens in the opposite direction" [stop pitting…, 2017]. The aim is to "minimize unwanted irreversible changes" by care for each person, not by gates, and people who detransition are owed services and support [follow up, 2016]. Most detransition reported in surveys has an external cause, family pressure and stigma above all [Spotting Bias…, 2023].

**3.10 Women's space and lesbian community.** FAAB-mentality is the habit in queer women's scenes of welcoming trans men as "really" butch women and filing trans women as men: "it centers our identities on nonconsensual acts committed against us" [FAAB-mentality, 2013]. Trans men who are men in the street and "not-men" in women's spaces leave trans women stranded: "We are treated as second-class citizens in the male-centered mainstream because of our femaleness, but then we are derided as being scary, untrustworthy “men”" [Having it Both Ways, 2007/2011]. Her ground for inclusion: "We are women because we move through the world as women." [TS-vs-TG postscript, 2011]. Shaming the partners of trans women as "chasers", "fetishists" or closeted gay men rests on the assumption "that we must be “fake women” or “really men.”" [In Defense of Partners, 2013]. "Privilege is not always permanent. But “contamination” is." Trans women are cast as contaminating and trans men as contaminated but redeemable, and appeals to "safety" and "purity" should be distrusted [Penises, Privilege…, 2022].

**3.11 Sexualisation and the deceiver.** The Predator/Prey script casts trans women as both predator and prey, "the worst of both worlds". That explains the sexual violence that follows "discovery", and the deceiver frame in news reports of trans murders [The Worst of Both Worlds, 2025]. Marginalised groups are "marked by sex", and the fetish mindset sexualises both trans women and the people who desire them [Sexed Up preview, 2022]. "Autogynephilia" should give way to female/feminine embodiment fantasies, which are common in cis women and fade after transition [FEFs, 2015].

**3.12 Reverse discourse, separatism and the doom loop.** Naming the unmarked group ("cis") decentres a binary. A *reverse discourse* flips it, so that the marginalised are superior and only they may speak. That rallies people and is cathartic, but "reverse discourses have no endgame: the distinction forever remains important, even if discrimination decreases over time" [Cissexism… Part 2, 2014]. Cultural feminism's flip (women pure, men poison) seeps into trans politics as a "doom loop": "It’s a zero-sum game where we all have to assert our female/feminine credentials if we want to be taken seriously." [On "Male Socialization"…, 2023]. Movements need alliances. She backs Namaste's call to ally with sex workers, prisoners and the homeless, and alliances with intersex, disabled and fat activists [TS vs TG Intervention, 2011]. Visibility is no cure: "Sometimes being more visible simply makes you an easier target for discrimination." No single strategy fits everyone, whether assimilation or flaunting [Rethinking Visibility, 2019].

**3.13 Seeing gender.** We sort people into "two filing cabinets", filter out cues that do not fit, and believe sex is constant. On learning someone is trans, people give her "the look" and *ungender* her, searching for the boy. People see what they expect: those primed to see "male infiltrators" see them in cis women, especially tall or short-haired women and women of colour. Male-coded cues outweigh female ones in perception. "You can only know for sure if they tell you." [Why Does "Transvestigation" Happen?, 2024]. Within six months of hormones strangers read her as female, while her friends noticed nothing [How Do We See Gender…, 2025].

**3.14 Bodies and hormones.** "For me, transitioning was first and foremost about my physical sex, not gender expression." Estrogen affected her "profoundly and beneficially" [WG FAQ on words, 2008/2011]. The change she loved most was her skin, being "comfortable in our own skins" in the literal sense [Skin, 2010/2011]. "sex is multifaceted, variable, and somewhat malleable": brains are not unsexed, and lived gender shapes the body too [Biological Sex Myths, 2017; Gametes, 2024]. Bans on anyone who has had "any part of male puberty" treat trans women as "permanently “corrupted”". Sex testing, triggered by looking too masculine, has mostly struck cis women of colour [Trans People and Sports, 2024].

**3.15 History, other cultures, the "third gender".** "there has to be some natural (likely biological) component to this gender diversity". Asked for the cause, she answers "Why do you even care?" She uses "third gender" neutrally, for categories "fairly common in non-Western cultures", and recommends Nanda, Herdt, Feinberg and Heyam [Pan-Cultural…, 2018]. In 2015 she regretted that her critique of Nanda and Roscoe "may have unknowingly/unthinkingly contributed to the systematic erasure that indigenous people have historically faced in academia and anthropological research". She denies ever saying that Indigenous non-binary identities reinforce the binary [Nobody reinforces…, 2015]. **Not covered:** no post discusses any particular non-Western or pre-modern community, any religious third-gender office, eunuchs, castration, or the medieval period, beyond the Dancing Plague jibe.

**3.16 Religion.** Almost absent. A Catholic upbringing "really fucked me over" as a trans child, and she first went out in public as a woman on Good Friday [Good Friday, 2011]. The Catholic coinage of "gender ideology" [2024]. The religious right's "alternative lifestyle" is the same false-consciousness tactic as the feminist "dupes" charge [Gendercator, 2007/2011]. "Hiding sex" lets abuse fester, as in church scandals [Sexual Dystopias, 2022]. **Not covered:** theology, ritual, sacred bodily change, clergy.

**3.17 Language.** "It is not the words themselves, but the negative assumptions and sentiments behind the words that are the problem". Save words rather than surrender them to enemies, as happened with "transgenderism" [Trans* and Transgenderism, 2015]. Reclaiming a slur sometimes works (queer, dyke) and sometimes does not (bitch), and who may reclaim one is itself fought over [T-word, 2014]. Appropriation is wrong when it erases, exploits or denigrates, and the charge was long used *against* trans women, as when "cisgender feminists have long accused trans women of “appropriating female dress” or “appropriating women’s identities”" [Appropriation, 2013].

**3.18 Identities are not up for debate.** Staging a trans woman's legitimacy as a two-sided "dialogue" legitimises the false-consciousness charge [Gendercator, 2007/2011]. "What is a woman?" is a slogan, not a question, and staged debates fail when they single out a minority [What Is a Woman?, 2023].
