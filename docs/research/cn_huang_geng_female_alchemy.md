# Huang & Geng, "Women Become Immortal through the Use of Procreation Ability" (2023), design reading

**Citation (verified from the PDF).** Huang Yongfeng and Geng Qiongke 耿瓊珂 (Xiamen University), "Women Become Immortal through the Use of Procreation Ability: A Study on the Fertility Concept of Female Alchemy," *Religions* 14, no. 6 (2023): 727, 11 pp. DOI 10.3390/rel14060727. Received 15 September 2022, accepted 29 May 2023, published 31 May 2023. CC BY 4.0. Geng drafted it; Huang revised it.

**What was read.** The whole article (§§1–6, notes 1–3).

## 1. The argument

- **Thesis.** Nüdan rests on the view that "the female body, like the Earth, is the form of a living goddess", and that menstruation concentrates its fertile energy; "indeed, every woman is a goddess" (abstract, p. 1; p. 10). This is goddess-feminist essentialism: womanhood is grounded in menstruation and the womb. For this mod's purposes that grounding is the problem, not the help (see §2).
- **Dating, inconsistent.** Nüdan "was developed between the end of the Ming and the beginning of the Qing" (p. 2). Yet "in the Song and Yuan Dynasties … books on inner alchemy emerged specifically for female practitioners" (p. 3). No Song or Yuan title is given. Treat the late date as the safe one (it agrees with Valussi and Xin & Xu).
- **Menstruation is a woman's store of primordial qi.** Li Hanxu (1806–1856): primordial qi accumulates to "one pound" at fourteen and is spent through the menses (p. 4).
- **Cutting it ends childbearing.** In the authors' words: "As a result, women will not marry or have children, but they will achieve immortality" (p. 4).
- **Women are classed by bleeding** (pp. 4–5):
  - A **virgin girl who does not menstruate** skips the first stage (blood into qi) and "proceeds directly to the third stage", starting from the upper elixir field.
  - **Post-menopausal women** first bring the menses back by meditating between the breasts.
  - **Menstruating women** cut the dragon.
  - So in nüdan, a woman who does not bleed is not behind on the path; the virgin girl is ahead.
- **The decisive moment.** In the hot, desirous days after the menses, "this is the threshold between becoming an immortal or a woman" (p. 5). In this framework "woman" means "the mortal, reproductive condition", not "female".
- **The end state, described as beauty.** "The face is like a peach blossom and the skin is like snow, and her feminine features will no longer be apparent, including the cessation of the menstrual cycle and the reduction of her breasts to the size of walnuts" (p. 6). These are conventional marks of feminine beauty kept beside "no longer apparent" femininity.
- **Heaven and earth.** The *Guanxin zhai jiwen*: men are heaven and women are earth, so "it is easier for women to achieve immortality" (p. 6).
- **Precepts.** The *Nü jindan* lists twelve: no lust, no anger, no stinginess, vegetarianism, respect for the master, and so on (p. 7).
- **A period case (Jin dynasty).** Ma Danyang 馬丹陽 (1123–1184), one of the Quanzhen Seven Perfected, wrote a poem for "Aunt Guo" (郭姑姑) of Xingping, who joined the Quanzhen hall. It praises her "manly ambition" (男子志): she "cast off worldly feeling", left her husband (遠夫; the authors' English omits this) and "sewed shut her *yinmen*" (縫合陰門, p. 8). The authors read it as proof of women's "masculinity, which is independent and strong" (p. 9). **This is a real twelfth-century Quanzhen woman who altered her own body, praised in male terms.** It falls inside the game's era (Jin, 1115–1234).
- **"Great man" rhetoric.** "Although I am a woman, I have manly ambitions and aspirations" (身为女子，志似男儿, p. 8). The source is a Qing anthology.
- **Married practitioners.** The *Kunyuan jing* advises a wife to "procure a concubine" for her husband so she can withdraw into practice (p. 9).
- **The goddess was "castrated".** Patriarchal religions deprived the goddess of fertility, "essentially 'castrating' her creativity" (p. 7). Daoism alone "never" had male gods displace goddesses (p. 8). This is apologetic and unhistorical; not usable as fact.

## 3. In-world lines

- Faith doctrine: "A girl who never bled begins where the alchemists hope to end."
- Gate image (period-safe generic): "a face like peach blossom, skin like snow".
- Hostile alchemist: "At the hot hour she stands between becoming an immortal and becoming a woman. You chose the wrong door."
- Faith answer: "No. I chose both."
- On Aunt Guo (narrator, neutral): "The Quanzhen master praised her for a man's ambition. The nüguan said she had a woman's, and that it was enough."
