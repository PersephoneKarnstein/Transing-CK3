## 1. Relation to existing notes

This issue is the founding statement, inside trans studies, of the "decolonial" programme that Bhatt attacks. Both introductions take Lugones as a guide. The general editors single out her view of resistance to coloniality (p. 306), and the issue editors cite her "coloniality of gender": gender and sexual diversity filtered through a colonising binary gaze into "sex" and "gender" (p. 311). Bhatt's ch. 5 is a close attack on that concept and on its source in Oyěwùmí (`book_bhatt_brown_trans_les.md` §2.6). The general editors also say the sex/gender distinction and the split of orientation from identity are "simply foreign to most places and times" (pp. 303–304). Bhatt would answer that hierarchy of sex was never foreign anywhere.

The issue also prints Gill-Peterson's essay (pp. 402–418). Bhatt names Gill-Peterson as an opponent, but the line she quotes ("hijras were not then ... transgender", `book_bhatt_third_sex.md` §1) is **not** in this essay, which is about testosterone, race and technology. Do not cite this issue for that line.

**Where the introductions agree with Bhatt.** More than one might expect.
- Gender is a hierarchy, not a pair. The general editors: "gender privileges not just men over women but also the legibly or functionally gendered over those who become inhuman waste" (p. 303). Compare Bhatt's "human, broodmare, or freak" and "waste of flesh".
- No ancestor-tourism. A transgender imaginary "can orient a transgender subject toward travel in foreign lands, in search of ancestors and of kin" (p. 305), blind to who is the tourist and who the local. Bhatt's complaint about the non-West as a "living past" for Western use is the same warning.
- No exporting the US model. The issue editors tell how a marketing slogan, "Changing the way the world thinks about gender", went out by mistake, and why it was colonial (p. 316). "The world knows plenty about transgender issues already" (p. 316).
- Trans people theorise their own lives; they are not only material for experts (p. 312).

**Where they disagree.** The introductions treat the binary itself as a colonial imposition (p. 311). They value gender ambiguity and "unrecognizable and uncategorizable" politics (p. 317). Bhatt holds that the binary is a native hierarchy everywhere, and that trans women are women.

**Joyas.** The issue editors cite Miranda 2013 ("Extermination of the Joyas") for the claim that colonisers used gender diversity to justify removal, "re-education" or genocide (p. 308). That is the same joya material behind beliefs H26, H27 and H30, which `book_bhatt_brown_trans_les.md` §7 already flags. The record was made by the men who killed them. It confirms that flag.

## 2. The introductions in brief

- **General editors (pp. 303–307).** TSQ is a US journal in a settler state; it must name that. "Transgender" rests on a sex/gender split and an orientation/identity split foreign to most of the world. Using Lacan's mirror stage and Emma Pérez's "decolonial imaginary", they warn that a transgender imaginary can mistake reflections of the same for the other. It can also be a space where colonised bodies find new ways to act. Every encounter is a "colonial encounter" (p. 306).
- **Issue editors (pp. 308–319).** Trans studies has been challenged on its whiteness but not on its settler location (p. 308). They draw on women-of-color feminism, Indigenous studies and Black studies of slavery (pp. 310–311). They want to displace stories of trans women of color that end only in death (p. 312), and to treat theory as made "on the fly, in the midst of a campaign" (p. 312). They summarise the articles (pp. 313–315), worry about who profits from trans studies (p. 315), and note TSQ's paywall and the casualised academy (pp. 316–317).

## 3. What is usable

1. **The ancestor warning (p. 305).** The mod is, in part, a search for ancestors in 867. The README already marks every faith as a reconstruction. This line is the reason to keep doing so.
2. **Gender as hierarchy (p. 303).** A clear line, from the general editors of the main journal, that agrees with Bhatt's first premise. Useful in design arguments; nothing for loc.
3. **The joya record (p. 308).** Supports retiring H30 and rewording H27 (already proposed in the Bhatt note).

## 6. Cautions

- The issue is the main platform of the framing Bhatt rejects. Cite its agreements with her as agreements across a real disagreement, not as support for the "coloniality of gender".
- Most pieces describe living communities (hijra, kothi, Stó:lō two-spirit people, *sarimbavy*, *tom*). None may feed a faith, a rite or a group.
- The introductions are programme statements. They contain no evidence about any period before the nineteenth century.
