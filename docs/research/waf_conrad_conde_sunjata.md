## 1. The argument in brief

**Conrad.** This is one bard's version, Djanka Tassey Condé of Fadama (d. 1997), recorded over six days in 1994 (pp. ix, xvii). Conrad calls the printed text a "compromise between rigorous scholarly methodology and pedagogical expediency" (p. x). He cut most digressions, merged the naamu-sayers' interjections and borrowed the opening from a second session (pp. x–xi). He says plainly that the epic is no reliable record of 13th-c. events (p. xvi) and that the Fadama Condé shape it around their own lineage (p. xvi). His Introduction argues three things.
- Mothers, not fathers, are "the sources" of heroes' greatness, and they suffer before the hero is born (p. xxi).
- Most important women in the tradition are sorceresses with *dalilu*. Their feats look domestic, but they are political (pp. xxii–xxiii).
- Kinship and alliance are the epic's main subject. The Fadama bards tie Sunjata to Fakoli and Sumaworo through women: Tenenba fostering Fakoli, Ma Sira Condé given as a "nephew wife", Kolonkan's marriage (pp. xxvi–xxx).

**Niane.** Conrad calls Niane's book "popular but reconstructed" (p. ix). Niane names Djeli Mamoudou Kouyaté of Djeliba Koro as his main source and calls himself "nothing more than a translator" (p. xxiv). He also thanks the masters of Fadama and Keyla, so the book is a blend of several bards (p. xxiv). `waf_bulman_corpus.md` gives the corpus editors' judgement: he "melded" the accounts into one narrative (Bulman et al. p. 9). It is prose, without line numbers, and the dialogue is literary French in English dress. Treat it as a 1950s literary rewriting with real oral sources behind it. The 2006 additions (Jones, pp. ix–xiv) use the Dieterlen/Griaule Bambara cosmology ("Faro", p. x): **do not use** that material.

## 2. What matters for the design (line and page refs)

### Do Kamissa, the Buffalo Woman: a sister denied her share

- **Her grievance is inheritance.** She and her brother Donsamogo Diarra "quarreled over the issue of the legacy" of their father; she was told to go and take it if she dared (ll. 586–600, p. 24). Niane agrees: she punished the king "for depriving me of my part of the inheritance" (p. 8).
- **A sister's first offering for a brother.** She vowed at puberty that whoever brought news of a brother's birth would get her larger gold earring and her outer wrapper. "I was the first to offer a sacrifice for my brother" (ll. 1080–92, p. 40). Then comes her anger: "should he tell me that women do not receive property?" (ll. 1093–94, p. 40).
- **She rules her own death and burial.** "I control my own life" (l. 1247, p. 46). Strangers must bury her in her hamlet, and her brother must never see her body (ll. 1115–28). If they bury her, her body "will not go to God as a bad body" (ll. 1100–03, p. 40). **Death content: myth only.**
- **Her weapon is a woman's tool.** She gives the hunters her spindle and distaff to shoot the buffalo with (ll. 1254–61, p. 46). Niane also has a distaff (p. 8).
- **She takes the blame for Sogolon's deformities.** She put her "far-seeing mask" on the girl too young and set her on her sorcery horse (ll. 1153–91, pp. 41–43).
- **Her skull becomes a war-totem.** The Condé made her buffalo skull their war-totem and used it to protect Fakoli (ll. 3206–17, p. 116).

### Sogolon Wulen Condé

- **Name and epithets.** Her "real name is Sogolon Wulen Conde"; the others ("Humpbacked", "Ugly") are what people "felt like" calling her (ll. 1196–1200, p. 43). Niane: Sogolon Kedjou, also Sogolon Kondouto "because she is a hunchback" (p. 8).
- **The bride's arrival.** The bard explains the bride-carrying custom here. The husband's sisters carried her because "men were not considered to be the closest relatives. It was women who were the closest relatives" (ll. 1749–50, p. 62). The co-wives' mocking song "heron-head" follows (ll. 1770–72).
- **Sorcery contest.** She attacks the husband three times: a dart from her eye, scalding breast milk, an iron-tipped staff (ll. 1781–1838). She lights the dance from her husband's bed with two fingers through the roof (ll. 1951–96). Conrad 1999 recorded these from the same bard. **Do not use** Niane's wedding night (p. 11): the king threatens to kill her and she "woke up already a wife". That is a marital-rape scene.
- **Seven years' "women's war".** The co-wives make her miscarry with medicine for seven years. She then prays outside the town: "I am only a stranger here" (ll. 2007–37, p. 73).
- **Sayings.** On her son: "It was rivalry that caused Jata to walk, / Because of the humiliation to his mother" (ll. 2158–59). Then the bard's aside: "The father is everybody's, the mother is personal" (l. 2163, p. 77). Niane: "Each is the child of his mother" (p. 22). Niane wraps it in a moral of wifely submission (p. 22); **do not use** the submission moral.
- **Her legacy.** Her brass ring from Do ni Kiri goes to Manden Bori. In danger he says "Ah, mother!", and the hunters' ring is called "Genie and man" (ll. 3877–96, p. 140). She stands to speak at a hunters'-style gathering while her sons sit, because she is "in charge" (ll. 3648–51 and n. 124, p. 132).
- **Her burial.** In Conrad the Nema king refuses land because the sons broke a custom. Strangers should say to the host "this is your corpse" (ll. 4013–23, p. 145). He then takes her and "The woman's funeral was conducted as if she were a man", with cows, guns and the special drum (ll. 4029–32, p. 145). In Niane he demands a price for the land (pp. 46–47). **FtM-adjacent honour: reaction only.**

### Tenenba Condé (Ma Tenenba, Soma Tenenba): the claiming mother

- **Who she is.** A Condé sister of Do Kamissa and Sogolon (Major Characters, pp. 200, 203). She is Yerelenko's **first** wife, childless for thirty years. She herself urges him to marry again (ll. 2618–60, pp. 94–95). He refuses: "If you do not bear a child, I will take the place of a child" (ll. 2641–42).
- **Fakoli's praise names her, not his birth mother.** "Soma Tenenba's son Fakoli" (ll. 2688–89, p. 96). Major Characters: "Also known as Soma Tenenba or Ma Tenenba" (p. 203).
- **The claiming.** After Kosiya's loss, Yerelenko tells her "That boy with you is now your son" (l. 3097, p. 111). She says: "I have given birth to a child without getting pregnant!" (l. 3110, p. 112). Conrad's note: "She has become Fakoli's foster mother" (n. 106).
- **The rounds of the sacred sites** (*dalimasigi*, "a journey to the sacred sites of Manden to acquire protective medicines", n. 108, p. 112). She takes the child to:
  - the Kulubali ancestor Kani Simbon, for a medicine bath (ll. 3107–29);
  - Kamanjan at Sibi, who carries him "through the entrance of Sibi Mountain" and out through "the exit of Tabon Mountain", the stone arch (ll. 3131–44, n. 109, p. 113);
  - Kirina, where Tenen Mansa Konkon rolls him "in the pit of Kirina … in the dust of the cave" (ll. 3145–53, p. 114);
  - Nema;
  - her brothers at Do ni Kiri.
- **Her brothers treat her as a new mother.** If she had given birth she would have spent three months at home "To care for your baby", so let her stay three months now (ll. 3172–79, p. 114). Her husband grants seven if needed (ll. 3182–85).
- **The kinship holds in the descendants.** "The Koroma kamalenw call us Condé 'uncle', / Although that Condé woman did not actually give birth to any Koroma" (ll. 3180–81, p. 115; quoted by Conrad, p. xxvi).
- **The headband.** Unmarried girls spin cotton in darkness on the 28th night of the moon. The weavers make a band for Fakoli; he says "Men do not wear head-ties, / But I will tie this on my head" (ll. 3218–53, pp. 116–117).

### Kosiya Kante and the genies' price

- **The price.** Jinna Maghan sells the bala, mallets, drum and three arrows only for "four people", and "They must all be related to you": "his mother", "his father", "his first wife", "his sister" (ll. 2835–55, p. 102).
- **Substitutes are refused.** Sumaworo's parents are dead, he is unmarried, and his only sister is married, so he asks to pay in "ordinary people". The genie answers: "Humans do that, but we do not do that" (ll. 2862–83, pp. 103–104).
- **Kosiya's choice.** She learns of it, leaves Fakoli with Tenenba ("Take care of Fakoli", l. 2991), and cuts off her ankle and neck beads in the bush (ll. 3024–28). At the cave she says "Whether you send me / Or you do not send me, I have come myself. / I prefer your success to my life", and enters (ll. 3042–46, p. 110).
- **The genies' answer.** They take her in place of all four kin: "We are giving you the bala and everything with it, / In exchange for your sister" (ll. 3058–63, p. 110). Sumaworo weeps for the only time in his life (ll. 3047–51).
- **The mourning names courage, not manliness.** Tenenba says "This Kante woman has real courage!" (l. 3084, p. 111). **The life-gift is myth only (no suicide content).**

### Kolonkan (So'olon Kolonkan, Ma Kolonkan): the sister

- **Who she is.** Sogolon's daughter, born after Sunjata walks (ll. 2192–98, p. 78). Her full brothers are So'olon Ma'an (Sunjata), So'olon Jamori and Manden Bori. Every child carries the mother's name.
- **Her sorcery as hostess.** She smells out her brothers' kill and takes the inner organs without cutting the carcasses (ll. 3536–52, p. 127). The bard's comment: "That is why it is good for children of the same mother to be in harmony" (l. 3595, p. 129).
- **The curse.** Manden Bori trips her and "Her wrapper came loose" (l. 3611). She says "You have shamed me in front of the Mande people" (l. 3616). His line will rule "But they will never agree on one ruler until the trumpet is blown" (ll. 3625–27, p. 130). The remedy is a cow per village, the meat and a wrapper to each Keita woman of Hamana (Conrad, Introduction, pp. xxviii–xxix). That is 1990s testimony.
- **Her counsel.** When Sunjata matches Kamanjan's tree-flipping she stops him: "This is not what my mother told you to do" (l. 4797, p. 172). Conrad 1999's form, "our mother", is a paraphrase.
- **Her marriage.** She has waited unmarried 27 years in exile by her mother's wish (ll. 4815–23). She marries Kamanjan of Sibi and bears Fadibali (ll. 4850–52, p. 175). The marriage founds the Danna praise (ll. 4843–49).
- **Niane.** Sogolon Kolonkan is the elder daughter, "well versed in the art of witchcraft", who watched over her brother unseen (p. 26). She finds the Mande envoys in the Mema market (pp. 43–45).

### Sansun Bereté and Nana Triban

- **Sansun Bereté.** Full name Sansun Tuman (Sansuma) Bereté (Major Characters, p. 202). Her brother is the Muslim karamogo Manjan Bereté; the mansa takes her by force over his refusal (ll. 404–461, pp. 15–17).
- **Nana Triban.** "She had a daughter who was called Nana Triban" (ll. 500–501). She is given in marriage with Tenenbajan to the two buffalo hunters and bears Turama'an Traoré (ll. 2039–48, p. 74; p. xxvi).
- **Niane's Nana Triban.** She is "sent … by force" to be Soumaoro's wife, wins his secret and escapes with the griot (pp. 38, 56–58). In Conrad she plays no such part.

### The nine sorceresses and other women

- **The nine sorceresses.** The co-wives named as the nine sorceresses: Tasissi Gbandimina, Jonmusoni Manyan, Jelimusoni Tunku Manyan Diawara, Nyuma Danba Magasuba, Maramajan Tarawele (ll. 2206–11, p. 80), and Flaba Naabi (ll. 235, 1970).
- **Paid in meat.** Sunjata buys them off with three antelope for one bull: "When you are popular, you must have an open hand" (ll. 2224–79, p. 80). They bless him: "Even a female genie will not harm you" (l. 2275).
- **Women on the delegation.** Tunku Manyan Diawara and Jonmusoni Manyan are among the four envoys sent to fetch Sunjata (Major Characters, p. 201). Niane's lone woman envoy is Magnouma, who sells the Mande vegetables (p. 45).
- **Keleya Konkon** cooks for 333 wives in one pot and is then held hostage (ll. 4471–4618; p. xxx).
- **Ma Sira Condé** is given to Fakoli as Sunjata's "nephew wife" (ll. 4659–86, p. 167). The note gives Kassim Koné's proverb about the uncle who fails to give his nephew a wife (n. 154).

### Names, the mother's name, and the bards

- **The hero's name.** Sunjata "originated as a term of praise, referring to the hero as his mother's lion (jara): Sogolon's Jara" (Conrad, p. xxxv).
- **The bards' own names.** Djanka Mamadi and Djanka Tassey were "identified by the name of their mother, Djanka" (p. xvii). That is a 20th-c. bard family naming by the mother.
- **Niane.** "Sogolon Djata (for it was thus that they called him, prefixing his mother's name to his)" (p. 15). His note calls this "a very frequent practice among the Mandingoes" (n. 27, p. 89). His daughters are "Sogolon Kolonkan" and "Sogolon Djamarou" (pp. xx, 18).
- **Naming day (Niane only).** The name is given on the eighth day. The king's aunt cuts the first hair while "poetesses" fan the mother, and the griot proclaims the name to the crowd. The aunt then "whispered the double name … in the ear of the newly-born" (p. 14). "The generosity of kings makes griots eloquent" (p. 14).
- **The jeli.** Jeliw are "guardians of 'The Word'". They praised, but they could also "admonish their patrons", and their own security depended on the ruler (Conrad, p. xv).
- **Jelimusow.** Female bards are among the women married in the quest for an heir (ll. 300–305), and one bears a child (Tunku Manyan Diawara, p. 201).

### Glossary (Conrad pp. 198–199, and notes)

*dalilu*: occult power, or any means to an end. *dabali*: a scheme, or secret power. *jamu*: family name. *jeli*, *jelimuso*, *jeliya*. *kamalen*: male youth. *karamogo*: Muslim teacher. *mansa*, *mansaya*. *mori*: Muslim diviner. *naamu*. *tana*: totem or taboo (n. 38, p. 15). *dalimasigi*: a journey to the sacred sites (n. 108). *buranya*: having an older in-law (n. 157). "Nephew wife" (n. 154). *nyamakalaw* (Epilogue, p. 196). *kuma koro* ("ancient speech"), *Sunjata fasa*, *Manden maana* (p. xix). *belentigi*: chief bard (p. xvii). Conrad's glossary has no entry for *badenya* or *nyama*. Badenya appears only as the bard's phrase "children of the same mother" (l. 3595).

## 3. Corrections to the candidate (§15) and to `waf_conrad_austen_sunjata.md`

1. **Kosiya Kante.** The design says "the djinn's price is a dependant's life". In Conrad 2004 the price is four named kin (mother, father, first wife, sister), and the genies refuse non-kin substitutes (ll. 2835–83). Kosiya comes unasked and is accepted **in place of all four** (ll. 3058–63). Cite Conrad 2004, ll. 2835–3099, pp. 102–111 (Episodes 16–17), not "pp. 206–07". The episode is LATER, a 1994 recording; `waf_bulman_corpus.md` finds no 1889–1959 account of the sister-for-the-bala bargain.
2. **Ma Tenenba.** Confirmed and strengthened.
   - She is the **senior** wife, not only "the barren co-wife". She asked for the second marriage herself (ll. 2622–60).
   - Her name stands in Fakoli's praise, "Soma Tenenba's son Fakoli" (ll. 2688–89), and she declares a birth without pregnancy (l. 3110).
   - She takes him to **five** places, not three: the Kulubali ancestor, Sibi/Tabon, Kirina, Nema and Do ni Kiri (ll. 3107–60).
   - "It was a Condé woman who gave him his dalilu" is ll. 3104–05 (p. 112).
   - Kirina's ruler is named: Tenen Mansa Konkon (l. 3147).
3. **Kolonkan.** Confirmed: the meat episode, the curse at the loosened wrapper (ll. 3596–3627), the counsel at Sibi (l. 4797) and the marriage to Kamanjan (ll. 4810–52).
   - The "vision from her opened wrapper" revealing Soso, reported in `waf_conrad_austen_sunjata.md` from Conrad 1999 pp. 207–208, **is not in the 2004 text** as printed. Treat it as unverified here, possibly from another bard or an omitted passage.
   - The quote is "my mother", not "our mother".
4. **The mother's-name prefix.** Confirmed by Conrad (p. xxxv; So'olon Ma'an, So'olon Jamori, So'olon Kolonkan, ll. 2192–96) and by Niane (p. 15, n. 27). The epic also gives a precedent for the **claiming** (foster) mother's name in a praise name: Fakoli as "Soma Tenenba's son" (ll. 2688–89). The prefix applies to daughters too (So'olon Kolonkan; Niane's Sogolon Kolonkan). The §15 wording "(Sun-Jata)" is right.
5. **Nana Triban.** Now verified as Sansun Bereté's daughter in both books (Conrad l. 501 and p. 202; Niane p. xix). The `waf_conrad_austen_sunjata.md` §3.4 "unverified" can be closed.
   - In Conrad she is a hunter's wife and Turama'an's mother. In Niane she is the sister who learns Soumaoro's secret.
   - Djamarou is **Niane only**: a full sister, Sogolon Djamarou (pp. 17–18, 26). Conrad has a full brother, So'olon Jamori, in that place. That they are one name-slot is my inference.
6. **Sogolon's burial.** Conrad's version turns on a custom of hospitality ("This is your corpse", ll. 4013–23), not only a price. The man's funeral is the host's honour to her (l. 4029).
7. **Niane's framing.** Do not cite Niane for "what the bards say" without a second source. Do not use his wedding-night scene (p. 11), his wifely-submission moral (p. 22), or the 2006 "Faro" cosmology (p. x).

## 4. Design implications

### Gate step 1 (the smith's road)
- **The precedent text, in faith voice:** "The djinn asked four of Kosiya's kin; she gave herself and no one else was given. I gave the djinn my manhood, and no one of mine was given." The narrator marks Kosiya as a 1994 recording.
- **Price menu.** Add "a kinsperson" as a price the **djinn will name and the daughter must refuse**, as Sumaworo first did. Add a non-lethal woman's price after Do Kamissa: "the larger gold earring and the outer wrapper" (ll. 1086–91). No event may offer a life.
- **Reaction line (hostile).** "Humans do that, but we do not do that" (l. 2878), spoken by a djinn to a seeker who tries to pay with strangers: a failure branch.

### Gate step 2 (the mother's house): use Ma Tenenba as the main model
- **The claiming.** The claimer says, in faith voice, "I have given birth to a child without getting pregnant" (after l. 3110). The claimed daughter takes the claimer's name, as Fakoli took Soma Tenenba's.
- **The rounds.** A short travel chain (*dalimasigi*, the epic's own word, n. 108): a medicine bath; the Tabon/Sibi arch; the dust of the Kirina cave; the claimer's brothers' house. Holy-site text can use all of these.
- **The lying-in.** The claimer's brothers grant "three months" at her birth-house, as for a new mother (ll. 3172–79). Use it as a three-month "lying-in at the mother's house" modifier on both women. It pairs with the new-mother washings in `waf_brett_smith_bamana.md`.
- **The descendants' tie.** The claimer's brothers become "uncle" to the daughter's descendants (ll. 3180–81). Use a dynasty-level opinion modifier between the two houses.
- **A gift from the brothers.** The claimer's brothers may give a headband spun by unmarried girls on a dark night (ll. 3218–53). The faith can read Fakoli's "Men do not wear head-ties, but I will" in its own voice. The narrator does not.

### Gate step 3 (the word)
- **The naming day, after Niane p. 14 and the Kanote account** (`waf_bulman_corpus.md`):
  - an older woman of the claiming house cuts the first lock of hair;
  - she whispers the name in the daughter's ear;
  - the jeli then proclaims it to the court.
  - The name has the shape "[claimer's name]'s [given name]" (So'olon Kolonkan, Soma Tenenba's Fakoli).
- **Payment.** "The generosity of kings makes griots eloquent" (Niane p. 14). An open hand saves the hero (Conrad ll. 2278–80): an ill-paid jeli sings satire, as already designed.

### The line, inheritance and sisters
- **Do Kamissa's grievance.** A ruler of the faith who refuses a sister her share of the legacy triggers "the buffalo's grievance", a stress event on the sister with a revenge branch. Use the faith-voice line "Should he tell me that women do not receive property?" (after ll. 1093–94).
- **Kolonkan's curse.** Unchanged from `waf_conrad_austen_sunjata.md`. The quote is now l. 3627.
- **Doctrine lines (faith voice):**
  - "Men were not the closest kin; women were" (after ll. 1749–50).
  - "The father is everybody's; the mother is one's own" (after l. 2163).
  - "Children of one mother should be in harmony" (after l. 3595).

### Burial ("The House buries its own")
- **Do Kamissa.** Her wish to be buried by the strangers she chose, unseen by the brother who wronged her (ll. 1100–28), is the faith's oldest myth of burial by chosen kin.
- **Sogolon.** Her burial abroad, honoured by the host (ll. 4013–32), is the model for a House that buries a daughter whom her own lineage refused.
- **The narrator** keeps the man's-funeral detail as an honour to a mother, not as a crossing.

### Holy sites
- `c_tabon` / Sibi: "the entrance of Sibi Mountain … the exit of Tabon Mountain" (ll. 3137–38), where the claimed child is carried through.
- Kirina: the cave dust and Tenen Mansa Konkon (ll. 3145–53).
- **Do (Do ni Kiri)**, Sogolon's and Tenenba's homeland "in the general region of modern-day Segu" (p. xxxix). Proposed as the open fourth holy site; `waf_bulman_corpus.md` adds al-Bakrī's "Daw" and the medieval tumulus fields.

### Reaction lines
- Hostile court (after Niane p. 22, inverted): "A woman should show humility before a husband; this one shows sorcery."
- Faith: "A source of greatness is not had without hardship" (after l. 1317, quoted p. xxi).

### Do not use
- Niane's wedding-night threat (p. 11) and his "Faro" background (p. x).
- Kosiya's or Do Kamissa's deaths as playable acts.
- Sogolon's man's funeral as a model for daughters (FtM-adjacent).
- The Fadama informants' names in game text.
- Komo material. The Kamabolon rites.
