# Bæddel faith: Norse and Anglo-Saxon reading list

This list deepens the "Northern Europe" bibliography for the Bæddel faith (seiðr + *ergi* + Old English *bæddel*). It covers what the README and `hist_norse_british_articles.md` already use (Berry 2022, Birch 2025, Ármann Jakobsson 2011, Karras & Pierpont, Tommony & Forster 2024). It adds the specialist scholarship and standard translations behind them.

**How to read an entry.**
- **Use** names the design hook: *gate* (seiðr trance, high seat), *níð/ergi duels*, *matriline* (fostering, sworn sisterhood, adoption), *Óðinn's host*, *outlawry* (stage 2), *holy sites*, or *Anglo-Saxon side*.
- **Priority:** P1 = must-read, P2 = useful, P3 = background.
- **Availability:** **OA** = free full text at the URL (checked unless noted); **Preview** = partial or borrowable; **Library** = paywalled or print, with ISBN or DOI.
- **Downloaded** = a copy sits in `docs/research/sources_pending/norse/`, which is gitignored. Source URLs are in `SOURCES.txt` there.

This file holds no extracted text. Every citation was checked on 2026-10-18 against publisher, library, Crossref or repository records, or against the downloaded copy. Anything not confirmed is marked *unverified*.

**Two findings that affect the design now**
1. **Saxo's Uppsala passage (Book 6).** Saxo does not say Freyr's priests dressed as women. Starkaðr leaves Uppsala in disgust at the sacrificial season: the mimes on the stage make effeminate gestures and the bells clatter in an unmanly way. The blog paraphrase in `hist_norse_british_articles.md` §19 overstates it. Flavour text should name the mimes and bells, not women's clothing for priests.
2. ***Bæddel* and *bædling* are two words with different attestations.** *Bæddel* glosses "hermaphrodite" in the Antwerp–London glossary. *Bædling* is the word in the Old English Canons of Theodore and the Cleopatra and Harley glossaries. Cite Fulk & Jurasinski and Fulk 2004 for the penitential.

## 1. Seiðr, the völva and magic (the gate)

1. **Price, Neil.** 2019. *The Viking Way: Magic and Mind in Late Iron Age Scandinavia*. 2nd ed. Oxford: Oxbow. (1st ed. Uppsala 2002, Aun 31.)
   - Use: *gate*. The core study of seiðr, the völva's equipment and staffs, its sexual and *ergi* dimension, and seiðr in war magic. Gives the high-seat trance its archaeological body.
   - P1 · Library, ISBN 978-1-84217-260-5 · Not downloaded.
2. **Gardeła, Leszek, Sophie Bønding and Peter Pentz (eds.).** 2023. *The Norse Sorceress: Mind and Materiality in the Viking World*. Oxford: Oxbow. (Crossref and JSTOR date it 2024.)
   - Use: *gate*. The newest multi-author survey of the völva: staffs, graves, ritual repertoire and gender.
   - P1 · OA (CC BY-NC-ND) at https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/93355. The OAPEN listing was confirmed, but the site's bot protection stopped the download. ISBN 978-1-78925-953-7, DOI 10.2307/jj.5699282 · Not downloaded.
3. **Jessen, Mads Dengsø.** 2023. "Miniature chairs: on seeresses, the future and conflict." In Gardeła, Bønding & Pentz (eds.), *The Norse Sorceress*, 451–461.
   - Use: *gate*. The material evidence for the seeress's high seat: the chair pendants. This is the object behind "the trance under the völva's high seat".
   - P1 · OA (inside the volume above) · Not downloaded.
4. **Varberg, Jeanette.** 2023. "Gender, Prophecies and Magic: Cult Specialists in Denmark before the Viking Age." In *The Norse Sorceress*, 267–282.
   - Use: *gate*, *holy sites*. Gendered cult specialists before the sagas. Gives the clergy a deep pedigree.
   - P2 · OA (inside the volume) · Not downloaded.
5. **Solli, Brit.** 2002. *Seid: myter, sjamanisme og kjønn i vikingenes tid*. Oslo: Pax.
   - Use: *gate*, *ergi*. The main argument that seiðr was a gender-transgressive ritual role and that Óðinn was a queer god. In Norwegian.
   - P1 · Library, ISBN 82-530-2403-7 · Not downloaded.
6. **Strömbäck, Dag.** 1935. *Sejd: textstudier i nordisk religionshistoria*. Stockholm. Reissued 2000 with other essays as *Sejd och andra studier i nordisk själsuppfattning*, ed. Gertrud Gidlund. Uppsala: Kungl. Gustav Adolfs Akademien (Acta 72). The series number and editor are unverified.
   - Use: *gate*. The founding philology of seiðr, *seiðhjallr* and *ergi*. Every later study starts from it.
   - P2 · Library · Not downloaded.
7. **Tolley, Clive.** 2009. *Shamanism in Norse Myth and Magic*. 2 vols. FF Communications 296–297. Helsinki: Academia Scientiarum Fennica. Reprinted with corrections 2023.
   - Use: *gate*. The sceptical, rigorous check on "seiðr = shamanism". Use it to keep the trance mechanic honest.
   - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-951-41-1027-6 (vol. 1) and 978-951-41-1029-0 (vol. 2) · Not downloaded.
8. **Mitchell, Stephen A.** 2011. *Witchcraft and Magic in the Nordic Middle Ages*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press.
   - Use: *gate*, *outlawry*. How law, literature and popular belief treated magic workers. Chapter 5 covers magic and the law, which bears on how seiðr-women end up outlawed.
   - P2 · Library, DOI 10.9783/9780812203714, ISBN 978-0-8122-4290-4 · Not downloaded.
9. **Heide, Eldar.** 2006. "Spinning *seiðr*." In Anders Andrén, Kristina Jennbert and Catharina Raudvere (eds.), *Old Norse Religion in Long-Term Perspectives*, 164–170. Lund: Nordic Academic Press.
   - Use: *gate*. Reads seiðr as spinning a mind-emissary, which ties the rite to the women's craft of spinning. Good for the art and event text.
   - P2 · Library or Preview (the author posts papers on Academia.edu) · Not downloaded.
10. **Heide, Eldar.** 2006. *Gand, seid og åndevind*. PhD thesis, University of Bergen. ISBN 82-308-0196-7.
    - Use: *gate*. The full version of the argument in item 9. In Norwegian.
    - P3 · OA at https://hdl.handle.net/1956/4441. The record is listed, but BORA redirected and was bot-blocked, so it was not opened · Not downloaded.
11. **Gardeła, Leszek.** 2016. *(Magic) Staffs in the Viking Age*. Studia Medievalia Septentrionalia 27. Vienna: Fassbaender.
    - Use: *gate*. The full corpus of the völva's staff. Use it for the faith's regalia.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN unverified · Not downloaded.
12. **Dillmann, François-Xavier.** 2006. *Les magiciens dans l'Islande ancienne*. Acta Academiae Regiae Gustavi Adolphi 92. Uppsala: Kungl. Gustav Adolfs Akademien.
    - Use: *gate*. An exhaustive catalogue of the magic workers in saga literature. In French.
    - P3 · Library · Not downloaded.
13. **Blain, Jenny.** 2002. *Nine Worlds of Seid-Magic: Ecstasy and Neo-Shamanism in North European Paganism*. London: Routledge.
    - Use: *gate*. An ethnography of modern seiðr practice, including how today's practitioners handle *ergi* and gender. Useful for the trans-inclusive reading, but it describes modern practice, not the medieval rite.
    - P3 · Library, ISBN 978-0-415-25651-3 · Not downloaded.

## 2. Ergi, argr, níð, insult and flyting (the declaration and the duels)

14. **Meulengracht Sørensen, Preben.** 1983. *The Unmanly Man: Concepts of Sexual Defamation in Early Northern Society*. Trans. Joan Turville-Petre. The Viking Collection 1. Odense: Odense University Press.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*. The standard study of *níð*, *argr* and the legal weight of the insult. The backbone of the "someone shouts *ergi*" declaration and of the insult-duel mechanic.
    - P1 · Library, ISBN 87-7492-436-2 · Not downloaded.
15. **Ström, Folke.** 1974. *Níð, ergi and Old Norse Moral Attitudes*. Dorothea Coke Memorial Lecture, delivered 10 May 1973. London: Viking Society for Northern Research.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*. A short, clear account of *níð* and *ergi*, the carved insult pole and the outlawry words.
    - P1 · OA at http://vsnrweb-publications.org.uk/Nid,%20ergi%20and%20Old%20Norse%20moral%20attitudes.pdf · **Downloaded.**
16. **Gade, Kari Ellen.** 1986. "Homosexuality and Rape of Males in Old Norse Law and Literature." *Scandinavian Studies* 58: 124–141. Reprinted in Wayne R. Dynes et al. (eds.), 1992.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*, *outlawry*. The laws that make *ragr*, *stroðinn* and *sorðinn* actionable. The legal frame for the duel.
    - P1 · Library (JSTOR; stable URL unverified) · Not downloaded.
17. **Clover, Carol J.** 1993. "Regardless of Sex: Men, Women, and Power in Early Northern Europe." *Speculum* 68.2: 363–387. Also printed in *Representations* 44 (1993).
    - Use: *ergi*, *matriline*. The one-sex "hvatr/blauðr" model: women can rise into the strong category and men can fall out of it. The theory behind the whole faith.
    - P1 · Library, DOI 10.2307/2864557 · Not downloaded.
18. **Clover, Carol J.** 1980. "The Germanic Context of the Unferþ Episode." *Speculum* 55.3: 444–468.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*, *Anglo-Saxon side*. Defines flyting as a genre that spans Old English and Old Norse. The template for a flyting duel that works in both York and Uppsala.
    - P1 · Library, DOI 10.2307/2847235 · Not downloaded.
19. **Jakobsson, Ármann.** 2008. "The Trollish Acts of Þorgrímr the Witch: The Meanings of *Troll* and *Ergi* in Medieval Iceland." *Saga-Book* 32: 39–68.
    - Use: *ergi*, *gate*. Ties *ergi* to sorcery and the uncanny. Pairs with his 2011 "Óðinn as mother", which is already cited.
    - P1 · OA at https://vsnr.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Saga-Book-XXXII.pdf (the whole volume) · **Downloaded.**
20. **Harris, Joseph.** 1979. "The *senna*: From Description to Literary Theory." *Michigan Germanic Studies* 5: 65–74.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*. Sets out the structure of the senna (claim, insult, counter-insult). Gives the duel its stages.
    - P2 · Library · Not downloaded.
21. **Swenson, Karen.** 1991. *Performing Definitions: Two Genres of Insult in Old Norse Literature*. Studies in Scandinavian Literature and Culture 3. Columbia, SC: Camden House.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*. A book-length study of senna and mannjafnaðr (comparing men) as performance. Useful for the duel's win conditions.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-0-938100-87-4 · Not downloaded.
22. **McKinnell, John.** 1986–89. "Motivation in *Lokasenna*." *Saga-Book* 22: 234–262.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*, *Óðinn's host*. How the insults in Lokasenna work, including the *argr* charge against Óðinn's seiðr.
    - P2 · OA at https://vsnr.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Saga-Book-XXII.pdf (the whole volume) · **Downloaded.**
23. **Dronke, Ursula.** 1981. *The Role of Sexual Themes in Njáls saga*. Dorothea Coke Memorial Lecture, 1980. London: Viking Society for Northern Research.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*. The beardless-Njáll insults and sexual slander in a full saga plot.
    - P2 · OA at http://vsnrweb-publications.org.uk/The%20role%20of%20sexual%20themes%20in%20njals%20saga.pdf · **Downloaded.**
24. **Evans, Gareth Lloyd.** 2019. *Men and Masculinities in the Sagas of Icelanders*. Oxford: Oxford University Press.
    - Use: *ergi*. The current standard on saga masculinity. Use it to tune what counts as "unmanly" in event text.
    - P2 · Library, DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198831242.001.0001 · Not downloaded.
25. **Jakobsson, Ármann.** 2007. "Masculinity and Politics in *Njáls saga*." *Viator* 38: 191–215.
    - Use: *ergi*. Shows how a charge of unmanliness works as a political weapon.
    - P3 · Library, DOI 10.1484/J.VIATOR.2.302082 · Not downloaded.

## 3. Gender, women and gender-crossing in Norse society

26. **Moilanen, Ulla, Tuija Kirkinen, Nelli-Johanna Saari, Adam B. Rohrlach, Johannes Krause, Päivi Onkamo and Elina Salmela.** 2022. "A Woman with a Sword? – Weapon Grave at Suontaka Vesitorninmäki, Finland." *European Journal of Archaeology* 25.1: 42–60.
    - Use: *matriline*, *Óðinn's host*. A possible XXY person buried in women's dress with a sword. The strongest material precedent for the faith's armed daughters. Read the primary article, not the summary in Berry.
    - P1 · OA (CC BY), DOI 10.1017/eaa.2021.30 · **Downloaded.**
27. **Hedenstierna-Jonson, Charlotte, et al.** 2017. "A female Viking warrior confirmed by genomics." *American Journal of Physical Anthropology* 164.4: 853–860.
    - Use: *Óðinn's host*. The Birka Bj 581 genomic result.
    - P2 · OA, PMC5724682 (DOI 10.1002/ajpa.23308) · **Downloaded** (Europe PMC full-text XML).
28. **Price, Neil, Charlotte Hedenstierna-Jonson, Torun Zachrisson, et al.** 2019. "Viking warrior women? Reassessing Birka chamber grave Bj.581." *Antiquity* 93.367: 181–198.
    - Use: *Óðinn's host*. Answers the critics of the 2017 paper and discusses gender identity beyond sex. Use it with item 27 for the "shield-maiden debate".
    - P2 · OA (CC BY), DOI 10.15184/aqy.2018.258 · **Downloaded.**
29. **Jochens, Jenny.** 1995. *Women in Old Norse Society*. Ithaca: Cornell University Press.
    - Use: *matriline*. Marriage, fostering, women's work and property. The social ground for an adoptive matriline.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 0-8014-3165-4, DOI 10.7591/9780801455964 · Not downloaded.
30. **Jochens, Jenny.** 1996. *Old Norse Images of Women*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press.
    - Use: *gate*, *Óðinn's host*. Covers the literary types the faith draws on: the seeress, the warrior woman, the whetter and the valkyrie.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 0-8122-3358-1, DOI 10.9783/9781512802818 · Not downloaded.
31. **Friðriksdóttir, Jóhanna Katrín.** 2013. *Women in Old Norse Literature: Bodies, Words, and Power*. New York: Palgrave Macmillan.
    - Use: *níð/ergi duels*, *gate*. Women's speech, insult, magic and power in the sagas.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-0-230-12042-6, DOI 10.1057/9781137118066 · Not downloaded.
32. **Friðriksdóttir, Jóhanna Katrín.** 2020. *Valkyrie: The Women of the Viking World*. London: Bloomsbury Academic.
    - Use: *matriline*, *Óðinn's host*. A readable life-cycle survey (fostering, marriage, widowhood, the völva). The quickest route into the whole field.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-1-78831-477-0 (hardback) or 978-1-350-23030-9 (paperback) · Not downloaded.
33. **Jesch, Judith.** 1991. *Women in the Viking Age*. Woodbridge: Boydell.
    - Use: background. Runic and archaeological evidence for real women. A sober check on the saga types.
    - P3 · Library, ISBN 0-85115-278-3 · Not downloaded.
34. **Clover, Carol J.** 1986. "Maiden Warriors and Other Sons." *Journal of English and Germanic Philology* 85: 35–49.
    - Use: *matriline*. Hervör as a daughter who stands in as a "son" where there is no male heir. The inheritance logic behind the faith's line.
    - P2 · Library (JSTOR) · Not downloaded.
35. **Mayburd, Miriam.** 2014. "'Helzt þóttumk nú heima í millim…': A Reassessment of Hervör in Light of *Seiðr*'s Supernatural Gender Dynamics." *Arkiv för nordisk filologi* 129: 121–164.
    - Use: *gate*, *matriline*. Reads Hervör's gender-crossing as what gives her the power to wake the dead. Joins seiðr to gender-crossing directly.
    - P1 · OA at https://journals.lub.lu.se/anf/article/view/18896 · **Downloaded.**
36. **Gardeła, Leszek.** 2021. *Women and Weapons in the Viking World: Amazons of the North*. Oxford: Oxbow.
    - Use: *Óðinn's host*. A full survey of women's weapon graves and the shield-maiden sources.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 978-1-78925-665-9 · Not downloaded. (The archive.org copy is an unauthorised upload and was not used.)
37. **Back Danielsson, Ing-Marie.** 2007. *Masking Moments: The Transitions of Bodies and Beings in Late Iron Age Scandinavia*. PhD thesis, Stockholm University (Stockholm Studies in Archaeology 40; series number unverified).
    - Use: *gate*. Masks, gold-foil figures and bodies that move between categories. Supports transformation as ritual rather than deviance.
    - P3 · Library. Only an unofficial third-party PDF was found, and the official DiVA copy is unverified · Not downloaded.

## 4. Valkyries, dísir and Óðinn's host

38. **Jefford Franks, Amy** (now writing as Eirnin Jefford Franks). 2019. "Valfǫðr, vǫlur, and valkyrjur: Óðinn as a Queer Deity Mediating the Warrior Halls of Viking Age Scandinavia." *Scandia: Journal of Medieval Norse Studies* 2: 28–65.
    - Use: *Óðinn's host*. Argues that the all-male Männerbund reading of the warrior hall comes from Höfler's Nazi-era scholarship. It reads Óðinn's company of valkyries and völur as gender-mediating. The single best citation for "Óðinn's host has no men in it".
    - P1 · OA at https://periodicos.ufpb.br/index.php/scandia/article/view/47861 (the site's certificate had expired at the time of checking) · **Downloaded.**
39. **Damico, Helen.** 1984. *Beowulf's Wealhtheow and the Valkyrie Tradition*. Madison: University of Wisconsin Press.
    - Use: *Óðinn's host*, *Anglo-Saxon side*. Finds the valkyrie figure in Old English verse. The York bridge for the host.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 0-299-09500-2 · Not downloaded.
40. **Kershaw, Kris.** 2000. *The One-Eyed God: Odin and the (Indo-)Germanic Männerbünde*. Journal of Indo-European Studies Monograph 36. Washington, DC: Institute for the Study of Man.
    - Use: *Óðinn's host*, as the counter-position. The fullest case for Óðinn's war-band as a male cult. Read it as the model the faith inverts.
    - P2 · Library, ISBN 0-941694-74-7 · Not downloaded.

Óðinn's own gender is already covered by Ármann Jakobsson 2011 (README) and by Solli (items 5 and 66).

## 5. Fostering, sworn kinship, adoption (the matriline)

41. **Miller, William Ian.** 1990. *Bloodtaking and Peacemaking: Feud, Law, and Society in Saga Iceland*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.
    - Use: *matriline*, *outlawry*. Householding, fosterage, kin obligation, feud and settlement. How an artificial kin group would actually function.
    - P1 · Library, ISBN 0-226-52679-8 · Not downloaded.
42. **Hansen, Anna.** 2008. "Fosterage and dependency in medieval Iceland and its significance in *Gísla saga*." In Shannon Lewis-Simpson (ed.), *Youth and Age in the Medieval North*, 73–86. Leiden: Brill. (Editor unverified.)
    - Use: *matriline*. Fosterage as a deliberate alliance-making bond. Gísla saga is also the classic sworn-brotherhood rite that fails.
    - P2 · Library, DOI 10.1163/ej.9789004170735.i-310.31 · Not downloaded.
43. **Dennis, Andrew, Peter Foote and Richard Perkins (trans.).** 1980–2000. *Laws of Early Iceland: Grágás*. 2 vols. University of Manitoba Icelandic Studies 3 and 5. Winnipeg: University of Manitoba Press.
    - Use: *matriline*, *outlawry*, *níð/ergi duels*.
      - The law on *ættleiðing* (formal adoption into a kin group) and inheritance. The section location is unverified, so check the index.
      - The outlawry grades.
      - The insult words and the cross-dressing clause.
    - P1 · Library, ISBN 0-88755-115-7 (vol. 1) and 978-0-88755-158-1 (vol. 2). The press sells the PDF, so there is no OA copy · Not downloaded.

Rapp on brother-making is already in the repo (`hist_rapp_brother_making.md`). No study of *women's* sworn sisterhood in the Norse sources turned up. Treat sworn sisterhood as a design extension of fóstbræðralag, built from items 41–43 and the sagas.

## 7. Uppsala, Saxo's Freyr cult and pre-Christian cult practice (holy sites)

## 8. Old English *bæddel*, *wæpned* and *wif*: Anglo-Saxon gender and sexuality

55. **Bosworth, Joseph, and T. Northcote Toller.** *An Anglo-Saxon Dictionary* (1898; online edition), s.v. *bæddel*.

56. **Fulk, R. D., and Stefan Jurasinski (eds.).** 2012. *The Old English Canons of Theodore*. Early English Text Society, Supplementary Series 25. Oxford: Oxford University Press.
