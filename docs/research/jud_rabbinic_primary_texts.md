## 1. Mishnah Bikkurim 4 — the *androginos* "a created being of its own"

**What it says.** A four-way table: the *androginos* is in some ways like men, in some like women, in some like both, in some like neither (4:1).
- **Like men (4:2):** white-discharge impurity; "wraps and cuts his hair like men" (מתעטף ומסתפר כאנשים); marries but is not married; bound by all Torah commandments.
- **Like women (4:3):** red-discharge impurity; may not be alone with men; may not testify; disqualified from the priesthood.
- **Like both (4:4):** protected against striking and cursing; inherits.
- **Like neither (4:5):** not valued as either.
- **Close (4:5):** "אנדרוגינוס בריה בפני עצמה הוא ולא יכלו חכמים להכריע עליו" ("the androginos is a created being of its own, and the sages could not decide about him"). The *tumtum* is set apart: "sometimes a man, sometimes a woman".

**Variants worth knowing.**
- **Attribution.** The vocalised Hebrew gives the closing sentence to **R. Meir**. Kulp's English gives it to **R. Yose**. The Tosefta (below) has R. Yose.
- **The nazirite clause.** Here the two versions disagree. The Hebrew 4:5 says one who vows "if this is neither man nor woman" is *not* a nazirite. Kulp's English says he *becomes* one. The Hebrew 4:4 has the "both man and woman" vow binding. Do not build anything on the nazirite clause.

**Design use.**
- In a faith's or character's voice only: "the sages themselves said she is a creature of her own".
- The *androginos* is an intersex legal category, not a male-to-female crossing. The Mishnah dresses him **as a man**.
- The narrator must not cite this as recognition of trans women or of "six genders".

## 2. Tosefta Bikkurim 2 — the *koy* and the *androginos*

**What it says.**
- The *androginos* list (2:2–6) follows directly on the *koy*, an animal between wild and domestic (2:1).
- The *koy* gets the **same closing formula**: "כוי בריה לעצמו ולא יכלו חכמים להכריע עליו" ("a *koy* is a creature of its own; the sages could not decide", 2:1).
- R. Yose then says the same of the *androginos* (2:6), adding "whether he is man or woman". The tumtum is only "a doubt, man or woman".

**Design use.**
- The phrase "a created being of its own" was first said of a half-wild animal. The juxtaposition is Strassfeld's point (2013, p. 143). A sect quoting R. Yose should know a rabbinic opponent can answer "he said it of the *koy* first".
- **Reaction line** for a hostile scholar: "R. Yose said that of a beast."

## 3. Yevamot 80b — signs of the *seris ḥammah* and the *aylonit*

**What it says.**
- **The *seris ḥammah*** ("eunuch of the sun", born so): "anyone who is twenty years old and has not grown two hairs". Signs include no beard, sparse hair and smooth skin.
  - R. Shimon b. Elazar adds a voice that is "defective, so it cannot be told whether a man or a woman" (שקולו לקוי ואין ניכר בין איש לאשה).
- **The *aylonit*** is defined in the same form: no breasts, pain in intercourse, a deep voice "so it cannot be told whether a woman or a man".
- **How many signs?** Rav Huna requires all the signs, R. Yoḥanan one of them.
- **Cause.** The Mishnah's *seris* is by the hand of Heaven, "like the *aylonit*". R. Akiva distinguishes the man made by human hand (בידי אדם), who is treated as a man for levirate purposes.
- **The scholars' test.** Rabba bar Avuh had the sages check whether Rav Naḥman's flesh steamed in the bath before giving him a daughter in marriage. Bodies were examined.

**Design use.**
- **Reaction only.** A Jewish court meeting a beardless member with a soft voice reaches for the *seris ḥammah* category: a man, born so, with a better legal standing than a man cut by human hand.
- Do **not** turn these signs into a mechanic that "proves" a character a woman. That would be the third-sexing the mod avoids.
- The by-heaven / by-man distinction is the one that matters against Dorleyism's knife (see §7).

## 4. Berakhot 60a — Dinah turned in the womb

**What it says.**
- **The Mishnah:** praying for a male child when one's wife is already pregnant is "a vain prayer".
- **Rav Yosef objects** from Gen. 30:21. Leah reckoned that six tribes had come from her and four from the handmaids; if this child were male, Rachel would have fewer than a handmaid. "מיד נהפכה לבת" ("immediately she was turned into a daughter"), and she was named Dinah from *din*, judgment.
- **Two answers:**
  1. "אין מזכירין מעשה נסים" ("one does not cite miracles").
  2. Leah's prayer fell within forty days of conception. The baraita schedules prayers: days 3–40 one prays "that it be male".
- **Further limit:** sex is fixed at conception by which partner emits seed first, so prayer works only if both emit at once.
- **The same page** carries the Mishnah "one blesses over the bad just as over the good". That is the rule Kalonymus cites for blessing *shelo asani ishah* "in a low voice" (*Even Boḥan* p. 18).

**Design use.**
- **The sect's precedent:** "God turned Dinah; Leah prayed and she was turned."
- **The rabbinic answers, as reaction lines:**
  - "One does not cite miracles."
  - "It was within forty days."
- The forty-day baraita is about prayer for a *male* child. The sect inverts it, and an opponent can point that out.
- **Name.** Dinah, "after her judgment" (*din*), is the strongest candidate for a name list (`jud_kalonymus_even_bohan.md` §9, event 0002). Keep the sect's own name *Ha-Nehpakhot*, and avoid "Daughters of Dinah": Genesis 34 follows.

## 5. Mishneh Torah, Avodah Zarah 12:9–10 — hair removal and cross-dressing

**What it says.**
- **12:9.** Removing armpit or pubic hair is forbidden only rabbinically, and only "in a place where only women remove it, so that he not adorn himself as women do". Where men and women both do it, there are no lashes.
- **12:10.** A woman may not wear a man's adornment (turban, hat, armour) or cut her hair like a man. A man may not wear a woman's adornment, "such as coloured garments and gold bracelets in a place where only women wear them". Then: "**הכל כמנהג המדינה**" ("all according to the custom of the country"). Either one "לוקין" (is lashed).
  - A man who plucks one white hair or dyes one hair black is lashed, "because he adorned himself as a woman".
  - **Tumtum and androginos:** they may not veil like a woman or shave the head like a man, but "if they do, they are not lashed".
- **Touger's notes (modern).** The note to 12:10 points to Isserles OḤ 696:8 for Purim and weddings, and says the Baḥ and others reject that leniency.

**Design use.**
- **Offence.** Under the law, what she does is "adorning as a woman" (עדה עדי אשה). The verb is the one Kalonymus uses of being adorned with gold (`jud_kalonymus_even_bohan.md` §8). That makes it a usable loc pun in the sect's voice.
- **"All by local custom".** The concrete items differ by place. In Provence the markers in our texts are coloured garments and gold bracelets (Maimonides) and anklets and necklaces (Kalonymus p. 29).
- **Reaction.** A Rabbinic court treats public daily wear of women's dress outside Purim as a lashable offence in principle. In the Arles setting the *kahal* had no jurisdiction to inflict corporal punishment under the archbishop (Chotzner p. 128). This is *unverified* as legal history and needs a Provençal source. So show the response as shunning or a ban, never lashes on screen.
- **Hair.** Plucking or dyeing a single hair counts. A member's beard removal is an offence in itself, and that is good event texture ("the elder sees her chin is smooth").

## 6. Mishneh Torah, Ishut 4:11 — doubtful betrothal

**What it says.** If a *tumtum* or *androginos* betroths a woman, or is betrothed by a man, "these are doubtful betrothals, and they need a *get* out of doubt". Touger cites the Tur, via Isserles EH 44:5, that the androginos counts as male, so his betrothal is valid.

**Design use.** Narrow. This covers intersex people, not the sect. Its use is as a model of how the law handles doubt: it applies the stringencies of both sexes. A rabbinic opponent may argue "even the androginos must divorce as a man". **Do not** model a member's marriage on this law. Marriage for members is a separate design question; no text read here addresses it.

## 7. Mishneh Torah, Issurei Bi'ah 16 — the maimed man and castration

**What it says.**
- **16:1–2.** A man with crushed testicles or a severed member (פצוע דכא וכרות שפכה) may not marry a native-born Jewish woman. He is lashed if he does. He may marry a convert or a freed bondwoman.
- **16:3–8.** The anatomy of disqualification: the shaft, the testicles, the seminal cords.
- **16:9.** All of this applies only where the cause was not "by the hand of Heaven" (a man, a dog, a thorn). One born so, or maimed by illness, "is fit to enter the congregation".
- **16:10.** "אסור להפסיד איברי זרע" ("it is forbidden to destroy the organs of seed") in humans, beasts and birds, in the Land and abroad, "not with their own bodies, nor with the bodies of others". "Whoever castrates is lashed by Torah law everywhere", even one who castrates the already-castrated.
- **16:11.** Several men cutting in turn are all lashed. Castrating a female is exempt (פטור).
- **16:12.** Giving a man a sterilising potion (עקרין) is forbidden but not lashed. A woman may drink one "so that she will not bear". Exposing a man to a dog, or to water or snow, until he is impotent is not lashed unless he castrates by hand, but the man deserves "stripes for rebelliousness".
- **16:13.** "It is forbidden to tell a gentile to castrate our animal." If the Jew acts by subterfuge, he is fined and must sell the animal to another Jew.

**Design use.**
- **A hard wall.** No Jewish-adjacent gate may use the knife (16:10) or the potion (16:12). The sect's Turning is prayer, name and garment only.
- **16:13 extends the wall to commissioning.** A Rabbinic character may not even *ask* a Dorleyite (gentile) to cut. So no event where a Jewish member travels to the Sisters for the knife. If such an event exists for other faiths, block it for Jewish faiths, or give it a "this would make you a *seris adam* and bar you from the congregation" warning.
- **Reaction to Dorleyism.** A Rabbinic court sees a daughter of the knife as a man maimed by human hand (16:9): barred from marrying a native-born Jewish woman, and the cutter liable to lashes. This is already finding 1 of the reading list, now confirmed against the text.
- **Avoid** any event in which a Jewish character castrates anyone. That is both a halakhic impossibility for a pious character and the antisemitic motif (`jud_phelan_slave_trade_motif.md`).

## 8. Shulḥan Arukh OḤ 696:8 with Isserles — what is permitted on Purim

**What it says.**
- **Karo:** "מותר לישא אשה בפורים" ("one may marry a woman on Purim").
- **Isserles' gloss**, citing "the responsum of Mahari Minz §17":
  - Marriage on both the 14th and the 15th; redemption of the firstborn all the more so.
  - "מה שנהגו ללבוש פרצופים בפורים וגבר לובש שמלת אשה ואשה כלי גבר אין איסור בדבר" ("the custom of wearing masks on Purim, and a man wearing a woman's dress and a woman a man's gear, there is no prohibition").
  - The reason: "**מאחר שאין מכוונין אלא לשמחה בעלמא**" ("since they intend nothing but mere merriment").
  - Shaatnez of rabbinic grade is allowed on the same grounds.
  - "Some say it is forbidden, but the custom follows the first view."
  - Playful seizing of each other's things is not theft.
  - All of it only "**שלא יעשה דבר שלא כהוגן על פי טובי העיר**" ("he may do nothing improper, according to the town's worthies").

**Dates.** Minz d. 1508. Isserles is 16th century. This is late for the mod's window, but *Even Boḥan* p. 29 shows the custom in Provence c. 1322 (see the Kalonymus note).

**Design use (the core of the sect's legal position).**
1. **The licence is conditional on intent.** It holds only "for mere merriment". The sect's whole claim is that her dress is *not* merriment. So the Purim licence is exactly what the elders will deny her once she keeps the dress on. This is the dramatic hinge of the Turning (event 0003 in the Kalonymus note).
2. **The words match Deut. 22:5.** Isserles' "גבר לובש שמלת אשה" and Kalonymus's "ילבש שמלת אשה" are the Torah's own words, now in a permitting clause. The sect's line: "On the fourteenth of Adar the law itself says *a man wears a woman's dress* and there is no sin."
3. **The town's worthies decide.** "על פי טובי העיר" is the textual basis for the assembly stage: the *kahal*'s worthies judge.
4. **Marriage on Purim is allowed.** A late-chain option: a member marries on Purim. *Unverified* whether any authority would allow such a marriage; it is flavour only.
5. **There is a dissent.** "Some say it is forbidden" (and, per Touger, the Baḥ). A strict rabbi's line: "I hold with those who forbid it even on Purim."

## 9. bYevamot 64a — barren ancestors, the pitchfork of prayer, Abraham and Sarah as *tumtumin*

**What it says.**
- **The Mishnah.** After ten childless years a man may not neglect procreation (64a). The *baraita* hedges: "perhaps he did not merit to be built from her".
- **Isaac and Rebecca.** Rabbi Yitzḥak reads "Isaac entreated the Lord *lenokhaḥ* his wife" (Gen. 25:21) to teach that **both** were barren (עקורים).
- **Why the ancestors were barren.** Rabbi Yitzḥak: "because the Holy One, blessed be He, longs for the prayer of the righteous" (מתאוה לתפלתן של צדיקים).
- **The pitchfork.** Rabbi Yitzḥak, punning on *vaye'ater* / *eter*: "just as this pitchfork turns over [מהפך] the grain from place to place, so the prayer of the righteous turns over [מהפכת] the attributes of the Holy One" from rage to mercy.
- **Abraham and Sarah.** "Rabbi Ami said: Abraham and Sarah were *tumtumin*" (טומטמין). The prooftext (Isa. 51:1–2) runs over onto 64b.

**Design use.**
- **The root ה-פ-ך.** The sect's name, Ha-Nehpakhot, now has a Talmudic anchor besides Berakhot 60a's *nehepkhah* (Dinah). Here prayer *mehapekhet*, "turns": it turns God's measure, not a body. In the faith's own voice: "Rabbi Yitzḥak said the prayer of the righteous turns the Holy One from anger to mercy, as a fork turns the grain." This suits `tm_hipukh.0001.desc` or the prayer option.
- **Patience.** "The Holy One longs for the prayers of the righteous" is the textual ground for the patience mechanic (+10 per unanswered Adar, never reset). Suggested failure-line variant: "Our mothers were barren because the Holy One longs for the prayers of the righteous. Pray again next Adar."
- **Narrator rule.** The *tumtumin* line is aggadah about Abraham and Sarah's bodies being formed for procreation. It is not a crossing of sex, so the sect may use it only as "God shapes bodies in answer to prayer".

## 10. bYevamot 64b — Sarah the *aylonit*

**What it says.**
- **Isa. 51:1–2.** The prooftext continues: "the rock whence you were hewn ... the hole of the pit whence you were dug" alongside "look to Abraham your father and to Sarah who bore you". In the Davidson gloss this means sexual organs were fashioned for them.
- **Sarah.** "Rav Naḥman said that Rabba bar Avuh said: Sarah our mother was an *aylonit*" (שרה אמנו אילונית היתה). Gen. 11:30's extra "she had no child" means "not even a place for a child" (אפילו בית ולד אין לה).
- **The New Year.** "On Rosh ha-Shanah Sarah, Rachel and Hannah were remembered" (בראש השנה נפקדו שרה רחל וחנה). This is cited to set a three-year wait.
- **The rest of the page** is not for use: impotence from Rav Huna's long lectures; the presumption (*ḥazakah*) after circumcision deaths in a family, "whose blood is thin"; the woman whose husbands die.

**Design use.**
- **Sarah, faith's voice only.** "Sarah our mother was an *aylonit*, without even a womb, and God made her a woman who bore." Mark it as faith's voice in the loc key comment. It is aggadah about an intersex or underdeveloped body healed for fertility. It is **not** a male-to-female crossing, and the narrator never says it is.
- **Rabbinic rejoinder for event writers.** "Sarah was a woman before and after. Her womb was made, not her sex." This is Fonrobert's point (§17): the *aylonit* is a woman who cannot bear.
- **Rosh ha-Shanah** as the day God "remembers" barren women gives an optional second date for a minor prayer event outside Adar. It is not a second Turning: the design keeps one gate a year, in Adar.
- **Avoid** the circumcision-death and thin-blood material entirely. It sits close to the blood tropes in `jud_male_menses_myth.md`.

## 11. Rashi on Yevamot 64b — "a female organ was made for her"

**What it says.**
- On "hewn": "נעשה לו זכרות" ("a male organ was made for him").
- On "dug": "נעשה לה נקבות" ("a female organ was made for her"), comparing Isa. 37:25 "I dug [*karti*]".
- On "she had no child": the verse is superfluous after "barren", hence the extra teaching.
- On the three remembrances: God "remembers the barren to hear their prayer on Rosh ha-Shanah, and decrees pregnancy upon them".

**Design use.**
- The word *nekevut* ("femaleness, the female organ") is the strongest single word in the corpus for the sect's own voice: "As Rashi says of Sarah: a woman's body was made for her." This holds only in faith's voice.
- The narrator says only that Rashi reads Isaiah as the forming of Abraham's and Sarah's organs.
- The verb is passive, *na'aseh*, "was made", with no human agent. That supports the faith's "not by a human hand" (`tm_hipukh.0001.a.success`).
- **Licence.** Public domain, so it can be quoted in Hebrew in loc.

## 12. *Sefer ha-Ḥinukh* 291 — no castration "not a man and not a beast and not a bird"

**What it says.** *Sefer ha-Ḥinukh* is a 13th-century Barcelona code, traditionally attributed to an Aaron ha-Levi (*unverified* here).
- **The commandment.** "Not to castrate one of all the species: not a man and not a beast and not a bird" (לא אדם ולא בהמה ולא עוף), from Lev. 22:24.
- **The root of the commandment.** God made the world perfect and willed that creatures multiply. So one who destroys the organs of seed shows himself "as one who is disgusted with the work of the Creator" (כמי שהוא קץ במעשה הבורא) and wishes the ruin of His good world.
- **The laws.**
  - Even telling a gentile to castrate an Israelite's animal is forbidden. A sly hint is penalised: the animal must be sold to another Jew.
  - One who castrates the already castrated is liable.
  - Castrating a female is exempt.
  - A "cup of roots" given to sterilise is forbidden but not lashed.
  - Chilling a man in water or snow earns "lashes of rebellion".
  - A woman may drink a cup of roots herself, "since women are not commanded to be fruitful and multiply".
- **Women are bound too.** The commandment applies "to males and females". Women are forbidden "to castrate the males — though not themselves — with a cup of roots" (שאסור גם להם לסרס הזכרים, אבל לא עצמן בכוס של עקרין).

**Design use.**
- **A stronger potion wall than Maimonides.** Women of the ḥevrah may not give a member a sterilising draught, because in law she is male. This confirms the hard limit "No knife and no potion" from a Catalan code nearer Kalonymus than Maimonides. Cite it beside MT Issurei Bi'ah 16 (§7) in the README and in §13 of `faith_design.md`.
- **Reaction line against Dorleyites only.** "Who cuts despises the work of the Creator." Never use it against the sect, which does not cut.
- **The gentile clause** supports §7's design point. A Jewish seeker may not even hint to a Dorleyite that she wants the knife.

## 13. *Sefer ha-Ḥinukh* 542 — "a woman shall not wear a man's adornment"

**What it says.**
- **Title.** "שלא תלבש אשה עדי איש" ("that a woman not wear a man's adornment").
- **Scope.** Onkelos reads *keli gever* as a man's weapon. By Torah law, local men's dress such as a turban is also forbidden.
- **Two reasons.** The first: to keep the holy nation from *zimmah* (lewdness), since "if men's and women's clothes were the same, they would mix with each other constantly, and the land would be filled with lewdness". The second: idolaters did it. The author says he later found both reasons in Maimonides (*Sefer ha-Mitzvot*, negative 40; *Guide* 3:37).
- **Penalty.** Lashes for a woman.

**Design use.** Women dressing as men is out of scope, so this is reaction data only. Note two things for event writers:
- The word *'adi* ("adornment") again (cf. MT AZ 12:10 and Kalonymus; §5).
- The **mixing** rationale. A hostile rabbi's line may use the general form: "the law keeps men's and women's dress apart, lest the sexes mix". He must never use the specific form "to go among the women" (Rashi §15 and Nazir 59a), which `faith_design.md` §13 already bars.

## 14. *Sefer ha-Ḥinukh* 543 — "a man shall not wear a woman's garment"

**What it says.**
- **Grooming as well as dress** (Makkot 20b). Plucking white hairs from among the black, of the head or **of the beard**, or dyeing hair as women do, is lashable. So is even "one hair".
- **Tumtum and androginos.** They take "the stringencies of men and of women", and are not lashed if they transgress "because they are a doubt" (לפי שהן ספק).
- **Scope.** The prohibition applies to males "in every place and at all times".

**Design use.**
- The beard detail confirms the "smooth chin" texture proposed in §5. An elder or worthy may note it in `tm_hipukh.0003`: "plucking even one hair of the beard is a woman's adornment."
- **The doubt clause** is a possible **sect** argument in a learned dispute: "Where the law is in doubt it does not lash." It is weak, since she is not a *tumtum*. The worthies' answer: "You are no doubt. You came forth male." That is already the 0040 line.

## 15. Rashi on Deut. 22:5

**What it says.**
- (a) A woman in a man's gear "to go among the men, which can only be for adultery".
- (b) A man in a woman's garment "to go and sit among the women". The other reading: he should not remove pubic or armpit hair (Nazir 59a).
- (c) "לא אסרה תורה אלא לבוש המביא לידי תועבה" ("the Torah forbade only dress that leads to abomination").

**Design use.**
- **(c) is the sect's best rabbinic text.** It is intent-based, like Isserles' "for mere merriment" (§8), and it is Rashi, the commentator every Provençal Jew read. In faith's voice: "Rashi says the Torah forbade only the dress that leads to abomination. Mine leads to the lamp and the dough." It suits `tm_hipukh.0003.a` (text against text).
- **(b) is the hard-limit text.** Never voice "to go and sit among the women" (`faith_design.md` §13, "never voice b. Nazir 59a's 'slip in among women' rationale"). An opponent may use the **hair** reading of (b), which matches §14.
- **Licence.** Public domain (Rosenbaum–Silbermann), so the Hebrew can go into loc.

## 16. *Bereshit Rabbah* 60:16 — Sarah's lamp

**What it says.** On Gen. 24:67, "Isaac brought her into the tent, Sarah his mother", the midrash gives four signs. While Sarah lived:
- a cloud was bound over her tent's door;
- the doors stood wide open;
- a blessing lay on the dough;
- "a lamp burned from Sabbath eve to Sabbath eve" (נר דולק מלילי שבת ועד לילי שבת).

When she died each ceased, and when Rebecca came each returned. When Isaac saw her "doing as his mother did, separating her *ḥallah* in purity", he brought her into the tent. R. Yudan then draws a rule about marrying off one's children first.

**Design use.**
- **The tenet's own logic.** Rebecca is recognised as Sarah's heir **by what she keeps**: the lamp, the dough, purity. `tenet_tm_three_lamps_desc` already says "a woman is made by what she keeps, week after week". The midrash is a precedent for that sentence, in the faith's voice.
- **Suggested addition** to `tm_hipukh.0020.desc`: "Sarah's lamp burned from one Sabbath to the next, and when Rebecca kept the lamp and the dough, Isaac knew her for his mother's heir."
- **Name note.** Rebecca/Rivkah is not in the scroll list. She could join it as the woman known by her keeping.
- **Wording.** "In purity" (בטהרה) refers to the dough. Do not gloss it as menstrual purity in loc (`jud_male_menses_myth.md`).

## 17. Fonrobert, "Gender Identity in Halakhic Discourse" (JWA 2009)

**What it says**, by section.
- **"Gender Duality."** Rabbinic law sets up "a functional gender duality, anchored in the need of reproduction". Its essentialism enters later, with Maimonides' Aristotelianism (and Ibn Ezra on Deut. 22:5).
- **"The Saris and the Ailonit."**
  - Bodily signs (beard, breasts, voice) are read "to infer a potential incapacity to reproduce", not character.
  - "The halakhic literature nowhere suggests that the saris or the ailonit are in fact not 'really' man or woman."
  - *Saris ḥammah* versus *saris adam* (Mishnah Yevamot 8:5–6).
- **"The Androgynos and the Tumtum."**
  - The *tumtum* is "a not-yet sexed person" whose organs may appear "or be uncovered surgically" (Yevamot 83b).
  - The *androginos* must dress as a man, and the male organ "has greater signifying force". Rashi on Bekhorot 41a calls "the place of femininity" "like a blemish".
  - **There is no rabbinic physiognomy** and no rabbinic *kinaidos*. The body's signs are "entirely disconnected from the question of character and virtue".
  - A 20th-century Israeli press line: "one who is born male remains so his entire life".
- **"Niddah and the Mikveh."** Menstrual law shapes women's identity "as wife and mother". Mishnah Niddah 5:3 lets a baby girl be a *niddah* from her first day.
- **"Gender and Talmud Torah."**
  - Women are excluded from study. Maimonides: women "transform the words of Torah into words of vanity" (Talmud Torah 1:13).
  - The rabbinic idiom calls the wife "the house" (Mishnah Yoma 1:1).

**Design use.**
- **The rabbinic body is not the Christian body.** No physiognomy, no *kinaidos*. This is the sharpest contrast with the Christian melancholic Jew of `jud_male_menses_myth.md`. Rabbinic opponents in events should argue reproduction and law ("what came forth male", "a man maimed"), never character or effeminacy. That matches the built 0040 lines.
- **The *tumtum* "uncovered surgically"** (Yev. 83b, unread). An opponent could answer the sect's Abraham-and-Sarah *tumtumin* line with "a *tumtum* is opened and found to be what he was". *Unverified* until 83b is read.
- **"The house" = the wife.** This grounds `tm_hipukh.0003.c` ("I am the lady of the house"). In rabbinic idiom, claiming the house is claiming to be the wife (Yoma 1:1). Keep the line.
- **Maimonides on women's learning** is a ready reaction line against the elder's learning, which the Turning's odds depend on: "Rambam says women turn the words of Torah into vanity." The sect's answer could be Ben Azzai (Mishnah Sotah 3:4), whom Fonrobert also cites.
- **Do not use** the Israeli press quote. It is modern. Medieval loc must use the medieval form ("what came forth male is male").
