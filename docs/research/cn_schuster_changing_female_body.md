# Schuster, "Changing the Female Body" (1981), design reading

**Source (verified from the PDF's title page and contents page, 2026-10-18).** Nancy Schuster (later Schuster Barnes). "Changing the Female Body: Wise Women and the Bodhisattva Career in Some Mahāratnakūṭasūtras." *Journal of the International Association of Buddhist Studies* 4.1 (1981): 24–69. Contents page: "by Nancy Schuster … 24". The notes end on p. 69. OA: https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/jiabs/article/view/8533 (local copy "8533-Article Text-8341-1-10-20110301.pdf").
- **Read:** pp. 24–56 (the whole argument) in full.
- **Notes:** nn. 1–16, 29, 47 and 58 read; the rest skimmed.
- **Sutras covered:**
  - four from Bodhiruci's Tang *Mahāratnakūṭa* compilation (706–713): *Sumati* (T 11.310(30)); *Gaṅgottarā* (310(31)); *Vimaladattā*, the "Pure Gift Sutra" (310(33)); *Viśuddhaśraddhā*, "Pure Faith's Question" (310(40));
  - compared with the Lotus, the *Vimalakīrti*, and the *Śrīmālā*.

## 1. The argument

- **The theme.** "the female protagonist causes her own body to change abruptly from female to male." Schuster calls it "most disconcerting" to a modern reader (p. 27).
- **Why maleness is at stake: the Great Man's body.** The five ranks barred to women include buddha and *cakravartin*. Both "conform to a specific physical type, the Great Man", one of whose 32 marks is "having the penis covered with a sheath" (p. 27). Mahāyāna answers that the marks, and "maleness, femaleness, and the rest", are "mere designations" (p. 28).
- **The term.** *Zhuan nü shen* 轉女身 ("change the female body") is used in all the Chinese versions of the *Sumati*, *Pure Gift*, *Vimalakīrti* and *Pure Faith*. **The three Chinese Lotus translations instead say "change into a man"**: *bianwei nanzi* or *biancheng nanzi* (n. 9, p. 58). The Sanskrit Lotus: "female organ" disappears and "male organ" appears (n. 9). The *Da zhidu lun* (*Ta-chih-tu-lun*, the great Perfection-of-Wisdom treatise) names only four of the five ranks barred to women, leaving out "buddha" on purpose (n. 10).
- **Sumati, aged 8** (pp. 29–31). She defeats Maudgalyāyana with two acts of truth. When Mañjuśrī asks "Why have you not changed your female body?", she answers that "dharmas are neither male nor female." She changes anyway, "to remove your doubts", into a young novice monk and then a 30-year-old monk. Her vow for her future buddha-land: no "women's demeanor" (p. 31).
- **Pure Gift (Vimaladattā), King Prasenajit's daughter, aged 12** (pp. 31–35). She silences eight disciples and eight bodhisattvas, Mañjuśrī included, in debate. Maudgalyāyana says one cannot attain enlightenment "with a woman's body". By an act of truth she changes into "a boy of 8". He then asks why she had not changed before, and she gives the **key retort**: "Why haven't you changed your male body?" She follows it with "Neither with a female body nor with a male body is true enlightenment attained" (p. 35, T 12.338: 96c).
- **Pure Faith** (pp. 36–37). She herself asks how to change her female body. Schuster reads this as a sign that she "has obviously not attained understanding", since she still "distinguishes between female and male". She and her 500 companions are promised a male rebirth **after death**, not an instant change (p. 37, p. 47).
- **Gaṅgottarā** (pp. 37–39) and **Queen Śrīmālā** (pp. 47–49). These are wise women for whom "a change of sex is … as irrelevant" (p. 49). Neither is ever asked to change.
- **The *Vimalakīrti* goddess** (pp. 41–42). This is the one case in the corpus of a male-to-female change. Śāriputra asks the goddess why she does not change her female body. She replies that she has sought femaleness for twelve years and not found it. Then she "exchange[s] forms with Sariputra, so that Sariputra appears in the form of the goddess". When asked why he doesn't change his female form, he "doesn't even know how he acquired this female body" (p. 42). She changes them back. The point: "no dharmas are either female or male."
- **The Lotus is the weakest version** (pp. 42–45). The dragon girl's "five ranks" scene "presents the basic argument clearly and boldly", against bodies "unfit" for the highest destinies (p. 44). But because emptiness is not its central teaching, "Change of sex does seem to be necessary, according to the Lotus, if a woman is to take the final step to Buddhahood" (p. 44). Schuster suggests that the Lotus's magic elements may have been added later, under the influence of the other sutras (p. 45).
- **Magic and the body made by mind** (pp. 49–52). There are two supernormal powers:
  - *vikurvaṇā-ṛddhi*, the power to take another form: one meditates, contemplates oneself in the new form, and resolves "May I be such and such a boy" (p. 50);
  - *manomayā-ṛddhi*, making a body by mind: one makes "the body … a hollow", contemplates "another body within one's own", and draws it out "as a reed from its sheath" (pp. 50–51).

  According to the *Da zhidu lun*, such transformation bodies "are true and not false, for no dharma has a fixed characteristic" (p. 51).
- **The act of truth (*satyakriyā*)** (pp. 52–54). It is "a formal declaration of fact, accompanied by a command" (Burlingame, quoted p. 53). In older Indian texts a woman's act of truth affirmed her duty "as devoted wife, or as successful prostitute". In these sutras it is grounded in "her true aspiration to the attainment of Buddhahood" (p. 53). It is performed in front of an assembly so that the doubters stop doubting (p. 54).
- **Conclusion** (pp. 54–56). Religious life is "a third alternative: it is a new birth into a new kind of creative living" (p. 55). The male body symbolises leaving worldly life, and "Despite the maleness of these new bodies", it signifies transcendence of sex (p. 55). The women act from compassion: "By their remarkable actions they resolve a man's doubts" (p. 56).
- **Tang context** (pp. 25–26). Bodhiruci came at Empress Wu's invitation (693). He worked on the *Ratnamegha* (*Baoyu jing*), which was interpolated to support her claim to be a bodhisattva and *cakravartin* (n. 6, p. 57). He compiled the *Mahāratnakūṭa* 706–713, under her sons. Schuster (p. 26): "at a period … when the prominence of women was a timely concern, Bodhiruci helped make available … a number of Mahayana scriptures which argue for the spiritual and intellectual equality of women."

**Critical reading for the mod.**
- Schuster's 1981 liberal-feminist frame is "sex is empty, so the change doesn't matter". It is close to the "transcend gender" move that Serano and Bhatt attack, and her "third alternative" is close to the third-sexing Bhatt warns against. Do not import that frame.
- What *is* usable is the material, not the frame:
  - Pure Gift's retort;
  - the goddess putting Śāriputra in a female body;
  - the body made by the mind and drawn out "as a reed from its sheath";
  - the act of truth as a public declaration that compels the world.

## 3. In-world lines (faith voice)

- "Asked why she had not changed her body, the princess asked the monk why he had not changed his."
- "The goddess lent Śāriputra a woman's form for one breath, and he could not say how he came by it. We can say how we came by ours."
- "Your sutras draw a body out of the body like a reed from its sheath, and call it a trick of the powers."
- (Buddhist, gently hostile) "Keep the precepts, sister, and the next life will make you a man."
