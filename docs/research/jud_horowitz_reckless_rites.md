## 1. The argument in brief

- **Purim as licence, including licensed aggression.** Horowitz argues that Purim, like Carnival, joined feasting and inversion to "ritualized aggression" (p. 269). Jewish historians from Güdemann to Abrahams, Gaster and Roth played this down in apology. They wrote under pressure from antisemitic scholars (Lagarde, Gougenot des Mousseaux, Frazer's speculations), who used the same evidence for ritual-murder and blasphemy charges (pp. 248–264).
- **Two strands he keeps apart.** (a) What medieval Jews did on Purim: drink, mask, cross-dress, stage mock trials, and sometimes mock Christian symbols. (b) What Christians imagined and alleged they did, from the edict of 408 to 20th-century fascist lists of "ritual murders" (ch. 8). The book is a polemic *against apologetics*. For the mod, the second strand is the warning.
- **Tone.** His thesis that "the rite to be reckless" includes deadly risk (p. 264) is his own reading. The mod should not adopt it in any voice.

**What this means.**
- **Manosque 1306 is the earliest dated Purim cross-dressing the book records, and it is Provençal.** It comes 16 years before *Even Boḥan*. It answers Rosen's "no evidence of transvestism" (R2003 p. 151), at least for Purim.
- **It is not the Revel.** At Manosque the woman's dress was put on a man in a mock punishment of adultery. It mocked a woman as a sinner in a public shaming parade. It was not young men dressing up for fun. Horowitz also reads the flogging as a veiled parody of Christ's Passion (p. 268). **Do not use the Manosque case in loc or in any faith's voice.** It joins shaming, sexual sin and an anti-Christian reading that is the core of the accusation literature (§2.3).
- **No source in the book has a rabbi permitting Purim cross-dressing before the 16th century.** Horowitz calls it "technically an infringement" (p. 252). The permission is Judah Minz (d. 1508) and Isserles, as `comparanda_reading_list.md` finding 4 already says. Minz's responsum falls after the game's 1453 end date (its exact year is *unverified*).

### 2.2 Medieval Provençal and Catalan Purim, otherwise

- **Drinking.** The Talmud's "until he cannot tell cursed Haman from blessed Mordecai" (Megillah 7b). Horowitz notes a 12th-c. testimony by R. Abraham b. Isaac of Narbonne (p. 264 n. 56). The content is not given here, so it is *unverified* beyond its link to drinking. It is usable for Narbonne, a holy site.
- **Purim charges brought before Christian authorities in the sect's region:**
  - Villafranca near Barcelona, 1291;
  - two Jews visiting Marseille, near the bishop's house, around 1300, in a responsum of Solomon ibn Adret;
  - Lunel, 1319;
  - Hyères, 1343 (all p. 267).

  In each case Purim "amusements" were taken as insults to Christianity. What the Jews did is not described.
- **Solomon ha-Levi of Burgos, 1389.** His Purim letter remembers wine, loosened tongues and "a wild scene of mingled love and rage" (p. 269, Abrahams's translation). This is Castile, late in the period.
- **Rome.** From the 1460s Jews were forced to run in the Carnival races (p. 270). This is Christian violence against Jews, outside Provence and late.

### 2.3 What the mod must avoid (the Christian Purim accusation)

These are the accusation tropes Horowitz traces. **None may appear in loc, in a faith's voice, in a Christian reaction event, or in the narrator, even as "what Christians said".**

1. **Haman hanged or burned on a cross-shaped gallows.** This is the Theodosian law of 408, quoted at p. 215: Jews were not to burn "a form made to resemble the saint cross in contempt of the Christian faith". Its Byzantine sequel is a pre-baptismal oath, dated between the 8th and early 11th centuries, in which Jewish converts cursed those who keep "the festival of the so-called Mordecai ... nailing Haman to wood" with the cross (pp. 158–159). **This oath falls in the Byzantine world of 867.** It is exactly the kind of "period-accurate" line a Byzantine reaction event might reach for. It is forbidden: the Dorleyaian and Orthodox voices must not use it.
2. **A Christian child killed in Purim sport.** Inmestar in Syria, c. 415, from Socrates (pp. 214–215). Antisemitic writers put it first on their lists of "ritual murders", from Burton (1870s) to the fascist Leese (pp. 222, 228, skimmed).
3. **A Christian executed or crucified by Jews on Purim.** Bray or Brie, 1191/2 (pp. 258–264). Chazan treats the Latin accounts as modelled on the Passion (p. 263).
4. **Jars smashed with a curse on Christendom.** First in 13th-c. Latin sources; Busti, 1488 (pp. 272–273).
5. **Purim as hatred of Christ in festive form.** Gregorovius's "as if he were Christ" (p. 215), Lagarde's "hate-preaching gluttony" (p. 250), and Frazer's "real man in the character of Haman" (p. 258).
6. **Castration of a Jew as a Carnival punishment.** Avignon in the late 14th c.; Mantua in 1569 (p. 268). This one is local to the sect and doubly forbidden: Jewish body plus castration. **Never use it.**

**Corollary for the Revel and the feast.** Keep Haman out of any effigy, gallows, bonfire or burning. The mod's existing loc already does this (`tm_hipukh.*` mentions Haman nowhere). Keep it that way. A Haman effigy is attested Jewish practice (Babylonian, Geonic; p. 257; Davidson p. 21 n. 33), but in a game where Christian courts react, it reads as the accusation. Safe Purim business: the scroll, the noise at Haman's *name* in synagogue, wine, the feast, gifts to the poor, games, and the dress.

**Avoid the opposite fault too.** Horowitz's other target is apologetics (Güdemann, Abrahams, Gaster), which made medieval Jews sober and polite. The Revel's drink and riot are not a trope. They are Megillah 7b and *Even Boḥan* p. 29. Keep the noise, and keep it inside the Jewish street.

## 4. Design implications

- **Elders event (`tm_hipukh.0003`).** Horowitz shows that in the sect's own region Purim antics were brought before Christian courts: Lunel 1319, Hyères 1343, Manosque 1306 (p. 267). A worthy can give that as his reason for caution, with no gentile shown acting: "Purim antics have gone before the bailiff before now. Put it away." This is the kahal protecting itself, and keeps within the hard rule that gentiles are not shown as tormentors inside the rite. It is optional.
- **Narbonne flavour.** A 12th-c. Narbonne rabbi on Purim drinking (p. 264 n. 56; *unverified* content) can ground a Narbonne holy-site line about the feast, if ever wanted.
- **Narrator.** Do not call Purim "the Jewish carnival". The phrase is Morosini's and Güdemann's, but in the reception history it carries Lagarde's and Gregorovius's charge (pp. 248–250). "The fourteenth of Adar" or "Purim" suffices.
- **Reaction lines.** A Christian or Dorleyaian character may object to the sect's doctrine (Dinah, the Turning). They may not mention Purim mockery, Haman on a cross, or anything from §2.3.
- **README bibliography.** Add Horowitz 2006 with a one-line note: "used for what the mod avoids: the Christian Purim accusations".
