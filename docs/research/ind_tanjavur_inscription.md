## 1. The inscriptions in brief

### No. 66: the four hundred temple women (Rājarāja I, 29th regnal year, c. 1014)

- **The order.** Rājarāja I had appointed stipendiaries (*nivandakkarar*) and "transferred (a number of) temple women from (other) temple establishments of the Chola country" to the new Rājarājeśvara temple (¶1). Each received shares (*pangu*) as allowance.
- **The share.** One share is the produce of one *veli* of land, fixed at "one hundred kalam of paddy", measured by the Āḍavallāṉ measure (¶1). Hultzsch gives a veli as 26,755 m² (intro).
- **Succession.** If a shareholder died or emigrated, "the nearest relations of such persons were to receive that allowance ... and to do the work"; if the relations were not qualified, they chose a qualified person, who did the work while they received the allowance; with no relations, the other incumbents chose a successor (¶1). The inscription does **not** say mother to daughter; it says "nearest relations".
- **The women.** ¶¶3–402 list exactly 400 women. Each entry gives a name, the temple or village she came from, her house, and "one share". Every woman gets one share.
- **The term.** Hultzsch's "temple women" renders *taḷiccēri peṇṭukaḷ* (his "thalisaeri pendukkal"), literally "women (who reside) in the streets near the temple" (n. 9). Each name is glossed "a girl"; n. 11 discusses *nakkan* at that point, so the Tamil prefix rendered "girl" is probably *nakkaṉ* (Hultzsch's etymology from Sanskrit *nagna* is doubtful; check against Saroja, item 3).
- **The streets.** The women are housed by address:
  - temple street on the south, southern row: houses 1–92 (¶¶3–94);
  - same street, northern row: houses 1–92 (¶¶95–186);
  - temple street on the north, southern row: houses 1–95 (¶¶187–281);
  - same street, northern row: houses 1–96 (¶¶282–377);
  - a fifth row, its name lost: houses 1–25 (¶¶378–402).
- **Where they came from.** Mostly Śaiva temples of the Kaveri delta (Tiruvārūr, Tiruvaiyāṟu, Nāgapaṭṭinam, Paḻaiyāṟu, Niyamam, Kaḍambūr). Some came from Vaiṣṇava *viṇṇagar* temples (Avaninārāyaṇa-viṇṇagar at Ambar, Śrītari-viṇṇagar at Ārapuram, Śrīpūdi-viṇṇagar at Pambuṇi) (intro; ¶¶32, 67, 107, 133).
- **The men (¶¶403–510)** get shares by office: dancing-masters (*nṛttam*) 2 shares each, singers in Sanskrit (*āriyam*) and Tamil, drummers, lute-players, a conch-blower, accountants and their clerks, parasol-bearers, lamp-lighters, potters, washermen, barbers, astrologers, tailors, carpenters, a brazier, a jewel-stitcher, a goldsmith. Their names carry royal titles (*Mummaḍi-Cōḻa-*, *Rājarāja-*).
- **Supervision.** "For superintending the temple women and the female musicians (Gandharvi)", two men receive two shares each (¶¶470–471). The women are a household under male overseers.
- **The word "Araiyan".** Several male musicians are "Araiyan" (¶¶403, 406, 408, 413–414, 427–428). Here it is a personal title ("chief"), not the *araiyar* hymn-performers of Śrīvaiṣṇava temples. Do not conflate.

### No. 67: the temple drama (Rājendradeva [II], 4th and 6th regnal years)

- After a victory over the Western Cālukya Āhavamalla at Koppam, the king grants "(one) tuni of paddy per day" (one share of 120 kalam a year) to the actor Tiruvālaṉ Tirumudukuṉṟaṉ, alias Vijaya-Rājendra-ācāryaṉ, and his troop, for acting the *Rājarājeśvara-nāṭaka* at the Vaikāci festival (¶¶2–3). The order was engraved "as long as the moon and the sun endure" (¶3).
- No women are named.

### No. 68: the paddy measurer (Vikrama-Cōḻa, 4th regnal year, c. 1115)

- A royal praise text, then a grant to the temple's paddy measurer, passed from a dead father to his son "and to his family" (¶¶2–3).
- Two queens are named: **Mukkokkiranadi**, compared to Umā, and **Tiyagapadagai** (Hultzsch: "i.e., Tyagapataka"), "the ornament of women", compared to Gaṅgā (¶1).

## 2. What matters for the design

1. **The line is real and inscribed:** 400 named women, each with a house on a named temple street and one share worth 100 kalam of paddy a year, transferable to kin who "do the work" (No. 66 ¶1). This is the strongest primary support for the Garland's "settled on the street" line.
2. **"Daughter of God" is not in this inscription.** The terms here are "temple women" (*taḷiccēri peṇṭukaḷ*, "street women of the temple", n. 9) and "girl" (*nakkaṉ*?). *Tēvaṉār makaḷ*, "daughter of God", comes from other inscriptions via Orr. Cite Orr for the title; cite No. 66 for the shares and the streets.
3. **Women from Srirangam.** Two women are named "Arangam", i.e. Srirangam (¶¶266, 387; n. 42). Others are named after Tiruvēṅkaṭam (¶175), Tirukkurukūr (Āḻvārtirunagari, ¶255 "Sikurugur") and Tiruvāli (¶50 "Ali") (intro). This ties the Garland's seat and holy sites to the four hundred.
4. **Royal and kingly names on women.** Women bear Rājarāja's own names and titles: Rājarāja (¶65), Rājarāji (¶268), Rājakesari (¶10), Arumoḻi (¶¶236, 369); and Kundavai (¶215, his sister's name), Ariñji (¶400, his grandfather's), Kannaradevi (¶244). These are honour-names given by a court, not evidence of anything about gender.
5. **Transfer as royal policy.** A king moves women from many temples to his own new temple and gives each a house and a share. This is the "transfer daughters to a new temple" diplomacy action in the candidate, and it is literal.
6. **Men oversee the women** (¶¶470–471), and Hultzsch calls them "evidently dancing-girls" (intro). The inscription says nothing of dancing for the women, of marriage or of sex. The "dancing-girls" gloss is Hultzsch's, the colonial reading Orr rejects.

## 3. Corrections to the candidate

- **"Daughters of God" is not attested in No. 66.** The reading list's "it gives a *line*: settled shares, a mother's inheritance, and the title 'daughter of God'" mixes sources: shares are No. 66; maternal inheritance and the title are from Orr (via Daud Ali's review). Keep the citations separate.
- **Succession is to "nearest relations", not to daughters,** and the heir must "do the work" or appoint someone qualified (¶1). The "Take as Daughter" mechanic is a design choice built on Orr's mother-to-daughter wealth, not on this record.
- **Exact count: 400** (¶¶3–402), each with one share. Saroja's reported "402" may count differently.
- **Streets:** the women live on two temple streets (south and north), in four numbered rows of 92–96 houses, plus a fifth row of 25 (¶¶3–402). The reading list's "temple streets (*taḷiccēri*)" is right in substance; the Tamil word in Hultzsch is the women's title, glossed by the streets.
- **Royal names:** confirmed (Kundavai ¶215, Ariñji ¶400, Kannaradevi ¶244), plus Rājarāja, Rājarāji, Rājakesari and Arumoḻi, which the reading list omits.

## 4. Design implications

- **Line mechanic, "settled on the street":** grant a holding styled as "one share, the produce of one veli" (100 kalam a year) and a house on the temple street. On her death the share passes to her nearest kin "to do the work", or to a successor she or the other incumbents choose. "Take as Daughter" is how a Garland woman names that successor.
- **Diplomacy action, "Transfer to the New Temple":** a ruler of any Hindu faith who builds a great temple can invite Garland daughters from other temples, granting houses and shares (prestige and piety for him; a House for them). Flavour from ¶1: "transferred ... from (other) temple establishments".
- **Holy-site text, c_cholamandalam:** "Here the king who built the great temple gave four hundred women each a house and a share, and engraved their names on stone." Narrator voice; true to the record.
- **Faith's voice:** the Garland may call these women its first daughters and its title "daughter of God"; the narrator may not.
- **Era flavour:** No. 67's temple drama on the founding (Vaikāci festival) can be a festival activity; No. 68's queens Tiyagapadagai and Mukkokkiranadi give 12th-c. Chola queen names (as Hultzsch gives them).
- **Do not:** call them dancing-girls; sexualise the street; make any of the 400 trans; present the inscription's women as Garland members. Their descendants are living communities.

**Naming notes for the mod.**
- Many women are named after places (Arangam, Tiruvaiyaru, Venkadu). A Garland daughter taking a shrine as her name has direct precedent here.
- Several names are men's titles or kings' names (Rajaraja, Paranderuman, Porkumaran, Somakon, Arikulakesari). Use them as the record gives them; do not read them as evidence of gender crossing.
- Prefer fully legible names (no *) for the culture name list; check starred names against the Tamil text or Orr before use.
- For CK3 name lists, the forms above can be used as given, or with diacritics restored from a Tamil source (e.g. Arumoḻi, Kuntavai, Umai).
