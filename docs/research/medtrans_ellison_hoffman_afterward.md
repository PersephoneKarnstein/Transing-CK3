## 1. The argument in brief

The article is the issue's coda. It is not about the Middle Ages but about **medievalism**: how later people live with medieval forms. Its subjects are two 20th-century trans women of colour, Marsha P. Johnson and Sylvia Rivera, founders of STAR.

- **Medievalism can be lived, not only depicted.** The authors present "a hagiography of sorts" (p. 268). They argue the two women's lives show "medievalism can be something not just represented but embodied—a powerful form of resistance" (p. 268).
- **STAR House as a mutual-aid household.** The first House was a trailer truck. Its ground rule was that "nobody had to go out and hustle her body," but those who did "had to kick back a percentage" (p. 274, quoting Duberman). The House fed and minded its neighbours' children: "Everyone in the neighborhood loved STAR House" (p. 274).
- **Johnson as living saint.** She was generous to the last blouse. A flower seller said she slept under his tables because "She's holy" (p. 275). She prayed at Catholic, Greek Orthodox and Baptist churches and a synagogue to "cover all angles" (p. 276), and said she was married to Jesus.
- **Kempe comparison, and ableism.** Like Margery Kempe, Johnson met orthodoxy on her own devotional terms and was treated as mad. For Johnson that ableism was "also shaped by transmisogynoir" (p. 279). A historian read her flower crowns and street-found jewellery as evidence of psychosis. The authors read the same clothes as "a beautiful practice of resilience in the midst of poverty" (p. 279).
- **Rivera's liturgy.** The House prayed to its own saints before work: Barbara, Michael, the Virgin of Cobre, and Martha, "the saint of transformation." To Martha: "Please don't let them see through the mask. Let us pass as women and save us from harm" (p. 280). They "kept the sword of St. Barbara at the front door and the sword of St. Michael at the back door" (p. 281). The authors read this as a medieval-style reworking of saints' lives, inside Santería's syncretism.
- **Exclusion by the movement's respectable wing.** Rivera was booed at the 1973 rally. A lesbian-feminist speaker then called her a man whose womanhood was "impersonation" (p. 285). That night Rivera attempted suicide, and Johnson came home in time.
- **Rivera's own position on the body.** She was "a transgender woman with no interest in getting a sex change" (p. 285).
- **Johnson's death.** Police ruled her 1992 death a suicide. Rivera refused the ruling: they had promised to "cross the river Jordan together" (p. 286). The case was reopened in 2012.
- **The medieval imaginary.** White supremacists at Charlottesville used medieval iconography (pp. 289-290). Popular "medieval" means violence beyond the pale. The authors call for "recusant medievalisms—medievalisms of embodied alterity and resistance" (p. 294).

## 2. What it changes in the design

**Relevance overall:** low for period mechanics, since this is a 20th-century subject. It is moderate for the faiths' community life and for how the mod handles its own medievalism. Everything below is structural homage. **No modern person should appear by name** (see cautions).

### Cautions for how the mod presents history

- **No real modern people in the mod**, named or thinly veiled. Johnson's death is an unsolved killing, and both women's legacies are still contested in their communities. The design's in-group easter eggs (§9) must not extend to them. The homage here is structural (the House rule, the two swords, the pact), never a character called Marsha or Sylvia.
- **Santería's saints belong to a living, colonised Afro-Caribbean religion.** Do not lift its orisha correspondences into a CK3 faith. Take only the *method*: oppressed women reworking a dominant church's saints to protect themselves. That method has period equivalents in Byzantium (local saints, apocryphal *Lives*).
- **Hagiography is the community's act.** The authors speak of a "hagiography of sorts" only because the community called Johnson a saint. When the mod's faiths venerate their dead, the House or community should do the veneration, not the mod's narrator.
