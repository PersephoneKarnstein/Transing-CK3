# Five Phases treatises (*Wuxing zhi* 五行志) and *Taiping guangji* 367: sex change, "human prodigies" and cross-dressing, design reading

## Corrections to the brief and the reading list

1. **The castration reading is not Jing Fang's own, and it does not cover palace women.** *Han shu* 27C1 quotes Jing Fang's *Yi zhuan*: 丈夫化為女子，茲謂陰勝，厥咎亡, "a man turning into a woman: this is called yin conquering; the blame is ruin". Only after that does it add an anonymous alternative, introduced by 一曰 ("one opinion says"): 男化為女，宮刑濫也；女化為男，婦政行也, "a man becoming a woman means castration is used to excess; a woman becoming a man means women govern". So castration goes with male-to-female change, and women's rule goes with female-to-male change. Loc and notes should say "an old commentary", not "Jing Fang", for the castration line.
2. **The *Taiping guangji* 人妖 rubric has 17 entries, not 16.** Four of them cross gender (Lou Cheng, Old Mother Meng, Huang Chonggu, White-Necked Crow), and all four are women who lived as men. Only two carry an explicit 人妖 verdict: Lou Cheng and White-Necked Crow.
3. **No record in any treatise read shows a man in women's dress inside 867–1453.** The only male case is He Yan (3rd century; *Jin shu* 27). The Tang and Song 服妖 sections read women's dress, eunuch fashions and Daoist robes as prodigies (rows 17–21, 24).
4. **Xu Deng was not executed.** See the correction in `cn_cheng_dissertation.md`.

## 2. What the omen-readers thought (synthesis)

- **Whose fault? Yin overcoming yang, and through that the ruler.** The theory (*Han shu* 27C1) files all human prodigies under 皇之不極 ("the sovereign fails to hold the centre"), and so 下人伐上之痾, "the sickness of inferiors attacking superiors". A sex change is a sign *about* the court, not a crime of the person. As Cheng (p. 201) puts it, such events are "signs (but not the cause)" of disorder.
- **Three readings of a man becoming a woman, each blaming someone different.** (a) Jing Fang: 陰勝，厥咎亡, yin conquers and the dynasty falls: the ruler's fault. (b) The anonymous gloss: 宮刑濫也, castration overused: the fault of the ruler's penal practice and, by implication, the eunuch establishment. (c) Chen Feng (6–3 BCE): 將亡繼嗣, the heir line will fail: a succession crisis.
- **Women who act as men are read as yin in excess and as foreign disorder.** "Northern barbarians disorder China and women play the hero" (row 23, 947). Lou Cheng: 陰為陽，事不可.
- **What the court did.** Usually nothing beyond recording it. A local official reports (太守以聞 in Han; 令執之以聞 in 681); the historian adds a reading after the event. Responses to the *person*: executed when tied to rebellion, espionage or "misleading the masses" (rows 4, 12, 22, 23); sent home and made to dress as a woman (11); admired and allowed to retire (21); **ordained by special edict** (26). The two in-window female-to-male children (row 20 and Zhou Shining in row 7) are told only in terms of their bodies.
- **Era drift.** *Xin Tang shu* (1060) still quotes Jing Fang. *Song shi* records the 1124 cases with no reading at all, and calls triple births 人民蕃息之驗, "proof that the people flourish". *Yuan shi* records the 1335 beard bare. *Ming shi* stops recording triplets as too common. This matches Lan Xing (reading list item 37): omenology lost its force after the 11th century. Its weight returns in hindsight: the 1124 records stand three years before Kaifeng fell in 1127, and Cheng's 14th-century reader cites 國家將亡，必有妖孽 ("before a state falls there must be prodigies").
- **Was anyone accepted?** Row 26 is the only person a court took in: a woman who grew a beard was kept a woman and placed in the Daoist clergy, outside a household and inside a category. She was not a trans woman, and the narrator must not call her a precedent. In the faith's own voice she is exactly the precedent the Way would cite: "the court had a body it could not name, and it named her a nüguan." Context, not from the treatise: Huizong's court (1100–1125) was at its most Daoist in these years.
- **Male pregnancy in the same year.** Row 25, the fruit-seller "pregnant and gave birth" and fled, is the period's nearest image to the Jade Embryo (an adept gestating her own body). It is recorded as a wonder, without blame.

## 3. "A Prodigy in the Prefecture": suggested event structure

**Trigger.** Yearly pulse on a Chinese-culture county holder (or the holder's liege) with a Yutai character in the county who has completed the month-10 birth, or a random commoner case with low weight. The opener uses the treatise formula: "In X county a man has become a woman" (某縣有男子化為女子). The reporting official is the county's steward-type courtier or vassal.

**Opening options (the holder).**
1. **Report it up the chain** (鄉→縣→州→朝, Cheng p. 193). Opinion gain with the liege and with scholar-official vassals; prestige. Starts a hidden 6–12-month timer for the court's reading.
2. **Hold the report** (久之未報 turned around: the prefect sits on it). Small intrigue-scaled chance per year that a secret inspector finds it (Hong Qi[wei]'s clerk, Cheng p. 194): "Concealed a Prodigy" modifier, opinion loss with the liege, and a hook for the palace censor's party.
3. **Take her into the Way** (row 26: 特詔度爲女道士). Costs gold (an ordination certificate). She becomes a nüguan courtier or joins a Sisters' House. Yutai piety and county fervour; opinion loss with Confucian vassals. If the holder is the emperor, use the exact edict form: "By special edict, ordain her a female Daoist."
4. **Send her past the gate** (the prefect Xin Zhongfu, Cheng p. 192: 恐其妖，遣出城). No court risk. She wanders the temples and can arrive at a Sisters' House through the arrival chain, where the faith remembers who turned her out.

**The court's reading (follow-up after option 1).** Weighted by era and by stage-2 faction strength:
- *Jing Fang* (more likely if the realm is at war or in a succession crisis): "Yin conquers; the blame is ruin." The emperor gains stress; the palace faction calls for the Way to be suppressed. Opens the month-3 censor's register event: "Enter her among the eunuchs, and the omen is only a punishment misapplied."
- *The castration gloss* (more likely when scholar-officials lead): "Castration is used to excess." The palace faction loses influence; option to limit eunuch intake. Rectified Names answers it: "You read our birth as your knife. That is the confusion we were born to correct."
- *Chen Feng* (more likely when the ruler has no heir): "The heir line will fail; an image of a line generating itself." Pushes the ruler toward adoption, a hook for Take as Daughter. The Way can take 自相生 as its own phrase.
- *No reading* (more likely after about 1050): "The historians wrote it down and said nothing." Nothing happens; she keeps her life.

**Follow-up if the court never answers** (Cheng p. 193): villagers raise a shrine to her. County modifier "Shrine of the Changed One" (small piety and development for Yutai holders; scholar-official opinion loss).

**Side events from the table.**
- *White-Necked Crow* (row 23) as hostile reaction text for any Yutai woman commander. Narrator voice only; she is not a sister.
- *Huang Chonggu* (row 21): "The Clerk Who Would Not Marry." An official in the holder's service turns down a marriage by revealing she is a woman. Options: admire her and let her retire; keep her in office; expose her. Only in the faith's voice may she be called a sister in spirit.
- *The fruit-seller* (row 25): a male character with the Yutai gestation in progress gets a "the midwives were changed seven times" flavour line at month 10.

## 5. In-world loc lines

- Memorial (official): "In such-and-such county a man has become a woman. Your servant dares not conceal it, and reports."
- Court astrologer, Jing Fang: "When a man becomes a woman, yin conquers. The blame is ruin."
- Censor, the gloss: "When a man becomes a woman, castration is used to excess. Enter her in the register with the others."
- Minister, Chen Feng: "Yang has turned to yin. The line will fail: a house that bears itself."
- Song historian (bare record): "In the sixth year of Xuanhe a woman grew a beard, fine and handsome, just like a man. By special edict she was ordained a female Daoist."
- Hostile scholar: "Yin acting as yang cannot be allowed."
- Sister (reply to the censor): "They wrote that a woman's birth means the knife is used too much. Write it down again: I was born, not cut."
- Sister (on the bearded nüguan of 1124): "The court had a body it could not name, so it gave her a name in the Way. We only ask for the same."
- Village (after an unanswered memorial): "The court never answered. So we built her a shrine ourselves."
