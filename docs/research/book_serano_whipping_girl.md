## 1. The argument in brief

- **Two sexisms, not one binary.** *Oppositional sexism* is "the belief that female and male are rigid, mutually exclusive categories, each possessing a unique and nonoverlapping set of attributes"; *traditional sexism* is "the belief that maleness and masculinity are superior to femaleness and femininity." The transgender slogan "transgressing binary gender norms" names only the first.
- **Transmisogyny.** Trans women sit "at the intersection of ... transphobia, cissexism, and misogyny." "When the majority of jokes made at the expense of trans people center on 'men wearing dresses' or 'men who want their penises cut off,' that is not transphobia—it is trans-misogyny." The threat is the choice itself: trans women, "despite being born male and inheriting male privilege 'choose' to be female instead."
- **Effemimania.** "An obsession with 'male femininity'": psychiatry, parents and media punish feminine males far harder than masculine females. Femininity, not gender crossing, is the target.
- **Deceiver and pathetic.** The two media archetypes "are designed to validate the popular assumption that trans women are truly men." Raymond's version is a double bind: "If they act feminine they are perceived as being a parody, but if they act masculine it is seen as a sign of their true male identity." On deception: "'Deception' is the scarlet letter that trans people are made to wear so that everybody else can claim innocence."
- **"Artificial" is applied selectively.** Transition is called artificial so the class line it crosses can look natural: "only those practices that seem to subvert those classes (rather than reaffirm them) are ever characterized as such." Hence the before-and-after fixation, which anchors a trans woman "in my assigned sex." "There is no such thing as a 'real' gender—there is only the gender we experience ourselves as and the gender we perceive others to be."
- **Subconscious sex.** "It seems as if, on some level, my brain expects my body to be female." Proof that cis people have one too: "If cissexuals didn't have a subconscious sex, then sex reassignment would be far more common than it is ... Criminals and spies would physically transition as a way of going undercover."
- **Intrinsic inclinations.** "Subconscious sex, gender expression, and sexual orientation represent separate gender inclinations that are determined largely independently of one another." Trans women can be lesbian, straight or bi, and femme or butch.
- **Gatekeeping (ch. 7).** The clinics sought to "(1) minimize the number of transsexuals who transitioned, (2) ensure that most people who did transition would not be 'gender-ambiguous' in any way, and (3) make certain that those transsexuals who fully transitioned would remain silent." The real-life test became "a hazing period designed to weed out transsexuals who were the least 'passable'"; patients were called "impatient"; access depended on money; patients were "required to invent gender-consistent (i.e., cissexual) histories." Her remedy: "No specific medical procedure should be required for one to have one's identified sex recognized, nor should any medical or psychiatric professional have the authority to prevent someone from living in their identified sex." "Gender dissonance has always been a 'self-diagnosed' condition."
- **Third-gendering (against her will).** She attacks Nanda and Roscoe for ranking "third genders" above transsexuals: "show me one where male- and female-bodied people are both included in every gender category." But the line is consent: "I object to people who actively third-gender people against their will or without their consent." Self-identified third genders "should be respected."
- **Cissexual privilege.** Gender entitlement is "the privileging of one's own perceptions, interpretations, and evaluations of other people's genders over the way those people understand themselves." Birth-right gender is caste thinking: cis people "frown upon birth privilege" for class "but hypocritically embrace it when it comes to gender." "Nobody in our society ever asks for permission to belong to one gender or another." She names passing-centrism, trans-objectification, trans-mystification and trans-interrogation ("Why do cissexuals exist?" is never asked) as the mechanisms.
- **Experiential gender.** Womanhood accrues: "gender is first and foremost an individual experience, an amalgamation of ... gender inclinations, social interactions, body feelings, and lived experiences." Early transition felt like being "a stranger in a strange land"; the otherness "steadily subsided with time." Hormones changed emotion, touch and smell, but not "my levels of aggression, competitiveness, nurturing."
- **Not about the penis.** "My desire to have SRS has virtually nothing to do with my penis. This is about me wanting to have a clitoris and vagina." She cites Kessler and McKenna: "To be male is to 'have' something and to be female is to 'not have' it."
- **Trans lesbians and women's space (ch. 12).** The exclusion arguments ("male energy", the penis, socialization, safety) are all antifeminist. "How can someone who identifies as female and currently lives as a woman have less in common with women than a male-identified person who ... currently benefits from male privilege?" Lesbian spaces that welcome trans men but not trans women are transmisogynist.
- **Sexualization.** Trans women are cast as having transitioned to lure men. Sex work comes from poverty and exclusion, not from desire to be objectified.
- **"Male privilege" rethought (ch. 17).** Male privilege is real but not the only gender privilege. Against "you had male privilege": imagine having "been forced against your will into boyhood."
- **Femininity is not a ruse (ch. 19).** Both "unilateral" (Daly, Greer, Jeffreys) and "deconstructive" feminism artificialise femininity. "There has been no act more bold and daring than embracing our own femininity." Empower femininity itself.
- **Against subversivism and "oneness" (ch. 20).** Ranking gender-bending above transsexual womanhood "is merely the original oppositional sexist binary flipped upside down." She makes the same charge against cultural feminism's flip (women superior) and its separatist "oneness": "there is no such thing as a 'safe space.'" Her alternative is alliances, and a target of gender entitlement rather than of anyone's gender.

### Where Serano and Bhatt agree and disagree

- **Agree:** against ranking androgyny or gender-bending above transsexual womanhood (Serano's "subversivism"; Bhatt on Nanda's "transgenderist"). Both attack Nanda by name. Both oppose gatekeeping and both affirm bodily transition. Both describe the transmisogyny asymmetry. Both reject socialisation-based exclusion.
- **Disagree on femininity.** Serano's ch. 19 is the position Bhatt's "femininity trap" rejects. Bhatt locates oppression in reproductive sex-class, not femininity. The design currently leans Bhatt (martial daughters, shield-sisters). Rows 6 and 17 add a Serano strand without removing the Bhatt one.
- **Disagree on cause.** Serano's subconscious sex is intrinsic and, she says, likely hardwired, and the Marked trait fits it. Bhatt's sex-class is assigned by the regime, which fits H2 (manhood as a class one can leave). Serano would not frame transition as class defection: "male privilege" is not the only gender privilege, and her transition was "about the personal relationship I had with my own body." Keep both. The Marked seed is Serano; the Refusal theology is Bhatt and baeddel.
- **Disagree on the third sex.** Bhatt treats it as structural; Serano makes it turn on consent (row 7).
- **Disagree on separatism.** Serano's critique of "oneness" and safe space presses against E1/H5 separatism more than Bhatt does. The design's resolution (separatist clergy, pluralist foreign policy) survives if the hall excludes no woman (row 13).
- **Where Serano pushes hardest against locked choices:** the Dorley path's non-Marked intake (row 2), the trials in the embryo and seiðr gates (rows 1 and 3), and the achievement events (row 5). None needs removing. Each needs the gate to recognise rather than test, and the target to be someone becoming herself rather than a man being punished.
