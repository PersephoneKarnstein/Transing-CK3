## 1. The argument

- **Thesis.** Ch'an doctrine holds that the One Mind "is without *laksana* (C. *hsiang*), distinguishing characteristics, including maleness or femaleness" (p. 20). Ch'an teachers therefore stand in the Mahāyāna stream for which one "moment of sincere faith and insight" outweighs karmic disadvantage (p. 21). Explicit statements on gender appear only from the 11th century (p. 22).
- **Hongbian to Emperor Xuanzong.** Buddha-mind "has no characteristics (*hsiang*), such as blue, yellow, red, white, masculine or feminine"; it "enables men (to be men) and women (to be women)" (p. 20, from the *Chuandeng lu*). Levering dates Xuanzong "846-863", but he reigned 846–859, so this is probably a slip. **This is an in-window Tang figure who can be quoted in the faith's voice.**
- **Dahui (1089–1163)** on Lady Tang: "Can you say that she is a woman, and women have no share" in enlightenment? (p. 20). Also: "One moment of insight and one is shoulder to shoulder with the Buddha" (p. 20).
- **The dragon girl's "double message"**: "a person born as a woman becomes a Buddha only after having traded her female body for a male body; on the other, 'a woman can achieve Buddhahood'" (p. 24). Ch'an puts the stress on the second message and on the *instant* of each step.
  - "Song of Proving the Way", late 8th century: "[If] right, then the dragon girl suddenly achieves Buddhahood / [If] wrong, then Ananda is born in hell" (p. 24).
  - Dahui places her enlightenment at the offering of the pearl: "it took place in her single instant of thought" (p. 27). "For him the important thing is not her change of bodily form" (p. 27).
  - Summary: "the story is not that of a woman who became a male Buddha, it is the story of a woman who, upon becoming a Buddha, is still thought of as a woman" (p. 27).
- **The nun who wanted to "open the hall"** (*Chuandeng lu* juan 12, record of Youzhou Tankong; pp. 24–25). The master: "Nun, as a woman you should not open the hall." She cites the nāga girl. He demands: "Try one change of form for me." She: "Even if I were to change, I would still be a fox spirit!" He drives her out with blows. Levering: changing would prove "nothing except that she has magical powers". Authority to teach "must be resolved in a different realm entirely" (p. 25).
- **The abbess Moshan Liaoran 末山了然** (*Chuandeng lu* juan 11; pp. 27–29).
  - The wandering monk Guanxi Zhixian comes intending to "overturn the Ch'an platform". She asks where he set out from; he says "From the entrance to the road"; she asks "Why didn't you cover it?" He has no answer and bows.
  - He asks "What is the occupant of Mo-shan like?" She: "(S)he has neither male nor female form (*hsiang*)." He shouts: "Why doesn't she transform herself?" She: "She is not a spirit, nor a ghost. What would you have her become?"
  - He submits and serves **three years as gardener at the nunnery** (p. 28). Later he says he got half a ladle from Linji and "another half-ladle" from Moshan (p. 28).
  - Dōgen: Moshan "became the mother who taught Chih-hsien", and Linji his father (pp. 28–29).
  - Chinese of her two replies, given from the standard *Chuandeng lu* text and not checked in this pass: 非男女相 and 不是神，不是鬼，變箇什麼.
- **Reading.** Zhixian's challenge asks her to do what the dragon girl did: "Why not become a male and then a Buddha … and thus prove that you are enlightened?" (p. 29). Moshan wins by refusing. "In the thought world of the Lotus Sutra, the same story could not be read as a triumph for Mo-shan" (p. 29).
  - The bow carries the argument. A monk outranks a nun, but a student bows to a teacher, so Zhixian concedes that "the status of teacher is a matter of demonstrated wisdom, not of phenomenal characteristics" (p. 30).
  - She is "the only nun who is given a record of her own" in the *Chuandeng lu*, "almost certainly" because a male disciple credited her (p. 28).
- **Dōgen on boundaries.** He rejects Japanese sacred precincts closed to women: "even though you set up boundary lines, they are of no use" (p. 26). "What demerit is there in femaleness? What merit is there in maleness?" (p. 31).
- **Coda.** Levering's Taiwanese nuns call the male body "part of the miraculous trappings of the myth rather than the heart of the myth" (p. 31). She argues that Ch'an "planted seeds of the conviction that gender differences were accidental, not essential" (p. 31).

**Dating for the game (not in Levering; standard reference dates, verify before use).** Linji Yixuan d. 866/867. Guanxi Zhixian d. 895. Liaoran was a disciple of Gao'an Dayu. This puts **Moshan Liaoran active in the 860s–880s, at the CK3 867 start**. Moshan 末山 is in the Gao'an area of Jiangxi (*unverified*).

**Critical reading for the mod.** The Ch'an position, that the mind has no male or female form, is gender-*irrelevance*, not transition. It can slide into Bhatt's "pure gender 'disruption' untainted by" the body, the move she calls eliminationist. Moshan's refusal is excellent against the demand to *prove* oneself by transformation. But the Way's gate *is* a bodily work, so the Way cannot adopt "no form" as doctrine. It can borrow the *refusal* and leave the metaphysics.
