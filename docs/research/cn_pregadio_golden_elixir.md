## 1. The argument in brief

- **Neidan is late and has no single school.** The earliest traces of neidan are in Tao Zhi, in the second half of the 8th century. "No alchemical or other source suggests that Neidan existed before the 8th century" (p. 21). The first identifiable lineage, Zhong-Lü, is 9th/10th century (p. 35). The *Wuzhen pian* is by Zhang Boduan, "around 1075" (p. 36). Neither alchemy as a whole nor neidan is "a Taoist 'school' with a definite canonical corpus" (p. 3).
- **The embryo is older than neidan.**
  - The *Laozi zhongjing* (in Jiangnan lineages by the 3rd century) places the Red Child (*chizi* 赤子) in the stomach, with "his mother ... on his right, embracing and nourishing him; his father ... on his left" (p. 15). Pregadio calls the Child "the precursor of the 'embryo' and the 'infant' that Neidan adepts, centuries later, would generate" (p. 16).
  - **Shangqing (late 4th c.).** Its scriptures have "methods for the creation of an immortal body ... by means of a return to a self-generated inner embryo" (p. 17).
  - In "untying the knots" (*jiejie* 解結), "from month to month, beginning on the anniversary of his conception", the adept re-lives his own gestation. Then "his Original Father and Original Mother issue breaths that join at the center of his person and generate an immortal infant" (p. 17).
  - **For design:** a month-by-month gestation of one's own body is a 4th-century Daoist practice. It was fully available in the Tang.
- **The birth through the crown is pictured, but late.** Fig. 7 (p. 17) shows "the Neidan embryo exiting from the sinciput of the practitioner". It is taken from the *Xingming guizhi*, a Ming-period text; Pregadio gives no date for it. The upper Cinnabar Field is the *niwan* 泥丸 ("Muddy Pellet") in the brain (p. 61). Pregadio's own text does not describe the exit; Wang Mu does (see `cn_wang_mu_internal_alchemy.md`, p. 112).
- **The three stages are later than the texts they are read into.** The stages last 100 days, ten months ("the duration of human gestation by Chinese reckoning") and nine years (pp. 65–66).
  - "Many texts point out that these lengths are symbolic" (p. 66).
  - **Key dating point:** "Several authors have matched various facets of this process to passages of the *Cantong qi* and the *Wuzhen pian*, but neither text mentions it. As far as we know, the elaboration of the three-stage process dates from around the 11th–12th centuries" (p. 66).
  - Some texts, including the *Lingbao bifa*, "describe other patterns or do not mention any pattern at all" (p. 66).
- **The second stage is the Barrier of the Ten Months (*shiyue guan* 十月關).** It is "Refining Breath to Transmute it into Spirit". Its purpose is to generate "a Spirit made of the union of Original Breath ... and Spirit", nourished "between the lower and the middle Cinnabar Fields" (p. 66).
- **The embryo is the self.** Neidan is often pictured as creating "a person outside one's person" (*shen zhi wai shen* 身之外身), the "true person". Its stages are "the conception, gestation, and birth of an embryo, which grows to become a perfect likeness of the practitioner and personifies his or her realized state" (p. 52). Pregadio writes "his or her" here; women are not otherwise discussed in the book.
- **The middle field is a womb with a feminine name.** The middle Cinnabar Field is also called the Yellow Court, the Crimson Palace, or the Mysterious-Female (*xuanpin* 玄牝) (p. 61). The *Daode jing*'s "essence" hidden "within the womb of the Dao" is equated with the refined elixir (p. 12).
- **The direction is pure yang.** Tang Waidan aims at "an elixir that is entirely devoid of Yin components" and has "the luminous qualities of Pure Yang" (p. 29). Chen Zhixu (14th c.) says that with the precelestial Breath "they refine and achieve Pure Yang" (p. 39). Every pathway ends in Qian ☰ (p. 58, fig. 20).
- **Gender appears only as emblem, apart from one physical line.**
  - Yin and Yang are boy and girl, husband and wife, Kan and Li, Dragon and Tiger (pp. 47–48).
  - In the sexual reading (the Yin-Yang branch), the "Yin from which True Yang ... should be 'collected' ... is the female body" (p. 48).
  - Li Daochun "thoroughly rejects sexual practices" (p. 49).
  - **The one physical sex line:** "The main materialization of *jing* is semen in males and menstrual blood in females" (p. 60). Women's alchemy (*nüdan*) is not mentioned anywhere.
- **The language is deliberately "empty".** Xiao Tingzhi (13th c.): "the Kan Boy and the Li Girl are nothing but empty terms" (p. 48). Terms are "pointers to the principles that they represent" (p. 47). This supports the Way reading the same emblems its own way.
- **Ritual setting (Taiqing, Six Dynasties).** "To receive texts and oral instructions, the disciple offers tokens to his master and makes a vow of secrecy" (p. 10). The adept then enters a sealed chamber that "only he and his attendants may enter".
- **Elixirs as protection for women.** From the *Jiudan jing*: "If a woman who lives alone keeps one pill ... demons, thieves, and robbers will flee and dare not come near her" (p. 12).
- **Tang court.** Elixir poisoning killed "at least two and possibly as many as four Tang emperors" (pp. 31–32). Pregadio rejects the idea that this caused the shift to neidan.
- **Figures for the timeline.** Ming prince Zhu Quan (1378–1448) wrote the "last significant Chinese alchemical writing" (p. 33). The Seven Perfected include one woman, **Sun Bu'er (1119–83)** (p. 37).
