## 1. What the documents are

A Paris archivist printed Arles notarial acts, most of them collected around 1876 by Isidore Loeb from Bibliothèque d'Arles ms. 225, an 18th-century compilation by the abbé Bonemant (41: 62). The *REJ* 47 instalment adds acts from the notaries' registers themselves (Pangonis, Raymundi, Olivari), mostly 1417–1459. The documents are Latin, with one long Occitan text (the 1407 school deed, 41: 87–90) and one Occitan protest (48: 57). They show the community from outside, through the Christian notary. Nothing here describes worship, Purim or women's religious life directly. What they do show is seats, officers, money, licences, families and names.

## 2. The argument in brief

Hildenfinger's introductions are short and descriptive (41: 62–77; 47: 229–238):
- **Officers.** The community was run by *bayloni* (*baylons*), usually four, sometimes renewed for several terms. They took office by swearing "embracing the scroll called in Hebrew *sefer*" (oath of 17 July 1435, 41: 63 n. 4). They used the *ḥerem* as a sanction, kept registers, and could not make a new rule "unless the majority consent" (petition of 13 Nov. 1349, 41: 63). Beside them sat *consiliarii* and *auditores compotorum* (48: 54).
- **Lords and dues.** The archbishop still drew rent "for the guard of the synagogue": 20 lb of pepper, 20 lb of wax candles and three lampreys at Palm Sunday, plus a florin for the Crau cemetery not being planted as vines (doc. VI, 1427, 41: 91–93). The *bayloni* carried them to the palace themselves. In 1435 they brought them on the Friday, "no doubt out of respect for the Sabbath", and the vicar took them only under reserve (41: 64 n. 5).
- **The count's tax** fell on all the *juiveries* of Provence jointly. Delegates met at Arles in 1420 and 1446 to divide it (41: 65; 48: 66–75). Arles left the union after 1382 and paid its share of the old debt in 1402 (doc. III).
- **The end.** Harassment at Carnival (1433, doc. VII), riots in 1484, exclusion from public offices in 1488, Charles VIII's expulsion letters of July 1493 (doc. IX), and the last Jews put out of the city in September 1494 (41: 72–76). All of this falls after the game ends (1453) except the 1433 Carnival case.

## 3. What matters for the design

### 3.1 The women's synagogue, below the men's (*REJ* 47: 233; 48: 75–76)

Doc. XII (*REJ* 48: 75–76), dated 31 Jan. 1451 in the notary's style (1452 new style). Astrugia, daughter of the late Master Creyssent Tefillos, physician and surgeon of Châteaurenard, sells three seats to Durantonus Dieulosal of Beaucaire. She sells in her own name and as guardian of her children by Bonmaquetus Bonfils, "who lately converted to the Catholic faith and is now called Robertus Francisci". Her daughter Blanqueta sells with her. The seats are:
- "two in the men's school above" (*duo in scola hominum desuper*);
- "one below in the school of the Jewesses" (*unam desubtus in scola Judearum*).
- All three are "within the school of the Jews of Arles" (*infra scolam Judeorum de Arelate*), so the two rooms were **in one building**, men above and women below. This is not the separate women's building of Worms or Speyer (Baumgarten 2016).
- The men's seats are described by their neighbours: one borders "the place of the lamp of the said school" (*loco luminarie dicte scole*); another lies "under the preaching-place of the said school, called in Hebrew *scacalh*". Hildenfinger's note: "or *seacalh*; could it be היכל, *hekhal*?" (48: 76 n. 1).
- The women's seat borders the seats of Bendich de Pertusio and Ysaac Nathan. **Seats in the women's room are listed under men's names.** Households owned seats, and the women of the house sat in them (*unverified* inference).
- Price for all three: 9½ florins (48: 76). Hildenfinger's summary of the same deed: "two in the upper synagogue of the men and one in the lower synagogue of the women" (47: 233).
- The seats had belonged to the late Astrugius de Bellicadro (48: 76). Two women, a mother and daughter, inherited, held and sold them.

Elsewhere: in 1355 a widow, Gardeta, sold two seats, one on the east wall "where the scrolls stand, called *aron*" and one by the north wall (doc. I, 41: 77–78). In 1443 a seat was bought beside "the place of the Alms of the Sick of the *carrière*" (47: 228). The *holim* owned a seat in the synagogue.

**Not in the documents:** any word for a gallery, a screen, or a woman reading aloud in the synagogue.

### 3.2 Women in the assembly and the *ḥerem*

- **1407, the school assembly** (doc. V, 41: 87). The roll of those present "according to custom, by licence of our lord the king, in the synagogue or Jewish school" lists about forty householders. The last two are **Estes de Cailario** and **Regina, widow of Master Abram Boneti Avicdor, physician**. They are the only two women. Stouff identifies Estes as Esther de Caylar, granddaughter of Bonjues Nathan (`jud_stouff_gross_arles.md` §2.3).
- **The donor's own terms** (in Occitan, 41: 88). Master Helias asked that "every head of household (*tot cap d'ostal*) … men and women (*homes et donas*)" assemble to bind themselves. Hildenfinger's gloss: "the heads of family, men and women, met to accept a donation" (41: 63). Women heads of household were part of the *kahal*'s binding assembly.
- **The licence to ban covers women.** In the 1405 licence the archbishop's vicar allows the *bayloni* to pronounce the ban over the declaration of wealth "by every Jew and every Jewess" (*per quemlibet Judeum et quamlibet Judeam*). He also lets them denounce "any Jew or Jewess" who breaks the Law of Moses or the community's rules (doc. IV, 41: 85–86).
- **Women swore the *ḥerem* in the synagogue.** In 1446 the tax delegates came to Arles "to see the Jews of Arles of both sexes (*utriusque sexus*) swear the anathema (which the Jews call *ḥerem*)" over their declarations, and "the Jews of the present city of both sexes … swore" (doc. X, 48: 66–67).

### 3.3 The *holim* confraternity of 1401 (*REJ* 41: 69–70; doc. II, 41: 78–81)

- **Re-founded, not new.** On 22 April 1401 the *bayloni* Master Crescas Salamias and Vitalis Calhi, with three others, told the royal judge that they and "diverse other Jews, moved by devotion" wished to revive "a certain alms or confraternity, called in Hebrew *holim*". Its purpose was "to feed the needy, healthy and sick, to clothe the naked and to bury poor dead Jews". It had existed before, "and its term is long ended" (41: 78). Hildenfinger reads *holim* as *biqqur ḥolim* or *le-ḥolim* (41: 69 n. 5).
- **Licensed by the Christian court, for a term.** The judge granted leave the same day for twenty years: to meet, elect priors yearly, make ordinances, levy a *tallia* "among the confrères, and not others", and keep accounts (41: 78–79).
- **Statutes of 2 May 1401** (41: 80–81):
  - Five men were elected to draft them. Two rectors and two auditors were named each year: "two Jewish men" (*duo homines Judei*).
  - Dues were 3 *gros* a year.
  - Every sick and needy Jew, member or not, got 2 *gros* a week.
  - After Passover every needy Jew got two loaves of 2 *deniers* "for each of his house".
  - **The sick-watch.** After three days' illness the rectors chose "two men of the confraternity" to sit up with the sick person "by night only". Someone who would not go had to pay two other Jews to watch. A draft had also said "and by day", which was struck out (41: 80 n. 1).
  - Members followed every member's funeral to the grave and brought the mourners home.
- **Membership.** The eighteen named *confratres* are all men: Creychent Garacausa, Maistre Bendich Bon Senhor, Baron Grescas, Gart de Gavalhon and the rest (41: 81).
- **Hildenfinger's caution.** The petition speaks of burying the dead, but the statutes say nothing of burial work beyond following the bier (41: 70 n. 1).

**Consequence.** No woman is a member, rector, auditor or watcher. The watch that the reading list proposed as the women's cover was a duty of two men, at night.

### 3.4 The 1407 school and its woman governor (doc. V, 41: 70–72, 87–91; 48: 51–52)

- **The foundation.** Master Helias, an Arles-born physician at Valence, gave 1,000 florins to reopen a free school for poor children. There were two masters: one for "the five books of Moses and the prophets" at 20 florins a year, one for "the Talmud" at 30 (41: 89). The conditions were read to the assembly in Hebrew and in Occitan (41: 87).
- **The governors.** Helias named governors "for ever, by succession". Among them were "Regina and her sons and their children" (41: 90). Hildenfinger lists the first governors as Bonsenhor Asday, Crescas Salamias "and Reine, widow of Abraham Bonet Avigdor" (41: 71 n. 1).
- **The 1423 appointment.** Her line still held the right. Her grandchildren, Abram Duranti Avigdor and Scereta, wife of Master Vitalis Astrugii of Carcassonne, took part in naming the Talmud master for 1423–24 (doc. IV, 48: 51–52). Hildenfinger reconstructs the family over four generations, from Bendig Aym through Reine to Durant Avigdor to Scerette (47: 232–233).
- **Simḥat Torah.** Helias asked that the cantor say a prayer for him and his children every year on Simḥat Torah (41: 89).
- Hildenfinger also notes a teacher's contract of 1447 and a "rector of the Jewish schools" paid at St Catherine's and St Nicholas's (41: 72).

### 3.5 A physician's house of daughters (doc. III, 41: 69 n. 1, 82–85)

Bendig Aym, physician to Queen Joanna, left **four daughters and no son**, each heir "for a fourth part":
- Creguda, married in Savoy to Léon Jacar;
- Englesia (also called Estes), wife of Master Tauros Bondias and mother of Dulcieta and Astrugueta;
- Regina (Reine Bonete), widow of the physician Abraham Bonet Avigdor;
- Bonafilia, wife of the physician Bonsenhor Brunel.

Their mother **Estes** acted as procurator for Creguda and as guardian of Dulcieta. The women "swore on the law of Moses" and gave the *bayloni* receipt for 100 florins (41: 84–85). A son of Regina and Abraham was baptised in 1409 as Louis Ramon, with King Louis as godfather (41: 69 n. 1).

### 3.6 Other women at work and at law

- **1422.** "Leave given to a Jewess to divorce for incompatibility of temper" (47: 222).
- **1431.** **Venguessonne Nathane** appears as creditor in about ten loan acts in one register. The *bayloni*'s 1423 contract for a wall was signed "in the house of the said Venguessone" (47: 224–226, 240–242).
- **1431.** Cassive, widow of Bonet Avigdor, and Bonosa his daughter, lend and buy vines. Dulcieta, widow of Bonsenhor de Monteux, lends. Cregudon Cohen leases a vineyard (sex not stated; *unverified* as a woman) (47: 224–227).
- **Dowries.** Five marriage settlements (docs. VII–IX, XIII; doc. I of *REJ* 47) for Donella (1422), Reine Vivas (1431), Dulcie de Gart (1431), Belestre of Carcassonne (1431) and Reine, granddaughter of Durant Dieulosal (1454). The *ketubbah* was written in Hebrew and described in the Latin act: "called in Hebrew *quessuba*", with its first and last words glossed in Occitan (48: 58, 65). Stes, wife of Bondias de Saint-Paul, ranked her own dowry claim behind her daughter-in-law's (48: 61–63). The 1454 settlement covers the young wife's lying-in costs (*jassinas*) for ten years (48: 78).

### 3.7 Licences and lords

- **The 1405 ban licence** (doc. IV, 41: 85–86). Raymundus de Ayraco, vicar of Archbishop Artaud de Mélan, grants it **for one year**. It is subject to two conditions: each sentence must first be notified to him, and absolution is reserved to him (41: 67 n. 5). Hildenfinger's introduction prints the date as 23 Nov. **1415** (41: 67). The document and the archbishop (d. 1410) show it is **1405**, as Shatzmiller has it.
- **The ordinary assembly met "by licence of our lord the king"** (41: 87). The *holim* needed the royal judge's leave to exist (41: 78).
- **1386.** A Jew was licensed by the judge of Arles to rule on the validity of a Jewish marriage (47: 222).
- **1428.** The *bayloni*, councillors and auditors ordered a new declaration of wealth under the ban "according to the book of the *mispatim*". Linen, books and household goods were now taxed (doc. VI, 48: 54–57). The physician Bendich de Borriano refused one article and protested in Occitan (48: 57).
- **Carnival, 1433** (doc. VII, 41: 93–94). At *carême-prenant* (Shrove Tuesday, dated by Hildenfinger to 24 Feb. 1433) the town's "abbot of youth" and his fellows took pledges from the Jews for the *pelote*. This was a due paid to the youth abbeys, properly by newly married couples (41: 73 n. 1). The lieutenant of the seneschal called it "a novelty never seen" and ordered the pledges returned. Here the Christian lord protects the community against the town's young men at Carnival, a few weeks before Purim.

### 3.8 Scholars and books (for loc only)

- 1420: a Hebrew Bible was sold for 36 florins (41: 72 n. 4).
- 1430/31: a "Bible called in Hebrew *Magdessia*", three columns to the leaf, in square letters (41: 72 n. 4).
- 1477: a silver *atara* (Torah crown) of 8 marks (47: 229). Stouff dates a Torah-crown commission to 1439 (`jud_stouff_gross_arles.md`).
- 1402: the medical licence of Salomonet Aviczor. He "stood and argued one question" before four physicians; the Christian swore on the Gospels and the three Jews "on the law of Moses, touching the Hebrew scriptures" (41: 67 n. 4).

## 4. Corrections to current docs and proposals

1. **`tm_hipukh.0010` "women's gallery" is wrong for Arles.** The women sat in their own room, **below** the men's, in the same synagogue building (48: 76). "Gallery" implies an upper balcony looking down on the men, which is the reverse. Fix in §5.1.
2. **`judaism_reading_list.md`, finding 1.**
   - The deed's words are Latin, *scola Judearum* ("the school of the Jewesses"). The Occitan *scola de las juzievas* is a reconstruction, not the source's wording. The Occitan documents do use *scola* (41: 89, "nostra scola") and *Jusieus* (41: 88). A feminine *juzievas* or *jusievas* is plausible but *unverified*.
   - The women's room is not a separate building, as at Worms, but a lower room in the same *scola*.
   - The 1452 deed is doc. **XII** of *REJ* 48 (Hildenfinger's own correction: 48: 76 n. 2).
3. **`judaism_reading_list.md`, finding 2, "the *holim* sick-watch is the cover".** The statutes put the watch on "two men of the confraternity", at night, and every named member is a man (41: 80–81). A women's circle *inside* the *holim* has no support. Women do appear in the **assembly** (1407), the **school governorship** (1407, 1423), the **ḥerem oath** (1405, 1446), seat ownership (1355, 1452) and lending. See §5.2.
4. **"The women of the *ḥevrah*"** (`tm_hipukh.0001.desc`, `0002`, `0010`; README; faith_design §13). The Arles sources call such bodies *elemosina* or *confratria*, "alms" or "confraternity" (41: 78; Occitan *almorna*, 41: 88). The Hebrew name recorded is *holim*. "Ḥevrah" is not attested in these documents. It is the usual later Hebrew term (*ḥevra qaddisha*), so keep it only as the sect's own word, *unverified* for 15th-century Arles.
5. **The 1405 licence** in `jud_shatzmiller_jews_of_provence.md` §3.3 is confirmed: one year, two purposes, prior notice, absolution reserved. Add that it names "every Jewess" as well. `tm_turning_niddui_effect` is sound.
6. **"Before the elders" / "the worthies".** The Arles officers are the *bayloni* (four), with councillors and auditors of accounts (48: 54). "The worthies of the town" can stay as a gloss. The loc could name them: "the baylons and the council".

## 6. Content to keep out

- Doc. IX's grounds for expulsion ("errors and heresies", 41: 96–97), the "usurious maw" phrasing, and the 1440 contract for a "gallows of the Jews" in the Crau (47: 228). None of these may become event text.
- Lending appears only as plain economy (loans, vines, shops), never as a trait or a motive.
- Conversions: the documents treat converts plainly (Robertus Francisci, 1452; Louis Ramon, 1409). They may appear as family facts. They must never appear as a goal or a reward.

## 7. Open questions

- Was the Arles lamp alms run by women? Stouff names it but gives no members. The testaments (ADB 405 E series) would tell. *Unverified.*
- Was the *carrière*'s women's room on a lower floor or a lower annexe? "Desuper/desubtus" allows either.
- Iancu-Agou, *Provincia Judaica* (2010), for the synagogue's plan and the Occitan word for the women's room.
