# Silva and Ornat, "Transfeminism and Decolonial Thought" (TSQ 2016): design reading

**Citation.** Joseli Maria Silva and Marcio Jose Ornat, "Transfeminism and Decolonial Thought: The Contribution of Brazilian Travestis," trans. Sean Stroud, *TSQ: Transgender Studies Quarterly* 3, nos. 1–2 (May 2016): 220–227. DOI 10.1215/23289252-3334415.

**What was read.** The whole article, notes and references included. Page numbers are the journal's. The three travesti activists it quotes are pseudonyms (n. 2, p. 225); this note paraphrases them except for two short lines.

**Hard rule.** Travestis are a living community. Nothing here becomes a faith, a rite or an in-game group.

---

## 1. Relation to existing notes

**Framework: the decolonial school Bhatt attacks.** The authors build on Mignolo and Quijano (n. 7, p. 225), the "coloniality of power" that Lugones extended into the "coloniality of gender" (`book_bhatt_brown_trans_les.md` §2.6). Their main charge is epistemic: Brazilian transfeminism imports Butler, Koyama and Stone and treats anglophone categories as universal, which does "epistemic violence" to travestis (p. 221).

**But the content is mostly not the golden-age claim.** The article makes no claim about a precolonial past. Its point is that theory must come from the women it names. Bhatt would not object to that.

**Where the travestis quoted agree with Bhatt.** One activist argues against deleting the word *travesti* from the dictionary: travestis were the ones beaten in the streets while gay men stayed home, and "If we erase the word travesti from the dictionary we will delete our history" (p. 224). Another says some women call themselves transsexual only so as not to be like travestis (p. 223, paraphrased). That is the respectability Bhatt calls betrayal.

**Where they disagree with Bhatt.** One activist says the transsexual is "forgiven by society; you are putting yourself forward to be corrected" (p. 223). Surgery as submission to correction is the "transition is conformist" view that Bhatt calls eliminationist (`book_bhatt_third_sex.md` §1). The authors report the view without endorsing it.

## 2. Argument in brief

- "Transfeminism" entered Brazilian academia around 2013–14 (pp. 220–221). A veteran travesti activist, asked about it, joked that she had never heard of it (p. 220).
- Travestis built their own movement inside a Brazilian LGBT movement that stigmatised them (p. 222). Wider access to medical transition since 2008 has sharpened tensions between travestis and transsexual women (p. 222; n. 10, p. 226).
- The activists deny fixed identities yet still define the difference by attitude to the genitals, borrowing medical language (pp. 223–224). Depathologisation matters to academics but worries activists, who fear losing public health care (pp. 223–224).
- At a 2013 national meeting, the transsexual women's group proposed deleting *travesti* from the dictionary; a travesti activist answered that the word should be redefined, not erased (p. 224).
- Conclusion: transfeminism in Brazil must take in "the everyday experiences of Brazilian travestis and female transsexuals" (p. 225).

## 3. What is usable

Almost nothing for the design. Two confirmations.

1. **Keep the hostile name; change its meaning.** "Redefine the word, don't erase it" (p. 224) is the logic behind a faith that takes a word of abuse as its own name. Bædling rests on Old English *bæddel*, a hostile word (`oe_sayers_baedling_etymology.md`). No change; this is a modern parallel for a choice already made.
2. **Theory has to answer to the women it names.** The opening joke (p. 220) is a fair warning to a mod whose tenets are built from theory. The mod already writes its faiths' voices from period institutions and marks the theory as the faith's own claim.

## 4. What it changes in the design

Nothing. No table.

## 5. Cautions

- Do not use *travesti*, or any Brazilian term, in the mod.
- The activists' views on surgery are theirs, in a specific modern conflict over health-care access. Do not carry the view that surgery means being "corrected" into any faith's voice. If it appears at all, it belongs to a hostile or accommodationist voice, attributed.
- The article is short and reflective, with little evidence beyond three interviews. Do not cite it for claims about travestis in general.
