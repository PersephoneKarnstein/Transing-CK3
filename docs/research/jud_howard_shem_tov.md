**Relation to existing notes.** **Not Kalonymus.** `jud_kalonymus_even_bohan.md` covers Kalonymus ben Kalonymus's satire *Even Boḥan* (*The Touchstone*), written in Catalonia c. 1322. This book is about a different work with the same title, by a different author, sixty years later.

## What it is

- **Author.** Shem Tov ben Isaac ibn Shaprut, physician. Howard says he was born at Tudela "in Castile" in the mid-14th century and practised medicine at Tarazona in Aragon (p. xi). Tudela was in **Navarre**, not Castile, so Howard's "Castile" is a slip.
- **Work.** *Even Boḥan*, "The Touchstone", an anti-Christian polemical treatise. It was completed at Tarazona in **1380** and revised in 1385, around 1400 and later, growing from 12 books to 15 or 16 (p. xi).
- **Contents** (p. xi):
  - book 1: principles of Jewish faith;
  - books 2–10: biblical passages disputed between Jews and Christians;
  - book 11: Talmudic aggadot used by Christians and converts;
  - book 12 (sometimes 13): the whole Gospel of Matthew in Hebrew, with Shem Tov's polemical comments.
- **Howard's thesis.** The Hebrew Matthew is older than Shem Tov and was not translated by him from the Vulgate or the Greek (Preface, p. vii; pp. 173–174). Biblical scholars dispute this, and it does not bear on the mod.
- **Manuscripts.** Fifteenth to seventeenth century. The base texts are British Library Add. 26964 and JTS 2426 (pp. xii–xiii).

## Does it bear on the mod?

**Hardly.** It postdates the sect's founding text by sixty years, it comes from the Ebro valley, not Provence, and it says nothing about gender, Purim or the Turning. Two small uses:

1. **Name collision.** Two medieval Hebrew works share the title *Even Boḥan*. Every loc and doc that says "the *Touchstone*" means Kalonymus's (c. 1322). If a bibliography cites "*Even Boḥan*", give the author.
2. **Disputation as a reaction context.** Shem Tov's book shows that Iberian Jews of the 1380s argued with Christians from a Hebrew Gospel and from rebuttals of the aggadot that Christian disputants used (book 11). A late-period Christian disputation event against the sect could use the same frame:
   - The Christian side cites aggadah against the Jews. A disputant could cite Berakhot 60a (Dinah) as proof that "even your Talmud says God turns men", to shame the rabbis.
   - The Jewish answer is the rabbis' own: "one does not cite miracles."
   - Dates: after 1380. Place: Aragon or Navarre.

   This is optional flavour, *unverified* as to any real use of Berakhot 60a in disputation. The male-menses limits apply (`jud_male_menses_myth.md`).
