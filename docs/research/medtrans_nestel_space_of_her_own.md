# Nestel, "A Space of Her Own" (2019): design reading

Source: Meghan Nestel, "A Space of Her Own: Genderfluidity and Negotiation in *The Life of Christina of Markyate*," *Medieval Feminist Forum: A Journal of Gender and Sexuality* 55, no. 1 (2019): 100–134. DOI: [10.17077/1536-8742.2064](https://doi.org/10.17077/1536-8742.2064). Volume, issue and pages are verified from the PDF's footer. The DOI comes from the journal's issue page (scholarworks.wmich.edu/mff/vol55/iss1/5), which gives the author as Meghan L. Nestel; the PDF does not print a DOI.

Read in full. Quotes are verbatim. Primary source: the *Life of Christina of Markyate* (St Albans, c. 1140–1166, in Talbot's edition and translation). Christina was born Theodora of Huntingdon c. 1096 and died after 1155. Framing is in `medtrans_kim_bychowski_visions.md`.

**Scope note.** Christina is a cis woman, read here as **genderfluid** through Bornstein, Butler, Barad, Halberstam and Vaccaro. Her gender movements are toward the masculine and toward a "third" religious estate. **Relevance to a trans-women mod is low to moderate.** The useful material is the mechanics of escape, refuge, succession to a religious house, stealth, and the Latin Church's enclosure of women. The article's celebration of "thirds" also sits in direct tension with the mod's Bhatt-era position that daughters are women and not a third thing.

## 1. The argument in brief

- **Christina moves among several "thirds".** She is a secular virgin, a cross-dressed fugitive, a virgin fighting a "masculinized" battle for chastity, a nun crowned with a bishop's mitre, and a visionary appearing in men's spaces. She is "neither woman nor man, but somewhere beyond the binary" (p. 101).
- **There are multiple thirds, not one.** Against Garber's single "third", Halberstam asks for "a fourth, fifth, sixth, or one hundredth space" (p. 103). Swanson calls the clergy's gender "emasculinity", and Salih treats virginity as a lived, unstable gender (p. 103). Monks and nuns are both "third" but not the same third: "the existence of multiple thirds" (p. 104).
- **The family and the Church enforce her gender.** She refuses Burthred: "I wish to remain single, for I have made a vow of virginity" (p. 109). Bishop Ralph of Durham, rebuffed, arranges the marriage in revenge. Burthred's friends tell him to "act the man". Her mother "swore that she would not care who deflowered her daughter" (p. 113). These are Bornstein's "Gender Defenders" (p. 114).
- **She escapes in disguise.** She leaves in "masculine garb" and hands her sister her silk garment (*bombicinum*) and **her father's keys** (p. 116). The narrator orders her: "Put on manly courage and mount the horse like a man" (p. 117). At Flamstead she sheds the disguise and hides in a "small dark chamber" with the anchoress Alfwen (p. 120).
- **She chooses against stealth.** Unlike Eugenia or Euphrosyne, she does not live on as a monk. Nestel quotes Bornstein: stealth trans people "are not allowed any history beyond their current gender" (p. 120).
- **A man says she is "more like a man than a woman"** when she resists a lustful cleric. The narrator retorts that she "might more justifiably have called him a woman" (p. 121). Women who conquer lust are called "viragos" (p. 123).
- **Her authority comes from heaven, not the Church.** Angels crown her with fillets "like those of a bishop's mitre" (p. 129). Later her courage is no longer called "manly" but simply hers. Nestel follows Vaccaro in calling this "elsewhere" rather than "in between" (p. 130), and notes that this is not meant to disparage binary transition (p. 130 n. 125).
- **She inherits a hermitage from a man.** Roger the hermit "chose Christina as his successor", and the Virgin confirms it: "She certainly shall have it" (p. 131). She refuses offers to head houses at York, Marcigny and Fontevrault (p. 130). In visions she appears in Abbot Geoffrey's bedchamber and at Simon of Bermondsey's altar, where "hardly ... any woman would be allowed to approach" (p. 132).
- **Enclosure was closing in.** Geoffrey founded the fully enclosed Sopwell Priory in 1140. Markyate was not enclosed, and later St Albans monks found that troubling. Enclosure culminated in Boniface VIII's *Periculoso* (1298) (p. 133 n. 135).
- **Conclusion:** "She is elsewhere, she is all and none" (p. 134).

### Event / historical-character flags
- **Christina of Markyate** (c. 1096 – after 1155), Huntingdon, then Markyate in Hertfordshire. She is a cis woman, so **not a sister**. She could appear only as a cited precedent in Houses within England ("a virgin of Huntingdon rode out in a man's cloak and kept her own cell"). Do not recast her as trans.
- **Places for flavour**: Huntingdon, Flamstead and Markyate (Hertfordshire), St Albans and Sopwell (enclosed in 1140).
- **Cross-dressing saints** named in passing (Eugenia, Euphrosyne, Marina/Marinus, Hildegund of Schönau, p. 115 n. 63) are all assigned female at birth and are out of scope.

### Methodological cautions
- **Hagiography is a portrait, not testimony.** The narrator is "a portrait painter" and not a transparent transmitter (p. 107). The *Life* gives no interiority (p. 108). Use it for social scripts, not for claims about what Christina felt.
- **Pronouns and labels.** Nestel keeps she/her for Christina and refuses to choose pronouns on her behalf (p. 108 n. 37). The mod should follow the same rule for historical figures.
- **Anachronism is analytic, not identificatory.** Nestel quotes Karras and Linkinen: "If we must think of medieval people only in medieval terms there would be no analysis" (p. 109).
