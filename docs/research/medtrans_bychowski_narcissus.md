Source: M. W. Bychowski, "The Necropolitics of Narcissus: Confessions of Transgender Suicide in the Middle Ages," *Medieval Feminist Forum: A Journal of Gender and Sexuality* 55, no. 1 (2019), special issue *Medieval Trans Feminism*, ed. **Content note:** the article is about suicide. This design note keeps to mechanics and framing and does not reproduce method detail.

## 1. The argument in brief

- **The text.** Gower's *Confessio Amantis* (1390s) retells Ovid's Narcissus without Echo's curse. The youth flees a hunting party of men, finds a well, sees "an ymage / Of such a nimphe" (1.2316-17), weeps with her, asks for grace, gets none, and kills himself. Flowers spring from the grave "in the wynter ... contraire / To kynde" (1.2355-57).
- **The reading.** Gower's Narcissus "reflects a medieval history of isolation, exclusion, dysphoria, and death in the trans community" (p. 209). The youth "comes to view themself as a woman" (p. 207 n. 2). Bychowski uses "they" for the youth and "she" for the image.
- **The frame is a wood of suicide.** Gower's narrator Amans wanders into a wood wishing for death until Venus says "Tell thi maladie" (1.164). Middle English *wód* means both "wood" and "mad". The wood is where the excluded go, and where confession can bring them back.
- **Necropolitics and slow death.** Following Mbembe, Berlant and Puar: "no suicide is a singular event or condition but is a symptom of a systematic wearing down" (p. 216). The question changes "from 'why did they die?' to 'how did they keep going so long?'" (p. 244).
- **Dysphoria is in the environment.** "Because dysphoria is in the environment rather than just in the body, it can be named as a social ill" (p. 236). Gower gives no curse, only a *condiciǒun* "without comparisoun". The youth is set above women by rank and sex and so has nothing to compare herself to.
- **Leaving manhood.** Narcissus rides "Til alle men be left behinde" (1.2303), ties the horse, and lies low at the well. Bychowski reads this as "stepping away and down from the privilege of a cis man" (p. 238). The well is "a site of gender euphoria" (p. 238).
- **Presumption.** Gower's sin of Presumption, ME *surquiderie*, means "seizure and occupation without right" (p. 227). Bychowski maps it onto trans-exclusive feminism. Raymond's siege image is a "walled city of truth" around womanhood (p. 229). Exclusion also comes from "defensive risk avoidance": "The city is made safe by sending the danger into the woods" (pp. 231-232).
- **The nymphs did nothing.** After the death, "the Nimphes of the welles" come to mourn (1.2343). "Narcissus calls for help and none came. Yet Narcissus was not alone. The nymphs waited and watched" (p. 244). Readers sit in the nymphs' place: "better at grieving the dead ... than interrupting paths that lead to suicide" (p. 245).
- **Necropolitics needs only indifference.** "Biopolitics wants hope or shame. Necropolitics suffices with despair or indifference" (p. 246).
- **Flowers in winter.** "An engendering not in the spring of birth but claimed in a later season. The flowers are like the trans lives that come after" (p. 246).
- **Method.** Medieval trans readers must be "polyglots" in medieval and modern dialects (p. 211). Dysphoria is treated like gravity: real before it had a name (p. 236). Bychowski calls on others to answer, "even those who disagree" (p. 209).

## 2. What it changes in the design

**Relevance overall:** high for tone and for one core mechanic, the answer to the cry. It is moderate for the Bæddel faith (wood, outlawry, *wód*). It is low for the Chinese faith.

### Methodological cautions

- **Reading a fiction as trans.** Bychowski reads a literary character, not a historical person, and calls the essay an invitation to others "even those who disagree" (p. 209). The mod should not present "Narcissus was trans" as fact. Any in-game use should be **an in-world reading**: a Sister's interpretation of a tale, in her voice.
- **Modern clinical language.** The article bridges medieval *condiciǒun* and DSM-5 "gender dysphoria" through the gravity analogy (p. 236). The mod should use only the medieval side: heat, thirst, the wood, the well, *condition* in its old sense of circumstances. No "dysphoria" in loc.
- **Suicide in a game.** The article's ethics oppose consuming trans suicide as spectacle and treating the ending as presumed. Hence row 3's rule: no suicide content added. Prefer mechanics that reward answering over mechanics that dramatise failure.
- **Statistics** (pp. 217-218) are modern and are not for in-game use.

## 3. Quotable lines for in-game text (in-world paraphrases)

- "The world does not need to kill us. It needs only not to answer." (Elder Sister; Letter from the Wood)
- "Ask not why she went into the wood. Ask how long she lived outside it." (funeral or "We Were Too Late" text)
- "She rode until the men were behind her, tied her horse, and knelt at the water. What she saw there was a woman." (Marked-reader event; the Narcissus reading in a Sister's voice)
- "Tell me your sorrow. There is no wrong answer; I am only listening." (`Tell Thy Sorrow`, Dorleyist)
- "The city keeps itself safe by sending the danger into the woods. We are the woods." (Bæddel band or House description)
- "Presumption fences the field and never once says thank you." (retort in `The Closed Gate`, after Gower 1.1902)
- "A flower in winter, out of season and against kind, and no less a flower." (`A Flower in Winter`)
- "She looked into the well and saw a woman. The vanity was yours." (reply to the hostile Narcissus slur)
- Memory text: "**We Did Not Answer.** A letter came from the wood, and we let it lie."
