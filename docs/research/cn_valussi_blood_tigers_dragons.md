## 1. The argument in brief

- **Nüdan is late imperial.** The women's practice "finds maturity in late eighteenth-century China" (p. 47). The general neidan process "had been formalised ... for an un-gendered audience of practitioners as early as the Song" (p. 47). The shift "happened between the end of the Ming ... and the beginning of the Qing" (p. 47). Full texts and collections appear "only in the seventeenth and eighteenth century", in spirit-writing cults (p. 48).
  - **Sources.**
    - The main collection is the *Nüdan hebian* 女丹合編, printed at the Erxian an in Chengdu in **1906** and edited by He Longxiang (p. 49).
    - It draws on Fu Jinquan's *Nüjindan fayao* of **1813**.
  - **Earlier attributions are apocrypha.** Attributions to Sun Bu'er (1119–1183) are "apocryphal", and Chinese studies that date nüdan to the Yuan are "fairly ahistorical" (n. 8).
- **Pre-Qing traces exist and centre on the breasts.**
  - Wang Chongyang (1112–1170) "mentions ... female practice" (n. 3).
  - The 12th-century *Daoshu* (Zeng Cao, fl. 1131–55), in Chongzhen zi's chapter, puts "the seat of female practice as being in the breasts" (n. 3).
  - **The firmest Song text** is Xue Shi's (d. 1191) commentary on the *Wuzhen pian* (*Wuzhen pian sanzhu*, 1169): "For a man practising to become immortal, we speak of refining *qi*, for a woman ... we speak of refining the form." She first gathers *qi* in the breasts, then applies "the refinement of the form of Supreme yin (*taiyin lianxing*)" (p. 72).
- **Who wrote it and why.** Qing nüdan was "offered to women, by male authors and editors, as a safe, correct and simple alternative" to "harmful practices". It was to be practised "at home and in isolation". Its prefaces stress "the superfluousness of an outside (male) teacher", against a background of "a heightened interest in ... chastity" (pp. 48–49).
- **The swap: blood for essence.** The triad *jing–qi–shen* becomes *xue–qi–shen* 血氣神: "the blood, not the seminal essence, was refined first into qi" (p. 47). Song physicians fixed "the leadership of blood in women (*nüzi yi xue wei zhu* 女子以血為主)" (p. 54).
- **"Beheading the red dragon" (*zhan chilong* 斬赤龍).**
  - **Definition.** "When a woman has completed the practice and the menstrual flow ... does not drip away anymore, this is called 'Beheading the red dragon'", parallel to the man's "subduing the white tiger" (p. 62). This is from He Longxiang's 1906 preface.
  - **Stage.** Both "mark the completion of the first stage" (p. 62).
  - **The term belongs to nüdan.** "Red Dragon ... only appears in nüdan texts, and does not have a correlate term in medical theory" (p. 61).
  - **Variants:** *zhan long* 斬龍, *duan long* 斷龍, *duan honglong* 斷紅龍 (n. 45).
- **Earliest attestation: 1310, inside the game window** (n. 45). It is in the *Chunyang dijun shenhua miaotong ji*, Miao Shanshi's collection of Lü Dongbin legends, "compiled in 1310". Episode 106:
  > a 16 year-old girl who, to escape her parent's plan to marry her, hides away on a mountain. Here she meets an old man who tells her: 'I will slay your Red Dragon.'
- **What the body becomes: a girl's, a maiden's, a virgin's, and sometimes "a man's".** Valussi's own summary is that the woman reaches "a state of pre-puberty or androgynity" and then progresses "in the same way as the man" (p. 47). The texts vary:
  - "the woman's body will change into that of an adolescent" (*Nannü dangong yitong bian*, 1903; p. 61);
  - "your breasts becoming like those of a maiden, the form of those of a young girl, then the woman transforms into a male body" (*Xiwangmu nüxiu zhengtu shize*, 1834; p. 75);
  - "the breasts will shrink and be like those of a man" (*Nüjindan*, 1892; p. 75);
  - after a 100-day breast massage the breasts take "the form of walnuts" or "almond pits" (p. 75);
  - "The body will be like that of a virgin" (p. 66).
- **Men return to boyhood the same way.** A Fu Jinquan text (p. 66) says that when a man's essence turns to *qi*, "The penis will retract to look like that of a young boy". In the same passage a woman's body "will be like that of a virgin". "For both men and women, the decay and depletion that comes with old age needs to be ... reversed" (p. 66).
- **The direction.** Woman is Kan ☵ (yin outside, yang inside) and man is Li ☲. "In order to complete the process of inversion and attain immortality, Kan must change into Qian ☰", although this return to unity "does not eliminate, but incorporates and overcomes the duality" (p. 62).
- **Calling the dragon back.**
  - **Practice open to older women.** Post-menopausal women may practise, but they first bring the menses back, then behead them. "For those who have stopped already it is to beckon it and cause it to come back" (*Jinhua zhizhi*, 1804; p. 58).
  - **The *Nannü dangong* (p. 65).** "Once it has been observed that it [the red dragon] has come back, as soon as it is beheaded the transformation will take place."
  - **The Jinhua zhizhi:** "you use the daoyin exercises to make it come, and then ... beheading and cutting [the dragon], you make it go away" (p. 65).
  - **Readership.** Valussi takes the attention given to older women as a sign that they were "an important cohort of the audience" (p. 66).
- **The cycle has a "message" and a "tide".**
  - **Timing (*Nügong lianji huandan tushuo*).** The best time to work is between the "message" (*xin* 信: dizziness, aching waist) and the "tide" (*chao* 潮) (pp. 59–60).
  - **The dragon's head and the tiger's tail (*Kunyuanjing*).** Working before the flow is "capturing the dragon's head" (*suo longtou* 索龍頭). Working after it is "capturing the tiger's tail" (*qin huwei* 擒虎尾) (pp. 60–61).
  - **Colours.** The flow changes "from red ... into yellow ... into white ... into nothingness ... into qi" (pp. 63–64).
- **Health warnings.** Amenorrhoea from illness is *not* the goal. "If blood is depleted to the point of exhaustion, then qi will be exhausted as well" (p. 64). The work must start "from a state of replenishment and not of depletion" (p. 64).
- **The womb gestates the immortal embryo.**
  - **Locations.** The practice runs breasts → navel → womb (*zigong* 子宮, "child palace"; *xuehai* 血海, "sea of blood"). The woman's *qi* cavity is "between the breasts", not below the navel as in men (pp. 70, 76).
  - **Ten months.** Refined *qi* unites with original *qi* "to form the embryo, within a symbolic period of 10 months" (p. 76).
  - **Valussi's conclusion.** "the uterus is utilised not for the conception and the nurturing of a human foetus, but for the conception and nurturing of an immortal embryo" (p. 80). The motto is "This is where creating humans and creating immortals part ways" (p. 81).
- **Fertility is against the goal.** In nüdan, "female fertility ... has a negative valence" (p. 58). "practice for immortality took away the reproductive capabilities of a woman" (p. 52). Valussi suggests women may have done "their reproductive duties before turning to this practice" (p. 52).
- **Female immortals named in the texts:** Wei Huacun and Magu (*Jinhua zhizhi*, p. 64), He Xiangu (attributed author of the *Nügong zhengfa*, 1880) and Xiwangmu (attributed author of the 1834 precepts).
