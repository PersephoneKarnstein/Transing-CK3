## 1. What the chapter actually says

**Structure.** The chapter has two halves.
1. The Buddha tells a jātaka. In a former life he was a king who served the sage Asita for a thousand years to hear the Lotus. That sage was Devadatta, his would-be murderer, who now receives a prediction of buddhahood (K 179–181).
2. Mañjuśrī rises from the sea palace of the nāga king Sāgara with the bodhisattvas he has converted there (K 182). Prajñākūṭa asks whether anyone can become a buddha *quickly* through this sutra. Mañjuśrī names Sāgara's daughter, "only eight years old", who "instantly produced the thought of enlightenment and attained the stage of nonretrogression" (K 183).

**The sequence of events, step by step (K 183–185):**
1. **Prajñākūṭa doubts.** It took Śākyamuni kalpas, so "It is hard to believe that this girl will instantly attain complete enlightenment" (K 183).
2. **She appears and praises the Buddha** in verse, saying only a buddha can know whether she will attain enlightenment (K 183–184).
3. **Śāriputra objects.** "The female body is polluted; it is not a fit vessel for the Dharma" (K 184). Moreover "the female body has five obstructions": it cannot become Brahmā, Śakra, Māra, a *cakravartin* (universal monarch), or a buddha (K 184). In W the line reads "a woman's body is soiled and defiled, not a vessel of the Law."
4. **The jewel.** She gives the Buddha "a jewel worth the great manifold cosmos, and the Buddha accepted it." She asks whether that was quick, and they say "extremely quickly!" (K 184).
5. **"Through your transcendent powers watch me become a buddha even more quickly than that!"** (K 184). W: "Employ your supernatural powers and watch me attain Buddhahood."
6. **The change.** "the assembly there all saw the daughter of the nāga king instantly transform into a man, perfect the bodhisattva practices, go to the vimalā world in the south" (K 185). There she sits on a jeweled lotus, attains complete enlightenment "endowed with the thirty-two marks", and preaches. W: "in the space of an instant change into a man"; Watson's next sentence switches to "**he** expounded the wonderful Law". Kubo and Yuyama avoid the pronoun.
7. **Aftermath.** The whole sahā assembly "saw in the distance that the daughter of the nāga king had become a buddha" and "honored her from afar". Kubo and Yuyama keep "her" here (K 185). Prajñākūṭa, Śāriputra "and the entire assembly accepted and believed in silence" (K 185).

**Answer to design question 1.** The README is literally true but misleading. She does change her body in an instant, and the change is **from female to male**. It comes **before** she attains buddhahood, as one of a run of steps (become male → perfect the practices → go south → sit on the lotus → attain enlightenment). The sutra never answers Śāriputra's premise. It sidesteps it with a demonstration that includes becoming male. On Schuster's reading (`cn_schuster_changing_female_body.md`), the Lotus is the version where the change "does seem to be necessary".

**What the text concedes, read closely** (these are the grain-against readings available):
- **The offering was accepted from a girl's hand.** The Buddha takes the jewel *before* any change (K 184). What the Buddha does contradicts what Śāriputra claims.
- **The change is staged for the doubters.** "Watch me" is said to the two men who doubted her, and the change happens "through your transcendent powers", meaning in their sight. The proof is made for their eyes. Song Ch'an readers later placed her enlightenment at the offering of the jewel, not at the change (Levering, `cn_levering_dragon_girl.md`).
- **She remains "the daughter of the nāga king" after buddhahood.** In Kubo and Yuyama the far-off assembly honours "her" (K 185). Only Watson's "he" fixes the new sex in the pronoun.
- **Speed over merit.** The whole chapter asks whether long merit is required, and the girl's answer is no. This sits awkwardly with the gate's ten-month gestation, so use it for recognition, not for the bodily work.

**The neighbouring chapters matter as much as ch. 12:**
- **Ch. XIII, the next chapter.** The Buddha's aunt Mahāprajāpatī Gautamī, "with six thousand nuns", and the nun Yaśodharā receive predictions of buddhahood. Nobody mentions changing their bodies (K 187–188). This is the best Lotus anchor for an order of women clergy, and it sits on the very next page.
- **Ch. XXIII.** "any woman who hears and holds to this chapter ... will never be reborn with a female body" (K 285). This is hostile in effect: the reward is to stop being a woman.
- **Ch. VIII.** Pūrṇa's future buddha-land will have "no troubled states of being nor any women" (K 140; verse "There will be neither women", K 144).
- **Ch. XXIV.** The bodhisattva Gadgadasvara "has transformed himself into a female in royal harems and taught this sutra" (K 293).
- **Ch. XXV.** Avalokiteśvara (Guanyin) takes the form of a nun, a laywoman, a wife or a girl when those forms will save someone (K 298). Answer to design question 3: **the only male-to-female changes in the Lotus are a male bodhisattva's temporary *upāya* (expedient) appearances.** They are disguises for teaching, not becoming a woman. Note the setting of Gadgadasvara's: the harem.

**Chinese terms.** These come from the Taishō, which this pass did not read directly; Schuster n. 9 supports the second point. Check them on CBETA T09n0262 before using any in loc.
- The Lotus does **not** use *zhuan nü shen* 轉女身 ("turn the female body"). It says *biancheng nanzi* 變成男子, "changed into a man". *Zhuan nü shen* is the term of the Vimalakīrti and the Mahāratnakūṭa sutras.
- Śāriputra's line: 女身垢穢，非是法器, "the female body is defiled; it is not a Dharma vessel".

- "Six thousand nuns were promised buddhahood, and no one asked them to change."
