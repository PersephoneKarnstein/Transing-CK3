# Peach, "Social Responsibility, Sex Change, and Salvation" (2002), design reading

**Citation (verified against the Project MUSE PDF).** Lucinda Joy Peach, "Social Responsibility, Sex Change, and Salvation: Gender Justice in the Lotus Sūtra," *Philosophy East and West* 52.1 (January 2002): 50–74. University of Hawai'i Press. DOI 10.1353/pew.2002.0003 (MUSE article 26574).
**Read:** the whole article, pp. 50–74, with notes. Peach works from English translations of Kumārajīva's Lotus (Watson 1993, Hurvitz, Katō et al.; p. 70 note) and from Schuster 1981 and Paul 1985 for the other sutras; she cites no Chinese text and no Chinese history. Lotus page numbers below are Peach's citations of Watson.

## 1. The argument in brief

- **Question.** Does the Lotus promote "gender justice", deny it, or stay indifferent? Peach's frame is a 1998 Risshō Kōsei-kai conference on the Lotus and social responsibility (p. 70 note), i.e. modern Japanese Buddhism. Her verdict on the sex-change passage is **mainly misogynist, with qualified positives elsewhere in the sutra.**
- **Polyvocal text.** Ch. 14 says "one should not make distinctions by saying, 'This is a man,' 'This is a woman'" (p. 51), but the same chapter tells a preacher not to talk with "young girls, unmarried women or widows" nor go near "the five types of unmanly men" (p. 55). Buddha-lands in chs. 8 and 23 have "no evil paths of existence there, nor ... any women" (p. 53).
- **The dragon girl (ch. 12).** Śāriputra: "a woman's body is soiled and defiled, not a vessel for the Law", and she has "the five obstacles" (p. 56). She hands the Buddha a jewel, changes into a man "in the space of an instant", then attains enlightenment (p. 56).
- **Against Schuster's positive reading.** Schuster (1981) holds the passage refutes the idea that a female body disqualifies; Peach answers that it "affirms the very view that Schuster claims it undermines, that is, that it is necessary to have a male body in order to become a Buddha" (p. 56). Its value against Śāriputra is "dubious"; it "reinforces the belief that it is in fact necessary for females to take on a male form" (p. 57).
- **Ch. 13 as the counterweight.** Mahāprajāpatī and Yaśodharā receive predictions of buddhahood with no sex change, but "in ages to come" (p. 58). Instant-with-change vs slow-without-change is her central contrast (p. 58).
- **Ch. 23 is worst.** A woman who upholds the Medicine King chapter makes her "last appearance in a woman's body" and is greeted in Amitāyus's land as "good man" (p. 59).
- **Comparison set.** Sumati and Pure Gift (acts of truth, then male); Pure Faith (asks how to change); *Gaṅgottara*, Jewel Brocade in the *Sāgara-nāgarāja* sutra (translated 285 CE, refutes the need for change: "the thought of Enlightenment is neither male nor female", p. 65), Śrīmālā, and the Vimalakīrti goddess, who swaps bodies with Śāriputra (p. 66). The Lotus sits mid-spectrum.
- **The rule she draws.** "the 'acts' of gender transformation always follow the direction of female into male, never the reverse (at least more than temporarily, as in the Vimalakīrtinirdeśa Sūtra)" (p. 70). Male-to-female change happens "only—and unwillingly!" in the Vimalakīrti (p. 68).
- **Change is not compassion.** Against Schuster's claim the women change "to resolve a man's doubts", Peach: the changes "are actually the antithesis of compassion" because they confirm the men's error (p. 68).
- **The T564 link.** Via Paul she quotes the *Sūtra on Changing the Female Sex* (*Fo shuo zhuan nüshen jing*): by the thought of enlightenment women gain "a man's state of mind" (p. 69). This is the text Balkwill studies (see `cn_balkwill_transforming_female_form.md`).
- **Guanyin.** One line: Avalokiteśvara "underwent a gender transformation to become the female bodhisattva Kuan Yin in China" (p. 62). Unsourced beyond Sangren 1983; no date. The feminine Guanyin takes shape in the 10th–12th centuries, inside the game window, but that needs a specialist source (Yü Chün-fang) before use.
- **Slips to note.** On p. 56 she writes "a physical change from male to female" where she means female to male; on p. 65 "Pure Gift" where she means Pure Faith; on p. 58 she calls the dragon girl's change a "sex-change operation" (metaphor).
- **Relevance.** Low on history: nothing on China, Tang, Wu, Dunhuang, or reception before Saichō (767–822, p. 70), who read the girl as proof of the Lotus's power over a being "female and clearly has faculties which are not good". Note 1: 11 of 56 lives in the *Biqiuni zhuan* (4th–6th c. Chinese nuns) involve Lotus practice (p. 71).

## 3. In-world loc lines (faith or Buddhist voice; never narrator)
- Nüguan to a monk: "Your dragon girl changed in an instant, and always into a man. Jewel Brocade, her own kinswoman, would not change at all. We take ten months and change the other way."
- Hostile abbot: "A woman's body is not a vessel for the Law. You have poured yourself into the wrong vessel on purpose."
- Friendly lay Buddhist: "The Lotus says do not say 'this is a man' or 'this is a woman'. I will not say it of her."
- Faith text, on the Vimalakīrti goddess: "She looked twelve years for her womanhood and did not find it. We looked, and found ours."
