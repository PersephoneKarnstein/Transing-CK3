## 1. The argument in brief

Two debates, framed as a continuation of Cadden's *Meanings of Sex Difference*.

### A. Why did God make woman? (pp. 104–110)

- Genesis has two creation stories (Gen. 1:27; 2:21–22). Two Jewish solutions to the contradiction circulated, and the scholastics rejected both (pp. 105–106):
  - **Lilith**, the first woman, made from earth and rebellious. Theologians seem to have met the legend only at the end of the twelfth century, "probably through personal contacts with Jewish informants" (p. 105). Albertus Magnus may have had it from converts in his Paris convent (n. 10). Scholastics "dismissed it ... as a frivolous Jewish fable" (p. 105).
  - **The androgynous first human**, later cut in two. Known to scholastics only indirectly through Augustine, who did not say it was Jewish, until Nicholas of Lyra (n. 17).
- **Peter the Chanter (1190s)** rejected the androgyne because it would give "homosexuals a justification" (p. 106). His Latin gloss says God made "non androgynos, id est non hermaphroditos" (n. 19).
- **The Franciscan treatise *On the Human Body*** (late 1250s, appended to Alexander of Hales's *Summa*): the hermaphrodite embodies *confusio*, which nature abhors, and permits sex-role inversion, an *abusio* (p. 107). God made woman separate "to avoid that the union of the sexes in one and the same person causes confusion" (p. 107).
- Against Aristotle's "failed male" (*mas occasionatus*): William of Auvergne said Aristotle was not to be heeded, since calling woman an imperfect man implies a man is a perfect woman, which "smacks of the sodomitic heresy" (p. 108, n. 24). *On the Human Body* insisted, citing Augustine, that "the female sex is not a sin, but a natural state" (*non est vitium sexus femineus, sed natura*) (p. 109).
- **Resurrection.** Bodies rise sexed, without sexuality (p. 110). Hugh of Saint-Cher (1230s): a hermaphrodite rises in the sex "which nature would have given him had she not been diverted" (n. 29).
- The Cathars, who rejected the body and marriage, made this urgent (p. 109).

### B. The hermaphrodite and the sacraments (pp. 110–113)

- **Short window.** The question is "concentrated in a rather brief period", the 1170s to the early thirteenth century (p. 111). Later canonists "lost interest".
- **Canonists were calm.** They discussed sacramental access "in morally neutral tones" and took a "highly pragmatic" view that "made room for social criteria" (p. 104). Theologians writing on Genesis were the harsh ones (p. 113).
- **Anatomy mattered less than you would think.** Beards counted as much as genitals, since they could be seen "without invasive examination" (p. 110).
  - Peter the Chanter: the Church let a hermaphrodite marry by the organ that gave "the most pleasure" (p. 111; n. 33).
  - **Huguccio: behaviour decides.** If a person "has a beard and always wants to do men's work ... and always wants to be with men", the male sex prevails. If a person "lacks a beard and always wants to be with women and do women's work (*feminea opera*)", the female sex prevails (n. 34, my paraphrase of the Latin). Van der Lugt: hermaphrodites could "influence their assigned identity by their behavior" (p. 111).
  - An equally balanced person is treated as a woman, "because it cannot be said that the male element dominates" (Huguccio, n. 34; p. 112).
  - The *Summa 'Omnes homines'*: before a court the person must first swear which member they can use, and is admitted accordingly (n. 33).
- **Baptism and names.** Sex is irrelevant to baptism except for the name. Peter the Chanter: use a male name when in doubt, and "its female variant may later be used for the sacrament of confirmation" (p. 112, n. 39).
- **Marriage.** Either kind may marry a partner of the opposite dominant sex (p. 112).
- **Ordination.** Only the male-prevailing may be ordained, since women cannot be priests (p. 112). Some canonists barred even them, not for ambiguity but for "monstrosity" (Huguccio) or "propter populi scandalum", for fear of scandal among the people (Laurentius of Spain) (n. 42).
- **The limit: no changing back.** Peter the Chanter (a theologian, not a canonist): a hermaphrodite must "adopt a gender and stick to it", or stay celibate, "to avoid any resemblance with the inversion of the sodomites" (p. 113). Van der Lugt did not find this wording among the canonists. Huguccio called remarriage in the other sex a "monstrous" question (p. 113, n. 46).
- **Comparison.** Islamic law treats the *khunthā* in far more contexts (prayer, pilgrimage, washing the dead, silk, inheritance), which she reads as reflecting "the more fundamental importance of gender distinctions" there (p. 111). Canon law was "relatively egalitarian": a woman could testify in a church court though not for a Roman-law will (p. 111).

## 3. Corrections to current docs

- **`comparanda_reading_list.md`, Latin section, item 18.** "Theologians and canonists on hermaphrodites and on women's ordination" is misleading. The article is about (a) the creation of woman in Genesis commentary and (b) the hermaphrodite's access to baptism, marriage and ordination. Women's ordination appears only as the premise (women cannot be priests, p. 112). Suggested wording: "Use: reaction. Genesis commentary on why God made woman (Peter the Chanter, William of Auvergne, *On the Human Body*), and canonists on the hermaphrodite and the sacraments: Huguccio's behavioural test, female names at confirmation, the theologians' sodomy charge (pp. 104–113)." Pages 101–121 are right. It is now downloaded and read.
- **Latin finding 1 (Peter the Chanter).** Add that van der Lugt calls the Chanter a theologian and did not find his sodomy wording among the canonists (p. 113). The "swear to one sex" practice is canonist; the sodomy rationale is theological.
- **Latin finding 1, design use.** "A Catholic court can *register* a daughter. It will not accept that she *changed*." Van der Lugt sharpens this: the canonists already accepted that conduct shapes which sex prevails (p. 111). What they refused was *switching back and forth* (p. 113). So a daughter who changes once and keeps to it fits the canonists' frame better than the note implies. The objection is to remarriage in the other sex.

## 4. Relevance to the Jewish sect

- The *androginos* the sect's founders might cite (the reading list's finding 2) has a Christian afterlife: the scholastics rejected the androgynous Adam for moral reasons and Peter the Chanter tied it to sodomy (p. 106). A Christian scholar arguing against *Ha-Nehpakhot* would have reached for exactly this.
- **Risk.** Van der Lugt reports the schools dismissing Lilith as a "frivolous Jewish fable" (p. 105). A Christian event that mocks the sect's Talmudic precedents (Dinah, the *androginos*) as "Jewish fables" would be accurate to period and would reproduce period contempt. If used at all, it must be framed as the speaker's prejudice and answered in the sect's own voice. Safer: give the Christian theologian the doctrinal objection only ("God made them male and female, not both").
- **Not an evidence base for the sect.** Nothing here shows Jewish practice. It shows Christian reception of Jewish exegesis.

## 5. Design implications

- **Event: "The Canonist's Questions" (Catholic diocese, c. 1170–1300).** Fires when a daughter or declared sister lives in a county whose bishop is Catholic. The bishop asks three questions drawn from the canonists: whom does she keep company with, what work does she do, will she swear to keep to one sex.
  - (a) Answer all three: the bishop registers her as a woman (minor opinion gain with the bishop; modifier `tm_registered_by_canonist`, "Registered by the Canonists", +5 opinion from Catholic clergy, 20 years).
  - (b) Refuse the oath: theologian path (opinion malus; the bishop reports her to the archbishop).
  - Loc for (a): "Her company is women and her work is women's work. By Huguccio's rule the woman prevails."
- **Decision flavour.** Under a Catholic liege, Declare Before Witnesses offers the line "at confirmation, the woman's form of her name". Flavour only; no extra mechanics.
- **Opinion pair.** Catholic clergy who are canonists (learning-focused) take a smaller malus than theologians (zealous trait). Unverified whether the trait split is worth the scripting cost; flag for the user.
- **Quotable in-world lines.**
  - "If she keeps the company of women and does women's work, the woman prevails." (Catholic canonist, after Huguccio, n. 34)
  - "Choose one, and keep it; the Church fears only the turning back." (Catholic bishop, after Peter the Chanter, p. 113)
  - "The female sex is no fault, but nature." (A Sister quoting the schools back at a bishop; *On the Human Body*, p. 109)
  - "God made them male and female, not both in one." (Catholic theologian; Peter the Chanter's gloss, n. 19)
