# Nirta, *Marginal Bodies, Trans Utopias* (2018): design reading

**Citation.** Caterina Nirta, *Marginal Bodies, Trans Utopias*. Routledge Advances in Critical Diversities 6 (series editors Yvette Taylor and Sally Hines). Abingdon and New York: Routledge, 2018. ISBN 978-1-138-22289-2 (hbk); 978-1-138-40022-1 (ebk). Front matter, l.66-131. The author is a senior lecturer in social sciences at the University of Roehampton (back cover, l.63). The book grew out of her PhD at the University of Westminster (Acknowledgements; "this PhD", l.1611).

**How cited.** Line of the plain-text file, with the printed page where the running head shows it, e.g. "l.2198, p. 45". The text stays in the session scratchpad, not in the repo.

## The argument in brief

1. **Utopia is now, not later.** Utopia is usually a place that does not exist yet. Nirta argues that a utopia put off to the future loses its force, because the "not-yet" may never come. To keep "its promise and be a generative force, it desperately needs to be located in the present" (l.307, p. 3). She wants an ethics that is "an act of the present, in the present and for the present" (l.311, p. 3), and cites Deleuze: "Only the present exists" (l.317).
2. **Against Muñoz's hopeful futurity, and against Edelman's refusal of a future** (ch. 2). She reads Bloch's hope and Muñoz's *Cruising Utopia* as a limbo. Muñoz's archive of performers and artists does not reach the lives in her diaries: "Muñoz's queer utopia resembles escapism: it is a luxury that not very many individuals can afford" (l.2198, p. 45). Those lives are "a life where nothing can be assumed, but everything is to be conquered every day" (l.2203, p. 45). She also rejects Bersani and Edelman's "anti-social" turn, which refuses community and the future.
3. **Recognition is a trap** (ch. 3). The Gender Recognition Act 2004 asks for a diagnosis of dysphoria, two years "living in the acquired gender", and evidence that the applicant "intends to continue to live in the acquired gender until death", judged by a panel of lawyers and doctors (l.2949-2985, pp. 62-63). The panel's ideas of what a woman looks like decide the case. Her summary: "the more gender is visible and therefore recognisable, the more it is governed and controlled" (l.3014, p. 63). Hegel's recognition makes the self depend on whoever grants it. She prefers Grosz's "politics of imperceptibility" (l.5179, p. 114).
4. **Against intersectionality.** She says it multiplies categories and so "minorities within the minority" (ch. 3, l.3399-3440, p. 72).
5. **Against a third box, too.** A recognised third gender, or a unisex toilet, may help in the short term. But if it is run by the old logic it stays an "average" space (l.3487, p. 73). Of toilets: "the unisex toilet may indeed provide some short-term management of the issue, but what is needed is a new direction" (l.5212, p. 115).
6. **Who guards the women's room** (ch. 4). Sex-segregated toilets were built in Victorian Britain to rebuild "separate spheres" in public (l.4700-4735, pp. 104-105). The case for them rests on privacy, safety and hygiene. Nirta reads these as cover for a "sanitisation of gender" (l.5126, p. 113). With Prosser and Halberstam she finds women's toilets more hostile than men's: "The bathroom is still an extension of home for women", "a sanctuary" (l.4961-4962, p. 110). There, "feminine appearing females that look proper are those who mostly feel entitled to judge different kinds of femininity" (l.4971-4974). Passing gives safety but also erasure; being seen brings surveillance (after Jamison Green, l.4870-4880, p. 108).
7. **The monster** (ch. 5). The trans body is cast as a monster, and the monster is the mirror of the norm: "the subject is always somebody else's monster" (l.6031, p. 137). After Sharpe, she notes a 13th-century legal line between the deformed, whom the law counts as human, and the monster, whom it does not (l.6092, p. 138).
8. **Conclusion.** "Marginal" means two things. It means being pushed out: "Marginal subjectivities on the periphery of life" (l.7698, p. 176). It also means power: "the term marginal has become synonymous with potent. Bodies that do not succumb" (l.7704-7705). The book ends: "Away from identity politics, from intersectionality, relationality, essentialisms" (l.7768, p. 177).

**Who writes.** "As a non-transgender individual" (l.1664, p. 33), working from Deleuze, Derrida, Grosz and Braidotti. The trans people in the book appear through public video diaries, not interviews.

## Relation to existing notes

- **Raha & van der Drift** (`book_raha_vdd_trans_femme_futures.md`). Close in one respect: Raha and van der Drift want "a futurity that starts today, not in a promise of a better tomorrow". Nirta's ch. 2 is the long theory of that line. Both distrust recognition and the third legal box. They differ on community. Raha and van der Drift build collectives, houses and mutual care. Nirta's utopia sits in the single body ("must find a site within the subject", l.2558, p. 53), and she quotes Philippopoulos-Mihalopoulos that there "can be nothing collective about Utopia" (l.2553). For the Houses, Raha and van der Drift remain the main source. Nirta adds only the present-tense frame and the analysis of who guards a women's room.
- **Bhatt** (`book_bhatt_brown_trans_les.md`, `book_bhatt_chu.md`, `book_bhatt_third_sex.md`). Nirta is the kind of theory Bhatt argues against, on three counts:
  - *Intersectionality.* Bhatt (ch. 2) defends Crenshaw as compatible with radical feminism. Nirta rejects intersectionality as a machine for making categories (l.3399ff).
  - *Categories.* Nirta's answer to the box is to stop asking for recognition and become "imperceptible". Bhatt's answer is that "not believing in them doesn't stop others from trying to shove you in anyway" (Bhatt, Concl. l.1000). The mod follows Bhatt: Declare Before Witnesses names a daughter, and the gate keeps the name (faith_design §12).
  - *Male supremacy.* Nirta puts the root of hostility in heterosexuality's "fear of contamination" by desire and in binary thinking (l.5010-5022, p. 111). She never names men as the group that benefits. Bhatt's conclusion says no analysis can leave men out. And in ch. 4 Nirta's sharpest picture of policing is of women judging other women. Bhatt also has cis women policing the line (ch. 1, l.89), but she puts it inside a system run for men.
  - *Where they agree.* Both refuse the third box (Nirta l.3487, l.5212; Bhatt's "Third Sex"). Both refuse to see trans women only through the clinic.
- **Serano** (`book_serano_whipping_girl.md`). Nirta cites Serano's "pathetic" and "deceptive" transsexual types (l.6118-6125, p. 139). Nothing new.
- **Stryker and the TSR notes.** Nirta's Stone, Prosser and Namaste material (Intro, l.560-625) is already covered there.
- **`book_transgender_marxism.md`** (Hoad's "rehearsal for utopia"; Rosenberg's "One Utopia, One Dystopia"). Same theme, materialist rather than Deleuzian. Nirta adds nothing for the Bread and Roses or Rehearsal for Utopia rows.
- **Medieval and Karras notes.** `hist_karras_medieval_sexuality.md` (E208) rejects both a golden age and "one big earth festival". Nirta's ch. 2 gives the same warning about the future.

**What the book does not cover.** It has nothing before 1765 except one 13th-century law remark taken from Sharpe (l.6092) and a passing Foucault line on the Middle Ages (l.6084). It has nothing on religion, Byzantium, the North, China, Judaism, India or West Africa. One sentence lists "Masai, Nandi, Nuba" ritual cross-dressing with no source (l.6129); see Cautions. Trans men appear in the diaries; they are out of scope.

## Usable material

1. **Present-tense utopia** (ch. 2; Concl. l.7600 "utopia is already, here and now"). It fits the Houses, which are present institutions (strain, the Little Work, splits) rather than promises. It extends §16 rule 2 (no golden past) to the other end: no golden future.
2. **The permanence clause.** The GRA's "until death" test (l.2958) is the modern form of a gate that asks a woman to swear she will never go back. The knife already answers it well: to "Will I ever repent?" (`tm_knife.0004`) she may say "Not today."
3. **The modesty plea.** Exclusion from a women's room is justified as protecting the other women's privacy, decency and cleanliness, and the protection is the cover (l.5087-5130, pp. 112-113). The mod's built bath event (`tm_world.0050`, "The Women's Hour") already has a keeper of this kind. Nirta gives a name for the shape.
4. **The "safe room that is not a woman's room."** Nirta's case against the unisex toilet and the third gender as short-term management (l.3487, l.5212) supports the built `tm_world.0041` ("It is a safe place. It is not a woman's place.") as written.
5. **Passing as safety and erasure.** After Green (l.4870-4880): passing protects, but it removes her from view. This bears on the open E3/H25 conflict in `beliefs.md` (visibility vs the veil, item 8 of the consequences list).
6. **Policing by the respectable.** Women who "look proper" police the door (l.4971). This is usable only inside Bhatt's frame (degraded classes police the line to keep their place), never as "women are the enemy".
7. **Hostile pattern: the earned title.** Nirta quotes Robin Morgan's 1973 speech refusing to call a trans woman "sister" because she had not earned the title by suffering (l.475-484, p. 7). The *pattern* (sisterhood as a title earned by years) suits a vanilla abbess; the speech itself is modern and is not quoted in loc.
8. **Hostile pattern: deformed vs monstrous.** A 13th-century legal distinction, via Sharpe (l.6092). Usable for an English or Latin jurist after c. 1250 *only after* the original (presumably Bracton) is found and read. Nirta gives no primary citation.

## Cautions

1. **Not trans-authored, and not materialist in Bhatt's sense.** Nirta writes as a cis academic (l.1664). Her frame is Deleuze's "becoming" and "multiplicity", and it prizes the trans person who refuses the binary over the one who wants to be a woman. She says the GRA serves those "well served by the gender binary" (l.3442) and saves her sympathy for others. The mod's daughters are women. Do not import her "beyond the binary", "n-sexes" (Deleuze and Guattari, quoted l.3661) or "transgender as the failure of gender" (l.7582) into any faith's voice. That is the romantic "third" framing in another form.
2. **Against Bhatt on intersectionality and on male supremacy.** See Relation above. Where they conflict, the mod follows Bhatt (§16).
3. **Women as gatekeepers.** Ch. 4's line on "feminine appearing females" judging others (l.4971) must not become a pattern of cis women as the main enemy. Hostile women in the mod are always placed inside a system that rewards their policing (Bhatt ch. 1, l.89), and the Houses take cis women in as sisters.
4. **The anti-social turn.** Ch. 2 discusses Bersani's sex as "a death drive" and Edelman's slogan against "the Child" (l.2155-2170, pp. 44-45). Use none of it. The first is near the no-self-harm rule, and the second plays straight into the child-lifter slander (§16 rule 4).
5. **Eliminationist quotations.** The book quotes Raymond and Daly, including the wish to mandate transsexuality "out of existence" (l.6584, p. 149), and Morgan's 1973 speech (l.475-500). These are modern voices, not vanilla faiths. Never put them in loc, even attributed. Row-level patterns (the abbess's earned title) are paraphrased and reattributed only.
6. **History claims to leave alone.**
   - "Many children were killed because their sex was ambivalent at birth" (l.6113-6114) has no period or place. It is not used (no text about a Marked child's body, §16 rule 5).
   - The list of "Masai, Nandi, Nuba" ritual cross-dressing (l.6129) is an unsourced generalisation about living peoples. It must not reach Badenya or any reaction text.
   - The 13th-century deformity/monstrosity line (l.6092) is second-hand; verify it (row 8).
   - Medical history (forced treatments, l.404) and the Victorian toilet history (l.4700ff) are outside the period.
7. **Private people.** The diary sessions quote YouTube diarists by first name and age. They are not used or named here, and nothing from them should reach loc.
8. **"Monster" reclaimed.** Ch. 5's reclaiming of the monster follows Stryker. Under §16 rule 9 it may be voiced only by a daughter, and a period word needs a period source.
9. **Standing rules.** Trans men are out of scope (the diaries include them). No antagonist: every hostile line above belongs to a vanilla speaker. She/her for trans women throughout.
