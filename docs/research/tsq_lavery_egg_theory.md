# Lavery, "Egg Theory's Early Style" (TSQ 2020): design reading

**Citation.** Grace Lavery, "Egg Theory's Early Style," *TSQ: Transgender Studies Quarterly* 7, no. 3 (August 2020): 383–398, in the section "(Un)Disciplining Trans* Knowledges". DOI 10.1215/23289252-8553034. © 2020 Duke University Press.

**What was read.** The whole article, notes and references included (`scratchpad/books/tr/15.txt`). Page numbers are the journal's.

**Quotes.** Verbatim, each 25 words or fewer, checked against the text.

**Why it matters here.** The README calls the *Marked by the Mother* trait a mark on trans women "before any rite, eggs who do not know it yet among them". Belief H15 is titled "Crack the egg". This essay is about exactly that word and the claim it makes.

---

## 1. Argument in brief

- **An egg exists only looking back.** "One only becomes an egg in retrospect, when one has hatched" (p. 384). Lavery resented being told she had been an egg: thirty-four years "are more than mere latency" (p. 384). The label is a reading, and "any such deployments reflect the observer's judgment as much as they depict the egg" (p. 384). "Eggs are not liars, again by definition" (p. 384).
- **Egg theory.** The abstract defines it as "the type of reasoning that trans people use, prior to transition, to prove transition's impossibility or fruitlessness" (p. 383). "Egg theory is not generally ethical, but technical. One simply cannot." (p. 384). It turns into a vague universalism that must say transition "is both impossible and inevitable" (p. 385).
- **Two cases.** A punk manifesto by Sybil Lamb parodies the egg's voice: transition is rare, ruinous and a "government plot" (pp. 385–386). Eve Sedgwick's "White Glasses" claims an identification with gay men by "stubborn magical defiance" while keeping transition at arm's length (pp. 388–389).
- **Queer universalism as egg theory.** When a reviewer says trans experience shows a potential "for all of us", trans becomes a part of queer and its difference vanishes: the move works by "eradicating the distinctiveness of one of them" (p. 392). The reviewer quoted is Greta LaFleur (2019), a contributor to *Feminism against Cisness*.
- **The claim to know.** The slogan "protect trans kids" can slide into treating children as property (Gill-Peterson, p. 395). And "the very idea of the egg, as I began by saying, seems to imply a claim to know others better than they know themselves" (pp. 395–396): a claim that, "however functionally indispensable to queer and trans relations, can hardly be generalized or scaled" (p. 396).
- **Transition.** Like a "second puberty" (p. 396), "transition rarely works perfectly; the more remarkable fact is that it works at all" (p. 396).
- **Freud and Schreber.** Freud cast Schreber's wish to become a woman as hidden desire for men. Lavery notes Freud's own wording leaves open that the change was possible, only slow: "a process which may occupy many years or even decades" (p. 393).

## 2. Relation to other notes

- **Bhatt.** No direct overlap. Bhatt's epistemic vandalism (ch. 8) and Lavery's "claim to know others better" both describe outsiders who override what a woman says of herself; here the outsider may be a friendly sister. Freud recasting a wish to be a woman as hidden desire for men is the move Bhatt traces in Benjamin and the autogynephilia literature (ch. 8, l.850–901).
- **Heaney volume.** Lavery's critique of universalism cuts against Heaney's "every single one of us" outside cisness and Velocci's "perhaps none at all" (`book_heaney_feminism_against_cisness.md` §2.1, §2.5, §5 row 13).
- **Serano.** Close to Serano on gatekeeping scarcity and the "real transsexual" card (`serano_blog_essays.md` #24, #58): Lamb's "1:12000" is egg theory's admission test.
- **§16.** Rule 1 ("the Mother's mark knows; the world's mark watches") sets who may *know*. Lavery says even a sister's knowing is a claim that "can hardly be generalized or scaled". Rule 5 (no text about a Marked child's future body) already limits it.

## 4. Cautions

- **Self-harm imagery.** Lamb's text describes a detransitioned figure tearing out a beard (pp. 386–387), and Lavery describes closeted women who can barely stop the drive from "pulling their ribs out of their flesh" (p. 396). Not for loc, even paraphrased.
- **Trans men out of scope.** The Lou Sullivan letter (p. 390) and Sedgwick's identification with gay men give nothing to build.
- **Slurs.** Lamb's manifesto is full of reclaimed slurs. None goes into loc (§16 rule 9: reclaimed words only in a daughter's voice, and these are modern English).
- **Named critics.** Note 2 lists anti-trans academics and a comedian. Nothing from them is used.
