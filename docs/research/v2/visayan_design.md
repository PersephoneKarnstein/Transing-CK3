## 1. Premise

1. **What it is.** A community of women at the court of the datu of Bohol and Cebu who keep the paganito (spirit sacrifice) with the old women and count among themselves the *daitan*: women assigned male at birth whose womanhood the old women recognised. It is a marked reconstruction. No text on religion or gender in the Philippines survives from before 1521, which is 343 years after the bookmark. Every rite in this design is attested only for 1521–1668 and is projected back by the narrator, who says so.
2. **Why Cebu.** Pigafetta's April 1521 hog rite at Cebu is the earliest dated religious text from the islands. It was kept by "two very old women", and "no one but old women consecrate the flesh of the hog" (D9–11). The Visayan record puts the asog inside a priesthood that was "most commonly women" (S1; A3, A12).
3. **The reading.** Brewer: femininity was "the vehicle to the spirit world" (B38). Ngu asks why asog femininity should count as "less than the performance of femininity by women" (N8). Ngu places the asog as "more like women than like men" and uses *they/them* (N9). Ngu does **not** call them women. Alcina: the asog "considered themselves more like women than like men" in life, relationships and work (A8). In its own voice the faith qualifies its daughters by **femininity lived as womanhood**: the women's wrap, the women's work, the women's line in the dance. The narrator does not say the asog were women and does not say they were a third sex.
4. **Insider voice.** Brenda Alegre (2022), a transpinay psychologist, calls the asog her "TransCestors" (D30). The faith treats the old asog the same way, as **kin, not proof** (rule 19). Her "matriarchal" past and her modern deity readings (Bulan and Sidapa as lovers, Lakapati as trans) are not used (rule 2; D30).
5. **Who it is not.** It is not the bakla, bayot or transpinay communities of Cebu today, and not the babaylan-revival spirituality ("babaylan feminism", Filipino-American babaylan groups). It is never named "Babaylan". It is no one's ancestors (rule 14; README line).
6. **How it plays differently** (memory: faiths should feel different). The world is diplomacy and the feast, not refuge or war:

| | Lambong |
|---|---|
| Gate | The Road (a kinswoman, a friend or a diwata's choosing), the Lambong (a season of women's work), the Hog (recognition by two old women) |
| Line | Apprenticeship (*alabay*) to an older babaylan, "frequently a relative" (S11). Bilateral equal inheritance (S38). Take as Daughter |
| World | The paganito economy (fees, the hog, the shore raft), funerals and secondary burial, envoys, the Song and Bornean trade, raids kept off-screen |
| Skills | Diplomacy, learning |
| Era | No arc inside the game: no suppressor reaches Cebu before 1453. Islam in Sulu is late and *date unverified*; Spain is after 1453 |

**Language caution.** "No early Cebuano lexicon is known" (Scott n. 10, p. 278). Every ritual word here (*daitan*, *alabay*, *taruk*, *bodyong*, *paganito*, *mapuraw*) is Waray (Sánchez 1617) or Hiligaynon (Méntrida 1637). The note says so.

### 3.2 Religion placement

- **The faith:** `tm_lambong = { main_rite = tm_lambong_rite; historical = no; faith_details = { religion = dayawism_religion … } }`. It is a second faith inside vanilla `dayawism_religion`, beside `dayawism` (`faith_types/00_faith_types.txt` l. 1427), the way Ha-Nehpakhot sits beside Rabbinic Judaism.
- **The religion supplies:** `pagan_hostility_doctrine`, `doctrine_polytheist`, `doctrine_no_head`, `doctrine_theocracy_temporal`, pluralistic, and the virtues (brave, just, diligent) and sins (craven, arbitrary, lazy). Vanilla Dayawist observers react mildly. The friction comes from datu politics, not doctrine.
- **`unreformed_faith_doctrine`:** vanilla's `dayawism` rite carries it. No mod faith does (Badenya inside `west_african_religion` omits it). Omit it, following that precedent.
- **The rite:** `tm_lambong_rite` carries the tenets and doctrine overrides, as `tm_badenya_rite` does.

### 3.3 Head of faith

**`doctrine_no_head`** (from the religion, kept). The sources show no hierarchy over the babaylan. Plasencia's male "bishop" (*sonat*) is Tagalog, comes from old men only, and is doubted by Brewer (B31). The House's Elder Sister is the only office above a babaylan.

### 3.7 Clergy, words and god names

- **Priest:** *babaylan* for women. This is the Visayan term (S p. 84). Vanilla's swap (`dayawism_priest_male: "katalonan"`) puts the Tagalog word on Visayans; the faith's own loc fixes this for itself only. The alternative *baylan* (Loarca, Chirino; S p. 168) would avoid echoing the revival movements (§8.4).
- **Apprentice:** *alabay* (S11). **Daughter:** *daitan* (S9; A10). **Elders:** "the old women" (Pigafetta's phrase).
- **God-name keys:** vanilla gives Tagalog names (Maykapal, Lakapati, Mayari). Proposed overrides, by function:
  - HouseholdGod: *the umalagad* (ancestor spirits, S22).
  - Death and crossing: *Siburanen* (Loarca via S32). Pandaki, who redeems souls through paganito (S32), is written as a rite, not a promise (rule 15).
  - Fate: *Sidapa*, who marks each newborn's span on a tree on Mt Mayas (Panay; D15). Not a lover of Bulan (§1.4).
  - HighGod and the rest: *the diwata*, generically, until Loarca is read directly (§9).

## 4. The gate and the office, step by step

**Who:** an adult (16+) of the faith or of vanilla Dayawism who is Marked or declared. Decision: **Take the Road** (`tm_lambong_road_decision`). Courtiers can reach it through the growth engine's courtier offer (§5.4). No cost in stress, and no stress or illness requirement (rule 22). Every step offers **"Not today."** and keeps her progress (rule 23).

### Step 1. The Road (choose one)

| Road | Who opens it | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The kinswoman** | A babaylan who is her relative, at court or in the county | Apprentices joined "some older babaylan, frequently a relative" (S11) | The quickest road |
| **The friend** | Any babaylan of the faith at court with opinion ≥ 20 | Chirino 1604: the office passed "through special friendship, or kinship, or as a legacy" (D18) | "Legacy" is folded in: a dying babaylan may name her (on_death hook) |
| **The diwata's choosing** | No one. A dream event; a yearly chance of about 15% while she waits | "Because the diwata chose them for this ministry, they believed" (A11); *daitan* means "befriended" by her diwata (S9) | Always reachable, so the road never closes for want of a teacher. The diwata chooses her **for the work**. It does not cause the Mark (rule 4) |

- **Excluded road: illness.** Scott says babaylan "came to their calling through attacks of illness or insanity" (S10). The source's commonest road is barred by the mod's rule 22, not missing from the record. The design note says so. A world-sent fever may be flavour, never a step. The death-trance (*linahos*, S24) is not a step either.
- **Excluded:** muteness. Alcina's witness at Sulat is a real person, and nobody became asog by falling mute (§10).

### Step 2. The Lambong (a season of women's work)

From the first day she wears the lambong, the women's foot-length wrap, recorded at Cebu in 1565 (S p. 29; A14). The text frames it as what women wear, never against the men's loincloth (rule 24). Progress counts work done (+1 each); she needs **4 of 6**. Nothing resets it, and a raid or a death in her house only delays it (rule 22; Serano).

| Work | Source |
|---|---|
| Weave on the backstrap loom | "by women or male transvestites" (S3; Scott's wording); A9 |
| Sell the cloth at market | Spinning, weaving and "the marketing of their products" were women's work (S pp. 72–73) |
| Make pots | "Also a female craft" (S pp. 72–73) |
| Work the swidden | "Weaving and cultivating" (A9) |
| Dance *taruk* or *kigal* in the women's line at a feast | S21 |
| Dress her hair in the women's rosette with switches | A14 |

- **Mapuraw:** she stays uninked. "All the Bisayan men usually tattooed themselves, except those who were called asug" (A1). Scott says such people were "socially acceptable" (S2). This is not a task, only a check that she has not taken the warrior's ink. In the faith's voice: *she was never owed the warrior's ink.*
- **Who keeps count:** the teacher of Road 1, or for the diwata's road "the old women" of the court (any two women of the faith over 40).

### Step 3. The Hog (recognition by women)

Event `tm_lambong.0003`. It needs **two elder babaylan** of the faith or of Dayawism (women over 40) at court or in the county. If they are missing, the rite **waits** (rechecked yearly; the diwata road's dream can bring a travelling babaylan). It never fails.

1. **The place:** decked with branches and cloths; red blossoms, fish, rice and millet cakes; a fattened hog bound on a mat; gongs, drums, porcelain plates (S14).
2. **The two old women:** one wears "a kerchief with two horns" (D9) or a horned headdress (S15). Both carry *bodyong* bamboo trumpets (S18). They bow to the sun (D9) and dance *taruk* round the hog in a spirit dialogue (S15). The horned one speaks with the diwata, and the other answers.
3. **The spear:** the horned one feints, then thrusts the hog through the heart (S16; D10). The spear stays an old woman's. This answers Ngu's "masculine components" point (N13–15) from inside the Cebu source: at Cebu a woman held the lance.
4. **The blood:** Pigafetta's officiant marks "first the foreheads of their husbands, and then the others" (D10). **Adaptation, marked as such:** her forehead is marked among the women. The bloodied mat is burned (S16).
5. **The meal:** the two women "invite only women [to eat with them]" (D11). She eats with them. Meat goes to the shore altar (*latangan*) and is set adrift on a raft (S17).
6. **Result:** trait `tm_daughter_of_the_lambong` (Daitan). Re-embodiment under the game rule. A name event (§8.9). If she is to be babaylan, the flesh the spear touched is hers (S17).

- **Excluded variant:** Colín's "girl of the best appearance … to give the spear-thrust" (D24). It is Tagalog (1663), and a girl may be a minor.

### Office and standing after the Hog

- **Babaylan (holy).** Chosen by the diwata or taught by an elder (S11; A11). Work:
  - healing for fees: choice cuts, the head, heirloom porcelain from datus (S12). The words *agaru*, *tawag* and *bawi* (S19);
  - paganito for crops, newlyweds, livestock, weather, war and the dead (S20);
  - cleansing the bones at secondary burial (S30).
- **Non-holy roads** (rule 7):
  - **Weaver-trader:** cloth sold at market and to Chinese buyers (S pp. 70, 72–76).
  - **Mourner:** the old women's dirges (S27).
  - **Envoy:** the timawa "were sent as his emissaries to open marriage negotiations" (S36), joined to Méntrida's asog as "ministers of the diwatas, sent with messages" (D19). A daughter in a timawa's role is **invented**. Court position `tm_rower_of_the_diwata` (displayed "Rower of the Diwata"; *test* whether a datu-level court may hold it).
  - **Wife:** she may marry under the faith's doctrines. The only Visayan text on companionship with men (A6) is in a hostile frame, so the faith draws on none of it.
- **The line:** `tenet_tm_alabay` teaching; Take as Daughter (adoption of a brother's daughter is attested, S38). Bolinao's "only after they were married" is Zambal and is dropped.

## 8. Open decisions (recommendation first)

1. **Witchcraft:** **`doctrine_witchcraft_shunned`** (recommended; Visayans executed suspected witches and sorcerers, S25). Alternatives: keep vanilla `_accepted` (less friction with Dayawist neighbours, against the record), or `_crime` (too close to the executions being shown). **User decision.**
2. **Name and key:** *The Lambong*, `tm_lambong` (recommended), or one of §3.1's alternatives. The spelling *lambong* needs Scott p. 29 checked.

4. **Priest title:** *babaylan* (recommended; the Visayan word, S p. 84) or *baylan* (keeps distance from the revival's word).
5. **Marriage:** `doctrine_concubines` (recommended, S38) or vanilla monogamy (Chirino calls polygamy "very rare").
