## 1. Verdict (six lines)

1. **Yes, the faith stands without Young.** Every claim it uses is now read in a primary text with a page number. Young would add synthesis and the Kriwe debate, not new evidence.
2. **It rests on four texts:**
   - Dusburg III.5 (1326): lots, sacred groves, fields and waters, hospitality, cremation, and spinning for the gods;
   - Dusburg III.4: a "lady held holy and a prophetess" (hostile exemplum);
   - the Treaty of Christburg (1249), via the thesis: funeral speakers and the harvest idol Curche;
   - Helmold I.1 (written c. 1170, at the bookmark): sacred groves and springs closed to Christians.
3. **Henry is comparanda only.** He adds the lot, horse and funeral customs of neighbouring Baltic and Finnic peoples, and the clearest record of how the crusade treated women. For the Prussians themselves he gives two transit mentions.
4. **Two lines carry all the gender weight:**
   - *Mulieres et viri solebant nere* (Dusburg III.5);
   - the Galindian *domina … sacra et prophetissa* (III.4), a hostile morality tale.

   Nothing else in either text touches women's cult.
5. **No source shows gender crossing among any Baltic people.** The one crossing phrase in Dusburg (III.48) describes *Christian* townswomen taking men's arms at Elbing. It runs the wrong way and is out of scope. The faith remains an openly invented inversion.
6. **Thinness: high.** It is enough for a small landed faith built on two attested domestic acts (spinning, lots) and a funeral rite. It is not enough for any claim about real Prussian women's religious office.

### Corrections to the first pass

1. **Row 16, spinning.** It is III.5: Latin p. 28, Italian p. 38 (Toeppen p. 54 stands).
   - The verb is **past**: *solebant nere*, "used to spin", not "are accustomed". Dusburg sets it among *former* customs, against the present-tense ones.
   - Women come **first**: *Mulieres et viri*.
   - It sits in a list of **individual** choices (*aliqui … alii*): bathing or not, linen or wool, which horse colour to avoid. That makes it personal devotion, not a communal rite, and it fits a personal-vow gate better than a public one.
2. **Row 17, the prophetess.** The word is *prophetissa*, not *phitonissa*. She is a *domina* (a lady, a mistress) "sacra et prophetissa".
   - The mass enslavement is done by the Sudovians and neighbours. The Christians kill the unarmed men.
   - Usable: the women go to her together; she is held holy; the affairs of the land are ruled by her word; *compatiens sexui suo*; she summons the leading men.
   - Never usable: infanticide, mutilation, the unarmed march, the massacre.
3. **Row 20, wives.** The feet are washed ***singulis diebus***, every day, not "on set days". She also "does not eat at table with him". Dusburg says bride purchase is still in use in 1326.
4. **Row 18, the Criwe.**
   - The lance mark is on **the Criwe's own lintel**, as proof the dead passed his house.
   - Three scholars Bugiani cites doubt that the Criwe existed. Do not model any office on him.
   - **Romow's placement at `b_insterburg` is design only.**
5. **Rows 24–28, Henry.** Chapters and pages are now Brundage's: I.10 pp. 27–28 (H3, H5); XII.2 pp. 79–80 (H16); XXIV.5 p. 194 (H35); XXIII.9 p. 185 (H32).
6. **Row 22, Christburg.** The thesis confirms the wording "mendacissimos histriones … quasi gentilium sacerdotes in exequiis". The horse-and-hawk vision clause was not seen here and stays as read in Philippi (first pass).
7. **New in-period voices for 1178:** Helmold I.1 (D30) and Kadłubek (D31). Use these instead of the generic "Bishop Fulco" line.

## 4. Design hooks, revised

**What changed from the first pass:**
- The ash rite is **Rügen-only** (Saxo). With no source for a Wendish flight, keep it only if the user keeps the invented exile thread.
- The Prussian texts give their own chance rite: **lots, "whether it will go well or ill"** (D9).
- Henry's Rügen prince of 1219 (H33) is Christian and fights for the Danes. He is a hostile-era cameo, not a link to the exiles.

### Gate: "Linen or Wool" (rows D14, D9, D29)
1. She chooses her fibre, linen or wool. Per D14 the choice is hers and both are pleasing.
2. She spins it before the household gods at the harvest feast (Curche's day, D29).
3. The House casts lots *utrum bene vel male*. *Bene* means the gods accept her; *male* means "not this harvest".
4. "Not today" is honoured equally (rules 22–23). There is no pain test and no outside judge.

*Invented:* that spinning makes a daughter; the lots' meaning; the feast as the gate's day.

### Office: "the Lady Held Holy" (from D2: *domina … sacra*)
- She is a House elder whose word orders the household's affairs.
- Dusburg's tale is a hostile exemplum; say so in loc.
- The faith's line on it can be: "They wrote that she ruled Galindia and then emptied it. They never wrote what she pitied."
- Do **not** call her a prophetess in the gate's mechanics (Bædling overlap).

*Invented:* that she is a daughter, and that the office is held by sisters.

### Doctrines (on the vanilla `baltic_pagan` baseline)
- **Sacred groves, fields and waters** (D4, D30). Not cut, tilled or fished; Christians kept out. This gives a holding modifier and an event hook.
- **Hospitality** (D11, D13, D30). Guests share all that the house has; the poor eat where they like; wife and husband drink with the guest.
- **"We sell no daughter."** This inverts D12 and D29. The faith never says it was first; Christburg is the hostile record.
- **Cremation with the tools of one's office** (D6). A sister is burned with her spindle. *Invented:* the spindle as her "office".
- **Funeral speakers** (D29, D7). A *tulisson* names the dead woman's dress and girdle as she passes, and finds the mark on the lintel.
  - It is flavour, not a seer gate.
  - Dress per rule 24: girdle, hair and colour.
- **Oaths** by the gods' power (D22), or by trampling swords (H25, Liv and Lett: comparanda).

### Holy sites
- `torun` (vanilla, eminent) and `pokaini` (vanilla, eminent).
- `c_allenstein`: Galindia, "desolata" (D3).
- `c_konigsberg`: Sambia, the Tuwangste forest (III.72, Lat. p. 57); Helmold's helpers at sea (D30).
- A Romow site **by design only**: its location is unknown (D32).
- `rugen` only if the exile thread is kept.

### Events
1. **"Linen or Wool"** (yearly, after harvest): the gate. Rows D14, D9, D29.
2. **"The Mark on the Lintel"**: a sister's funeral.
   - Kin ask whether the dead passed; the tulisson describes her girdle and hair; a mark is found.
   - Hostile counter-voice after 1249, the legate: "mendacissimos histriones".
   - Rows D6, D7, D29.
3. **"The Guest Drinks Last"**: a traveller (a Gotland trader, or a daughter on the road) is fed and drunk with until everyone drinks, "uxor cum marito".
   - Options trade hospitality for prestige or gold.
   - Rows D11, D13, D30.
4. **"Martens for Faldones"**: a Saxon or Gotland merchant offers woollen cloaks for martens.
   - The sisters' own spinning can undercut him.
   - Cloth is money in Henry (H6, H39). Helmold is the narrator's in-period source (D30).
5. **"The Grove Not Cut"**: a Christian lord's men want timber or fish from a sacred water. Rows D4, D30, H32.
6. **"Lots Before the Raid"**: lots *bene vel male* before a raid.
   - If a sister casts them, the old men grumble and the ruler chooses whose lots stand.
   - Rows D9, D26, H16, H20, H31.
7. **"Restore the Women and Children"** (Livonian-crusade era from 1198; Order era from 1230).
   - After a defeat, the House sues for the return of captured sisters.
   - The attested outcomes are both ways: refused (D19, 1260) or granted (D25, 1284; D18).
   - Raiders' pattern per H17 and H29, with hostile tagging.
   - Never a mechanic that harms the faith's women for show.
8. **"The Lady Held Holy"** (era event, after 1326): a priest of the Order reads III.4 at table. The faith's answer is in its own voice. Rows D2, D3, D16 (n. 541, table reading).

**Hostile speakers by era:**
- 1178: Helmold (D30, mixed) and Kadłubek (D31).
- 1198–1227: Henry's bishops (§2 list) and the legate (H40).
- 1249: the Christburg legate.
- After 1326: Dusburg.

## 5. What is still missing, and where to look (not Young)

### Missing
1. **Any Prussian woman in cult before 1230**, other than D2 (legendary) and D14 (shared spinning). Henry adds nothing on Prussians at all.
2. **Prussian grave evidence for women.** Do spindle whorls occur in Prussian women's cremation burials? That would decide whether "the spindle in the pyre" is attested or invented.
3. **The Christburg clauses** in full (horse-and-hawk vision; who the tulissones were).
4. **Curche's gender.** Open question (D29).
5. **Helmold I.1 and Kadłubek in Latin**, for quotable in-period lines.
6. **What Galindia actually looked like c. 1178.** Was it really "desolata"?

### Books and articles that would fill these
Ranked; open-access items first.

1. **Christburg, the Latin.** *Preußisches Urkundenbuch* I.1, ed. R. Philippi (Königsberg 1882), no. 218, pp. 158–65. OA on archive.org (first pass link). → gap 3.
2. **Kregždys on Curche.** Rolandas Kregždys, "OPr. *Curche*: etymology of the theonym, functions of the deity …", *Studia Mythologica Slavica* 12 (2009), pp. 249–320. The journal is OA (*unverified* for this issue). → gap 4.
3. **Nicolaus von Jeroschin**, *Di Kronike von Pruzinlant*, ed. E. Strehlke, in *Scriptores rerum Prussicarum* I (Leipzig 1861), pp. 291–624. OA (archive.org). → cross-checks III.4–5 in a 1330s German version, including spinning and *prophetissa*.
4. **Helmold, in Latin and English.**
   - *Helmoldi presbyteri Bozoviensis Cronica Slavorum*, ed. B. Schmeidler (MGH SRG 32, Hannover 1937), I.1; OA (dMGH).
   - Or F. J. Tschan, trans., *The Chronicle of the Slavs* (New York: Columbia UP, 1935), ch. 1.
   - → gap 5.
5. **Kadłubek in Latin.** *Chronica Polonorum*, ed. A. Bielowski, *Monumenta Poloniae Historica* II (Lwów 1872), p. 421. OA (Polona, *unverified*). → gap 5.
6. **The source corpus.** Norbertas Vėlius (ed.), *Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, vol. I: *Nuo seniausių laikų iki XV amžiaus pabaigos* (Vilnius: Mokslo ir enciklopedijų leidybos institutas, 1996), ISBN *unverified*.
   - It gathers every pre-1500 Baltic religion text with Lithuanian translation and is the best single fill for gaps 1 and 3.
   - Edited by a Lithuanian folklorist (insider), and a scholarly corpus, not Romuva.
7. **Bojtár.** Endre Bojtár, *Foreword to the Past: A Cultural History of the Baltic People* (Budapest: CEU Press, 1999). Read pp. 321 (Romow) and 327–29 ("The tuliss and the ligash"). → gap 3.
8. **Archaeology.** Aleksander Pluskowski, *The Archaeology of the Prussian Crusade: Holy War and Colonisation* (London: Routledge, 2013), ISBN *unverified*. Read the chapters on Prussian burial and sacred landscape. → gaps 2 and 6.
9. **Matuzova.** V. I. Matuzova, "Mental Frontiers: Prussians as Seen by Peter von Dusburg", in A. V. Murray (ed.), *Crusade and Conversion on the Baltic Frontier 1150–1500* (Aldershot: Ashgate, 2001), pp. 253–60. → how far III.5 can be trusted.
10. **Rowell.** S. C. Rowell, *Lithuania Ascending: A Pagan Empire within East-Central Europe, 1295–1345* (Cambridge UP, 1994), pp. 30–40, 125–28. → the Criwe (D32). Lithuanian-born scholar.
11. **Bugiani on the Criwe.** Piero Bugiani, "Sul prussiano Criwe e la sua preistoria", *Res Balticae* 12 (2012). Same author as the thesis.
12. **Vaitkevičius.**
    - Vykintas Vaitkevičius, *Studies into the Balts' Sacred Places* (BAR IS 1228, Oxford, 2004), pp. 51–52.
    - Vaitkevičius, "The Sacred Groves of the Balts: Lost History and Modern Research", *Folklore* (Electronic Journal of Folklore) 42 (2009). OA.
    - → holy-site candidates. Insider archaeologist.
13. **Mažeika.** Rasa Mažeika, "Violent Victims? Aspects of the Just War Theory in the Chronicle of Peter von Dusburg", in A. V. Murray (ed.), *The Clash of Cultures on the Medieval Baltic Frontier* (Farnham: Ashgate, 2009), pp. 123–37. Use it with her 2023 OA article (first pass).
14. **German annotated Dusburg.** K. Scholz and D. Wojtecki (trans.), *Peter von Dusburg, Chronik des Preussenlandes* (Darmstadt: WBG, 1984). → its III.5 commentary.
15. **The Rhymed Chronicle.** Jerry C. Smith and William L. Urban (trans.), *The Livonian Rhymed Chronicle* (Bloomington, 1977). → Curonian, Semgallian and Lithuanian women after 1227.

**Bottom line.** Items 1–6 are OA or nearly so, and they cover gaps 1, 3, 4 and 5 without Young. Gap 2 (grave goods) needs Pluskowski (8) or Prussian excavation reports. Until then, "the spindle in the pyre" stays marked as invented.
