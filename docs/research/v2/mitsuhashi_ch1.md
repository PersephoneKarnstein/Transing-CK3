## 1. Verdict

1. Mitsuhashi's thesis is in this book, in her own words: the **双性原理 *sōsei genri*, "dual-sex principle"**, and **双性力 *sōseiryoku*, "dual-sex power"** (pp. 26, 36–38). So §7 item 1 of `japan_sources.md` is answered: we no longer depend on Tsuji's summary.
2. She offers it as a theory of how premodern society *placed* people who crossed sex, as feared and revered rather than despised. She is not saying they were a separate sex. Her own self-definition (p. 5) is binary: a male-to-female crosser.
3. **There are only two items in 1150–1210:**
   - the *Nenjū gyōji emaki* scene, c. 1170 (an Edo copy; she says herself that it is uncertain);
   - the *Ryōjin hishō* song on the eastern 男巫 *otoko miko* (= RH 556; compiled by 1179).
   Everything else is ancient/myth or ≥1261.
4. **Not in this range:** Yamai no sōshi (二形), *Torikaebaya*, asobi or kugutsu as such, the Rokuhara 1178 *dengaku*, the *Honchō mudaishi*. 遊女 appears only in passing (pp. 41, 72).
5. **New for the design:** she reads the *jisha* (持者/ぢしゃ) as **adult men in women's dress**: one has a moustache and beard, and the 1500 figure is adult. That bears on the survey's exclusion of the *jisha* as possible minors. **User to decide** (§5).
6. The phrase "a woman among women" does **not** occur in pp. 3–77 as her self-description. In this range it appears only as narrative: Yamato Takeru slipping in "among the women serving the banquet" (pp. 25, 27).

**Reading notes.**
- She applies 双性 to both directions of crossing: Jingū in men's dress, and Takeda Sachiko's suggestion that Himiko and Kōken dressed as men (p. 37). Tsuji's objection to a single abstract "dual sex" covering both (Tsuji p. 10) targets exactly this.
- Her parallels put "transgender" and "intersex" (半陰陽者) side by side (pp. 34, 36). She reads the 1605 Ryukyu case medically, as possible intersex. **Do not import that** (rules: no cause, no medical frame).
- 双性 is a theory of *social placement* ("awe and reverence"), not a claim to be a third sex. In p. 48 (inside the skipped chigo section) she does use "third gender (第三の性)" of others. That stays out, under the brief.

## 4. Which items fall in 1150–1210

Only these:
1. ***Nenjū gyōji emaki*, c. 1170** (Kyoto outskirts; an Edo copy of a lost original): a possibly bearded *miko* diviner. She calls it uncertain.
2. ***Ryōjin hishō*, 男巫 song** (= RH 556; by 1179): eastern male shamans. She says it does not show they were in women's dress.
3. *(context only)* Kiyomori's *shirabyōshi* (Giō, Hotoke): out of scope.

Nothing she gives sits on the Yodo river ports, and she does not discuss the asobi as *josō* in this range.

## 5. Items for the 1178 faith

1. **The 双性 decision can now rest on her own words.** `japan_sources.md` §4.3 kept *sōsei* out of doctrine because "we know it only through her opponent's summary". We now have her text (T7–T16). It is a theory that people who crossed sex were held in **awe and given an office** (T10, T14, T15), not a third-sex identity.
   - It still conflicts with rule 2/18 and "no third gender" if made a tenet ("power comes from holding both sexes").
   - **Recommendation:** keep it out of the narrator and the tenets. Her T15 line, "a way to live and a social role of their own", is the part the faith can carry. It matches the house giving a daughter a song line.
   - A single mother's voice may still voice *sōsei* (as §4.3 allows), answered by another mother. **User to decide.**
2. **The house's long kinship (continuity) now has her own sources.** In a mother's voice only:
   - the Hirota shaman "buried as the women were" (p. 32–33);
   - Yamato Takeru "in his aunt's robe" (myth; an adult framing only);
   - the eastern 男巫 the god possesses (RH 556; she cites it, p. 44).
   The narrator never calls them daughters, as already decided.
3. **New event seed: "The diviner at the cockfight".** Kyoto outskirts, c. 1170. A tall *miko* reads fortunes for women who come from far away. The capital's eye sees a shadow on her lip.
   - Source: *Nenjū gyōji emaki* vol. 4 (M. pp. 39–40). Flag the narrator's framing as "an old scroll's picture"; the evidence is an Edo copy and M. herself is unsure.
   - Hostile capital gaze, house reply in its own voice. Adult. Fits `b_kyo`.
4. **The *jisha* exclusion should be revisited.** The survey dropped the *jisha* on Yanagita's "child in female dress". Mitsuhashi's reading of the same figure points the other way:
   - the 1261 figure has a moustache (p. 40);
   - the c. 1500 地しゃ is plainly adult;
   - the role is an adult office (divination at Tsurugaoka, linked to Izusan, Hakone and Mishima).
   - But both are **late** (1261, c. 1500) and **eastern**, not Yodo. Even if cleared, they suit a later Kantō event, not 1178.
   - Her p. 72 also speculates that some *jisha* had been temple *chigo* earlier in life. That is another reason to keep it out unless the user decides otherwise.
   - **User to decide.**
5. **Her "a woman's likeness" line** (我も女のまねかたぞかし, c. 1500) is a good *flavour* for a late-era *jisha* event. Never for 1178.
6. **Medical frame stays out.** She herself uses 半陰陽 and the 1605 "body changed" case (p. 34). The faith does not (no cause, no medicine).
7. **Shirabyōshi:** still out as subjects. Note for accuracy that she and Tsuji disagree on their dress (boy's or *chigo* dress, vs. incomplete men's dress).
