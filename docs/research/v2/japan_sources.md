## 1. Verdict

1. **Buildable now, as a faith of the singers' houses.** The institution is in period and in place. Eguchi and Kanzaki (Settsu) and Aohaka (Mino) are recorded c. 1087 (Masafusa) and 1159–79 (*Kudenshū*). The houses had women heads, song passed down female lines, a house god (Hyakudayū), Buddhist practice, and ties to Go-Shirakawa. All of it is datable within 1087–1188.
2. **The world is strong.** It includes Go-Shirakawa's song circle (1159–79), the *Ryōjin hishō* (1179), his Itsukushima (1174) and Kumano pilgrimages, the Rokuhara night (1178.11.10, Tsuji), the Saigyō–Eguchi exchange (supposedly 1178), the *Senzaishū* (1183–88), and the Genpei war.
3. **The daughters themselves are thin.** No source read records a person assigned male living as a woman inside a singers' house. The nearest in-period traces are all brief:
   - two songs about male shamans (*otoko miko*): RH 352 and RH 556, from before 1179;
   - the Rokuhara guise (a single night);
   - the *Yamai no sōshi* drum-diviner (hostile; late 12th c.);
   - a *kugutsu* woman mocked by a court poet for her moustache (*Honchō mudaishi*, c. 1162–64; Pfl. 359 n. 38). She is new to the project and in period, but the record is about her body;
   - the *Torikaebaya* (fiction, c. 1100–1170). Its heroine lives her adult life as a palace lady, and the tale ends by "returning" her to a man's life.
4. **The faith is therefore what the other five faiths are:** invented daughters inside a documented women's institution, plus one in-period tale that the faith reads in its own voice. This is stronger than the Visayan or Prussian cases. It is weaker than Cebu's described office.
5. **Base it on four things:** the song lineage (Kim pp. 15, 21, 156), the house head (Kim p. 9), Hyakudayū and the Sumiyoshi–Hirota pilgrimages (Kim pp. 9–10), and *kyōgen kigyo*, the idea that worldly song becomes praise of the Buddha (Kim pp. 40–42).
6. **Framing follows Mitsuhashi (§4).** The daughters are women made "women among women" in the house. The faith claims kinship with earlier figures only in its own voice. *Sōsei* (dual-sex power) is not doctrine.
7. **Sex work is real in every source.** It stays as the houses' world and as some women's work, said plainly and rarely. It is never the gate, the office, a scene or a tenet (§5, §6).
8. **Strength:** world and institution *strong*; gender evidence *thin to plausible*; overall *plausible-strong*, still rank 1 among the new candidates.

## 3. *Torikaebaya* for the faith

**What it is.**
- The tale is courtly fiction, c. 1100–1170, and was very likely read at court by 1178.
- Its child raised as a girl lives her adult life as a great lady: Naishi no Kami, in the women's palace, beside a female heir to the throne. The heir is verified, and she is an archaising invention of the tale.
- The tale treats her as a "strange fate", not a sin. It never names what she is, and it makes her a paragon in both roles (Pfl. 351–57).
- Pronouns: the Spanish edition uses feminine forms while she is Naishi no Kami ("la dama"). Pflugfelder uses "he" throughout. This note follows the edition and calls her "she".

**The hard fact.** The tale ends by turning her into a man, and it frames that as setting things right.
- She chooses it out of duty, to find her sister, not for her own sake (III.14).
- She finds it "muy desagradable transformarme de repente en hombre" ("very unpleasant to turn suddenly into a man", III.22 L6134).
- She will "tener paciencia con este aspecto de varón" ("be patient with this man's appearance", III.40–41 L6994).
- So the tale is not the faith's proof (rule 19). It is a book the faith *reads*, as the Adelphai read the monk-saints (§16 Saints): in its own voice, against the grain.

**Reading it in the faith's voice.**
1. **A lady of the inner palace who served well.** The house reads her years as Naishi no Kami as the tale's true middle: calligraphy, painting, the koto, the office (I.~18). Possible in-voice line, from her own words: "si siguiera siendo la mujer que soy en estos momentos" ("if I stayed the woman I am now", III.14 L5725–26).
2. **She set down the name for her sister.** The faith reads the turn as *Set Down the Name* (rule 23), honoured equally, and not as truth restored. A mother's line (invented): "She put her hair up to go and find her sister. That is a reason, not a verdict."
3. **No cause.** The *tengu* and the dream's "El varón será varón y la mujer, mujer" belong to hostile voices: a preacher or a physician of the *birth* family (rule 3). In its own voice the house says only that the dream told the father what he had prayed to hear. The narrator gives no cause (rule 4).
4. **The father's first answer was "a nun".** I.~18 L1487 ("que se haga monja"): even her father first imagined a woman's religious road for her. Use this only as the faith's ironic aside, never as acceptance (rule 7). It ties to Pflugfelder's note that the clergy may have been a less fixed category (p. 352 n. 21).
5. **Her own words of difference.** Use them sparingly and never as despair (rule 17). Two candidates:
   - "Cada vez me doy más cuenta del extraño sino que nos persigue" ("more and more I see the strange fate that follows us", III.3–5 L5202–03);
   - "Yo también he cambiado; ya no soy la persona que solía ser" ("I too have changed; I am not who I was", III.40–41 L6987–88).
6. **"Both sexes".** I.~21 L1719–20 ("había aprendido cosas de los dos sexos", "had learned things of both sexes") leans toward *sōsei*. Leave it out of loc (§4).

**Game use.**
- One event: **"The tale of the two who were exchanged".** A court lady, or a daughter in service, reads the tale aloud. Options give the house's reading (points 1–3). Since the tale is court fiction, the event can come in the court-service road.
- The date coincidence is a flavour line at most and is never claimed: her presentation falls on the 10th of the 11th month (I.~18), the same day as the Rokuhara *dengaku* (1178.11.10).
- The heir she served may be named only as "the heiress she served". **No scene with the princess.**
- The tale does not supply a gate, an office or a saint. Court service as a lady-in-waiting is a *road not holy* (rule 7).

**Must stay out** (TK-es lines; Pflugfelder pages).
- **Childhood:** I.1–I.8 (L393–~909), including the coming-of-age rites (n. 35).
- **The princess:** the night scene (I.~18 L1547–61), where the princess is "débil e infantil" ("weak and childlike") and her age is unstated (possible minor). Also the pregnancy, the birth and the secret son (III.15, III.40–41, IV.4–12; Pfl. 366–67).
- **Coercion:** Saishō forcing Shi no Kimi (I.23 L1948–77); his night-and-day siege of Naishi no Kami (II.5–6 L3617–3702; Pfl. 364); his assault on the sister (II.10; Pfl. 364–65).
- **Drowning and death-wishes:** the Uji poems (III.56 L7809, n. 152; IV.39 L9908; IV.41 L10003; IV.57 L10757); "quiero morirme" ("I want to die", I.32 L2474); "mi vida toca a su fin" ("my life is reaching its end", III.3–5); "no podría continuar en este mundo" ("I could not go on in this world", III.14 L5727, probably meaning withdrawal, but ambiguous); "me moriré" ("I will die", III.45).
- **The ending's verdict** ("auténtico aspecto", "true appearance") in the narrator's voice, and the appendix's "hijo travestido" ("cross-dressed son").
- **Lines of hers that need care:** "He estado muy deprimida" ("I have been very low", III.3–5 L5187) is despair (rule 17). "Yo era un hombre y sucumbí a mis inclinaciones para llevar una vida de mujer" ("I was a man and gave in to my leanings to live a woman's life", III.14 L5708) frames her life as a lapse. Neither goes into loc. The second may be a hostile echo, put in another's mouth.

## 4. Mitsuhashi and Tsuji: the framing decision

**Tsuji** (2024; `japan_tsuji.md` §§4–5):
- Every case of men in women's dress is a guise. The 1178 case is a one-night ritual stand-in, done under orders and felt as a disgrace. Later *furyū* dress had "no special meaning".
- He "hesitates" to put men's and women's cross-dressing together under an abstract "dual sex" (p. 10).
- He warns that calling it "dual-sex power" "may rather harm understanding" (p. 27).
- His expertise is the dated records. He reads them well, and he says nothing about people who lived as women.

**Mitsuhashi**, in her own published words (QV 2007):
- She is an MtF transgender woman who lives socially as a "woman" (p. 296) and wants to be "a woman among women" (p. 310).
- Her life is a cross-dressing *culture*, not a medical condition (p. 309).
- She belongs to a community "more than two thousand years" old, with a hero in the founding myth (pp. 310–11).
- Her history-writing is the community's own memory (p. 307).
- Through Tsuji (p. 9), her 2022 essay holds two more claims: premodern performance loved cross-dressing as "a supreme beauty beyond sex", and holding both sexes (*sōsei*) gave power and sacredness. We have not read this essay in her own words.

**Decision (insider rule).** Her framing decides who the daughters are and how the faith speaks of its past. Tsuji's work decides what each record was and when it happened (narrator level).
1. **The daughters are women, made "women among women" in the house.** The gate is the women of the house receiving her into their song line (§5). This is Mitsuhashi's social womanhood, and it needs no knife (rule 8 holds anyway).
2. **The faith claims a long kinship, in its own voice.**
   - A mother may say that the god "swept down" on the eastern *otoko miko* too (RH 556), or that the Rokuhara men "wore our hair for a night".
   - The narrator never says those men or shamans were daughters (narrator rule). Tsuji's reading stands at narrator level: a guise.
   - This follows Mitsuhashi's continuity claim within rule 19: foremothers are kin, never proof.
3. ***Sōsei* is not doctrine.**
   - We know it only through her opponent's summary.
   - Rules 2 and 18 and the brief's "no third gender" also bar a tenet saying power comes from holding both sexes.
   - It may appear as one mother's belief in one event, answered by another mother. Never in the narrator's or the tenet's voice. **User to decide** whether even that is wanted.
4. **No medical frame.** The faith follows her refusal of the "disability" model. No word in the loc pathologises; hostile physicians keep their rule-3 families.
5. **The faith takes no modern word.** It uses none of *new-half*, *okama*, *josō*, *toransujendā*. Mitsuhashi is never quoted in game, and she and the editors are never a character, a name or a saint.

## 5. Revised design

**Name.** Survey's *Imayō no Ie*, "Houses of the Present-Day Song" (keep). Daughters: *uketsugi*, "those who received the line" (**invented**; check the font for ō/ū per the glyph memory).

**Family and rite.** A rite under `mahayana_faith`, beside `pundarika` (Tendai), with Hyakudayū as a kami of its own (honji suijaku, Kim pp. 89 ff.).

**Gate: "The Complete Transmission"** (Kim p. 21: "a complete transmission … with absolutely no omission").
- A mother of the house teaches her the house songs over seasons (Kim pp. 15, 19–21). At a gathering on Hyakudayū's night (Kairaishiki, Kim p. 14) she sings them back with the drum. The mother then names her in the house line.
- The *house* gives transmission, as Otomae did (p. 15). An outsider's patronage cannot buy it (Sasanami, p. 15). This is not an outside probation (rule 23): the judges are her own house.
- **No pain test** (rule 22). Tori's plucked eyelashes and Go-Shirakawa's lost voice (pp. 15, 18–19) are kept out of the gate and may appear only as stories of others' zeal.
- "Not today" is honoured (rule 23).

**Office: the head of the house** (Kim p. 9; *chōja* per GB p. 54 via Kawashima).
- Her powers are those Kim lists: to keep order, to protect members from customers, and to share out goods.
- She succeeds by song and by the house's choice. A daughter by birth or by taking may succeed (rule 20). Adoption is not attested in the sources read (*unverified*; check Goodwin).

**Line.** Mother to daughter in song (Kim pp. 15, 156; Otomae's daughter, p. 20). A daughter's seat passes as in §16 B.

**Roads not holy (rule 7).**
- The river trade: boats, lodging, the patrons' gifts of rice and silk (p. 11 n. 48).
- Poems in an imperial anthology: Kokō in the *Senzaishū*, p. 13.
- Court service, the *Torikaebaya* road: a lady-in-waiting (§3).
- A house built near a patron (Otomae, p. 20).

**Sex work, handled.**
- The sources say plainly that some singers sold sex (Kim pp. 5, 8–9; GB pp. 53, 57). The faith neither hides it nor builds on it.
- One tenet or house text in a mother's voice may say the house keeps every woman who works on the river, song or bed, and the head protects them from customers (Kim p. 9).
- No event shows a client scene. No tenet or doctrine rewards it. **`tenet_carnal_exaltation` is rejected.**
- Loc says "singer" or *asobi*, never "courtesan" or "prostitute" in the narrator's voice. Hostile outsiders (a Masafusa-like literatus) may say "lewd" (Kairaishiki).
- The design must also not repeat the modern paradigm that puts trans women in the entertainment and sex trade (QV pp. 26–27). The daughters are singers, boat-keepers, house heads and ladies-in-waiting, and the sex trade belongs to the house's world, not to them as a kind.

**Doctrines and tenets** (vanilla names checked in `rite_types/00_rite_types.txt`; `pundarika` has knowledge, literalism, inner journey).
- `tenet_ritual_celebrations` (Hyakudayū nights, the song gatherings).
- `tenet_pursuit_of_knowledge` or a custom **"Song Is Dharma"** (*kyōgen kigyo*, Kim p. 42; *Kudenshū* p. 40).
- `tenet_inner_journey` (kept from Tendai).
- Clergy `doctrine_clerical_gender_female_only`; `doctrine_pluralism_righteous`; `doctrine_pilgrimage_local_rites`.
- Gender: female-dominated, *check* against the other five faiths.

**Holy sites** (map ids from the vanilla files).
- `b_watanabe` (`c_settsu`, prov. 10599): the Yodo mouth (Eguchi, Kanzaki and Sumiyoshi nearby). *Check* which is nearest on the map.
- `b_fukuhara` (`c_settsu`): the Hirota shrine (Nishinomiya), and the 1179 *naishi* (Tsuji).
- `b_fuwa` (`c_mino`): Aohaka, Otomae's house. Aohaka was in Ōno district; Fuwa is the nearest barony on the Tōsandō, *check*. The other Mino baronies are `b_gujo`, `b_kani`, `b_motosu` and `b_mugi`.
- `b_itsukushima` (`c_aki`): shared with Shintō; Go-Shirakawa's 1174 pilgrimage; the *naishi*.
- `b_kyo` (`c_yamashiro`): Hōjūji (the 1174 awase) and Rokuhara. Alternatively `b_kumano` (`c_kii`), but Kumano carries the Nachi drowning theme, so prefer `b_kyo`.
- For an event only: `b_izumi` (`c_kawachi`), for the Hakata shrine of RH 352 (*unverified*).

**Event ideas** (row in brackets; anything not sourced is **invented**).
1. **"Hair let down at Rokuhara"** (1178.11.10; Tsuji). The house hears of the empress's men dancing as the Itsukushima maidens. A mother's line: "they wore our hair for a night". The narrator calls it a guise.
2. **"A night's borrowed lodging"** (1178, legendary; Kim p. 12). A monk caught by rain at Eguchi is refused a room and the exchange of poems is sung on the river. A daughter may answer in kind. The monk stays unnamed in options; Saigyō and Tae are not claimed.
3. **"The Complete Transmission"** (gate; Kim pp. 15, 21). Includes a branch where a patron's protégée demands the songs and the mother refuses, as with Sasanami.
4. **"Hyakudayū's night"** (yearly; Kairaishiki, Kim p. 14). Drum and dance for the house's fortune. No objects described.
5. **"To Sumiyoshi and Hirota"** (pilgrimage; Kim p. 9). The road where singers and court pilgrims cross.
6. **"Summoned to sing"** (1170s; Kim pp. 17, 21–22). The retired emperor's circle calls for singers. After 1179.11 he is under house arrest, so the call is cancelled. A road to prestige, not holiness.
7. **"The god sweeps down"** (RH 556, Kim p. 148; RH 352, p. 143). A male shaman from the east, or the Hakata dancer, at a warrior's house. Capital singers scorn him. The house's reply is in its own voice. The narrator never calls him a daughter.
8. **"Why can't we, too?"** (RH 208, Kim p. 82). A Tendai preacher gives the *henjō nanshi* sermon (the hostile Buddhist line). A daughter answers with RH 208. RH 117 ("out of their female bodies") is the preacher's line, never hers.
9. **"In this body there's a heart"** (Kokō, SZS 819, Kim p. 13; *Senzaishū* 1183–88). A compiler gathers poems for an imperial anthology, and a daughter's poem may be taken: prestige and renown.
10. **"The road turns east"** (after 1186; Kim p. 16). Eguchi and Aohaka decline, and a house may move toward the Tōkaidō. Late-era only.
11. **The tale** (see §3).

**Epigraphs and loc lines** (Kim's translations, ≤25 words, page given; Japanese romanised as Kim prints it).
- RH 208 (p. 82): "If the Dragon King's daughter / became buddha, / why can't we, too, somehow?". Use: the tenet answering *henjō nanshi*.
- RH 380 (p. 10): "A courtesan's favorite things: / her many arts, the drum, / the little boats". Use it with "singer" in our own loc, or GB's Spanish (p. 56). Cut before "a man's love".
- Kudenshū (p. 42): "Why shouldn't secular words, too, / transform themselves into praises of buddha". Use: the Song-Is-Dharma tenet.
- Kudenshū (p. 40): imayō "sung with sincerity at shrines or temples … bring about divine revelations". Use: the pilgrimage event.
- Miyagi (p. 12): "In Settsu Province / anything at all / becomes Dharma". Use: the faith's holy site at the Yodo mouth.
- Kokō, SZS 819 (p. 13): "I don't count. / But in this body / there's a heart". Use: the anthology event, and the daughter's trait text.
- RH 359 (p. 133): "Was I born to play? / Was I born to frolic?". Use: the aged mother's line.
- RH 37 (p. 73): "Great compassionate Kannon is a raft". Use: the river houses.
- RH 556 (p. 148): "the god sweeps down on them". Use: the RH 556 event only, in a mother's voice.
- RH 560 (p. 6): "Look! / The gods have got hold of her!". Use: Hyakudayū's night.
- Kudenshū (p. 21): "a complete transmission … with absolutely no omission". Use: the gate's name.
- Avoid as epigraphs:
  - RH 117 ("long out of their female bodies") and RH 292 ("she had to change into a man"): hostile lines;
  - RH 158 (drowning); RH 364 (a minor); RH 113 (the age of eight);
  - any love-bed song.

**Invented** (say so in the design doc): the daughters; *uketsugi*; the gate's form; any adoption rule; Hyakudayū as a faith's kami beside a Buddhist rite; all named characters.

## 6. Risks
- **Thin gender base.** The case rests on a documented women's institution plus fiction and fragments. Do not over-sell RH 352 or 556 or Rokuhara in the narrator's voice.
- **Sex trade.** See §5. Watch for Hyakudayū's phallic cult: never describe the objects (rule 5; no body joke).
- **Body records.** The *Yamai no sōshi* drummer and Kuchihige-no-kimi are both records of a body being mocked or exposed. Neither becomes a scene, a trait or a joke. Pflugfelder's "drawn toward such professions" (n. 38) must not become a narrator line that trans women "belonged" in entertainment (QV pp. 26–27).
- ***Torikaebaya*'s ending.** If the tale is quoted at all, the turn back to a man's life must be read as *Set Down the Name*. It is never the narrator's truth, and never the faith's fear.
- **Minors.** *Chigo* (QV p. 6), the *jisha*, RH 364, Otomae at 12–13, the eight-year-old Dragon girl (as a figure), the 14-year-old in the *Sarashina* singers (Kim p. 14), and the young childhood in *Torikaebaya*: all out.
- **Drowning and suicide.** Nachi (Kim p. 33), Dan-no-ura, RH 158's "drowning" image, and any drowning in *Torikaebaya*: all out.
- **Orientalising.** Kim's translations use "courtesan" and, once, "floating world" (a catalogue song, pp. 52–53). Do not reuse either. No geisha, no Edo.
- **The shaman-entertainer thesis.** Kim (p. 5) and GB (pp. 49–53) derive the singers from ancient priestesses. Goodwin rejects this. No golden past (rule 2): the faith never says "we were priestesses once".
- **Living people.** Mitsuhashi, Nakayama and the editors are never characters or saints. No modern terms.
- **Historical figures.** Go-Shirakawa, Otomae, Tanba no Tsubone, Kokō, Miyagi, Tae, Saigyō and Kiyomori are never claimed.
- **Shirabyōshi and the *naishi*'s male dress.** Not subjects.
