Notes written 2026-10-25. All quotes are 25 words or fewer and carry the book's printed page numbers. Everything else is paraphrase. No book text is kept in the repo; the working text sits in the session scratchpad only.

**Bibliographic line.** Greta LaFleur, Masha Raskolnikov and Anna Kłosowska (eds.), *Trans Historical: Gender Plurality before the Modern*. Ithaca, NY: Cornell University Press, 2021. ISBN 978-1-5017-5908-6 (hardcover); 978-1-5017-5950-5 (paperback). LCCN 2021016397.

## 1. Verdict

- **High value, narrowly.** Two chapters matter for the mod:
  - Betancourt (ch. 12), the best single source here for trans-feminine *Byzantium* and the Adelphai's hostile voices.
  - Bychowski (ch. 4), the Rykener reading that `rykener_and_mobilities.md` asked for.
- **Method: the volume's lasting use.** The book offers a vocabulary for "trans before trans": read invective against the grain, give figures "the freedom not to 'be' transgender", and keep historical terms instead of modern ones.
- **Little else for v2.** The book is overwhelmingly Western European and early modern. The editors admit "the lion's share" is European and North American (p. 15).
- **Missing entirely:** Kalonymus, *Even Boḥan*, rabbinic sources, Norse, South Asian, premodern Chinese or Japanese, West African, Visayan, and Islamic legal material. There is no chapter on any of them. The Islamic world appears only as the early modern Ottoman köçek (ch. 7) and one footnote (*khuntha*, p. 22 n. 25).
- **Most material is post-1453.** Elenx de Céspedes, Marin le Marcis, Wojciech, Bosch's Wilgefortis, Vasco da Lucena, the köçek, the films and early America all fall after the mod's window.
- **Usable pre-1453 trans-feminine material:**
  - Rykener (1394);
  - Betancourt's late antique and Byzantine invective, chronicles and letters (1st–13th c.);
  - the Gregory of Tours convent case (6th c.);
  - hostile frames: monstrous races, the bestiary hyena, English mayhem law.

## 3. Usable pre-1453 trans-feminine material (with pages)

Nothing here is a claim that any figure belonged to a mod faith. Each item is labelled as a source for hostile voice, friendly voice, or flavour.

### 3.1 Betancourt, ch. 12 (Greek East)

**Thesis and method.**
- AFAB monk-saints are well attested. Trans women are not: "the image of women assigned male at birth is all but absent in Byzantine sources" (p. 297).
- Such women appear "only in slanderous texts and invective" (p. 297).
- Method: "read simultaneously along and against the grain of the archive's transmisogyny" (p. 306).
- Close: "A future Byzantine trans studies must begin with a history of transphobia and transmisogyny" (p. 315).
- Betancourt uses Serano's *transmisogyny* explicitly (p. 306, n. 44). That fits the README's own word.

**Theatre and pantomime (hostile setting).**
- A 6th-c. Carthaginian epigram describes a dancer "moulding his pliant torso to suit either sex" (p. 300; *Latin Anthology* 100).
- Church and imperial authorities linked male dancers playing women to *porneia* and to "the desire to become a woman" (p. 301).

**Pseudo-Chrysostom, *De paenitentia* (PG 59:670).**
- Feminine dancers are said to be "grieving that they had not been born women from their parents" (p. 301).
- Betancourt notes modern doubt about the attribution, but the manuscripts carried Chrysostom's weight (p. 301).
- **Use:** a patristic hostile voice that nonetheless *names the wish*. For Adelphai events, a priest can quote it as an accusation; the daughter can hear it as recognition.

**Amphilochius of Iconium (4th c.), *Iambi ad Seleucum* 90–99.**
- Such people "desire to become women".
- The pairing "effeminate men and masculine women" (*andres gynaiki kai gynaikes andrasin*) (p. 301).

**Canon law.**
- Dress and grooming rules in the Councils of Gangra (canons 13, 17) and Trullo (canon 62) and in the Theodosian Code, all after Deuteronomy 22:5 (p. 301, n. 20).
- **Use:** the ready legal citations a Byzantine bishop would throw at the Sisters.

**Clement of Alexandria, *Paedagogus* 3.3 (c. 200).**
- Shops for depilation, dyeing and coiffing. Gold worn "out of effeminate desire" (*thēlydriōdē epithymian*) (p. 304).
- "Rather we ought not to call them men [*ouk andras*], but lispers [*batalous*] and effeminates [*gynnidas*]" (p. 305).
- Betancourt reads Clement's real target as gender expression, not same-gender sex: "his true preoccupation is the latter", the transphobic over the homophobic (p. 305).
- **Use:** hostile vocabulary; and the urban beauty trades (waxing, dyeing, perfumers) as the ordinary city world a daughter moves in.
- **Caution:** Clement's genital "unless you saw them naked" line (p. 305) is the transphobic core. Do not reproduce it in loc.

**Elagabalus (r. 218–222) in Byzantine chronicles.** Betancourt calls Elagabalus "she" and a trans woman (p. 307).
- Dio Cassius (80.13): the ruler would "go to the taverns by night, wearing a wig, and there ply the trade of a female huckster" (p. 307). She chose the titles "wife, mistress, and queen" (p. 307).
- The surgery story is "only attested across Byzantine writers" (p. 307):
  - Zonaras (12th c.): "asked the physicians to contrive a woman's vagina in his body by means of an incision" (p. 307);
  - Kedrenos (12th c.): *diphuē*, by "an anterior incision" (pp. 307–308);
  - copied verbatim by Symeon Logothete (mid-10th c.) and Glykas (12th c.) (p. 308).
- Theodore Skoutariotes (late 13th c.) gives a wholly positive portrait: "righteous, sharp in war, gentle, thoughtful, servicing all, and loved by all" (p. 308).
- Aelian's lost "Indictment against the Little Woman" (*katēgoria tou Gynnidos*) (p. 308).
- The *Epitome de Caesaribus*: "turned himself into a woman" (*in se convertens muliebri*) and asked for a woman's name (p. 308).
- **Use for 1178:** this is what a literate Byzantine of Manuel I's reign could read in Zonaras or Kedrenos. It is in period, in Greek, and in the Adelphai's own library world.
  - Hostile chroniclers can invoke Elagabalus as a byword.
  - A Sister can cite Skoutariotes-style praise. Skoutariotes is late 13th c., so it fits only later starts.
  - **Never** make Elagabalus a founder or saint of the faith in narration (see §7).

**Eunuchs.**
- Ringrose's "third gender", and Basil and Cyril on "an accursed gender [genos] … neither feminine nor masculine" (p. 309).
- "Byzantine authors understood castration as feminizing the body and in a sense transforming men into women" (p. 309).
- Nicaea canon 1 (325) bars the man who "in sound health has castrated himself". It admits those cut for medical reasons, by barbarians or by masters (p. 309).
- **Use:** the *court* frames eunuchs as a third *genos*. That is the frame the Sisters refuse ("rises a daughter, not a third thing", README). Use Basil and Ringrose's frame as the out-group voice, not as the faith's own.

**Gregory of Tours, *History of the Franks* 10.15 (late 6th c., Frankish).**
- A convent dispute alleges the abbess kept "a man in the monastery who wore woman's clothes and was treated as a woman" (pp. 310–311).
- The person, castrated as a child for a groin disease "in the way I had once seen physicians do in Constantinople", had chosen women's clothes (p. 311).
- Betancourt: the person "chooses to live their life as a woman", and the story appears "without challenge or judgment" (p. 311).
- The accuser says the abbess "makes men eunuchs" and lives "as if she were an empress" (p. 311).
- **Use:** a Latin-West, pre-1178 precedent that ties a feminine eunuch life to a *women's house*, and Constantinople as the place where such surgery is learned. It is good flavour for Adelphai intake, or for any women's house in Francia.
- The book does not name the convent. The v1 report's "Poitiers convent of Radegund" is outside knowledge and should be verified.

**Michael Psellos (c. 1018–1081).**
- "With regard to nature I am feminine [*thēlys*]" (p. 312; Letter S 180).
- "My soul is indeed simply feminine [*thēlys*]" (p. 312).
- "I am not like the strings that are either only high-pitched or in harmony, but contain every melody" (p. 312; S 57).
- Papaioannou calls this "rhetorical transvestism". Betancourt pushes it toward "a marginalized transgender identity" but finally reads Psellos as **non-binary** (p. 313), not as a trans woman.
- **Use:** a quotable courtly voice for Constantinople. The mod already has `byz_psellos_papaioannou_letters.md`. Do not make Psellos a daughter or a Sister (§7).

**Matrona of Perge (correction to the v1 report, P67).**
- Matrona is **assigned female**, living as the eunuch "Babylas" in a men's monastery. The pierced-ears passage, "many of those who saw me said that I was a girl" (p. 310), is a woman explaining her own ears.
- Betancourt uses it only to show that a eunuch's adornment could read as female.
- It is **not** evidence that castrated men were taken as women, and it is TM/AFAB context. Do not use it for daughters.

### 3.3 Hostile frames (pre-1453, not about daughters)

- **Monstrous races (DeVun).**
  - *Marvels of the East* survives in three English manuscripts: Cotton Vitellius A.xv (c. 975–1025, Old English), Tiberius B.v (11th c.), Bodley 614 (12th c.) (p. 35 n. 1).
  - The *Liber monstrorum* (7th/8th c., Anglo-Latin) gives the race "a right breast like a man for performing work, and a left breast like a woman" (p. 30).
  - The Hereford Map puts its "race of both sexes, unnatural in many of their customs" in **Africa** (pp. 30, 33).
  - DeVun's point is that these maps made binary sex a European ideal and stigmatised whole foreign regions (p. 33).
  - She also warns that "placing present communities within these past conceptual frameworks might cause real harm to living people now" (p. 35).
  - **Use:** at most, a Latin cleric's othering voice. Never make it flavour for Badenya (see §7).
- **The bestiary hyena (Campbell).**
  - Second Family Bestiary (c. 1180): the hyena is "sometimes male, sometimes female, and for that reason it is an unclean animal" (p. 236).
  - The Latin B-Isidore (10th/11th c.) compares the hyena to the Israelites turning to idols (p. 245).
  - Philippe de Thaon (c. 1121–1135) turns it on covetous men who become "like a woman" (pp. 246–247).
  - Guillaume le Clerc (c. 1210/11) says Jews "became female" through indulgence, adding the verb *adenter*, "tamed" (pp. 247–248).
  - Some illuminations give the hyena a circumcised penis to mark it Jewish (p. 249 and n. 54ff.).
  - **Use:** see Ha-Nehpakhot, §4.
- **The law of maims (Goodrich).**
  - Bracton (13th c.): "Castration, though it is hidden, is deemed mayhem" (p. 284).
  - *Fleta*: the maimer "should lose a member for the like member which he took away" (p. 285).
  - Grosseteste, *De decem mandatis*: "it is hateful to mutilate the members of people" (p. 286).
  - Coke's later *vita et membra sunt in potestate regis* (p. 285) is 1632, so cite it only as a gloss on Bracton.
  - **Use:** English law against the knife, for any English realm meeting the Adelphai or an English daughter.
- **Peter Damian** and the 11th-c. "invention of sodomy" (Stahuljak, p. 212). Damian was a Ravenna churchman, so this is hostile vocabulary for Lombardy and the Romagna (Good Ladies, §4).

## 4. Per-faith implications

The book never mentions any of the mod's six faiths' homelands except Byzantium and (in passing) England. Where this section says "nothing", that is a finding.

**Adelphai Dorleyaiou (Byzantine).** Strongest gain. Betancourt supplies a Middle Byzantine (843–1204, p. 299) hostile library:
- *Hostile voices for event text* (court, bishop, rhetor): Pseudo-Chrysostom, Amphilochius, Clement, Gangra 13/17 and Trullo 62, and Basil's "accursed *genos*". A bishop might cite Nicaea canon 1 against a self-cut Sister and in favour of one cut by another. Check this against how *Take the Knife* frames the knifer and sponsor; it may already fit.
- *Chronicle voice:* Zonaras and Kedrenos on Elagabalus are in-period reading for 1178. A Constantinopolitan character mocks a Sister with "the Little Woman" (*gynnis*).
- *Friendly or ambivalent voices:* Skoutariotes' praise (late 13th c. only); Psellos's "feminine nature" letters as a quotable courtier who is never claimed.
- *Flavour:* the theatre and the waxing and dyeing shops as places in the city. The Gregory of Tours eunuch (who went to a women's house, after an operation learned in Constantinople) as a Latin traveller's tale told at Dorylaion.
- **Guardrails:** Betancourt's n. 46 cites galli/Attis scholarship. Do not import Attis imagery from it. Do not adopt Ringrose's "third gender" as the faith's self-description.

**Bædling (Norse/Anglo-Saxon).** Nothing Norse in the book (no *ergi*, *argr* or saga material; the 12 "ergi" hits in the text are parts of "energies").
- Anglo-Saxon only: the *Marvels of the East* manuscripts (Old English Vitellius A.xv) and the Anglo-Latin *Liber monstrorum* (pp. 30, 35). This is a monk's othering bestiary of distant peoples. It is not a source for the hall.
- For Norse hostile law, keep using Price in `rykener_and_mobilities.md` §3 and Meulengracht Sørensen.

**Yutai Dao (Dunhuang/Chinese).** Nothing premodern. One further-reading pointer: Howard Chiang (ed.), *Transgender China* (2012), cited at p. 22 n. 25.

**The Pāvai faith (Tamil).** Nothing South Asian. Betancourt borrows Anjali Arondekar's "read without a trace" from the colonial Indian archive (p. 300; n. 9, p. 316). This is method only. Nothing here touches hijra or any living community.

**Badenya (Mali).** Nothing West African. The only African material is hostile:
- the Hereford Map's "race of both sexes" in Africa (pp. 30, 33);
- the intro's note that *sudan* could mean origin, appearance or unfree status (p. 11, citing Hannah Barker).
- **Guardrail:** never echo the monstrous-race frame in Badenya content, even as a European traveller's aside, unless the event is explicitly about European ignorance.

**Ha-Nehpakhot (Jewish; Arles, Provence, Maghreb).**
- **Kalonymus.** The book contains no mention of Kalonymus ben Kalonymus, *Even Boḥan*, the prayer to be a woman, or any rabbinic text (0 hits).
- **What the repo already has.** Kalonymus is used across 40+ files, including:
  - `docs/research/jud_kalonymus_even_bohan.md`, `jud_davidson_renan_kalonymus.md`, `jud_rosen_circumcised_cinderella.md`;
  - `docs/faiths/ha-nehpakhot.md`;
  - `localization/english/tm_hn2_l_english.yml`, which cites *Even Bohan* p. 17 and dates it 1323 at lines 3 and 26.
  - `comparanda_reading_list.md` lists Dunkelgrün 2013 on dating *Even Boḥan*.
  - **This book adds nothing new on Kalonymus.**
- **What is new: the Christian neighbours' voice** (Campbell). From the 10th to the 13th c., Latin and French bestiaries cast the sex-changing hyena as a figure for Jews, who "became female" through appetite (Guillaume le Clerc, *Bestiaire divin* 1613–15, pp. 247–248). This is the hostile counterpart to `jud_neis_raven.md`'s rabbinic hyena.
  - It suits an event in which a Christian preacher in Arles uses the hyena against the street.
  - The narrator must frame it as antisemitic slander, and must never let it echo the women's own Turning.
  - Campbell also ties Guillaume's "coat" (*vestement*) to Lateran IV's 1215 dress-marking of Jews (p. 247; n. 46, p. 264). That is a usable date hook for Provence after 1215.
- **De Souza.** The Inquisition painted Jews and Muslims as sodomites (pp. 43–44). This is post-1453 Castile, but the pattern can inform hostile voice. His contributor note says he is writing a history of sodomy in Jewish tradition (p. 380); see §8.

**Imayō no Ie (Japanese singers' houses).** Nothing premodern Japanese. Joubin's Japanese references are modern flower-boy subculture (p. 329). Two cautions carry over:
- Arvas's köçek (p. 166) and Betancourt's pantomime dancers (pp. 300–301) show how hostile sources sexualise performers and blur them with sex work. Do not frame the singers' houses through the köçek model of coerced, enslaved boys performing for men.
- Keep the Japanese faith free of doll and porcelain imagery (memory rule). Nothing in the book pushes against that.

**The Lambong (Visayan).** Nothing Visayan. Method cautions apply:
- the intro's note that Native and Indigenous scholars object to "transgender" displacing their own frameworks (p. 12, n. 23: Boellstorff et al., "Decolonizing Transgender", *TSQ* 1.3, 2014);
- DeVun on harm to living communities (p. 35).

**Good Ladies (Rhineland, France, Lombardy).**
- **Christian neighbours' material, none of it trans-feminine:**
  - the Wilgefortis/Ontcommer/Kümmernis cult (from the 14th c., strong in German lands; p. 136), with a Lower Rhine strand (Gorissen, n. 14, p. 155);
  - the 8th-c. Volto Santo of Lucca, dressed on feast days in crown, embroidered skirt and silver shoes (p. 136).
- **Use:** a pilgrim stop or relic in Tuscany and Lombardy; an "Uncumber" saint that wives invoke against husbands (p. 139), as neighbour flavour. Wilgefortis is AFAB with a miraculous beard, so TM-adjacent context only; Mills declines a trans reading (pp. 149–152).
- **Hostile vocabulary:** Peter Damian (p. 212); Isidore's *monstrum* < *monstrare* (pp. 31–32); Campbell's French bestiaries.
- *Le Roman de Silence*'s queen's lover "disguised as a nun" (pp. 182–183) is the deceiver trope. Avoid it.

## 5. Rykener: additions and corrections to `rykener_and_mobilities.md`

That note's "Read next" item is now read: M. W. Bychowski, "The Transgender Turn: Eleanor Rykener Speaks Back", pp. 95–113 here. The note itself is not edited. These are additions to apply by hand.

**Additions.**
1. **Archive reference confirmed.** London Metropolitan Archives, Plea and Memoranda Roll A34, CLA/024/01/02/035, m. 2 (p. 111 n. 2).
2. **Self-naming formula, Latin.** "Se Elianoram nominans veste muliebri detectus", translated "Discovered in women's attire, she named herself as 'Eleanor'" (p. 109).
   - My own observation, not Bychowski's: *detectus* is a masculine participle. The scribe's grammar misgenders her even inside the clause that records her name. This fits Henningsen's pronoun count (A p. 251 n. 6).
3. **Consent and pay, Latin.** "Qui ab eo argentum pro labore suo petens sibi consentiebat": "Requesting money for [her] labor, Rykener consented" (p. 106).
   - Bychowski builds the chapter on *consentiebat*, *labore* and *argentum* (p. 98).
   - This supports §2.2 of the note (workers have their own voice).
4. **Other Latin tags from the roll** (p. 96): *vicum regium de Chepe*; *stallum* in *Sopereslane*; the act called *nephandum*; *civitatis ministros*; *anulum aureum* (the gold ring). From p. 106: *quampluribus monialibus* (very many nuns) and *quampluribus mulieribus* (very many women).
5. **The scribe's deadnaming, counted.** The scribe names her "John" (*Johannes*) "twenty-five times" (p. 104).
6. **Who speaks.** The record has "eighteen instances of Rykener saying something (five instances of 'dictus' and thirteen instances of 'quod' connected to her)" against Britby's two *fatebatur* (p. 106).
   - Bychowski's conclusion: it is "first and foremost a record produced about and by Rykener" (p. 106).
   - Design echo: in a court event, she gets most of the lines.
7. **Authorship.** Bychowski argues "Eleanor Rykener should be listed as an author or coauthor" alongside Britby and the scribe (p. 110). If the mod quotes the record in a codex or loading tip, credit it as *Eleanor Rykener, with John Britby and an unnamed clerk, 1394*.
8. **Her name.** Bychowski reads *Rykener* as suggesting "the profession of reckoner or accountant" (p. 98; again p. 110). This is an etymological gloss. Verify it before using it in loc (open question).

## 6. Methodology and terminology ("trans before trans")

**The editors' position (introduction).**
- **Claim the past without forcing it.** Gender-crossing figures' "stories can and should be united under the mantle of the transgender past" (p. 4). The editors also commit to "guaranteeing the figures … the freedom not to 'be' transgender" (p. 11).
  - For the mod this is the same line as the README: the narrator never claims a historical figure. Faith voices may claim them in character.
- **Anachronism charges.** Calling trans history "anachronistic" is at best "a knee-jerk historicism, and at worst … a thinly veiled form of transphobia" (p. 8).
- **Terms, not excavation.** The task is often "less excavating ancient trans people from some long-lost or forgotten archive than just figuring out which terms" were used (p. 9). This supports the mod's practice of period vocabulary in each faith's own language.
- **Structures over individuals.** The volume looks beyond single lives to "religious cultures, legal architectures, and other forms of structural power" (p. 7). That suits CK3's institutions-first design.
- **Race and religion shape gender** (pp. 10–11):
  - *sudan* and *sqâliba* (Arabic: Slav, enslaved person, or eunuch) show that gender words carried enslavement and origin;
  - Heng: the Crusades already built racial and religious hierarchies.
  - Use with care: *sqâliba* is a slave-trade term, not a gender identity.
- **Indigenous critique** (p. 12). "Transgender" frameworks can displace Native and Indigenous ways of knowing. This is the same principle behind the README's living-communities rule.

**Chapter-level methods worth reusing.**
- **Bychowski: "cistory".**
  - The default assumption that every figure is cisgender (pp. 95–98). Her three demands (pp. 107–110):
    1. affirm her body;
    2. use her name;
    3. credit her as co-author.
  - For writing: the narrator's pronoun and name follow the person, and the hostile record is labelled as such.
- **Betancourt: read invective as flotsam.**
  - Hostile texts are "the flotsam of trans women's lives, the violences they faced and the freedoms they might have carved out" (p. 314).
  - Betancourt also warns, via Hartman, that recovery re-performs the violence (pp. 313–314).
  - For writing: quote slurs only in hostile characters' mouths. Never make them the narrator's terms.
- **Raskolnikov: pragmatic, non-confessional gender.**
  - Silence offers "a psychology-free, internality-free version of genderqueer existence" (p. 186). The modern demand for a "standardized narrative of relentless lifelong dysphoria" is "coercive" (p. 186).
  - For the mod: daughters' rites should not require a dysphoria confession. Choosing the life is enough.
- **Mills: "a site of possibility rather than diagnosis"** (Halberstam's trans*, p. 151). Mills shifts Wilgefortis to *they* and declines a fixed identity (p. 152). This is a model for figures the mod cannot read with certainty.
- **Larson: refuse the examining gaze.** The archive "lays open" bodies. Do not re-stage examinations as content (ch. 14).
- **LaFleur (epilogue): keep historical terms.**
  - She hesitates to call premodern people "nonbinary" because the binary itself has a history (Velocci) (p. 368).
  - Her preference is "to sit with the terms that these figures developed for themselves" (p. 368).
  - She keeps "hermaphrodite" as a historical term with explanation, not as a modern label (pp. 371–372).
- **DeVun: separate intersex from trans.**
  - DeVun uses "intersex" for bodies and "transgender" as an analytic, and keeps them distinct (pp. 29–31).
  - For the mod: do not use medieval "hermaphrodite" or monstrous-race material as evidence about daughters. That conflates intersex people with trans women.

## 7. Guardrails (including conflicts with the v1 report)

1. **Never claim a figure for a faith in narration.** This applies to Rykener, Elagabalus, Psellos, the Gregory of Tours figure, Matrona and Wilgefortis. A faith may cite them in character. The editors' "freedom not to 'be' transgender" (p. 11) is the same principle.
2. **No Attis imagery.**
   - Betancourt's n. 46 (p. 319) cites galli and Attis art scholarship.
   - The v1 report's P75 (galli clamp) and its "Rite of the Clamp" tenet go against current rules. The v1 report's claim that the clamp was "found in the Thames, London" is also not in this book.
3. **No "third gender" romanticisation.**
   - Ringrose's "third gender" and Basil's "accursed *genos*" (p. 309) are the court's and the Church's frames, used for hostile voice only.
   - The v1 report's tenets "The Capon Is a Woman", "Monstra Dei", "Gens Utriusque Sexus" and "Ranks of the Made" lean on third-sex, intersex and monstrous-race framings. Review them against current design. The README already says vanilla castration does not make a daughter.
4. **Do not conflate intersex with trans.** The v1 report suggests "hermaphrodite" as an in-game umbrella term (P84, P87). That runs against DeVun (pp. 29–31) and the mod's terms. Keep "hermaphrodite" for quoted hostile or learned voices only.
5. **Trans men out of scope.** Wilgefortis, Silence, Elenx, Marin, the monk-saints, Matrona and Larson's subjects are context only.
6. **Living communities.** The book does not discuss hijra, khwaja sira, bissu or two-spirit people. Keep it that way in any use. Do not route Ottoman köçek material (enslaved boys, p. 166) into any faith.
7. **No suicide or self-harm.** Do not use Bychowski's "Necropolitics of Narcissus" (p. 379). Goodrich's self-maiming material is about beggars' bodies and the law (pp. 284–287). Use only the legal-voice quotes, never self-injury scenes.
8. **Antisemitic and racial source material.**
   - Campbell's Jewish hyena (pp. 245–249) and DeVun's African "race of both sexes" (p. 33) may appear only as clearly hostile voices, framed by the narrator.
   - No illumination detail (circumcised hyena) in loc.
9. **Sex work.** Dio's "female huckster" (p. 307) and the dancers-and-*porneia* link (p. 301) are slurs. Follow `sw_books_a.md`, `sw_books_b.md` and the Rykener note §2.2.
10. **Genital focus.** Clement's "unless you saw them naked" (p. 305) and the examinations in chs. 3, 5 and 14 are transphobic structures. Do not reproduce them.
