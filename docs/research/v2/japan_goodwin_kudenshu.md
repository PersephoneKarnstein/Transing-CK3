Texts read in full, from `scratchpad/v2/ocr/`:
- **G**: Janet R. Goodwin, *Selling Songs and Smiles: The Sex Trade in Heian and Kamakura Japan* (Hawai'i 2007). Intro, chs. 1–4, conclusion; notes checked where a date or source mattered. Printed pages.
- **K**: Yung-Hee Kim Kwon, "The Emperor's Songs: Go-Shirakawa and *Ryōjin hishō kudenshū*", *Monumenta Nipponica* 41:3 (1986), pp. 261–98: introduction (pp. 261–72) and translation of Book 10 (pp. 272–98). Printed pages. Quotes are Kwon's English (OCR spacing restored).
Status key as in JS §2 (**firm**, **hostile**, **late** = after 1210 or before 1050, **sec.**, ***unverified***). "Start" = relevant to a 1178 start.

## 1. Verdict

1. **The office is now firm and richer.** Heads (*chōja*) are named in diaries (Kinju of Kamusaki and Sonboshi of Eguchi, 1119; Ōi of Aohaka, 1150s), held a seal, gave written leave, kept the gifts, and later sued in court (G 24–26, 29, 33–34).
2. **Adoption is attested in the Aohaka song-line itself.** "Mei adopted Otomae as her daughter" (K 277), reported by a rival singer. Goodwin adds that adoption and fictive kinship "may have" been used to recruit and to control labour (G 34). Both faces must stay in the design.
3. **The trade can be part of the religion, on Goodwin's terms.** The singers' own sources tie the work to the god (Hyakudayū, prayer for lovers and fortune) and to the Dharma (Miyagi's "even love play" line, imayō rebirth). Goodwin argues hard that this does *not* make the work sacred sex. So it becomes a tenet about the house's livelihood under the god, not a hierogamy.
4. **The shaman-entertainer thesis is out.** Goodwin's case (G 87–102) is fair and strong. The faith may say "the god hears singers"; it never says "we were priestesses".
5. **The *Kudenshū* fixes the Aohaka lineage**: Shisan → Mei → Otomae → Go-Shirakawa → Suketoki and Moronaga (K 277–84, 297–98), with other mother–daughter lines (Waka → Akomaro → Kosawa; Sasanami trained in her mother's way). Otomae came to court in **1157**, not 1159 (K 276).
6. **Start-year hooks:** Suketoki's transmission began 1178.3.23 at Takijiri (K 297); an all-night imayō vigil at Iwashimizu 1178.9.20–26, with *shirabyōshi* dancing on 9.24 (K 295).
7. **No person assigned male appears in or around the houses** in either text, beyond kugutsu men, husbands, patrons and male imayō lineages (§2f). The gender base stays where JS §1 put it: invented daughters in a documented women's institution.
8. **Strength:** institution *strong → very strong*; religion of the houses *strong*; gender evidence unchanged (*thin to plausible*). Still rank 1.

### 2e. Goodwin's argument, stated fairly

- **Against the shaman-entertainer thesis (pp. 87–102).** Yanagita, Nakayama, Gorai, Saeki and others derive asobi from shamans or call them shamans. Goodwin: "All of these theories are intriguing, and none can be disproved; but I think they are based on inconclusive evidence" (p. 90). There are no patrons' memories of shaman-prostitutes and no penitent-shaman tales, where both abound for asobi (pp. 90–91). The RH shaman songs do not show paid sex; one cited "shaman-prostitute" is a child (p. 101). Virginity-for-shamans is itself a later idea (Yoshie, pp. 94, 102). What joins them is music, not sex (p. 101). The religious colour of the houses shows the "interpenetration of sacred and secular": "If asobi had shunned the sacred, that would have been the surprise" (p. 102).
- **Nor polluted by trade (pp. 103–11).** Kamakura pollution charges attach to specific acts and to all women's bodies, not to the profession.
- **Against romance.** Sources show how elite men saw the women, not how they lived (p. 5); Heian writing is a "literature of appreciation" (p. 128); singers' complaints would be bad business at a banquet (p. 36). Yet she also refuses pure victimhood: "Occasional glimpses of agency — of entrepreneurship, resistance, or even regret" (p. 5).
- **Her own frame.** They were "social actors" to be read through "marriage, law, and property" (p. 119), "regarded not as promiscuous women but as professionals" (p. 156). Status was ambiguous from the start and was "clarified in a negative way" in Kamakura (p. 134).

### 2f. Asobi, kugutsu, *shirabyōshi*; change; men

- **Asobi**: river ports (Eguchi, Kamusaki, Kashima, Kaya), Uji, Sakamoto, Muro; boats; imayō (pp. 15, 30). Eastern asobi worked at inns and blur into kugutsu (p. 39).
- **Kugutsu**: Masafusa's alien, tented, untaxed troupe of men and women; women sing and trade sex; Mino, Mikawa, Ōmi best (pp. 27–28). Goodwin and Amino doubt the outlaw picture; by c. 1200 they live at inns and in *shuku* (pp. 136–39).
- ***Shirabyōshi***: late Heian dancers, at first in men's dress (p. 28). Out of scope as subjects.
- **Change 1100–1250**: *shirabyōshi* displace imayō at court (1202: "asobi were not given clothing", p. 29); warrior patrons and inns move the trade east (pp. 29, 147); Yodo floods (p. 148); Aohaka "few houses … the asobi seem gone" by 1280 (p. 148); men as managers (pp. 127–28); 1212 *nakadachi* edict (p. 125); the 1267 and 1279 property blows (§2c).
- **Men and the houses**, all that the book holds:
  - kugutsu men: archers, jugglers, puppeteers, gone from later sources (pp. 27, 139);
  - husbands and parents who press for more customers (Yukitoki, hostile, pp. 15–16);
  - a scribe of kugutsu birth who joins the troupe's song (Konjaku; p. 137);
  - the male dancer Kimikata summoned by Yorinaga (1148; p. 26), **sexual context: not for use**;
  - an Inari festival skit (mid-11th c.) in which one actor "took the role of a young woman" in a mock coupling (p. 87). The actor's sex is not stated, so it is **not usable**;
  - a monk "feminine in appearance" disguised as a nun in a rape tale (p. 60). **Excluded**;
  - asobi "dressed up as children" for Sanetomo, 1212 (p. 36). **Excluded**.

## 4. Corrections to `japan_sources.md`

1. **Otomae's teaching dates** (JS §2a, Kim pp. 19–20 row): she came to court **1157.1.10** (K 276) and taught "for ten years" (K 284) until 1169. Replace "1159–69" with 1157–69.
2. **Adoption** (JS §5 Office, "*unverified*"): attested once, in the Aohaka line: "Mei adopted Otomae as her daughter" (K 277), told by a rival. Goodwin p. 34 makes adoption a likely recruiting tool and warns of control. Change to "attested (one case), with the control risk".
3. **Hyakudayū figurines** (JS row Kim pp. 9, 163): Goodwin's Yūjoki has a conditional, "If one were carved for each customer …" (G 19). Do not say singers "kept hundreds and thousands".
4. **Asobi vs *yahochi*** (JS row Kim p. 9): Goodwin reads *Wamyōshō* as day vs night and says "we do not have enough information" (G 14). Kim's "only imayō singers were asobi" is not supported. Downgrade to sec., contested.
5. **The chōja's powers** (JS row Kim p. 9): add the seal, the written leave and custody of gifts (G 25–26, 33–34), and the reading that members' services could be "property of the chōja" (G 34). Kim's "protected members from exploitation" stays *sec.*; the office cuts both ways.
6. **Nabiki**: Jikkinshō names "The Aohaka kugutsu Nabiki" as a *Shikashū* poet (G 133). She is a named Aohaka singer-poet; "third-generation head" remains Konishi's inference.
7. **Two Tanba no Tsubone.** Go-Shirakawa's consort (d. 1197, mother an asobi; K 283 n. 131) is not the *shirabyōshi* Tanba no Tsubone, a later retired sovereign's concubine who died in childbirth in 1205 (G 133). Never merge them. Neither is claimed.
8. **Yūjoki date**: Goodwin puts it in Masafusa's last years (d. 1111; G 18); the Kairaishiki is c. 1087. Write "c. 1087–1111".
9. **Shōkū and the Fugen-chōja** (JS §2b, GB p. 54): text 1212–15, and such tales do not appear before the 1180s (G 112). Not something a 1178 house "knows" as scripture; a late-era tale at most.
10. **Hōnen at Muro**: Goodwin gives no year; the scrolls run 1230s–14th c. and grow harsher (G 115–16). 1207 is ***unverified*** here.
11. **Toneguro/Tonekuro**: same woman (Kamusaki; *Hōbutsushū*, 1180s; K 296–97 "caught up in some fighting"; G 112 "pirates"). Keep only "reborn through song".
12. **Line passing to men**: the *Kudenshū* shows Go-Shirakawa certifying two men (1178–79). JS row "until male aristocrats were admitted" is right; add the 1178 date.
13. **Doctrine-core quote**: Kwon 1986 has "secular literature, too, transform itself"; Kim 1994 has "secular words". Pick one translation per loc line and cite it. The line follows directly on "sink deep in sin" (K 297): quote the second half, never the first in the faith's voice.
14. **Shamans in RH**: RH 364 (the "roaming" daughter) is a child, and Goodwin says it shows no sex trade at all (G 100–101). Already excluded; now doubly so.
15. **1180 Muro**: Goodwin writes "Go-Takakura" for Michichika's 1180 Itsukushima journey (G 129); the retired sovereign was Takakura (JS §2c / Tsuji agree). Typo in G.
16. **"Sex work is never a tenet" (JS §1.7, §5)** is superseded by the user's new stance; see §5 here.

## 5. Revised design (with the work in the religion)

**Name and family.** *Imayō no Ie*, daughters *uketsugi* (invented). Rite under `mahayana_faith`, Hyakudayū as the house kami (honji suijaku). Keep.

**Framing sentence (design doc, not loc).** The houses are women's houses whose trade is song, lodging, boats and, for some women and some nights, the bed. The faith's daughters are women of the houses. Some work in the trade, many do not, and all keep the song (G 20, 35: part-timers and leavers).

**Gate: "The Complete Transmission"** (K 284; Sasanami K 282). As in JS §5, now with two doors, both judged by the house:
- *By song*: seasons of teaching, then she sings the house's line back on Hyakudayū's night.
- *By taking*: a grown woman taken as a daughter of a mother of the house (K 277: Mei and Otomae; G 34: fictive kinship). Invented: the adult-only rule and the form.
- The trade is **never** part of the gate. No pain test (Tori's eyelashes stay others' zeal, K 282).

**Office: the chōja, head of the house.**
- Chosen by the house (Yūjoki, G 19). She holds the seal, gives leave to work elsewhere, keeps and shares gifts, speaks for the house in suits (G 25–26, 29, 33–34, 136).
- The design says plainly that the post could be used to control women (G 34, 122). Events test it (6, 7 below).
- Succession: by the house's choice among daughters by birth or by taking (rule 20). Invented.

**Doctrines** (vanilla keys; *check* against the other five faiths and the Japanese baseline).
- `doctrine_clerical_gender_female_only`; `doctrine_pluralism_righteous`; `doctrine_pilgrimage_encouraged` (Hirota, Sumiyoshi; G 19, 25).
- Head: `doctrine_no_head` (houses autonomous, G 32–33; Wakita's "outside hierarchies", G 121). *Check*.
- Bastardry: `doctrine_bastardry_legitimization` (children by courtiers acknowledged, G 132). *User call.*
- Adultery: leave at the Japanese baseline. Goodwin shows Heian marriage was "a social process without a name" (G 8), but an "accepted" doctrine would read as a modern claim. *User call.*
- Gender: female-dominated, *check*.

**Tenets (three).**
1. `tenet_ritual_celebrations`: Hyakudayū's nights (G 28), flower dedications (K 281, 288).
2. **Custom "Song Is Dharma"** (replaces `tenet_inner_journey`): K 296–97; Miyagi (Kim p. 12). Effects: piety from song events; a pilgrimage song can heal or bless (K 296). Parameter for events.
3. **Custom "The House Keeps Its Trade" (new; the work in the religion).** Text in a mother's voice: the house's living, river, lodging, song and bed alike, is honest work under Hyakudayū. The god is asked for lovers and fortune (Yūjoki G 19; RH 380 G 37–38; Kairaishiki G 28). The head shares the gifts fairly and guards the women from patrons (G 23, 33). No woman of the house is shamed for working, or for not working, or for leaving by marriage (G 20, 35).
   - Grounding: the singers' own voice, Miyagi: "In Settsu Province / anything at all / becomes Dharma, / even flirting, even love play" (Kim p. 12). Goodwin's guard: it is livelihood under the god and within the Dharma's reach, **not** sacred sex and not a kami-marriage (G 90–102, 111).
   - Mechanics (proposal): `lustful` is neither sin nor virtue for this faith (vanilla tenets that move it: check); the head (office holder) gets a small yearly "house's share" income per working member and an opinion bonus from members if she shares evenly (event 5); no opinion loss between members over the trade; a woman who marries out keeps the house trait and seat claims.
   - Rejected again: `tenet_carnal_exaltation`. It makes chastity a sin, contradicting "some daughters do not work", and it is the sacred-sex reading Goodwin rejects (§16 list already rejects it: "liberating sex without liberating women").

**Event ideas** (source; **invented** parts named; all work-related events give the woman's own choice as an option).
1. **Hyakudayū's night** (yearly). Kairaishiki (G 28), RH 380 (G 37–38). Drums, dance, a prayer for lovers *and* for fortune; options name what each woman asks. Invented: the house's own night, the options. No objects described.
2. **The Complete Transmission** (gate). K 282, 284. Branch: a patron's protégée wants the songs without the seasons (Sasanami); the mother says "not complete". Invented: the house's judging.
3. **A letter of leave.** Chōshūki 1119 (G 25–26, 33–34). A patron asks the head to send a daughter to his banquet up-river. Options: grant under the house seal *if she wants to go*; send a willing sister instead; refuse. Invented: asking her (the sources never show it). Off-screen; the event ends at the boat.
4. **Dividing the gifts.** Yūjoki (G 19–20), 1048 (G 23), Kinju (G 25). After a pilgrim party pays in rice and silk: equal shares (the 1048 way), shares by rank (Kinju), or shares by work. A visiting courtier may sneer that they "lose all sense of shame" (Masafusa, hostile, given to a named vanilla courtier). Invented: the meeting.
5. **A patron's debt.** No set prices; payment a patron's obligation (G 32); spendthrift patrons (G 140–41). A provincial custodian owes the house for a season of banquets; his estate's people say he wastes their dues. Options: press him, forgive it for his favour, take it to his proprietor. Invented: all specifics. Dated plausibly from the Konjaku type (1120s).
6. **Horses and cloth.** *Chūyūki* 1114 (G 136). An estate official seizes the house's horses and bolts of cloth; the head petitions the proprietor; on success he is dismissed. Invented: names, the house. In-period precedent.
7. **"Not taught as her own daughter."** K 277 (Akomaro on Mei and Otomae). A rival house says a daughter taken as a grown woman cannot hold the line. She answers by singing the secret piece (*furukawa*, K 280). After 1185 a late variant goes to court as a property suit (1249, G 143). Invented: the dispute; adult only.
8. **The preacher and the singers.** K 297 (sin line), *Hosshinshū* type (G 115), RH 208 (Kim p. 82), Miyagi (Kim p. 12). A Tendai or nenbutsu preacher at the landing says the singers are "sunk in sin" and must quit or chant. Replies: "In Settsu anything at all becomes Dharma"; "why can't we, too?"; some women chant *and* stay; one chooses to retire (honoured, not rescued). After 1207 the preacher may be "a holy man from the capital" at Muro (Hōnen legend, unnamed). Invented: the scene.
9. **To Hirota and Sumiyoshi** (pilgrimage). Yūjoki (G 19); Chōshūki 1119 (G 25); K 296 (sincere song at shrines). The road where court pilgrims and singers cross; a song at the shrine. Invented: outcomes.
10. **Summoned to Hōjūji.** K 281, 288–89; Iwashimizu vigil 1178.9 (K 295). The retired emperor's flower dedication calls singers from Eguchi, Kanzaki and Aohaka; a style quarrel; a brocade reward. After 1179.11 the call stops (house arrest). Prestige, not holiness (rule 7). The emperor is never claimed.
11. **A song at the bedside.** K 282–83, 296 (RH 32, Yakushi; Mei and Kiyotsune). An old mother of the house falls ill; a daughter sings the Yakushi song; health roll; if she dies it is of age, and the house keeps her death-day yearly (K 284; Hokkeji, G 142). Invented: all names.
12. **Good fortune in old age.** K 282 (Kiyotsune), K 281 (Mei died a nun). An aged singer chooses: teach the line, take vows and stay, or both. No despair text (rule 17).

## 6. Risks, and how the work is handled well

- **The premise risk** (QV pp. 26–27). Never "trans women belong in the trade". Daughters are women of the houses; the trade is the houses' trade. Loc must show daughters as singers, teachers, heads, boat-keepers, poets and ladies-in-waiting at least as often as in the trade. The gate never touches the trade.
- **Voice.** Workers speak for themselves (Tokenoboru's anger, RH 338–39; Miyagi; Kokō). Patrons and preachers are the hostile voices, each named and of a vanilla faith or court (rule 3).
- **No rescue narrative.** Hōnen- and Saigyō-type tales are the Buddhist voice. A woman who retires does so as her choice, with the same honour as one who stays (rule 23 logic). No text says the trade is despair (rule 17).
- **No sensationalism, no scenes.** All work events end at the landing, the boat or the door. Never use Akihira's *Shinsarugakuki* euphemisms, Yorinaga's diary, the *Kokon chomonjū* ironcaster tale, or any love-bed song. Hyakudayū's objects are never described (rule 5).
- **No sacred sex.** No kami-marriage, no "the act is worship", no temple-prostitution analogy (G 90–102). The god is asked for lovers and luck, like any patron god of a trade.
- **No romance, no victimhood.** Write the office's power both ways (G 34, 122): an event where a head abuses the seal must exist (event 3 can turn on a head who sends a woman unwilling; the house may unseat her).
- **Money is plain.** Rice, silk, robes, fans; "rewards", not prices (G 32). No coin before mid-12th c. (G 6).
- **Minors** (hard exclusion): the *Sarashina* fourteen-year-old, Otomae at 12–13, Mei's adoption *age*, the bought girls (G 34–35), RH 364, the 1212 "dressed as children", Tokuishime (age unknown). Adoption appears only as a fact and as adult-only in the faith. No child entrants.
- **Death.** Toneguro (violent), the Heiji story at Aohaka (drowning), Nachi, Tanba no Tsubone of 1205 (childbirth): out. Only Otomae's old-age death and memorial are used.
- ***Shirabyōshi*, women in men's dress, the 1178 Iwashimizu dancers**: world only.
- **Historical figures** (Go-Shirakawa, Otomae, Mei, Kane, Enzu, Kinju, Ōi, Nabiki, Miyagi, Kokō) are never claimed; houses and daughters are invented.
- **Orientalism.** Avoid "courtesan" and "pleasure quarter" in the narrator's voice; no Edo, no geisha; never Masafusa's "northern barbarians" except as a named hostile line.
- **Rule 18 / 2.** No "every singer was a little one of us"; no "we were priestesses once".
