Scratchpad note, 2026-10-25. No repo file edited. ## 1. The precedent: how Yutai and Badenya filled gaps

**Badenya is the real precedent; Yutai is a different kind.** I looked for a modern Chinese trans community behind Yutai and found none. `east_transgender_china.md` skims Chiang's modern chapters (Taiwan cross-dressing shows, Hong Kong) only for hostile rumour shapes, and nothing in Yutai's docs or loc borrows a living Chinese community.

**Badenya** (`faith_design.md` §15; `docs/faiths/badenya.md`):
- **Borrowed:**
  - "Mothers" who take in a newcomer by referral and teach her a craft.
  - Networks ranked by seniority.
  - A feast with a "mother" and a "father", where the young serve and the elders sit (Gaudio; Broqua; Geoffrion; Mackin).
  - Burial under one's own name, against kin who claim the body, the goods and the birth name (Broqua 2017 ¶3; Gaudio pp. 192–204).
  - The people's own self-words, "born so" and "God made us", and their choice of "woman" over "transgender" (Larmarange and Broqua 2023, p. 11).
- **Transformed into:**
  - the claiming mother ("my mother's child", grounded in the period by Tenenba);
  - the two-stage naming feast (serve three times, then host);
  - *The House Buries Its Own* as a doctrine;
  - the 7-yearly age-set (built on Mande house practice).
- **Where it lives:** event mechanics and tenets, plus design notes. The README says it plainly: "This texture comes from contemporary West African studies and is marked as reconstruction" (§15 "World").
- **Limits:**
  - "Borrows roles, never the identity of any living community."
  - The narrator marks it as reconstruction.
  - The material is Wolof, Hausa, Akan and Abidjan, "analogy only, and no community in them is the faith or its descendant" (badenya.md "Ethnography").
  - It names communities that are *not* mapped onto the faith ('yan daudu, góor-jigéen, woubi, sasso) because their members mostly see themselves as men.
  - No modern word reaches game text. The English "mother" and the period's *jeli*, *nyama* and *badenya* carry it.

**Yutai Dao:**
- **Borrowed:** modern transfeminist *values*, not a community. "Gates recognise, not test" (Serano 2016, cited in yutai-dao.md for *The Guarantor's Question*): the guarantor vouches for the house, not for her sincerity, and no answer turns her away. The shared House structure also runs through it: Sisters' Houses, Elder Sister, *Take as Daughter*, *The Sisters Choose*.
- **Where:** gate mechanics and the design note.
- **Limits:** every concrete form is Tang or Song (she associations, name-panels, nüguan ordination names), so the modern layer is invisible in loc.

**Mod-wide.** The House itself (houses led by "mothers", chosen kin, adoptive matrilines) is the modern house-and-chosen-family structure, given institutional shells: convent, guild, godparenthood (`beliefs.md` C1, "ST 'The Street Is Our Hearth' (houses led by 'mothers')"; TM#27 "Chosen Kin"). The governing rule is `beliefs.md` §G.6: **"Borrow institutions, not identity concepts … modern identity language stays in the design doc."**

**Slang in game text: never.** No faith puts modern community slang in loc. The mod's own word "dolls" is in the title only and is banned from the Japan faith's text (JD decision, 2026-10-25). So the precedent licenses **structures, roles and values in period dress, marked as reconstruction in the docs and README**. It does not license words.

## 2. The sources

### 2a. Mitsuhashi Junko, tr. Hasegawa, "The transgender world in contemporary Japan: the male to female cross-dressers' community in Shinjuku", *Inter-Asia Cultural Studies* 7:2 (2006), 202–227 (Japanese original 2003). INSIDER, highest weight. Read in full.

**Standing.**
- A trans woman writing as "an insider/member of the community" (p. 204).
- She learned the work as a "volunteer hostess" at the bar Ju-ne in 1995–98 and as a guest employee at Misty in 1999–2001 (p. 204).
- She "would welcome critiques of my academic legitimacy" (p. 204).
- The community described is chiefly the semi-professional *josō* (MTF cross-dresser) world of about ten bars. Many members lived as men on weekdays (p. 215). It is not a portrait of trans women in general, and she separates it from the *new-half* (nyūhāfu) trade, the closed members' clubs and the GID groups (pp. 204–209).

**What the community does (structures, with pages):**
- **The *mama*.** Each bar is run by a "mama". "'Mama(s)' are not simply the proprietresses of bars and clubs. They also play a role as guardians or mentors" (p. 225 n. 1). A second mama, the *chii-mama*, introduces newcomers to guests (p. 219).
- **Daughters and sisters.** "The members are called 'daughters' of the 'mama'", and "the seniors and juniors of the members are regarded as 'sisters'" (p. 215). She calls the whole "like a matrilineal family-based community" (p. 215).
- **Protection.** In serious trouble with customers or outsiders, "the 'mama' and the entire club will promise to protect 'their own daughter'" (p. 215).
- **Teaching by sisters.** Newcomers learn "from their senior members/sisters" (p. 215):
  - where to buy, and how to make up and dress;
  - how to talk with guests;
  - how to manage unwanted advances.
  The "techniques, attitudes, and philosophy" are handed down (p. 215).
- **Lineage of houses.** Former Ju-ne staff opened their own bars, then their trainees opened theirs, "as if the family tree was expanding" (p. 210). Rival bars "shared a fellow feeling" and gave each other's members discounts (p. 210).
- **The *heya*.** Members pay a monthly fee for a shared dressing room near the bar. It holds lockers, dressing tables and a place to nap, "to store and hide their female accessories" (pp. 214–215). Members are expected to call at their own bar when they use it (p. 215).
- **Considerate prices.** Members pay about half what men pay, so the mama can "be considerate of CD women's spending to keep up their clothes, cosmetics" (p. 214).
- **Help asked, never forced.** Members help when it is busy. "They are never scolded or penalized by the 'mama' for not helping" (p. 215). Helpful members sometimes get an allowance (p. 215).
- **Refugees received.** From the mid-1990s Shinjuku took in women leaving the closed Elizabeth kaikan club, and people from the web (p. 210).
- **Privacy.** "Interrogating someone's privacy is basically taboo" (p. 215).
- **Age.** Most members are in their 30s; "the oldest one I know is in her 70s" (p. 215).
- **Recognition logic.** "A MTF cross-dresser with feminine performance is considered as a 'woman,' regardless of one's physical and biological conditions" (abstract, p. 202). When shop clerks and hotel staff see through her, "the validity of the mimicry is maintained, albeit exposed as such" (p. 224); an inn lays out a woman's *yukata* anyway (p. 224). This is the IACS form of her book's "being read does not end being treated as a woman" (M6 §1.2).
- **A self-named rule.** Her model member keeps "the old hostess theory that prohibits her from sleeping with customers" (p. 220). Working rules are the women's own.

**What she says that the mod declines** (consistent with M6, OG §6f):
- "Japanese traditional society … allowed the existence of transgendered people" and *mitate* as Japanese tradition (p. 224): the tolerant-Japan claim (OG guardrail 3).
- "Transgender community (neither man nor woman)" (p. 203): a third framing (rule 18; brief).
- "Dual fictions" and "female mimicry" (pp. 220–224) describe men who are part-time women. The daughters are women and their dress is their life (rule 16), so the fiction frame is not borrowed.
- The "quasi-heterosexual" economy of patrons and *kama zuki* chasers (pp. 216–218), sexual detail, and sex taught between sisters (p. 219): not borrowed at all (§6).

**Offers:** the strongest insider template for the house's inner life:
- the mother as both head and guardian;
- daughters and sisters;
- teaching by senior sisters;
- a lineage of houses;
- a place to keep one's things;
- mutual protection;
- no questions about the past;
- recognition that holds when she is "read".

### 2b. Fujimoto Yukari, "Transgender: Female Hermaphrodites and Male Androgynes", tr. Flores and Nagaike, ed. Orbaugh, *U.S.-Japan Women's Journal* 27 (2004), 76–117. OUTSIDER (a manga critic: "I am female and basically heterosexual", p. 107).

**Summary.**
- Shōjo manga "began with hermaphroditism" (Tezuka's *Ribon no kishi*) and Takarazuka (p. 78).
- Cross-dressing heroines (Oscar) read as freedom.
- Men's manga ties "becoming a woman" to "humiliation and defeat" (p. 100), postwar defeat (p. 100), and men "who want … to 'flee'" manhood's responsibilities (p. 102).
- Editor's note: *okama* means an "effeminate" gay man and is potentially offensive, so it is left untranslated (n. 20).

**Offers:**
- hostile-voice *shapes* only (flight from duty, defeat, the castration fear), which a 12th-c. speaker would put in period idiom, such as a father's "he fled the house's name";
- confirmation that *okama* is never usable.

Its manga is fiction about fiction, much of it about youths. No structure is borrowed.

### 2c. Salenius (ed.), *Gender in Japanese Popular Culture* (Palgrave, 2023, Open Access CC BY). Mostly OUTSIDERS; no trans woman author.

- **Intro (Salenius), p. 4.** "x-gender" (*x-jendaa*) as "a form of anti-identity" (Johnson). Mention only; out of scope.
- **Ho, "From Dansō to Genderless", pp. 29–60.**
  - *okama* / *onabe* (n. 11, p. 38);
  - GID (*seidōitsuseishōgai*) as a self-identity many prefer to "transgender" (p. 39 n. 17);
  - the 2003 GID Act conditions: over 20, unmarried, "full" surgery, no children (p. 39 n. 16);
  - x-gender, late 1990s Kansai, "neither male nor female, or … both" (p. 40).
  The chapter is about AFAB *dansō* and out of scope otherwise.
- **Miyazaki, university *josō* contests, pp. 123–150.** Men in women's dress for a festival gain status. "Being a trans woman is seen in a negative light, creating *kenokan* or a feeling of disgust" (p. 132). This is the modern twin of Tsuji's Rokuhara guise (JD e9): a one-night costume is applauded, a life is not.
- **Furukawa, pp. 151–174.** *Onee* "comes from the Japanese word for older sister and typically refers to an extremely camp and feminine gay man" (p. 151); the 2000s *onee kyara* boom (p. 152). So *onē* is not a trans women's word, and is a TV stereotype.
- **Nozawa, p. 191.** Mitsuhashi dates the second "LGBT boom" from 2012.
- **Glasspool, p. 250.** A trans woman cosplayer, Hiromi, read as a woman for the first time: "at last, I feel natural" (*47 News*, 2019, via Glasspool).

**Offers:**
- terminology hazards (*onē*, *okama*, *otokonoko*, x-gender);
- the costume-versus-life hostile shape;
- legal context.
No house structure.

## 6. What must not be borrowed

1. **The living community itself.** Shinjuku's *josō* bars, Ju-ne, Kozue, Misty, Elizabeth kaikan, *new-half* show pubs, any named mama. No bar, person or slogan is the faith, its ancestor or its model in loc or README. The README names Mitsuhashi's study as the source of the *structure* only, in Badenya's wording: "borrows roles, never the identity of any living community".
2. **The bar economy.** Hostess service, the "quasi-heterosexual" fantasy, patrons as "conspirators" of a fiction, *kama zuki*, the love-hotel geography, karaoke duets, half-price-for-dressers as a sales strategy (IACS pp. 214–222). The house's trade is already handled on Goodwin's terms (JD tenet 2, SWA/SWB).
3. **Sexual content.** Sex teaching between sisters (IACS p. 219); any account of bodies in bed (pp. 218–219); Fujimoto's assault plots (p. 100).
4. **The part-time frame.** "Mr A becomes Ms A"; the dressing room as a place to change back to a man (IACS pp. 219–220). Her dress is her life (rule 16; JD e9).
5. **The mama's gate on looks and body** (Ishii pp. 21, 29). This includes stopping or demanding any bodily change for the house's profit. It breaks rule 22 and SWB 5b.7, which says never read beauty.
6. **Tolerant-Japan and *mitate*-as-national-trait claims** (IACS p. 224; M6). OG 3 and 16.
7. **Third and "neither" framings:**
   - "neither man nor woman" (IACS p. 203);
   - 双性 (decided in JD and M6);
   - *X-gender* (a modern Japanese non-binary identity, Salenius pp. 4, 40), mentioned only, outside this faith.
8. **Medical-legal categories.** GID / 性同一性障害, the 2003 Act's conditions and the 2023 ruling are context for the designer only. No "registry" analogue, no diagnosis gate (rule 23: no outside probation). JD's hostile *koseki*-like voices stay period: the Masafusa, preacher and steward lines.
9. **The intra-community split.** GID people who "discriminate against other transgender groups" (IACS p. 208). At most a future schism note (`beliefs.md` E5, "do not rank oppressions"), never in this faith's first build.
10. **Minors and youth material.** Dohi's school case, Fujimoto's and Salenius's youth and manga material, *otokonoko*, *chigo* and *jisha* (already excluded).
11. **Doll, porcelain, china, girl, child imagery,** including "unhelpful girls" (IACS p. 215) and "granddaughter" as a house word.
12. **Edo and modern objects in period dress:** kimono or *yukata* as words, red lanterns, karaoke-as-song (OG 1).
13. **Despair framings:** the "fled", "defeated" and "humiliated" origin stories (Fujimoto pp. 100–102), and anything near self-harm (rule 17).

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Take Badenya's model, not Yutai's, for this pass.** Borrow the Shinjuku house structure from its insider historian, transform each piece into 1178 forms (§4), and mark it as reconstruction in the design doc and README. Suggested README line: "The houses' inner life (mothers, elder sisters, a seat among the women, no questions about the past) is drawn from a trans woman historian's study of a modern Tokyo community and set in period form; no living community is the faith."
2. **Split the gate in two** (G12):
   - The welcome makes her a woman of the house. The vouching sister, the *ane* and a seat among the women are recognition, not a trial.
   - The complete transmission makes her a holder of the line.
   This brings the gate in line with rules 8 and 22, with Mitsuhashi, and with Dohi's "recognition, repeated".
3. **Rename the trait** to *Ie no Onna* ("Woman of the House") and keep *uketsugi* only for the act of receiving the line, or drop it. The user decides. Either way, event text says "daughter of the house" and "sister".
4. **Add the *ane* office** (G2) through the existing House framework (Elder Sister → *ane*). It gives Mitsuhashi's second mama a period shape at no mechanical cost.
5. **Build three small pieces:**
   - the robe-chest and its "inside/outside, her choice" flag (G7);
   - the mother-house / daughter-house link with free lodging (G8);
   - the "no one asks her former name" rule as a narrator line and an opinion penalty for a sister who presses (G6).
6. **Give the *chōja* a duty to protect, with its dark twin.** The protection promise (IACS p. 215) is a duty she may fail. Ishii's gatekeeping mama (pp. 21, 29) sets the limit: no head ever grades a woman's looks or body. Fold this into e2 and the unseating petition.
7. **Old age:** a dead mother's name is spoken on Hyakudayū's night (the 遺影 transformed), the purse keeps the old, and old women teach (G10). This extends e14 and needs no new event.
8. **Words:** only *chōja*, *ane*, *haha* (prefer English "Mother"), *ie*, *onna*, *karabitsu* (once) and *nazuke* (optional). No modern word in loc (§5 rejected list).
9. **Before build:**
   - Check *ane* as a form of address and *imōto*'s Heian sense in a dictionary of classical Japanese (古語辞典).
   - Check memorial-tablet form for 1178.
   - Check the Dohi insider status from her own published self-description.
   - Ask the user whether G4's robe gift risks reading as costume. Recommend keeping it, framed as a gift that is hers.

## Dictionary check (2026-10-25, light pass)
Morris, *Dictionary of Selected Forms* (1965), Katsuki-Pestemer, *A Grammar of Classical Japanese* (2009), Vovin, *A Reference Grammar of Classical Japanese Prose* (2003): grammars and a forms dictionary, not lexicons. *haha*, *onna*, *ie* and *asobi* (play, music-making) occur in their classical example sentences; Vovin has *imouto* for a sister from a man's side. No entries for *ane* as address, *chōja*, *nazuke*, *karabitsu* or *uketsugi*: still to check in a classical lexicon (Shirane's *Essential Dictionary* is a scan, not OCR'd, low value). Treat those words as plausible, not verified.
