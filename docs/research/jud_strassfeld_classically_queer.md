## 1. The argument in brief

- **Thesis.** The rabbis use eunuchs and androgynes "instrumentally to map the boundaries of normative masculinity" (p. 97). Their long discussions of these bodies are "central to the rabbinic project of (re-) imagining a gendered legal system" (p. 250). Rabbinic masculinity comes out "fractured and multiple" (abstract, p. iv; p. 54).
- **The categories.** There are "primarily five kinds of eunuchs and androgynes": *seris chammah*, *seris adam*, *aylonit*, *androginos*, *tumtum* (pp. 2-3). Three biblical categories sit beside them: *p'tzua daka* (crushed testicles), *chrut shofchah* (severed member) and *m'roach ashech* (p. 3 n. 5; p. 58 n. 101).
  - *Seris adam*, "human eunuch": made a eunuch later in life, "like a castrate". *Seris chammah*: congenital, "more analogous to our concept of intersexuality" (p. 3).
  - *Aylonit*: "the female version of a *seris chammah*" (p. 3). Sarra Lev argues the rabbis invented her as the mirror of the male congenital eunuch (p. 76 n. 127; p. 250).
  - *Androginos*: dually sexed, a Greek loan word. *Tumtum*: genitals hidden by a flap of skin (p. 3).
  - Strassfeld maps them only "roughly" onto modern trans and intersex categories (p. 4). He warns against collapsing the two (p. 4 n. 9).
- **The missing third eunuch.** Matthew 19:12 lists three eunuchs: born so, made by men, and self-made "for the sake of the kingdom of heaven". Strassfeld: "The third type - eunuch for God - does not appear in rabbinic literature" (p. 3 n. 6).
- **Two tropes of the eunuch.** Mishnah Yevamot ch. 8 moves from the eunuch as *damaged* (the biblical *p'tzua daka*, tested through the priest's heave offering) to the eunuch as defined by *timing* (the *seris*, tested through levirate marriage) (pp. 57, 78-79). The Bavli sugya (bYevamot 79b-81a) plays the two against each other (pp. 99-138).
- **Not effeminised.** Unlike Greco-Roman sources, this chapter of Mishnah makes neither the damaged man nor the ethnic outsider effeminate (p. 86). The rabbis do not use the eunuch as an insult here (p. 83). Strassfeld's summary of the Tannaitic position is that "the *seris* is, for the most part, fully male" (p. 176).
- **Roman law reversed.** In Kuefler's reading of Roman law, the hermaphrodite is assigned a sex and the castrate is the slippery figure. The rabbis invert this: the eunuch is "primarily disambiguated/male", and the *androginos* cannot be fully disambiguated (pp. 82, 175-177).
- **Against redemption.** Strassfeld's project is "skeptical at the prospect of reclaiming 'authentic' intersex voices from the past" (p. 248). Even so, he takes "refuge" with Kukla in the idea "that our taxonomies have a history" (p. 258).

## 2. What matters for the design

### 2.1 Made eunuch vs born eunuch: the classification

- **The Mishnah (mYevamot 8:4).** R. Yehoshua had heard both that the eunuch does *chalitzah* and that he does not (p. 72). R. Akiva splits the eunuch in two:
  - The *seris adam* performs *chalitzah* "because he had a time when he was fit [Heb. *kasher*]".
  - The *seris chammah* does not, "because he never had a time when he was fit" (p. 72; discussed p. 73).
  - R. Eliezer reverses this. The congenital eunuch has a cure, so he is in; the man-made eunuch has none, so he is out (pp. 72-73).
  - Testimony supports Akiva: ben Megusat, a *seris adam* in Jerusalem, whose brother took his widow (p. 72).
- **Strassfeld's gloss.** The Mishnah's preference for Akiva rejects "reproductive futurity in favor of a hypothetical (fictional) past procreativity" (p. 75). Note that "fit/*kasher*" is the same word the Mishnah uses for the *chrut shofchah*'s penis (pp. 74-75).
- **The congenital eunuch defined.** R. Yochanan: "anyone who has not seen even one moment of fitness" (bYevamot 79b-80a, p. 111). The Palestinian Talmud makes it theological: God "stretches out his hand" and makes him a eunuch from within the womb (pYevamot 8:4, p. 138 n. 212).
- **"Eunuch of the sun" = hands of heaven.** The Bavli reads the Mishnah's "so too the *aylonit*" as an analogy. Like the *aylonit*, the congenital eunuch is made "[by] the hands of heaven" (*bidei shamayim*). The *seris adam* is made "in the hands of humans" (bYevamot 80b, p. 136).
  - Strassfeld: "'created by God' is the rabbinic formulation of what we would call 'natural'" (p. 137).
  - Being natural is exactly what puts the born eunuch and the *aylonit* outside levirate marriage (pp. 137-138).
- **Damage still haunts the born eunuch.** Abaye's infant test: the urine "does not form an arch". The cause is his mother baking at midday and drinking diluted beer while pregnant. Rav Yosef calls him "damaged (*lakuy*) in his mother's womb" (bYevamot 80a, pp. 112-113).
  - Strassfeld: at this point in the sugya, eunuchs are "made, not born" (p. 110). "Damaged men are produced through their mother's incautious behaviors" (p. 114).
- **A whole-body condition.** The Bavli refuses to treat the congenital eunuch like a cataract (a blemish on one limb): he is afflicted "in his entire body" (p. 116). "The whole body is singular and queer" (p. 116).
- **The signs** (baraita, bYevamot 80b = tYevamot 10:6-7; pp. 117-118). These are the most usable bodily details in the dissertation.
  - *Seris chammah*: twenty years old without two pubic hairs; no beard; smooth hair; soft skin; urine that does not foam or arch; watery semen; skin that does not steam in a winter bath. R. Shimon b. Elazar adds a voice by which the listener "cannot recognize whether it is a man or woman" (p. 118).
  - *Aylonit*: twenty years old without two pubic hairs; no breasts; painful intercourse; no mons "like women"; a "thick" voice not recognisable as woman or man (p. 118).
  - In the Erfurt manuscript the grammar makes the *aylonit herself*, not her voice, unrecognisable as man or woman (p. 118 n. 187).
  - Lev reads the passage as saying "this woman is to some extent not fully woman and that this man is not fully man" (Lev, quoted p. 119). Fonrobert disagrees: halakha "nowhere suggests" they are not "really" man or woman (p. 117 n. 184).

### 2.2 Can either change category?

- **The born eunuch.**
  - R. Eliezer holds there is a cure for the congenital eunuch (mYevamot 8:4, pp. 72-73). The Bavli places its origin in Alexandria (bYevamot 80a; p. 203 n.).
  - The diagnosis does not reverse, though. A man diagnosed at twenty who grows two hairs later "is considered a eunuch in all respects" (p. 117).
  - The Bavli does not fear a cure in between: "Since his beginning and ending were damaged we are not concerned" (bYevamot 80a, p. 114).
- **Retroactive status.** Suppose someone ate forbidden fat between twelve and eighteen, showed eunuch signs, and later grew two hairs (pp. 128-129).
  - Rav: "He becomes a eunuch retroactively", and so was liable as an adult.
  - Shmuel: "he was a minor at this time".
  - Strassfeld: "It is only in hindsight that we can distinguish between the vagaries of bodily timing and a deviation from the norm" (p. 131). Such a person is consigned "to living between two legal frameworks, until their status can be later discovered" (p. 132).
- **The made eunuch.**
  - He changes from fit to unfit at a known moment, and R. Eliezer gives him no cure (p. 73). But he changes from *fertile man* to *eunuch*, not from man to woman.
  - The Bavli (via R. Ami) applies Deut. 23:2 to him. Strassfeld: "damage to any male genitalia (priestly or not) renders him unfit to marry within the community" (p. 102).
  - Every man is exposed to this: "all men are always only an accident away from becoming a *seris adam*" (p. 242).
- **Every man as eunuch.** Rava: if sterility disqualified, no widow could ever be taken in levirate marriage, "since her husband is made a congenital eunuch an hour before his death" (bYevamot 79b, p. 105).
  - Strassfeld: "every man becomes queer in the hour before death" (p. 109).
  - The anonymous voice pushes back: dying is weakness, not sterility. This restores the eunuch as a distinct minority (p. 109).
- **The tumtum can be "torn open".**
  - The Tumtum of Biri was "placed on a [surgical] chair" and opened, and "fathered seven children" (bYevamot 83b, pp. 201-202).
  - R. Yehudah's imagined reply: go and check "from whence" his children come (p. 204). Rashi adds that the wife had illicit sex (p. 204).
  - In the Tosefta variant, a torn-open *tumtum* may be "found to be a congenital eunuch". This "rhetorically positions eunuchs as an option distinct from male" (p. 208).
  - The Bavli's emendation adds that ze may be found female. So "the *tumtum* can become a woman, or a man, or a congenital eunuch". Strassfeld calls the result "a portrait of one sex—masculinity" with grades of distance from it (p. 210).
- **Upshot for the design.**
  - No rabbinic text discussed here lets anyone pass from male to female.
  - What exists: a hidden sex revealed (*tumtum*), a status discovered late and applied backward (Rav), a congenital condition that might be cured (R. Eliezer), and a fertile man becoming a eunuch (*seris adam*).
  - The *Ha-Nehpakhot* claim has to rest on Dinah (bBerakhot 60a) and Kalonymus, which are aggadic and literary, not on this legal material.

- **Communal roles** (minyan, cantor, judge) are **not addressed** in the dissertation. Anything about them needs medieval codes (*unverified* here).
- **The aylonit's status.** Rav Huna: "the *aylonit* is a wife/woman and is not a wife/woman" (bKetubot 101a, p. 252). In context the point is technical: whether she must disclose her status before the marriage contract.

### 2.4 Where a trans-feminine sect could speak (in its own voice only)

None of these is a recognition of trans women. Each is a hook that the sect, not the narrator, can take up.

1. **"By the hands of heaven"** (bYevamot 80b, p. 136).
   - The rabbis themselves divide bodies made by heaven, the "natural" ones (p. 137), from bodies made by human hands.
   - A no-knife sect can claim this in its own voice: *we are turned by Heaven's hand, as Dinah was; what a knife makes is a seris adam, and him the verse shuts out*.
   - This gives the no-knife rule a reason taken from the tradition's own grammar, and it sets the sect against Adelphai Dorleyaiou on the knife.
2. **The aylonit's unrecognisable voice** (p. 118 and n. 187).
   - In one manuscript the rabbis' own sign list describes a woman not recognisable as woman or man.
   - The sect could take the *aylonit*, not the *androginos*, as its legal ancestor, since she is the one *female* category. Lev reads her as derived from the male eunuch (p. 250), which suits a sect of women turned from male.
   - Narrator caveat: the *aylonit* is a congenitally infertile woman. Using her as a trans precedent is appropriation, so it must stay in the sect's voice.
3. **Retroactive status** (Rav, pp. 128-132).
   - Doctrine in the sect's voice: *Rav taught that a status may be found late and count from the beginning; so we were daughters from the womb, and learned it at the Turning*.
   - This fits Dinah, turned "in her mother's womb", and Kalonymus's "turn me".
3a. **Rava's universal eunuch** (pp. 105-109): every man is made a eunuch in the hour before death. This can be used for deathbed flavour: the sect says all men end where we began.
4. **Isaiah 56:4-5.** Eunuchs who keep the Sabbaths are promised "a monument and a name better than sons or daughters".
   - Strassfeld notes the rabbis "could be framing the *seris* and the question of procreation biblically, but choose not to". They treat the verse only in aggadah (bShabbat 118b, bSanhedrin 93b) (p. 92 n. 156).
   - The sect can claim the verse the sages left aside.
   - **Do not** use the Hebrew phrase *yad va-shem* as a name or institution. It is now the name of the Holocaust memorial.
5. **"A created being of its own".**
   - R. Yose: the *androginos* is *beriyah le-atzmo* (Strassfeld's Tosefta text, בריה לעצמו), "and the sages could not decide about hir whether ze is a man or a woman" (tBikurim 2, p. 162). The same formula is used of the *koi*, a half-wild animal (p. 147).
   - Strassfeld reads it as **exclusion**. It "would appear to dismiss the *androginos* completely from the rabbinic legal system" (p. 162). It "prioritizes the gender duality of the legal system" (p. 167).
   - Fonrobert's line on the list as a whole: "Sex is variable but gender is not" (Fonrobert, quoted p. 163).
   - So the sect *may* quote it as a boast. The narrator must know it is the clause that puts such a being outside the law. The *androginos* is also an intersex category, so the sect claiming it is a second appropriation.

### 2.5 The "six genders" reading and Kukla

- **Strassfeld never uses "six genders"** (or seven or eight). His own count is "primarily five kinds of eunuchs and androgynes", plus three biblical categories of damaged men (pp. 2-3, 58). They are legal classes of bodies, mostly of male bodies, not genders.
- **Activists.** Trans and intersex Jewish activists "have adopted these categories as trans/intersex Jewish history" (p. 97 n. 164, citing Plaskow and the Kukla–Zellman pieces on TransTorah.org). Whether the rabbis knew actual intersex or trans Jews "is hotly debated" by activists (p. 39 n. 84, citing Dzmura).
- **Kukla.** Strassfeld quotes Kukla's 2006 HUC thesis, "A Created Being of Its Own" (p. 256). Then he:
  - sets aside "the problematic language of 'authentic' connections" (p. 257);
  - notes that the legal texts are "rhetorically freighted, outright violent, or subtly pejorative" (p. 257);
  - calls the reappropriation "deliberate, creative" (p. 257), and calls the activists' use of the texts "creative acts negotiating within a limited realm of options" (p. 258).
- **Narrator rules from this.**
  - Never say the Talmud "recognised" multiple genders, a third sex, or trans people.
  - Say instead that the rabbis classified unusual bodies to fix the line between male and female.
  - Strassfeld's own line on R. Yose is that, read that way, the *androginos* would figure "as a third sex, and that the law is situated to deny the possibility of a third sex" (p. 176).
  - The sect may do what Kukla did, in its own voice. That is a creative act, not a recovered history.
- **The "monster" frame.** Roman law exposed hermaphrodite infants as portents (Twelve Tables via Cicero) and later made them "entertainments" (Pliny) (pp. 168-170). The rabbis include them in the law instead (pp. 156, 161-162). The narrator may note this contrast. It must not become a "tolerant Judaism vs cruel Rome" story: the inclusion serves the binary (p. 166).

## 4. Design implications

### 4.1 Rabbinic court reaction to Dorleyite daughters of the knife

- **Classification.**
  - A Jewish court sees a *seris adam*: a man made a eunuch by human hand. Not a woman, not a third thing, and not a "eunuch for heaven" (p. 3 n. 6).
  - The Dorleyite claim that she "rises a daughter" has no legal hearing. The Sisters' rejection of "a third thing" happens to match the rabbis, who also refuse a third sex (p. 176), but for the opposite reason.
- **Tone.**
  - The Mishnah does not mock or effeminise eunuchs (pp. 83, 86). Jewish characters' reactions should be **dry and legal, not contemptuous**. They should differ from Byzantine and Latin invective.
  - Loc idea (rabbi character): *"In our law she is a seris adam, made by a man's hand. What Heaven has not made, a knife cannot."*
- **Jurisdiction.**
  - Gentile Dorleyites are outside halakha. The reaction is classificatory distance: a small opinion malus, never persecution.
  - Real consequences only fall on a **Jewish** character who joins Adelphai Dorleyaiou or is cut. Such a character:
    - may not marry a born Israelite (Deut. 23:2 as read in the Bavli, p. 102), but may marry a convert or freedwoman *as a man* (mYevamot 8:2, p. 64);
    - if a *kohen*, keeps the heave-offering privilege, though his wife may not eat it if they have had sex since the cutting (pp. 59-63);
    - for levirate, follows Akiva: performs *chalitzah* (p. 72).
  - A trait or modifier "Seris Adam (in Jewish law)" could carry these marriage rules as an AI and marriage-scope restriction.
- **The knifer.** Liability for castrating comes from Maimonides (already in the proposal), not Strassfeld. Keep it there.

### 4.2 Rabbinic reaction to *Ha-Nehpakhot*

- **First response: she is a man.** An uncut Turned woman is legally a whole (*shalem*) man (cf. Rashi's "whole" brothers, p. 211). Purim dress aside, she is bound to men's commandments and counts as a man.
- **Counter-arguments** a rabbi character can use against the sect (event options or opinion text):
  - "One does not cite miracles" (Berakhot 60a, already in the proposal).
  - "What Heaven turns, it turns in the womb, within forty days; a grown man is not a fetus."
  - "R. Yose said of the *androginos* that the sages *could not decide*. He did not say the sages should" (p. 162).
- **The Biri precedent** (pp. 202-206).
  - If a Turned woman had fathered children before her Turning, a hostile court may suspect her wife rather than accept the turning: "go and check whose children they are".
  - Use sparingly, as a hostile rabbi's line. The narrator stays at a distance: the move blames a woman (Strassfeld p. 206 calls it "the specter of female deception").
- **Marriage and priests.**
  - If the sect claims the *aylonit* as ancestor, a *kohen* character who wants to marry a Turned woman meets R. Yehudah's ruling that the *aylonit* is a *zonah* to priests (p. 252). The Sages' contrary view gives a lenient rabbi a way out.
  - Event: "A Kohen and a Turned Daughter", where the court splits between R. Yehudah's and the Sages' readings. *Design suggestion, unverified as medieval practice.*

### 4.3 Doctrine and loc for *Ha-Nehpakhot* (faith voice only)

- **Tenet "By the Hand of Heaven".** No knife, no potion. The sect's reason is rabbinic: what human hands make is a *seris adam* (p. 136).
  - Faith loc: *"The sages divided the made from the born: one by the hands of men, one by the hands of Heaven. We were turned by Heaven's hand."*
- **Tenet or decision "Found From the Beginning"** (Rav's *le-mafrea*, retroactively, pp. 128-131): the Turning reveals a status that counts from birth.
  - Faith loc: *"Rav said a sign found late counts from the beginning. So she was a daughter from her mother's womb."*
- **Scripture of the sect: Isaiah 56:4-5**, the eunuchs promised a name better than sons or daughters. The sages left it to aggadah (p. 92 n. 156), and the sect claims it.
  - Fits a "no heirs, a name instead" mechanic: a legacy or renown modifier for childless members. Do not use *yad va-shem* as a label.
- **Deathbed flavour (Rava).** *"Rava said every husband becomes a eunuch in the hour before his death. We only came early."* (pp. 105-109).
- **Narrator text** (for the faith description and the encyclopedia):
  - The rabbis sorted unusual bodies (eunuchs born or made, the *aylonit*, the *androginos*, the *tumtum*) to fix the edges of manhood.
  - They had no category for a man who becomes a woman.
  - The *Nehpakhot* read these texts against their grain, as some modern Jewish trans writers have.
  - Do not say "six genders". Do not say "the Talmud recognised".

### 4.4 Wider uses

- **Byzantine contrast.** The Byzantine court ranked its eunuchs as a third order, and the Sisters reject that. The rabbis put eunuchs back into the male category. A Jewish courtier at Constantinople would find both the court's third order and the Sisters' "daughter" alien. *Design suggestion.*
- **Bodily signs as event text.** The baraita's list (beardless, soft skin, a voice "not recognised as man or woman", p. 118) is period-true description that does not judge. Jewish characters can use it neutrally in examination events. Do not use the infant urine test or the mother-blaming cause (pp. 112-114) as flavour: it blames the mother.
- **Masculinity as fragile.** Strassfeld: "Guarding masculinity requires vigilance" (p. 243). This works as the background mood of Jewish reaction events: the threat is to the male household, not to the trans woman as such.
