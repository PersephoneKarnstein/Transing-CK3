## Chengtian and the rite (pp. 202–203)
- The chieftain's, later the emperor's, rebirth was supervised by an old man and an old woman.
- Some empresses dowager underwent the "magical rebirth normally reserved for the emperor alone" (p. 202): the mothers of Xingzong and Daozong, while their sons were young.
- Chengtian, "the mother and tutor of the boy emperor" Shengzong, obtained rebirth in 984 and in 986, twice at short intervals (p. 202).
- The authors read it as power put into ritual terms, and link it to her leading the army against the Song in 986 (pp. 202–203).

## The rite (*Liaoshi* 53, 14b–15a; trans. pp. 273–274, document VII 2 (86))
- Every twelve years, in the last winter month before the year of the emperor's birth-animal.
- North of the Forbidden Gate: a rebirth building, a building for the emperor's mother, and a chariot with the late emperor's tablets.
- Three forked timbers set upside down; inside, a boy and an old midwife; outside, a married woman with wine and an old man with a quiver.
- The emperor undresses and passes under the timbers three times, followed by the boy; the midwife recites words and strokes his body.
- The old man strikes the quiver and cries "A boy is born" (p. 273); the head shaman covers the emperor's head; courtiers congratulate him.
- Swaddling clothes and coloured ribbons are offered; seven old men each propose a name, and the emperor chooses one.
- The rebirth also opens the Firewood Investiture (document 87, p. 274).

## Use in the mod
- Used: the dates, the building beside the mother's, the timbers, the midwife, the swaddling clothes, the new name.
- Not used: the cry "A boy is born". Neither source says what was cried for Chengtian, and McMahon's design note warns against giving her rite a gender meaning. The narrator does not claim her.
