## 1. The passage

### 1.1 Where it is

- **Location.** *Guixin zazhi*, *houji*, entry **禁男娼** "Banning male prostitutes", the 71st of the part's 73 entries, between 張約齋傭者 and 趙春穀斬蛇.
- **It is not headed 人妖.** The *qianji* has a separate entry headed **人妖** ("human prodigy"). It tells of a maid in the household of a Zhao staff officer at Yangzhou who was found to have "two forms" and was put to death, then lists precedents: the Buddhist "five kinds of not-male" (五種不男, 生、劇、妒、變、半), the *Jin shu* Five Phases treatise, and Chu Cheng's *Yishu*. Zhou Mi closes it calling such bodies "the ungoverned qi of heaven and earth". So the word 人妖 is Zhou Mi's, but for intersex bodies, in a different part of the book. Whether a later anthology reprints 禁男娼 under the heading 人妖 is *unverified*; I found none.
- **Date.** The *houji* refers to the Deyou years (1275–76) in retrospect and calls Lizong by his tomb name, so it was written after the fall of Lin'an. The *xuji* fire entry says the fire of 1231 was "exactly one sexagenary cycle ago", which dates that entry to 1291. Hinsch gives the book as "pub. ca. 1298" (n. 28). The quarter is undated in the text.

### 1.2 What it says (*houji*, 禁男娼)

1. **Framing.** It opens with the classical favourites (Lord Longyang, Mizi Xia, the Han favourites), who "applied powder and rouge to please", and the historians' line that ruinous softness "is not only a woman's virtue". This is the *nanse* frame: the entry is about men who sell themselves, as its heading says.
2. **Kaifeng and the law.** In Kaifeng's heyday, Zhou Mi says, men also lived this way; in the Zhenghe era (1111–18) a law first allowed their denunciation and arrest: "a man acting as a prostitute" (男子為娼) got "a hundred strokes", and the informer fifty strings of cash.
3. **Lin'an.** 吳俗此風尤盛，新門外乃其巢穴. "In Wu custom this is especially strong; outside the New Gate is their nest." 皆傅脂粉，盛裝飾，善針指，呼謂亦如婦人，以之求食. "All of them powder and rouge, dress in full finery, are skilled at needlework, and are addressed as women; by this they make their living."
4. **The head.** 其為首者號師巫行頭。凡官府有不男之訟，則呼使驗之. "Their leader is titled *shiwu hangtou*. Whenever the courts have a 'not-a-man' suit, they summon the leader to examine it."
5. **Verdict.** "Nothing ruins customs more than this", yet "I have never seen anyone invoke the old article to ban it". Zhou Mi wonders whether this is because the matter is too ugly to speak of.

### 1.3 The earlier witness: Zhu Yu

Zhou Mi's Kaifeng sentence is close to a near-contemporary source. Zhu Yu, *Pingzhou ketan* 3, says "now, in the capital and the prefectural towns", idle men make their living this way. It "was never named and banned before; in the Zhenghe era a law was first made" with the same penalty and reward (100 strokes; 50 strings to the informer).
- This puts the practice in **both the capital and the provincial towns under Huizong**, before the court fled south, and dates the law from a writer of Huizong's reign.
- In the line just before it, Zhu Yu says the courtesans of his day were trained to sing and dance and "their chief is called the *hangshou* 行首". Zhou Mi's *hangtou* matches the title the entertainers' trade gave its head.
- Hinsch reads Zhu Yu's text as treating Mizi Xia and all passive partners as prostitutes, and calls Zhu Yu's tone contempt (pp. 93–94; snippets only).

### 1.4 How the passage reads as evidence

- **What it attests:** a named place in the capital (outside the New Gate); a group, not individuals; femininity as dress, cosmetics, needlework and being addressed as women; a living made from all of these; an organised head with a title and a duty to the courts; and a law that existed but was not enforced in Zhou Mi's day.
- **What it does not attest:** any self-description, any name, any ritual, or the date of the Lin'an quarter. The voice is wholly hostile.
- **1178.** The quarter is attested only for Zhou Mi's lifetime (he was born in 1232 and lived in Lin'an), and the practice for Kaifeng and the towns c. 1119. Placing it outside the New Gate in 1178 is an inference across about a century. It is a reasonable one, since the court and much of Kaifeng's population moved to Lin'an after 1127.

## 2. Scholarship on the passage

- **Hinsch 1990, pp. 92–95** (snippets and index only). It sits in his Song chapter, after Tao Gu's *Qingyi lu* (pp. 92–93) and Zhu Yu (pp. 93–94). He cites Zhou Mi through Wang Shunu's *History of Chinese Prostitution* (n. 28). The one snippet seen describes the entry as recording "the plight of a flamboyantly effeminate prostitute threatened with prosecution". That reads the passage as being about an individual and about prosecution; the Chinese is about a group, and says the law was not used. The frame is homosexuality and male prostitution, not gender. *Hinsch's full paragraph is unread.*
- **Sommer 2000, p. 119.** This is the only scholarly reading found that turns on gender. Song sources, he writes, punished "young males who act as prostitutes". "*Chang* implied female gender; male prostitutes were referred to as *nan chang*—literally, 'male female-prostitutes.'" He concludes that Song lawmakers "associated the penetrated role with cross-dressing" and sought "to prevent males from being degraded into females". He cites Zhou Mi and Zhu Yu.
  - **A misreading to avoid copying.** Sommer says another Song text "mentions the prosecution of cross-dressing male prostitutes for the offense of *bu nan*". In Zhou Mi the "not-a-man" suits are cases the group's head is *summoned to examine*, not charges against the group (§4).
  - Zhu Yu's 無賴男子 means "idle" or "good-for-nothing" men; "young" is Sommer's gloss.
- **Sommer 2024** (repo note): *bu nan* 不男 / *fei nan* 非男 are the medical and legal "deformed males who cannot become fathers" (p. 181, from Li Shizhen). The cross-dressing *dan* "learned and performed" femininity (pp. 32–33).
- **Stevenson and Wu 2013, item 5.2.** A translation of this passage in a sourcebook. It renders the law sentence as "In the middle years of the Zhenghe reign period (1111–18) the first law was prom[ulgated]". *Not read in full.*
- **Yao 2011, pp. 256–257.** An essay by an organiser with a Hong Kong network of male and transgender sex workers, published in an academic volume. It quotes Tao Gu and Zhou Mi as the first evidence of "male/transgender sex workers", and glosses 呼謂亦如婦人 as their calling each other by women's names. It is the one work found that reads the quarter through gender identity. It reads the title as two titles, 師巫 and 行頭, and pairs them with the Ming 牽頭. It is advocacy, not philology: cite it for the reading, not for facts.
- **Song 男娼 generally.** Tao Gu's *Qingyi lu* (10th century), entry 蜂窠巷陌 (quoted in Yao 2011, p. 256): in the capital, "men sell their whole bodies" and "make honeycomb lanes". It shows commercial male prostitution in Kaifeng before 1000, with no mention of women's dress. Hinsch, Kang (*Obsession*, ch. 1) and Gernet were not read on this.

**Verdict.** No study treats the New Gate passage at length. The secondary literature reads it as male prostitution (Hinsch) or as law policing male penetrability and gender inversion (Sommer). The design can lean on Sommer: his reading puts *gender*, and not only sex acts, at the centre of the Song concern.

The same Sun Zigui is the troupe head (戲頭) in three other troupes. *Zhuangdan* is a role taken within a troupe, not a performer's identity. *Mengliang lu* 20 also has girls playing the male *mo* role (女童裝末).
3. **The Buddhist "good women" day.** The Taiping Xingguo Chuanfa monastery's Pure Land society met men on the 17th and women on the 18th of each month. *Ducheng jisheng* (1235) describes it as current, and *Mengliang lu* 19 (1274) says it was "long abandoned". It is closer to 1178 than the pass implied.

## 4. What 師巫行頭 means

**The parts.**
- *Hang* 行 is the guild of a trade. *Ducheng jisheng* (諸行) says trades are called *hang* "because of official requisition", and "even doctors and diviners have duties". *Mengliang lu* 13 (團行) repeats this: every trade, "even medicine, divination and labour", has assigned service, which the state pays for.
- *Hangtou* 行頭 is the head of a *hang*; Zhu Yu's courtesans had a *hangshou*.
- *Shiwu* 師巫 in Song usage means ritual specialists who heal and exorcise through spirits (spirit mediums). The word is standard in later law: the Ming code's article against them is titled 禁止師巫邪術.
- So 師巫行頭 most naturally means **"head of the spirit-mediums' guild"**: the people outside the New Gate were registered, or known, as a guild of mediums.

**Mockery or a real role?** Both readings are possible, and the evidence leans toward a real one.
- **For a real role:** guild registration was how the city knew any trade. The guild's duty to the courts (it gives evidence in suits) is the kind of *chaishi* 差使 the guild sections describe. A Song medium often worked from home, in finery, with needle trades on the side, and *Mengliang lu* lists 針線 needlework as hired domestic service.
- **For mockery:** Zhou Mi is sardonic throughout, and *shiwu* was already a word of reproach.
- **Not settled.** Whether the people outside the New Gate performed rites at all is *unverified*. The design should keep the title and leave the rites open.

**What "not-a-man suits" were.** In Zhou Mi's own vocabulary, 不男 is the Buddhist list of five kinds of men who cannot act as men (*qianji* 人妖), and in medicine and law it is the man who cannot father (Sommer 2024, p. 181). A 不男之訟 is therefore most likely a suit over a husband's impotence: a wife seeking to leave a marriage, or a family contesting one. The guild head was the court's examiner of men's bodies. That is an intimate, coercive role; keep it offstage.

**A second hostile law.**
- **The 1023 edict.** As prefect of Hongzhou, "where custom honours spirits and many mediums mislead the people", Xia Song found "more than a thousand households" of them, sent them back to farming, and tore down their *yinsi*. An edict then banned them "everywhere south of the Yangzi and in Zhe" (*Song shi* 283). Hinrichs and Barnes give "more than 1,900 shamans", from his memorial, and date the peak of the campaign to 1023 (*snippets only*). Lin'an lies in Zhe, so the ban covered the city.
- **Southern Song practice.** Judges in the *Qingming ji* tore down *yinsi* for timber, beat a "divine elder" and exiled a usurping officiant (McKnight and Liu 1999, pp. 479–483; repo note). Those cases date from the 1220s–50s.
- **For the design.** The New Gate guild is exposed twice: as men "acting as prostitutes" under the Zhenghe article, and as *shiwu* under the anti-medium edicts. Neither law was being enforced in Zhou Mi's day, as far as he knew.
- **A precedent for mixed roles.** In 1022 Liu Demiao, who "came and went in Ding Wei's house as a *wushi*", was told by him to speak in the name of Lord Lao rather than as a medium (*Song shi* 283). The line between medium and Daoist was porous, and crossing it was political.

## 5. Lin'an in 1178

**The city.** Lin'an was the "temporary capital" (*xingzai*) from 1138. In 1178 (Chunxi 5) Xiaozong reigned, and the retired Gaozong (d. 1187) lived in the Deshou Palace. Peace with the Jin had held since 1165.

**The New Gate (新門).**
- It is the **Xinkai Gate 新開門**, the last of the seven gates on the south-east wall (*Mengliang lu* 7). It is distinct from the Chongxin Gate (called the Jianqiao Gate) on the east wall, and *Wulin jiushi* 6 lists their entertainment wards separately.
- **What stood outside it:**
  - the **New Gate *wazi*** 新門瓦子, "formerly called the Sitong Hall" (*Mengliang lu* 19);
  - the **Jinglong Daoist abbey** 景隆觀, "outside the New Gate at the Water Office" (*Mengliang lu* 15);
  - the imperial **Fujing Garden** 富景園, "outside the New Gate in the east of the city", which Xiaozong visited escorting Empress Xiansheng (*Mengliang lu* 19; see also 7).
- **Its later history.** Zhou Mi's fire of 1231 burned east as far as the New Gate (*xuji* 上).
- So the "nest" sat between an entertainment ward, a Daoist abbey and an imperial garden.

**Wazi.** *Mengliang lu* 19 says Lin'an's wazi were founded in the Shaoxing years (1131–62) by the commander Yang Cunzhong, for soldiers from the north-west, and counts seventeen. *Ducheng jisheng* glosses *wa* as "easy to gather, easy to scatter", and calls the wazi a place where young men are ruined. When the New Gate *wazi* itself was founded is *unverified*.

**Guilds.** See §4. *Ducheng jisheng* adds that teahouses served as meeting places for the trades' elders (*hanglao*) and for hired performers.

**Societies (社/會).**
- **Daoist.** A Lingbao society 靈寶會 of rich households recited Zhengyi scriptures monthly (*Mengliang lu* 19).
- **Women.** A Gengshen society 庚申會 of officials' and palace-office wives, who recited the *Yuanjue jing* in their jewels and were nicknamed the "treasure contest". There was also the Pure Land society's women's day (§3).
- **Others.** Dozens of guild, sport and poetry *she* (*Ducheng jisheng*, *Mengliang lu* 19).
- **Private Daoist halls.** *Ducheng jisheng* (三教外地) notes private Daoist halls in great houses (府第道堂), and Daoist cloisters run by "people who have left lay life" (捨俗道人). This is an attested form for a small, household-sponsored House.

**Daoist convents.** *Mengliang lu* 15 lists "**nine** women's (*nüguan*) palaces and abbeys in the city and its suburbs", naming eight: 福田、新興、明貞、神仙、承天、西靖、靈耀、長清. It also says Daoist houses were "not one in ten" of the Buddhist ones in Hangzhou. They were a small minority, but a real one, in the city itself.

**Performers who played women.**
- *Wulin jiushi* 4: the 裝旦 of the Qiandao–Chunxi troupes (1165–89; §3).
- *Wulin jiushi* 6 marks women performers 女流 and lists a *zaban* player 自來俏 with the note "(旦)". Those lists are of Zhou Mi's own day.
- *Ducheng jisheng* gives the zaju roles (末泥、引戲、副淨、副末、裝孤) without a 旦.
- **Neither *Ducheng jisheng* nor *Mengliang lu* mentions the New Gate quarter or a *shiwu hangtou*** in the sections read. They are silent witnesses: the quarter is Zhou Mi's alone.

**A dated hostile voice of 1178.** Yuan Cai wrote his *Precepts* in 1178 (Ebrey 1984). In §3.50 he says that Buddhist nuns, Daoist women (道婆), matchmakers, brokers, "and women who go about under the name of selling, or of needle and moxa", must not be let into the house; "it is all this sort" who steal and lead women astray. The needle trade's way into the inner quarters is exactly what a moralist of the start year shuts. Yet Yuan Cai also counts medium-healers (巫醫) among livelihoods that "do not shame one's ancestors" (juan 中, 子弟當習儒業).

**Vanilla's faith map.** The county's rite is `daoxue` (Neo-Confucian, under `confucian_faith`) from 1150. The emperor's personal rite is `dhyana` (Chan, under `mahayana_faith`).

## 6. Yutai Dao in Lin'an

- **The 1119 renaming, verified.** *Song shi* 22, Xuanhe 1, first month, day *yimao*: monks become *deshi*, temples become *gong*, and "*nüguan* were renamed *nüdao*, nuns *nüde*" (改女冠為女道，尼為女德). The reversal, also verified: Xuanhe 2, ninth month, day *yisi*, "monks restored" (復德士為僧). The text names only the monks; whether 女道 reverted at the same time is *not stated there*.
- **Cao Wenyi.**
  - Despeux and Kohn give her as **fl. 1119–1125**: summoned by Huizong and titled; author of the only woman's commentary among Peng Helin's twenty (pp. 133–134; repo note).
  - Chinese reference works agree on the Xuanhe dating and the title 文逸真人, and say she left in the Jingkang disorder (1126–27), after which nothing is known. They make her a granddaughter of Cao Bin (d. 999), which is chronologically doubtful.
  - The **1039–1115** dates on English Wikipedia contradict her Xuanhe summons and have no source I could find. **Use fl. 1119–1125.** Her *Lingyuan dadao ge* (the *Song of the Great Way of the Numinous Source*) is the one Song woman's alchemy text; Valussi's warnings about later *nüdan* apply to everything else (repo note).
- **How the Jade Embryo gate lives here.**
  - The texts it needs, the *Wuzhen pian* (c. 1075) and Zhong-Lü, are in circulation.
  - The city has nine women's Daoist houses and the Lingbao society.
  - The court that renamed the *nüguan* and ordained the bearded woman of 1124 (*Song shi* 62) came here in 1127.
  - The House outside the New Gate does not need a convent's licence: it lives as a private Daoist hall under a patron (an attested form, *Ducheng jisheng*), registered to the city as a guild.
- **A local foremother.** Du Guangting sets Wang Fengxian's death on Mount Qianqing in Yuhang, in 886 (Cahill 2006, pp. 176–185; Jia 2018, p. 202; repo notes). Yuhang is a barony of vanilla `c_hangzhou`. Called "Guanyin", she said "If people consider me an ox, then I will act like an ox" (Cahill, p. 182). The Way can claim her, in its own voice, as the Lin'an house's foremother on names: the people outside the gate were "addressed as women", and the Way answers that the address is the thing.

   - She runs the House as a private Daoist hall under a patron's roof (the attested 道院 form).
   - Learning 3, patient, temperate. She carries the *Wuzhen pian* and Cao Wenyi's *Song*.
2. **Seventh Sister Lu 陸七姐**, about 42, a daughter of the embryo, the guild's *hangtou*.
   - She makes a living from the needle and from household rites, and the prefecture summons her as examiner in "not-a-man" suits. That gives her a hook on a prefectural clerk.
   - Intrigue- and stewardship-leaning. In the faith's voice: "The court asks her who is not a man. No court has ever asked her who is a woman."
3. **Lady Fan 范氏**, about 47, a cis woman of the faith (a sister).
   - The widow of a minor official and heir of an extinct household (*hujue*): the House's Protectress and the owner of the house it lives under.
   - Her property and her lawsuits carry Temple Societies at Lin'an, where that law actually ran (McKnight and Liu 1999, pp. 244, 297; repo note).
4. **Shuangshuang 雙雙**, 17, Marked; the game codes her as male, and she is registered under the pet name A-De 阿得 (a form attested among the city's performers).
   - An apprentice embroiderer the House took in from a failed household. She has not yet declared, and is the gate's first test.
5. **Xiaoqiao 小喬** (stage name), about 24, Marked and declared: she has taken a daughter's name but not yet begun the gestation.
   - She plays the *zhuangdan* in a troupe at the New Gate *wazi*, which is sometimes hired for palace banquets. This is the court-adjacent route. Do not name, imply or portray Sun Zigui.
6. *(Optional NPC, not a starter)* **Clerk Pan 潘** of the Lin'an prefecture, who keeps the guild rolls; the informer's door. He is a person, not a faction.

### 7.4 How one becomes a daughter here

The gate is *Gestate the Jade Embryo*, unchanged, with three Lin'an variants:
- **The guarantor is the hangtou.** In the first bathing (month 3, `tm_embryo.0010`), the censor becomes **a neighbour who threatens to denounce her under the Zhenghe article** for the fifty strings. New option: *Let the hangtou answer*. It spends Seventh Sister Lu's hook on the clerk, costs no gold, and gives her stress.
- **The second bathing (month 6)**: the letter from home stays. Add a variant in which a patron's household, citing Yuan Cai's rule, refuses her at its gate: "no woman who goes about with a needle comes in here". Options: send the sister with Lady Fan's card; send someone else; let it go (prestige or income cost).
- **A Name Before Witnesses** (`tm_embryo.0002`), with the House's needle: the new daughter sews her own robe first. The name is entered as *nüdao*. In the faith's voice: "The throne wrote 女道 once; we write it again."

### 7.5 Pressures and hostile voices (all attributed)

- **The Zhenghe article** (Zhu Yu, *Pingzhou ketan* 3; Zhou Mi, 禁男娼): 100 strokes, and a 50-string informer's reward. It is dormant, but anyone can wake it. A denunciation threat, with its weight falling over time (Zhou Mi: "no one invokes the old article").
- **The anti-medium edicts** (*Song shi* 283, 1023; *Qingming ji* judgments of the 1220s–50s): a prefect reviving the ban can strike the guild off the rolls as *shiwu*.
- **Zhou Mi's verdict**: "nothing ruins customs more". Usable as loc, attributed, from 1260 at the earliest (he was born in 1232).
- **Yuan Cai, 1178**: nuns, Daoist women and needlewomen shut out of the inner quarters.
- **Sommer's law of penetrability**, as a narrator's frame: the state's interest is in keeping males from "being degraded into females". The pressure falls on femininity itself.
- **Buddhist dominance** (*Mengliang lu* 15: Daoist houses "not one in ten"). The existing −5 opinion "A Man Become a Woman" applies.
- **Neo-Confucian *daoxue*** is the county rite in vanilla. Zhu Xi's school rises after 1200.
- **War**: the Kaixi war (1206–08), the Mongol conquest, and the surrender of Lin'an in 1276. The Yuan arc of Magistrate's Inventory applies from 1264.

### 7.6 Event ideas

1. **Summoned to the Prefecture.** A wife seeks to leave a husband she says is "not a man", and the court calls the *hangtou*. Everything happens offstage: the summons, a closed door, a verdict. The choices are whose side the verdict serves: the wife's freedom, the husband's face, or the clerk's fee. Each sets a hook or an opinion.
2. **Fifty Strings.** A rival guild or a neighbour denounces a sister under the Zhenghe article. The options: Lady Fan's lawsuit, a bribe, the *hangtou*'s standing, or sending the sister to Dunhuang. It is rarer after 1250.
3. **The Prefect Who Read Xia Song.** A new prefect revives the ban on mediums. Re-register the guild as needlewomen (losing the court summons and its hook), as a Daoist society on the Lingbao model (Lady Fan pays), or go quiet.
4. **The Emperor Goes to the Fujing Garden.** A procession passes outside the New Gate. The House is asked to sew banners: income, and a moment of being seen.
5. **Lantern Night at the New Gate Wazi.** Xiaoqiao plays the *zhuangdan*. A palace steward hires the troupe: a route into the palace-offices offer, with its usual cost to the Houses.
6. **Wang Fengxian's Mountain.** A pilgrimage to Mount Qianqing in Yuhang, with the ox-and-horse debate on names already proposed in `cn_cahill_divine_traces.md`.
7. **The Gengshen Night.** Officials' wives hold their "treasure contest". Lady Fan brings a sister, and they are seen or snubbed.
8. **The Fire of 1231** (dated: the third month of 1231; Zhou Mi, *xuji* 上). The fire burns to the New Gate. The House's store is lost or saved, and the society's dues fall due.
9. **The Surrender of Lin'an** (1276). The court leaves, and the House stays.

### 7.7 What carries over and what is new

**Carries over unchanged:**
- *Gestate the Jade Embryo* and its events. Ghost Fetus, whose 1130–1280 band suits this start (Xu Shuwei; Cheng 2021).
- Temple Societies: dues, the righteous store and Petition for a Name-Panel.
- Magistrate's Inventory. Take as Daughter, Declare Before Witnesses, Sisters' Houses and The Sisters Choose.
- The call (China is Yutai's region), arrivals, A Prodigy in the Prefecture, and the palace-offices offer (Xiaozong's court is celestial).
- The Buddhist opinion.

**New script:**
- a landless title and its loc;
- a 1178 branch in the start script: no county conversion, the House seeded at `c_hangzhou`, and Dunhuang kept;
- fixed-ID history characters for the five starters, in `history/characters/tm_characters.txt` (vanilla supports `employer =` in dated blocks);
- a 1178 bookmark entry;
- three variant options in `tm_embryo.0010`, the new month-6 variant and the needle step of `tm_embryo.0002`;
- events 1–9 above, with the Zhenghe threat as a decaying weight;
- after Silk & Silver, an optional company conversion.

**Growth**: slow, as for the other small faiths. The Lin'an House should not convert the city; it grows by the call and by arrivals.

## 8. Attested, reconstructed, invented

**Attested in the sources read:**
- People outside Lin'an's New Gate whom Zhou Mi counts as men, in cosmetics and finery, skilled at needlework, "addressed as women", living by this, with a head titled 師巫行頭 whom the courts summoned in "not-a-man" suits (*Guixin zazhi*, *houji*, 禁男娼; late 13th century).
- The Zhenghe law of 100 strokes and a 50-string reward (Zhu Yu c. 1119; Zhou Mi).
- The New Gate's location; its *wazi*, Daoist abbey and imperial garden. The guild system; the Lingbao and Gengshen societies; nine *nüguan* convents (*Mengliang lu*; *Ducheng jisheng*).
- The *zhuangdan* in the 1165–89 troupes (*Wulin jiushi* 4).
- The 1119 renaming and the 1120 restoration of monks (*Song shi* 22). The 1023 ban on mediums south of the Yangzi and in Zhe (*Song shi* 283).
- Yuan Cai's rule against needlewomen and Daoist women, 1178 (Ebrey 1984, §3.50). Cao Wenyi fl. 1119–1125. Wang Fengxian's death on Mount Qianqing.

**Reconstructed (inference, flagged):**
- That the quarter existed in 1178.
- That 師巫行頭 names a real mediums' guild.
- That "not-a-man suits" were impotence suits.
- That the House could live as a private Daoist hall.

**Invented by the mod:**
- The House, its landless title and all its characters.
- Any link between the New Gate people and Yutai Dao, and any claim that they were women. The faith reads them as its sisters **in its own voice only**. The narrator says what Zhou Mi says, and attributes it.
- The Lin'an variants of the gate.

## 9. Content to keep out

- **No examination scenes.** The *hangtou*'s duty to the courts stays a summons and a verdict.
- **No execution event** from the *qianji* 人妖 story (a two-formed maid put to death at Yangzhou).
- **Sex work.** Do not reduce the House to it, do not erase Zhou Mi's frame, and keep it offstage. The project's stance applies (`web_third_wave_transfeminism.md` §4.11): sex work is licit, and that it is necessary is the injury. Zhou Mi's "nest" is his word, not ours.
- **No "third gender" framing** of the New Gate people, and no claim that Sun Zigui, Wang Fengxian, Cao Wenyi or anyone else named was trans.
- **No Orientalist "pleasure city" tone.** Lin'an's riches are described by its own Song writers, who were already worried about decadence (*Ducheng jisheng*, the Siku editors).

## 10. Open questions

1. **Hinsch in full** (pp. 92–95): his exact wording, and whether he dates the quarter. Also Stevenson and Wu 5.2 (their notes may treat 師巫).
2. **The Zhenghe article itself**: is it in the *Song huiyao* (*xingfa*) or the *Qingyuan tiaofa shilei*? That would give a date within 1111–18 and the exact wording.
3. **Shiwu guilds**: is there any other Song attestation of a 師巫行, or of mediums registered as a *hang*? Davis (2001) on Lin'an mediums.
4. **Zhu Yu's date**: confirm that the *Pingzhou ketan* preface is dated 1119.
5. **The New Gate wazi's founding date**, and whether the Jinglong abbey stood there in 1178 (check the *Xianchun Lin'an zhi* or the *Qiandao Lin'an zhi* of 1169).
6. **Landless vassal under a celestial hegemon**: does a landless holder at Xiaozong's court work, and can she be bookmark-playable? Does the map need All Under Heaven?
7. **Silk & Silver**: can a merchant company be placed in 1178 history files at `c_hangzhou`?
8. **Whether 女道 reverted to 女冠 in 1120**: the *Song shi* line names only the monks. Check the *Song huiyao* (*daoshi*).
9. **Ebrey's translation of §3.50**: check her rendering of 道婆 and 針灸 before writing loc from it.
10. **The 人妖 entry's Zhao commander** (趙忠惠): identify him only if the entry is ever used, which this design does not do.
