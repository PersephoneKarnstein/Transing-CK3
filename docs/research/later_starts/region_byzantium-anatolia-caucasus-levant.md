# Starts at 1066 and 1178: Byzantium, Anatolia, the Caucasus, the Levant

Region: the Byzantine world, Anatolia, the Caucasus (Georgia, Armenia), the Levant and the Crusader states. Our faith here is **Adelphai Dorleyaiou**, seated at Dorylaion (`b_dorylaion`, `c_phrygia`).

Written 2026-10-20 for the Silk & Silver question. Nothing in the repo was edited. "Note" means a project research note in `docs/research/`; page numbers come from those notes unless I say I read the source myself. **Unverified** marks anything I could not check.

## 0. The short answer

- **1066: same start, new flavour.** The seat, the host society and the institutions are the same as at 867 in every way the test cares about. Leo VI's Novel 60 is still law, eunuch offices still exist and the city still stands. What has changed is the direction of travel: decline since 1057, the schism of 1054 with Humbert's charge about eunuch clergy, and Turkmen raids reaching Phrygia. Stand-ins plus a "last decade of the road" event set are enough.
- **1178: new start with new characters.** The premises have changed at the root. Dorylaion was **abandoned for almost a hundred years**. Turkmen herders grazed its plain, and Manuel I rebuilt it in 1175 as a garrison on a new site 3 km from the old city. A Mother House cannot have sat there unbroken from 867. The court is now governed by the Komnenian family, and eunuchs are out of the front rank. The church has the 1140s Bogomil trials behind it. And the texts have changed: Anna Komnene's circle produced the first Byzantine commentary on Aristotle's *Generation of Animals*, the faith's main authority. The best 1178 start is **the House in exile**: a small seat in the west, at Kyzikos, a daughter inside the Constantinople convent system, and a decision to go home to the new Dorylaion.
- **New hubs:** none in the region is strong. Constantinople in 1178 is the most plausible second centre. Thebes (a house of lay virgins, 1166–c.1190) and Chonai (the archangel's shrine, with a eunuch bishop) are plausible flavour. The Seljuk and Turkmen frontier, Zengid Aleppo, Crusader Jerusalem and Tamar's Georgia are thin.

## 1. Verdicts for Adelphai Dorleyaiou

### 1066: same start, new flavour (stand-ins are acceptable)

**Who holds the seat.** In history, Byzantium holds it. Spingou calls Dorylaion "a thriving city during the tenth and eleventh centuries", abandoned only after the Turkish invasions (Spingou 2011, p. 138, citing Vryonis 1971, p. 123). In the game, vanilla `c_phrygia` passes in 1060 to a Greek count (char 2052701, Alexandros, born 1042; `history/titles/k_nikaea.txt`). So the map premise holds.

**The gender order and its categories.** These are unchanged in law. Novel 60, which allows castration on a physician's word, and Novel 98, which names three kinds of people, were never repealed (Guilland 1943, pp. 199–201). The *Kletorologion* offices are still in place.

What has shifted is practice and politics:
- **Isaac I (1057–59).** From his reign, emperors "do not seem to have undergone the domination of eunuchs" (Guilland 1943, p. 230).
- **Michael VII and Nikephoritzes.** The eunuchs return briefly in the 1070s under Michael VII, with Nikephoritzes as "the last example of the all-powerful eunuch ministers" (Tougher 2008, p. 119).
- **Female sole rule is recent memory.** Zoe and Theodora were acclaimed *autokratores* together in 1042. Theodora ruled alone in 1055–56, starting with eunuch ministers. The patriarch Keroularios opposed her appointing clergy, which was "deemed a masculine privilege" (Garland 1999, pp. 144, 166–67).
- **A regent under oath.** Eudokia Makrembolitissa becomes regent in 1067 and is bound by a written oath not to remarry (Garland 1999, pp. 169–70).

**Institutions.** All the 867 institutions still exist: the court offices, the empress's chamber, eunuch monasteries and convents with eunuch stewards. Attaleiates' house for eunuch monks follows in 1077 (Sidéris 2010, note). Nothing new opens up.

**Texts.** The texts are mostly the same. Two additions:
- **Psellos.** His *Chronographia* (already on the TODO as "Psellos's 'feminine nature' (c. 1066)").
- **The schism.** Humbert's bull of 1054 accuses Keroularios of castrating his friends and making them bishops "like Valesius" (Tougher 2008, p. 70). This gives a fresh, *Latin*, hostile voice.

**Neighbours.**
- **The Seljuks are now a real neighbour.** The Seljuqs practised castration inside their own lands "for sexual and political motives" (El-Azhari 2019, pp. 332–34, note). At Manzikert in 1071, Alp Arslan's forces were commanded by the eunuch Taranges (Tougher 2008, p. 126, note).
- **Raids reach the region.** Turkish raids reached Phrygia and its neighbours in 1067–70. **Unverified:** the exact dates of the raids on Amorion and Chonai are from memory, so check them in Vryonis 1971.

**Why not a new start.** None of the four premises (seat, gender order, institutions, neighbours' law) is different at the start date. The difference is that the clock is running: within about 15 years the seat is lost. That is flavour, not a new premise.

**Suggested flavour at 1066:**
1. **"The last decade of the road."** An event chain keyed to Manzikert (1071) and the loss of the plateau. The Mother House may evacuate its books and its Protectress west to Kyzikos, and this sets up the 1178 start.
2. **"Like Valesius."** A Latin legate repeats Humbert's charge against the Sisters.
3. **Theodora's precedent.** A Keroularios-type reaction to a woman of the faith who appoints clergy (already proposed in the Garland note, row 3).
4. **Ladder timing.** Start the suppression ladder at its 1057 setting. The 1081 acceleration stays as it is.

### 1178: new start with new characters (relocated seat, with a return)

**Who holds the seat.** In history, a new imperial fortress, three years old.
- **Abandonment.** The city "was abandoned for almost a hundred years" because of continuous warfare (Spingou 2011, p. 139, citing Vryonis 1971, p. 188).
- **Fighting over the site.**
  - The First Crusade drove Turkish settlers out in 1097.
  - Nomads came back.
  - Manuel drove them out after 1159, and they returned again (Spingou 2011, p. 139).
  - Kinnamos says two thousand nomads were settled there with their flocks (Kinnamos 295, cited in Spingou 2011, p. 139 n. 13).
- **The new site.** The 1175 Dorylaion was "a new construction, 3 km north of the old city" (Spingou 2011, p. 137 n. 3, citing Bonis and Stone 2003).
- **After Myriokephalon.** After the defeat of 17 September 1176, Manuel agreed to demolish Dorylaion and then kept it, and Kılıç Arslan II answered by ravaging the Meander valley. Source: Wikipedia, citing Choniates. **Unverified** against Choniates himself; see Magoulias' translation and Magdalino 1993, pp. 95–98.
- **A court poem.** One poem on the rebuilding, possibly by Tzetzes, describes the Turkish conquest of the city as the rape of a young girl (Spingou 2011, p. 141). Any use of it needs care, and it must stay out of loc.

In the game, vanilla gives `c_phrygia` **and** `c_mysia` (Kyzikos) in 1178 to John Doukas, the *megas hetaireiarches* (`doukas_3`). Culture stays Greek. So the map does not show the abandonment. The mod has to say it.

**The gender order and its categories.** These have changed:
- **Family government.** Komnenian rule replaced eunuch ministers with the emperor's family. Alexios's right hands were his mother and his wife (Tougher 2008, p. 125; Guilland 1943, p. 232). Under the Komnenoi "the eunuchs make little noise", still numerous but no longer in front-rank posts (Guilland, p. 232).
- **Who left the eunuchs useful.** Tougher's reason: the Paphlagonian dynasty had shown that "eunuchs could have agendas of their own" (p. 125).
- **Recruitment.** The Anatolian recruiting grounds, Paphlagonia above all, were lost or disrupted (Tougher 2008, ch. 8, pp. 119–27).
- **Women in power.** Anna Dalassene ruled by chrysobull from 1081 for perhaps fifteen to twenty years. Garland's rule: women "retained [authority] in periods of transition and crisis". In 1178 Manuel is alive and has an heir, so women at court have little power until his death in 1180 (Garland 1999, pp. 190–92, 198).
- **The law is unchanged.** Novel 60 was still never repealed (Guilland, p. 199 ff.).

**Institutions.** These have both narrowed and changed shape:
- **The Kecharitomene typikon (c. 1110).** No men may enter except two priests, the steward and the confessor, and all four must be eunuchs (Tougher 2008, p. 78; Guilland 1943, p. 205). In 1178 this is the *live* model for the faith's Houses, not a precedent from the future, as it is at 867.
- **The Pantokrator typikon (1136).** The hospital's women's ward is staffed by a woman physician and women attendants. Source: secondary web summaries; **unverified** against Gautier 1974 or Thomas and Hero 2000.
- **The Athos ban (1083).** Eunuchs are barred from Athos (Guilland, p. 232). The Kosmosoteira typikon (1152) bans eunuchs except wealthy ones (Tougher 2008, p. 76).
- **The Theban parthenon (1166–c. 1190).** Founded at Thebes by the metropolitan John Kaloktenes, it housed lay virgins who lived ascetic lives *without monastic vows* (Wu, *Journal of Ecclesiastical History* 77.1, pp. 26–41; DOI 10.1017/S0022046924001477; first published online c. 2025, **year to confirm**).

**Texts.** These have changed the most, and in the faith's favour:
- **Michael of Ephesus.** He wrote the earliest surviving commentary on Aristotle's *Generation of Animals*, inside the Aristotelian revival "conceived and guided by" Anna Komnene. Sources: Browning 1962, via the publisher's description of Coughlin's translation (Bloomsbury, *Ancient Commentators on Aristotle*, books 1–2); Garland 1999, p. 196. **Unverified:** whether Michael comments on *GA* 784a or 787b, the "female condition" passages. If he does, the Aristotelian rite gains a contemporary Byzantine gloss to argue with.
- **Theophylact of Ochrid, *In Defence of Eunuchs* (c. 1100–07).** It argues that castrating a consenting boy within his family is a Christian act, while adult self-castration is abhorrent, and that the laws against castration are "outmoded" (Tougher 2008, pp. 75, 98, 108). It is the period's own apologetic, and it is the faith's natural opponent: it defends the cut while keeping them "not women". Adult self-castration is exactly what the Gift Alone does.
- **Balsamon's canon commentary (c. 1170).** Commissioned by Manuel and Patriarch Michael III; it covers Trullo canon 62 on cross-dressing (Wikipedia/New Advent; Perisanidi 2014). **Unverified:** that Balsamon describes clergy or notaries of his own day dressing as women at festivals. I recall the claim but could not confirm it.
- **The Bogomil record.**
  - *Alexiad* 15.8–10: Basil the Bogomil is burned in the Hippodrome, and Anna calls his female disciples "wretched women of loose habits" (Fordham Sourcebook).
  - 1143: the Cappadocian bishops Clement of Sasima and Leontios of Balbissa are condemned.
  - 1144: the monk Niphon is condemned.
  - 1146–47: Patriarch Kosmas II is deposed for favouring Niphon.
  - The scholarly caution: in the 1140s "Bogomil" was often a label pinned on people who had nothing to do with dualism. Sources: Wikipedia and a Łódź journal; read Hamilton and Hamilton 1998, *Christian Dualist Heresies*, before using any of this.

**Neighbours and law.**
- **The church already knows how to try heresy.** Mechanically, the suppression ladder is *already climbed* in 1178.
- **The Seljuk sultanate of Rūm** under Kılıç Arslan II is the dominant Anatolian power. 1178 is about the year he finished off the Danishmendids.
- **Latin hostility is stronger.** Latins live in the capital, and Latin canon law is hostile to eunuch clergy (Liudprand; Humbert).
- **What comes after 1178.**
  - Manuel's death in 1180 opens a regency under Maria of Antioch.
  - Andronikos I follows in 1182, with the massacre of the Latins.
  - Eunuchs "return under Andronikos I and the Angeloi" (Tougher 2008, pp. 125–26).

**Verdict.** Every one of the four premises has moved: the seat (abandoned and rebuilt on a new site), the institutions (convent stewardship in place of front-rank office), the texts (Michael of Ephesus, Theophylact, Balsamon, the Bogomil trials) and the law around it (the heresy machinery of the 1140s). A 1178 start deserves its own characters. **Do not drop the faith**: the historical record of the Phrygians who fled west gives a plausible way it survived. **Do drop Dorylaion as the start seat** and make returning there the arc.

**No better hub than Dorylaion exists for 867 or 1066.** For 1178 the better hub is **not one place**: it is the pairing of the exile seat at Kyzikos with Constantinople's convent system.

## 3. Start sketch: 1178, "The House in Exile"

**Seat and holder.** The Sisters' House at **Kyzikos**. The Mother of Mothers holds it as a theocratic vassal, either of John Doukas, who holds Mysia and Phrygia in vanilla, or of the emperor directly; the vassal relationship is the same as at 867. Phrygia stays with John Doukas. The faith starts with:
- a **pressed claim on Dorylaion** (`b_dorylaion`), turning the 867 "pressed claim on Phrygia" into the arc;
- a scripted **"Go Home to Dorylaion"** decision.

The decision needs the new fortress to stand. If the Mother House is re-seated at Dorylaion before the Seljuks take the plateau, the faith gets the eminent holy site back. The evacuated books, relics and Protectress line come home.

**Fictional starter characters.** All of them are invented, and no historical figure is claimed.
1. **The Mother of Mothers in exile** (about 60). Born at Kyzikos to a refugee House, she has never seen Dorylaion, and she knows the Mother House only from the evacuation inventory her predecessor kept. Her wing's House of the knife is chosen in the first year, as at 867.
2. **A daughter in the convent ring at Constantinople** (about 40). Cut as a Paphlagonian child for a palace career that dried up under the Komnenoi, and taken as a daughter by the House as an adult. She is now **steward of an aristocratic convent** whose typikon requires a eunuch steward, on the Kecharitomene model. The typikon counts her a eunuch; the House counts her a woman. This is the existing TODO "Steward at the Gate", now as a starting character. She is the faith's ear at court and its route to adoptive daughters.
3. **A Marked young scholar** (about 20). She copies Michael of Ephesus's commentary on *Generation of Animals* for a Constantinopolitan patron. She drives the Aristotelian-against-Galenic council fight and the faith's answer to Theophylact's *Defence*.
4. **A Marked young woman of the Phrygian frontier** (about 16). Her Greek family lived under Turkmen herders on the plain and came in when the fortress was rebuilt in 1175. She is the faith's first possible arrival at New Dorylaion. Avoid exoticism: her family are ordinary Greek Christian farmers.
5. **Antagonist (fictional).** A canonist in the patriarchal chancery, in the mould of Balsamon. He holds the Bogomil files of the 1140s and opens the "Cybele in Christian Clothes" case.

**Pressures:**
- **The road east.** Kılıç Arslan wants Dorylaion razed, and Myriokephalon has just shown the empire's limits (Spingou 2011, pp. 137–38). The return decision can fail if the fortress falls.
- **Family government.** Court offices go to kin, so the four court offices for daughters should cost more or be gated behind a crisis. They come back with a regency, as in 1180–82, or with Andronikos.
- **Heresy machinery.** The suppression ladder starts at its post-1081 speed, with the books already burned in the Bogomil trials. A charge of "Bogomil" (the 1140s label) can be pinned on the faith, and it is always voiced by an attributed hostile character.
- **Latins in the capital.** Latin-faith neighbours carry Humbert's charge, "like Valesius".
- **The succession crisis ahead.** If Manuel dies in 1180, a "transition" window opens for women of the faith. This is Garland's rule (p. 198), and it is good for the faith.

**The gate.** Keep the knife: the Day of Blood and the Hilaria, and Leo's law on a physician's word. Two 1178 additions:
- **"The Veil Without the Vow".** Optional novice stage, after the Theban parthenon. A Marked woman can live in a House as a consecrated lay virgin before, or without, the knife. This gives the Gift Alone wing a gentler on-ramp. Source: Wu, JEH 77.1.
- **"The Steward's Daughter".** The adoption path runs through the convent ring. A cut child of the court system who comes into a convent's service can be fostered by the steward-daughter, extending the existing *Foster as Daughter* interaction.

**Existing mechanics that carry over:**
- **Unchanged:** the three Houses of the knife, *Take* and *Foster as Daughter*, the Protectress and the lot (the Kecharitomene is *contemporary* in 1178), Sister-Making, *Rescue the Daughters*, "Cybele in Christian Clothes", the suppression ladder, and growth.
- **Changed:** the court offices (re-gate them) and the seat (Kyzikos plus a return decision).
- **The 867-only events** (Liudprand's *carzimasia*, the emperor halting at the Mother House) must be date-gated out. Replace the second with Manuel's army stopping at New Dorylaion in 1175–76: Dorylaion is an *aplekton* again (Spingou 2011, p. 137).

**Silk & Silver fit.** S&S roads form where trade runs for years. The return to Dorylaion is the rebuilding of a road station on the road east, which fits the existing note in `docs/silk_and_silver.md`. A Thebes or Constantinople textile link is possible but needs sources.

## 4. Counter-evidence: what closed or worsened

- **The seat itself.** Dorylaion was abandoned for about a century, about 1080 to 1175 (Spingou 2011, p. 139). The 867 premise of a continuous Mother House fails here.
- **Eunuch office.** It declined from 1057 and sharply from 1081 (Guilland 1943, pp. 230, 232; Tougher 2008, pp. 119–27). Athos was closed to eunuchs in 1083. The Kosmosoteira typikon of 1152 bars eunuchs unless they are rich.
- **Recruitment.** Paphlagonia and Anatolia were lost as the court's recruiting ground (Tougher, ch. 8). That means fewer cut children in the system, and so fewer daughters to adopt from it.
- **Heresy prosecution.** It sharpened: Basil the Bogomil burned (c. 1100; **date unverified**), the 1140s trials and a deposed patriarch.
- **Popular and literary hostility to eunuchs in the 12th century:**
  - Kedrenos: "If you have a eunuch, kill him."
  - Manasses' viper that dies of a eunuch's poisonous blood (Tougher 2008, p. 103; Guilland, pp. 234–35).
- **Crusader law.** The Council of Nablus (1120) prescribes burning for sodomy (canon 8) and castration as a punishment for male adulterers and for sex with Muslims (canons 5, 12–15). It also bars Muslims from Christian dress (canon 16). Kedar (1999) derives much of this from the Byzantine *Ecloga*. Source: Wikipedia, Council of Nablus; read Kedar, *Speculum* 74 (1999) and Mayer 1982 before use. Frankish lords should treat daughters at least as harshly as the Orthodox; the knife as punishment is the dark mirror of Aunt Bea's House.
- **Latin canon law against eunuch clergy:** Liudprand (968) and Humbert (1054) (Tougher, pp. 70, 116–17).
- **Female power under the Komnenoi** depended on crisis and kin, not on office (Garland 1999, p. 198). It is no new opening for a sect of daughters.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Exact date Dorylaion fell** after 1071. Read Vryonis 1971, pp. 110–23, 185–88, and Stone, "Dorylaion Revisited", *REB* 61 (2003), 183–99. Also: was anyone (a bishop, a village) still there? Dorylaion's bishop is reported active through the period (Wikipedia), but **this is unverified and probably titular**.
2. **What happened to Dorylaion after 1176**, and when did the Seljuks finally take it? This decides how long the return decision can stay open.
3. **Michael of Ephesus on *GA* 784a and 787b.** Read the CAG XIV.3 Greek or the Coughlin translation. If he glosses the eunuch "female condition", it is the strongest new text for the faith.
4. **Balsamon on Trullo canon 62.** Does he record people of his own day, clergy or notaries, dressing as women at festivals? **Unverified.**
5. **Was Kyzikos Byzantine and inhabited in 1178?** If not, use Nikomedeia, Nicaea or Constantinople's suburbs as the exile seat.
6. **The Pantokrator women's ward.** Check the woman physician against Thomas and Hero 2000.
7. **Wu's article.** Confirm the year, and whether the parthenon's virgins had any steward or eunuch staff.
8. **Christian sects under Seljuk Rūm.** Was there real legal shelter for heterodox Christians? Read Peacock and Yıldız 2013.
9. **Gender categories in Turkmen and Seljuk society.** I found no scholarship on eunuchs at the Rūm court before 1200, beyond Seljuq practice generally (El-Azhari).
10. **Design choice for the user.** At 1066, should the faith start with the evacuation already done, as a "doomed seat" start, or keep Dorylaion and let the player try to hold the plateau? The second is more fun, but it is counterfactual. A 1066 Byzantine Phrygia holding out after 1071 would be the player's story, not history.
