## 1. The argument in brief

The regional pass was right that 1178 needs a new start for the Sisters, and right about why. It was wrong about one thing, the seat, and the sources make the start more interesting than the sketch.

1. **Dorylaion.** The city lay empty of Greeks for about a century, from the Turkish takeover of the 1070s–80s. The best evidence is Kinnamos (Bonn 294–295, Brand p. 220), Vryonis (pp. 123, 153) and TIB 7 (pp. 117, 240). No source gives a year for its fall.
   - Manuel rebuilt it in forty days in 1175 and settled "Romans" there with a garrison (Kinnamos Bonn 297, Brand p. 222).
   - After Myriokephalon (17 September 1176) he promised to demolish it. He razed Soublaion instead and **kept Dorylaion** (Choniates vD 189, 192; Mag. pp. 107–108).
   - It was probably lost c. 1180–82, after his death. No source dates the loss (TIB 7 pp. 119, 240).
   - So in 1178 New Dorylaion is a three-year-old frontier fortress whose fate is open. That is a good place for a return arc to aim.
2. **Kyzikos is not a viable seat.** The 1063 earthquake ruined it. Settlement and the metropolitan's see moved "gradually" to nearby Artake (TIB 13 pp. 708–709, 714).
   - Vanilla agrees: province 743 (Kyzikos) has `holding = none` from 1063.1.1.
   - In 1178 the House cannot sit on a barony that does not exist. Kyzikos stays a holy site and a memory. The seat goes next door, to **Mysia** (`c_mysia`): the Rhyndakos country, where Manuel mustered the 1175 army (Kinnamos Bonn 294, Brand p. 220).
3. **The texts.** The texts of the 1170s are the heart of a 1178 start, because each one speaks to the faith's central claim (that the cut are women):
   - the *Logos* in defence of eunuchs (c. 1100–07);
   - Michael of Ephesus on *GA* V;
   - the Kecharitomene, Pantokrator and Kosmosoteira typika;
   - Balsamon and Zonaras on the canons (commissioned 1170–78).

   They argue for the court's eunuchs and against them, and none of them simply calls the cut men:
   - Michael says the castrated animal "changes into the female" as a whole, through the heart (CAG XIV.3 p. 245).
   - Zonaras says self-castrators are "neither men nor women" (R&P II 31).
   - Theophylact's defender declines even to answer the charge that eunuchs are "reduced to the state of a woman" (Gautier c. p. 317, page *unverified*; Ringrose p. 199).
   - Balsamon describes clergy who "act out women's works" for laughter in Hagia Sophia at Christmas and Epiphany (R&P II 451).
4. **Galli and Kybele survive only in footnotes.** No Komnenian source found mentions a living galli or Mother cult. They appear only as lexicography, in Eustathios on the *Odyssey*. The faith's memory of Pessinus has no contemporary echo except a scholar's glossary. That suits the faith page's own warning against nostalgia.

**The site.** Spingou (p. 137 n. 3, citing Stone) puts the 1175 town 3 km north of the old city. Stone p. 183 places it at Sarhüyük, northeast of Eskişehir. TIB 7 p. 240 insists it is the same site. Vanilla's Turkish cultural name for `b_dorylaion` is `cn_sarhoyuk`, so the map already follows Stone. Loc can say "the new walls" without settling the dispute.

**Bishop.** TIB 7 p. 240 lists no bishop of Dorylaion after a 10th-century seal. In 1178 there is no see to compete with the House, and none to protect it.

**The poem.** Spingou argues the refortification poem was performed at Dorylaion for the court and soldiers, not for locals (pp. 141–142). Its image of the conquered city as a ravished girl (p. 141) is kept out of loc (§5).

### 2.2 Kyzikos and the alternatives

**Kyzikos** (TIB 13, all verified):
- **Ruin after 1063.** The 1063 earthquake ruined the great Hadrianic temple, perhaps a church by then, and Psellos asked for money to rebuild it. Settlement and the see then moved "presumably gradually" to Artake (Erdek), 8 km west-northwest (pp. 420, 708). Later "Kyzikos" means Artake (pp. 709, 714).
- **Turkish occupations, four times:**
  - 1080/81 (*Alexiad* 2.3, Sewter p. 54, n. p. 488);
  - after 1086, by the emir Elchanes, retaken by Opos "at the first assault" (*Alexiad* 6.13, Sewter pp. 180–181). This is book 6, not book 10 as some summaries say;
  - 1113, by Monolykos' force (*Alexiad* 14.5, Sewter pp. 414–415);
  - then nothing until the Latins in 1206 (p. 708).
- **A working metropolis.** Metropolitans are attested throughout the century, including Ioannes in 1170, 1173 and 1177 (p. 713). So in 1178 Kyzikos-at-Artake is a working Orthodox metropolis under a resident metropolitan, a hostile neighbour for a heretic House.

**Refugees moving west.** This is attested repeatedly:
- Alexios I hunted out former inhabitants to resettle the coast (Vryonis p. 169).
- In 1116 he marched the Greeks of the Philomelion and Konya region west inside his army (Vryonis pp. 169 n. 205, 172).
- In 1146 Manuel resettled Philomelion's freed captives in Bithynia (TIB 7 p. 360; Magdalino p. 42).

A Phrygian House that went west to the Marmara is therefore plausible. Its survival as a *heresy* across a century is the mod's invention.

### 2.3 The typika: eunuchs and women's houses in the twelfth century

All of these are in force in 1178. For the 867 start they are precedents from the future; here they are the living institutions.

- **(27) Kecharitomene** (Irene Doukaina, 1110–16; ch. [80] added c. 1120s per Gautier). From `byz_thomas_hero_typika.md`, checked against Gautier 1985:
  - **The steward** "must be a eunuch of godly life" ([14], pp. 677–678).
  - **Two priests**, "eunuchs and monks" ([15], p. 678).
  - **The confessor** is "preferably" a eunuch. The Greek *malista* is softer than Jordan's translation ([16], p. 679).
  - **The doctor** is "a eunuch or an old man" ([57], p. 696).
  - **The enclosure** is "continuously closed even to eunuchs themselves" ([80], p. 710).
  - **The protectorate** runs down Anna Komnene's female line "as long as the female line continues" ([80], p. 709).
  - **Anna Komnene** retired there with her mother "not tonsured" (Garland 1999, p. 198, via the project note) and was dead by 1178. The protectorate in 1178 is held by one of her daughters or granddaughters, under [80]. Who held it is *unverified*.
- **(28) Pantokrator** (John II, 1136):
  - The hospital (*xenon*) has fifty beds in five wards, one for women ([36]–[38]).
  - The women's ward has "two doctors… accompanied by one female doctor, four certified female assistants, two auxiliary female assistants, and two female orderlies" ([38], p. 757).
  - The female doctor is paid "three similar nomismata including her food allowance" ([52], p. 763). The editors note that "the male doctors were paid more than the female doctors" (general introduction; page not recorded, *to check*).
  - This verifies the regional pass's unverified claim.
  - On eunuchs, the Pantokrator commemorates "the late eunuch John the *mystikos*" among the emperor's household (p. 743). It makes no rule about eunuchs.
- **(29) Kosmosoteira** (Isaac Komnenos, Anna's brother, 1152, near Bera in Thrace):
  - No eunuch monks: "this cause of turmoil to natural habits and to morals" ([3], p. 800).
  - The exception: a very wealthy man who endows the house may be received "even if the individual joining it is a eunuch, prominent on account of his station in life, and his wealth" ([55], p. 824).
  - It also forbids alms to women at the gate, to keep "the harm… from the arrival of women" away ([56], p. 824).
  - The rule as a whole: eunuchs and women alike are a danger, and money opens the door.
- **(31) Areia** (Leo of Nauplia, 1143 / c. 1149):
  - He founded a convent for thirty-six nuns, then moved it inland to Bouze for fear the nuns "might fall prey to pirates" (pp. 954, 961).
  - Leo sat on the 1157 synod that condemned Soterichos Panteugenos (p. 954).
  - Women's houses in the 1140s were provincial and exposed, and their bishops sat on heresy synods.
- **(30) Phoberos** (1113/40): its eunuch story concerns the abuse of a child (pp. 941–942). **Keep out** (§5).

**What this means for 1178.** There are two live models, and the faith sits between them:
- **Kecharitomene**: eunuchs serve women at the threshold and are kept outside the wall.
- **Kosmosoteira**: eunuchs are barred, unless rich.

The Sisters' Houses answer both: the cut are let in, and not for money.

### 2.4 Theophylact's *Logos* and the three Houses

**What the text is** (Mullett 2002, verified):
- **Title.** It is a *logos*. "In Defence of Eunuchs" is a modern title (pp. 180, 191 n. 1).
- **Date.** Most likely c. 1100–07, set "at Thessalonica while the king was there". Theophylact's brother, almost certainly the eunuch Demetrios Hephaistos, died probably in 1107 (pp. 178, 183–184). Ringrose's "eleventh-century" (pp. 26, 70, 87) is the outlier.
- **Form.** Two speeches overheard in Thessalonike. An ascetic rebukes a eunuch for having his young nephew castrated, and the nephew is present throughout (pp. 179–180, 184).
  - **Correction to the regional pass and to `byz_ringrose_perfect_servant.md`:** the critic is not "from Athos", and Ringrose's "hid behind a curtain" (p. 27) is not borne out.
  - Mullett and Ringrose disagree on whether Demetrios was clergy of Hagia Sophia (Ringrose p. 27; Mullett p. 178).

**The arguments.** Gautier pages are *secondhand*, via Ringrose, Mullett and Sidéris:

|---|---|---|

| The ascetic also "modified nature"; the barren fig tree; the sixth finger; fasting reshapes the body too | 297.13–299.23 | Ringrose pp. 48–49, 196 |

**Matthew 19.12.** The *Logos* does not set out the three kinds of eunuch. Theophylact's Matthew commentary reads the verse metaphorically and calls self-cutting accursed (Mullett pp. 181–182; PG 123, cols. 352–353, *unverified*).

**How scholars read it:**
- Ringrose: the crown of a rising acceptance (pp. 27, 120–127, 194–202).
- Mullett: a personal piece that proves no general shift (pp. 183–186). She also says it is "less about gender or even masculinity than about… eunuchism and asceticism" (p. 186).
- Tougher 2008, p. 108 (via the project note): the defender keeps the "traditional view that eunuchs are indeed inherently feminised". The amateur text does not plainly support that reading; *check against Gautier and Messis 2012*.

**The same author elsewhere.** He also wrote an invective calling a eunuch "a Priapus in your deeds" (poem 13, Gautier 1980, p. 367; Ringrose p. 200).

**Mapping onto the faith's three Houses.** The *Logos* draws exactly the line the Houses argue over: the cut boy is acceptable, the self-cut adult is not.

- **Grandmother's House** is the *Logos*'s natural ally: someone else decides and the cut stay eunuchs. Its only quarrel is with the age. Its loc can quote the fig tree and the sixth finger with approval, then stop at the nephew. The project's age line already holds there (`byz_ringrose_perfect_servant.md` row 5): no knife before adulthood, in any House.
- **Aunt Bea's House** takes the *Logos*'s middle argument: ascetics reshape their bodies by fasting, and "the physicians of the Churches" must adapt the old canons by *oikonomia*. The uncle who arranges the nephew's cut is a dark mirror of the sponsor, and the dossier should say so in-world.
- **The Gift Alone** is precisely what the *Logos* condemns: grown people who ask for the knife, "their own murderers". But it can steal the climax. Purity "from choice… and therefore rewarded" is the Gift Alone's own claim, as long as the choice is the woman's and not her uncle's.
- **The Aristotelian rite** (main) gets its best period line from the *Logos*'s silence. The court's own apologist was told the cut are "reduced to the state of a woman" and would not argue the point. In the Sisters' voice: "He did not deny it. He said it was beneath him."
- **The Galenic splinter** takes Zonaras (§2.6): "neither men… nor women".

### 2.5 Michael of Ephesus on *GA* 784a and 787b

**The commentary.** It was printed as Philoponus (CAG XIV.3, 1903) and is now assigned to Michael of Ephesus. The attribution is usually credited to Praechter (*unverified*).
- The date (in or after 1138?) and Anna Komnene's patronage come via Browning 1962 and the Bloomsbury blurb. Both are *unverified*.
- Coughlin's English covers books I–II only, so **there is no English for the passages below**.

- **On 783b–784a (baldness), p. 230.25–32** (verified):
  - Women and children do not go bald, since "neither the child nor the female emits fertile seed".
  - Aristotle's "mutilation" is glossed as "the excision of the testicles".
  - **He does not expand** Aristotle's "change into the female condition" at this point.
- **On 787b–788a (voice), pp. 243.25–246.13** (verified):
  - The testicles tension the seminal ducts like loom-weights; without them the heart's tension slackens, so the voice rises "as in females" (244.32–245.5).
  - Then the key sentence: when the heart slackens, the castrated "necessarily change to the female voice and the rest of the form (*morphē*)… into the female (*eis to thēly*)" (245.24–28).
  - When the testicles go, the principle changes and so "the animal" changes as a whole (246.1–6).

**Reading.** Michael goes a step past Aristotle's list of signs (no baldness, voice, hair). For him the change is of the *whole form*, from the heart outward. This is the strongest twelfth-century text the faith could cite. It is also a Byzantine scholar's gloss in Anna Komnene's circle, in Greek, available in Constantinople in 1178.

**What it does not say.** It says nothing about eunuchs as persons, and nothing about women. "Into the female" is a claim about animal bodies. The faith reads it as a claim about women, in its own voice.

### 2.6 Balsamon and Zonaras on the canons

**Dates** (verified):
- The commission came from "the emperor kyr Manuel Komnenos" and Patriarch Michael of Anchialos (R&P I 31), so it falls in 1170–78. The canonist is therefore at work in the start year.
- Later strata call Manuel "of blessed memory" (R&P I 153), so the work was revised after 1180.
- He became titular patriarch of Antioch c. 1185/89 and died after 1195 (Viscuso 2005; the year is *unverified*).
- Balsamon was *chartophylax* of the Great Church before that, which he mentions himself (R&P II 115–116).

**Eunuch clergy:**
- **Apostolic canons 21–24 (R&P II 30).** Balsamon describes current practice. Careful parents who mean to cut a child "for illness" first come to the Great Church and declare the illness and the excision.
  - He names three kinds: *thlibiai*, crushed in infancy by parents; *spadones*, born so; and *kastratoi*, cut "by iron".
  - The first two may be ordained freely; the third sometimes.
- **Zonaras on the same canons (R&P II 31).** Self-castrators "remould themselves into another, strange nature", neither men, since they cannot beget, nor women, since they cannot bear. He is a Komnenian canonist, writing c. 1150s–60s (*date unverified*).
- **Nicaea canon 1 (R&P II 115–116).** Castration with Church permission is no bar even after ordination. But Balsamon "has not seen" a priest allowed to be cut for illness, "though many asked", while he was chartophylax and after. The operation was feared.
- **Trullo canon 4 (R&P II 316).** "Today" the order of cantors is formed of eunuchs only (Witt in Tougher ed. p. 247, citing PG 137.532).

**Festivals and dressing up (Trullo canon 62; R&P II 448–452).** All verified:
- Clerics at Christmas and Epiphany, "and especially in the most holy Great Church", put on masks and process in the nave as soldiers with swords, as monks or as four-footed animals. Asked why, they said "from long custom" (p. 451).
- Some precentors paint their faces and "act out women's works… to move the onlookers to laughter" (p. 451).
- The Kalends are still kept by peasants in early January; the Rousalia after Easter; the Brumalia "to Pan/Dionysos" (p. 450).
- The masked procession of "the notaries' schoolmasters on the feast of the Holy Notaries" was abolished "some years ago" by Patriarch Loukas Chrysoberges, 1157–70 (pp. 451–452; also a note on Trullo canon 71, p. 471).
- **Correction:** the "1 January" date for the notaries' feast is not in Balsamon's text.

**Reading.** The regional pass's suspicion holds, and more strongly than it guessed. In the start year, clergy of the Great Church played women for laughter at the two great winter feasts. The canonist saw it, disapproved, and was told it was custom. For the Sisters this is a hostile mirror, not a precedent. The city laughs at men dressed as women, and the same city calls the Sisters a mask.

### 2.7 Galli, Kybele and the Mother in Komnenian texts

- **Eustathios of Thessalonike on the *Odyssey*.** This is lexical only (verified):
  - "Kybēbē" is Rhea, and one possessed by her is loosely called *kybēbos* (Stallbaum I 79). Nearby, a stooping man is mocked as "half-man and half-woman" (*hēmiandros*, *hēmigynaix*).
  - *babax* glosses "*gallos*, according to the ancients" (Stallbaum I 164).
  - *mētragyrtein* means going about with drums "collecting food for the Mother, that is for Rhea" (Stallbaum II 150).
  - **Dating:** Eustathios was a teacher in Constantinople who became metropolitan of Thessalonike c. 1178. When the *Odyssey* commentary was written is *unverified*.
- **Eustathios on the *Iliad*.** He equates "*gallos*, *ektomias*, eunuch" (Rome ed. 234.31). Known only from the 1828 index, so *unverified*.
- **Scholia on Lycophron 1110** (Scheer 1908). Rhea is called *Kybēlis* from Kybella in Phrygia. Whether Tzetzes wrote this is *unverified*.
- **Balsamon on Trullo 62** names Pan and Dionysos, not the Mother (verified).
- **Not found:** no Kybele in Choniates's account of the statues destroyed in 1204, the *De signis* (not fully checked). Tzetzes' *Chiliades* could not be searched (bad OCR). Anna Komnene, Michael Choniates and Prodromos were not searched.

**Conclusion.** No Komnenian source found treats the Mother or the galli as anything but antiquarian vocabulary. A Sister in 1178 who wants her foremothers in a Byzantine book finds them in a scholar's glossary, defined next to "madman" and "half-man". That is a strong, sad event.

### 2.8 The heresy record (still mostly unverified)

These items come from the regional pass and are not re-verified here:
- Basil the Bogomil burned in the Hippodrome (*Alexiad* 15.8–10). The date is disputed: c. 1099, or c. 1111–18.
- The Cappadocian bishops Clement of Sasima and Leontios of Balbissa condemned in 1143.
- The monk Niphon condemned in 1144.
- Patriarch Kosmas II deposed in 1147.

Read Hamilton and Hamilton 1998 before writing loc.

**Verified here:** Leo of Nauplia sat on the 1157 synod against Soterichos Panteugenos (Thomas and Hero p. 954). The church of the 1140s–60s held synod after synod on heresy, and provincial bishops attended them.

### 3.1 The setting in 1178, and who holds what in vanilla

Successors on these titles from 1180.9.24 are the Komnenoi: Alexios II, then Andronikos I (215529) from 1183.9.24.

**Provinces:**

- **`b_dorylaion`** (749): a city holding, Greek and Orthodox, with no abandonment shown.

**The frontier:**

**What the map gets wrong for us.** Vanilla shows Phrygia as an ordinary Greek, Orthodox city county. It does not show the century of abandonment or the 1175 resettlement. The mod has to tell that in events, not in history files.

### 3.2 A seeding bug this start must fix first

`tm_on_game_start` (in `common/on_action/tm_on_actions.txt`) runs for every bookmark. It does three things to the holder of Phrygia that are wrong at 1178:
1. It sets `c_phrygia`'s county faith and culture to the faith. It also converts the county's holder, who in 1178 is John Doukas, a historical person who also holds Nicaea, Prusa, Mysia and the duchy.
2. It grants Phrygia to the Mother of Mothers.
3. It gives the old count `tm_daughter_of_the_knife`.

At 1178 this makes a named twelfth-century official a daughter of the knife. That breaks the project rule never to claim or imply a historical figure was trans, and it re-seats the faith in a city the sources say was a three-year-old garrison.

**Fix**, whatever else is built: gate the Phrygia block to starts before 1080 (`current_date < 1080.1.1`). At 1178, do not touch `c_phrygia`, its holder or his faith. The same applies to the stand-in at 1066, where the holder (2052701, Alexandros) is a vanilla character. Check whether he is historical before giving him the trait.

The "Mother House never forgets Phrygia" pressed-claim pulse (around line 860) would, at 1178, give the Mother a pressed claim against John Doukas at once. Replace it at this start with the return decision (§3.8).

### 3.3 The seat

**Recommendation: Mysia (`c_mysia`), held by the Mother of Mothers as a theocracy, a vassal of the emperor.** This is the same top-liege pattern the 867 code uses.
- Mysia is the Rhyndakos country, a day from the ruins of Kyzikos and on the road east that Manuel's army took in 1175 (Kinnamos Bonn 294).
- It costs John Doukas one county of four and moves no named figure.
- In loc it is "the House on the Rhyndakos", whose sisters keep the memory of the Dindymene sanctuary across the water at Kyzikos (holy site `tm_kyzikos`, county `c_nikaea`).

**County faith.** Make the House a heretic court in an Orthodox county: change only the ruler and her court, and leave the county's faith alone. A whole Greek county converting by 1178 has no support. **Tuning risk:** at 867 the faith already struggled to hold its land. If growth tests show it dying, the fallback is to convert one county, but say plainly in the faith page that this is the mod's choice.

**Alternatives considered:**
- **An unlanded start in Constantinople** (character 2 as the player character): truer to the sources, harder to play, and the faith has no landed ruler. Keep it as a second playable.
- **Nicaea or Prusa**: rejected in §2.2.
- **Kyzikos itself**: impossible without a holding. A late decision could found one (§3.8).

### 3.4 Starter characters (all fictional)

The names are common Byzantine Greek women's names. Their attestation in the 12th century specifically was not checked name by name. None is a historical figure, and none claims one.

1. **Mother Theodote, Mother of Mothers in exile** (about 62; daughter of the knife).

2. **Kale, steward of a convent in Constantinople** (about 42; a eunuch by the court's count, a daughter by the House's).
   - She was cut as a child for a palace career that dried up under the Komnenoi, an ordinary story for the 1130s (Tougher 2008, ch. 8). As an adult she came to the House and took the rite's name.
   - She is now **steward of an aristocratic convent whose rule requires a eunuch steward**, on the Kecharitomene model ([14]). The protectress confirms her; the enclosure is closed to her ([80]).
   - She is the House's ear in the City and its route to adoptive daughters. Courtier of the Mother, living in Constantinople. This puts the existing TODO "The Steward at the Gate" into a starting character.
3. **Eirene, a copyist** (about 20; Marked, not yet cut).

4. **Zoe, of New Dorylaion** (about 16; Marked).

5. **Kyriake, a lay virgin of Thebes** (about 30; a cis woman, of the parthenon).

### 3.5 How one becomes a daughter in 1178 (the gate)

**Keep the knife:** the Day of Blood on 24 March, then the Hilaria. Keep the adult-only line in every House. Three 1178 additions:

- **"Declared at the Great Church."** Balsamon's own contemporary practice: careful parents declare the illness and the excision before the Church first (R&P II 30). The 867 "physician's note" becomes, at 1178, a physician's note *and* a declaration to the metropolitan's court.
  - The declaration can be **refused**. Balsamon "has not seen" permission granted to priests (R&P II 115–116), and the *Logos* calls self-requested cutting murder.
  - Options: declare and risk refusal (an opinion hit with the local bishop, and possibly a suppression rung), go without (a secrecy flag), or have Kale arrange it through a convent physician.
  - No new rite is needed, only a branch inside `tm_knife.0004`.
- **"The Veil Without the Vow" (optional novice stage).** After the Theban parthenon (Wu 2026): a Marked woman may live in a Sisters' House as a consecrated lay virgin *before* the knife, or instead of it.
  - Mechanically, a character flag or modifier that counts her as of the House for opinion and arrivals.
  - It suits the Gift Alone best: no one is hurried to the knife.
  - Mark the Theban source in loc only as "a house of virgins without vows". The staffing is unknown.
- **"The Steward's Daughter."** Kale's adoption path. A cut youth of the court system who enters a convent's service can be fostered by her when an adult. This extends the existing *Foster as Daughter*.

### 3.6 Pressures and hostile voices

Every hostile line is attributed to a source or to a named in-world speaker.

- **The road east.** Kılıç Arslan wants Dorylaion razed under the 1176 truce (vD 189). Soublaion is already gone. The return can fail.
- **Family government.** Komnenian rule put kin, not eunuchs, in office (Guilland 1943, p. 232; Tougher 2008, p. 125). The four court offices for daughters should be harder to give under a stable Komnenian emperor with an adult or betrothed heir.
- **The 1180 window.** Manuel's death (vanilla 1180.9.24) opens Maria of Antioch's regency and then Andronikos I (1182–85). Garland's rule: women kept authority "in periods of transition and crisis" (Garland 1999, p. 198, via the note). Ch. 12 on Maria was not read.
- **Heresy machinery.** The synods of the 1140s–60s are recent memory, and the Bogomil label is available (§2.8, mostly unverified). A local metropolitan sits at Artake (TIB 13 p. 713).

**Hostile voices, all attributed:**
- **Theophylact's defender:** adults who cut themselves are "their own murderers".
- **Zonaras:** "neither men… nor women".
- **The Kosmosoteira:** eunuchs are "this cause of turmoil to natural habits and to morals".
- **Kedrenos's proverb** (Tougher 2008, p. 103): use sparingly.
- **Eustathios's** "half-man and half-woman", as a word in a glossary.
- **Latin voices:** the Latins are in the city in 1178. A Latin-faith neighbour may carry Humbert's "like Valesius" (1054). No antagonist nation: each is a person.

## 5. Content to keep out

- **The Phoberos eunuch story** (Thomas and Hero pp. 941–942). It involves the abuse of a child.
- **The refortification poem's image** of Dorylaion as a violated girl (Spingou p. 141). Not in loc.
- **Theophylact's nephew.** The *Logos* rests on a child castrated by his family. Events may cite its arguments, but no event shows or rewards cutting a child. The age line stands in every House.
- **The Council of Nablus** (1120) penal castration, from the regional pass. If it is used at all, it is a Latin legal fact in a reaction event, never a punishment for a daughter.
- **Latin and Turkish neighbours as types.** No "decadent" Turks, no "tolerant" sultanate, no antagonist nation. The Turks at the Chonai fair are traders.
- **Bogomil material** until Hamilton and Hamilton 1998 has been read.
