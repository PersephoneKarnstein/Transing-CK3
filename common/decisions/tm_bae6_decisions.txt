# Crusader Dolls — The Níð of the Land (docs/wave15/baedling.md §2). Law treats níð against a king as its own category,
# and the Icelanders, wronged by Haraldr Gormsson, made a níðvísa "for each nose" in the land (Evans and Hancock
# pp. 176-78; research/norse_evans_hancock_masculinities.md row 4). A ruler of the Bædling rite whose house is owed a
# wrong by a king's house (the Account, tm_feud_effects.txt), her own top liege or a king beside her realm, has every
# Bædling in the land make a verse against him. Each pays a little prestige; the king loses prestige and legitimacy by
# the number of noses, and his halls hear the verses for ten years. It is collective, so no one is outlawed for it,
# but a liege may count it a crime against the crown (tm_bae6.0101). Twenty years between, and ten for each king.
