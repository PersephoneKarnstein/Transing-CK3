# Daughters in a hostile world: items 33, 34, 36, 37, 38 and 42 of the October-reading revisions (faith_design.md §16).
# Hooked in common/on_action/tm_world_on_actions.txt. Every hostile line belongs to a named speaker of a vanilla faith or
# court, and is tagged here by family (inner: the seed's heat; birth: "I baptised a boy"; trace: "born to take") and
# by shape (unpersoning: "a thing"; exposing: "a deceiver"), as §16 rule 3 asks. The narrator never confirms a rumour,
# and never says a child was taken. No hostile line calls a daughter "he": where the speaker would, the text has him
# say "you" or "this one".
#
#   0001  Whispers: a tale about a daughter of the House, drawn from what the teller fears of her faith (33)
#   0002  the child-lifter rumour: a child is missing near the House (33)
#   0003  the child is found, never with the House (33)
#   0010  Many Ledgers: the church's register, the lord's roll and the House book disagree (34)
#   0011  the physician's record, and the House's counter-record (34)
#   0012  the copyist writes her in the masculine (Ha-Nehpakhot, the Jade Embryo) (34)
#   0020  questions in hall: the double bind; a sister answers for her; she may refuse a question about her body (36, 38)
#   0030  the Court Wonders: marvel, miracle or deceit; none of them friendly (37)
#   0031  the abbess keeps a monk-saint's feast and bars the gate; the Sisters answer "we imitate" (37)
#   0040  the qadi's ruling: the Sunni and Shi'i voices; the threatened re-dressing set against the chosen rite (38)
#   0041  Those Without Desire: a place in the women's rooms as one "without desire" (38)
#   0042  she insists she is a woman: the household answers (38)
#   0050  the Women's Hour at the baths (42). Never for Ha-Nehpakhot: no mikveh scene without a source.
#         Name the Purse (T23): the keeper's petition carries a seal.
#   0051  A Woman in the Line (T30a): follow-up to "go back with the sisters"; a townswoman speaks for her
#   0060  The Confessor's Book (T28): a Latin lord with a House, 1000-1346; the herb-woman and the penitential
#   0061  Women Without a Rule (T28): a House under a Catholic lord, from 1312 (Vienne)
#   0070  Ruled as a Man (T26): a chronicler of another faith writes a dead daughter-ruler down as regent or "manly"
#   0080  the mask law (T32): an Italian or Provencal commune, from 1325
#
# Round 2 voices (T13-T18). Two more shapes beside unpersoning and exposing (§16 rule 3):
#   the stranger's custom  the court denies that daughters ever lived among its own people (Oyewumi row 5)
#   the modesty plea       exclusion spoken as protecting other women (Nirta row 3): bath keepers, stewards, abbesses.
#                          The narrator never endorses it, and no text says the women were in any danger.
# and the Orthodox tag "the dualist" (Federici rows 1-2): the Sisters lumped with Paulicians, or from c. 950 under
# Bulgarian and Serbian lords the Bogomils' class charge. An accuser's charge only: the narrator never links the
# Sisters to either sect, and the Adelphai are never written as dualists.
#
# Scopes. scope:tm_world_daughter = the daughter (root in 0012 and 0041). scope:tm_world_court = the world's court
# (her liege of another faith, or a neighbour). scope:tm_world_speaker = the one who speaks against her.
# scope:tm_world_sister = the daughter of the House in 0001–0003; scope:tm_world_county = the House's county.

# Root = a ruler of a Sisters' faith; scope:tm_world_daughter; scope:tm_world_court. A man of the world's court puts
# a question to her before the hall (Serano §4 row 2). Answering patiently and objecting both cost something, and
# neither is labelled correct; patience earns no piety. A sister or friend of the House, if there is one, may answer
# for her. A question about her body she may refuse (tsq_khan_khwaja_sira.md row 1). scope:tm_world_question:
#   rib     Adam's rib, the theme the three Abrahamic neighbours share (tsq_almarri_khuntha.md row 3): Christian,
#           Islamic or (vanilla) Jewish speaker. Birth family; exposing shape. Body question.
#   body    "born to do the taking; silk does not change it" (van Anders via the Keywords note). Trace; exposing. Body.
#   origin  "How did this come about?": the court marks her (Serano row 1), and offers its causes. Inner; unpersoning.
# Round 2 questions (T13-T16), chosen by the speaker's faith and the date:
#   dualist   Orthodox speaker, any date: "Paulicians in a woman's veil; they curse the marriage bed as the devil's"
#             (Federici row 1). The dualist tag; birth family; exposing. The Adelphai answer in their own voice.
#   class     Orthodox speaker at a Bulgarian or Serbian court, from c. 950: the Bogomils' class charge, "they teach
#             servants to refuse their lords" (Federici row 2, after Cosmas). The dualist tag, class variant; a charge
#             against the House, not the body. The House may take in the servant who ran to it.
#   crow      Orthodox speaker, from 900: Lucian's crow, neither dove nor raven (Hornsby row 5). Birth; unpersoning.
#             Answer: "A crow is a bird. I am a woman."
#   heretic   Catholic speaker. Split at 1347 (Federici row 4, her dating): before, the ascetic ("they scorn the
