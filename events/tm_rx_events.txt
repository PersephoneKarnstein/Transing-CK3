#         (1250-1390) (van der Lugt 2010, p. 111 n. 34; DeVun 2021, p. 128)
#   0002  a master from Paris: the Chanter's "not both in one" and "choose one and keep it", harder after Lateran IV
#         (1191-1300) (van der Lugt pp. 106, 112-113; Baldwin pp. 13-15; Barney p. 517; Goodich p. 52)
#   0003  a consilium from Perugia: Baldus's "settled choice" rule for an heir, from 1395 (DeVun pp. 128-129)
#   0010  within our borders: Ibn Hazm's charge against al-Hakam I, at an Andalusi court (1030-1250)
#         (de la Puente 2023, nn. 15, 73-74)
#   0020  the forbidden made permitted: istihlal al-muharramat at a Mamluk court, from 1250; Ibn al-Baqaqi charged
#         1287, put to death 1302 (Rowson in Islamicate Sexualities, pp. 213-214)
#   0030  the penance ladder under a Slavic Orthodox liege, from 900, in place of the Byzantine heresy ladder
#         (Levin 1989, pp. 29-34, 73, 209, 257, 260, 297)
#
# Every hostile line belongs to a named speaker of a vanilla faith or court (faith_design.md §16 rule 3), and is
# tagged here by family and shape. The narrator does not endorse it. Where the source's category is the hermaphrodite
# or the castrate, the text says that is the speaker's book reaching for the one rule it has; she never claims it. No
# one looks at her: the canonists judged by what anyone could see and by what a person said (van der Lugt p. 110).
# No hostile line calls her "he". Daughters of Ha-Nehpakhot are never drawn into the Latin events (0001-0003).
#
# Scopes: root = the daughter (0001-0020) or the Dorleyite ruler (0030). scope:tm_rx_court = the world's court (her
# host, or her liege). scope:tm_rx_cleric = its chaplain, if any. scope:tm_rx_kin (0003) and scope:tm_rx_friend
# (0020), if any.
